# Bansal Roofing Products Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-25 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported strong YoY revenue growth of 45.77% and PAT growth of 79.19%, backed by completed Phase 5 & 6 capacity additions and entry into solar structures.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Raised (from Rs 180–200 crore to comfortably cross Rs 200 crore top-line in FY27).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted more confident; operational scaling is fully underway with order book supporting strong revenue visibility for H2 FY27.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Steel price volatility highlighted as a risk to project execution margins; details disclosed for a proposed Rs 25–30 crore insulated panel joint venture via Durapuf Insutech Panels; credit risk policy for Solar MMS clarified (strictly avoiding credit sales to unrated EPC contractors).
- Last quarter's promises: Phase 5 and 6 expansions expected by mid-September 2026 are completed and operational. Solar MMS trial production was successful, and commercial dispatches commenced in October 2026.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** Rs 49.69 crore (up 45.77% YoY from Rs 34.09 crore in Q2 FY26). H1 FY27 Revenue reached Rs 95.58 crore (up 35.99% YoY).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 4.23 crore (up 64.95% YoY from Rs 2.56 crore). H1 FY27 EBITDA stood at Rs 8.35 crore (up 48.67% YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 8.47% (expanded by 97 bps YoY from 7.50%). H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin stood at 8.74% (up 75 bps YoY).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 2.62 crore (up 79.19% YoY from Rs 1.46 crore). H1 FY27 PAT stood at Rs 5.28 crore (up 51.77% YoY).
- **PAT Margin:** 5.27% (expanded by 98 bps YoY from 4.29%).
- **Diluted EPS:** Rs 1.99 per share (vs Rs 1.11 per share in Q2 FY26). H1 FY27 EPS stood at Rs 4.01 per share.
- **Product Mix (Q2 FY27):** Roll forming products contributed 55%; Pre-Engineered Buildings (PEB) contributed 45%.
- **Geographic Mix:** Gujarat accounts for 91% of total sales revenue.
- **Rooftop Solar Output:** Meets ~53% of total factory power consumption (vs ~45% in Q1 FY27).

### Highlights
- Phase 5 and Phase 6 manufacturing expansions are fully completed and operational, adding 300 to 400 tonnes per month of lightweight fabrication capacity in PEB.
- Painting and blasting operations have shifted to Phase 6, while light fabrication has moved to Phase 5.
- Solar Module Mounting Structure (MMS) business completed 135 metric tonnes of dispatches (Rs 1.21 crore revenue) through October; holds 200 metric tonnes of orders in hand.
- Net worth increased to Rs 44.83 crore as of September 2026 compared to Rs 42.20 crore in March 2026.
- Reported return metrics for H1 FY27: ROCE at ~31% and ROE at ~24%.
- Order book stands at ~Rs 30 crore; combined with Rs 120 crore execution to date, gives strong visibility for FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- **FY27 Revenue:** Management expects to cross Rs 200 crore top-line, with H2 FY27 revenue expected to exceed Rs 100 crore.
- **FY28 Outlook:** Management expects revenue to more than double over coming years as existing capacity utilization ramps up.
- **Solar MMS Targets:** Management expects to produce and supply at least 1,000 metric tonnes (~Rs 10–15 crore revenue) of MMS by the end of FY27.
- **Insulated Panels (Durapuf JV):** Proposed continuous insulated panel line projected to generate minimum Rs 50 crore revenue in year 1 and Rs 75 crore in year 2.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Company faces minimal direct competition from major PEB players (Phenix, Interarch, Kirby) in Gujarat, as BRPL targets smaller project sizes (up to 3,000–5,000 tonnes) while major players focus on large 10,000+ tonne orders.
- Primary demand expansion focused on Gujarat, with targeted marketing efforts expanding into Vapi, Surat, Maharashtra, MP, and Rajasthan.
- Built a target database of over 1,000 solar EPC contractors across India to drive Solar MMS inquiries.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Steel raw material price increases (e.g., steel rising from Rs 50 to Rs 70/kg) directly squeeze execution margins on ongoing fixed-price projects.
- Advance collections of ~30% are immediately utilized to pre-purchase raw materials to hedge against steel price spikes.
- Automation (e.g., HB machines, prospective robotic welding) is being deployed to counter rising labor costs and statutory compliance expenses (PF, gratuity).
- Financial costs temporary offset by Gujarat state industrial policy incentives (25% capital subsidy and 5% interest subsidy).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Q2 FY27 capex was minimal and confined to routine machinery additions.
- Proposed investment of Rs 25–30 crore for a continuous sandwich insulated panel manufacturing facility under Durapuf Insutech Panels Private Limited (machinery line alone costs ~Rs 0.2 crore). BRPL will hold a majority stake (>50%).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt to equity ratio stood at 0.13x in September 2026 vs 0.07x in March 2026 (increase due to term loan taken for high-speed solar MMS machinery).
- Current ratio moderated to 1.45x in September 2026 from 1.57x in March 2026.
- Trade receivables reduced significantly to Rs 1.63 crore from Rs 3.50 crore in March 2026.
- Inventory stood at Rs 19.88 crore vs Rs 19.46 crore in March 2026.
- Standard supplier terms: Payments made within 7 days; key raw material suppliers (POSCO, JSW, Tata) require advance payments.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Steel raw material price volatility disrupting project-level profitability.
- Labor availability bottlenecks due to seasonal festivals (local labor during Navratri in Gujarat; migrant labor during Chhath in Bihar).
- Rapidly rising labor and compliance expenses.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Keshav Garg (Counter Cyclical PMS): Is Durapuf an existing concern and how much capital has BRPL paid?** Management answered that BRPL has not invested any capital yet. Durapuf is an existing entity trading in PUF panels; BRPL will acquire a majority stake (>50%) once finalized.
- **Manish Kela (Individual Investor): What gives confidence to expand Phase 5 & 6 when capacity utilization is only 50%?** Management responded that utilization will adjust down to ~40% including new phases; focus over FY28 is to ramp utilization up to 70%+ before initiating further physical expansions.
- **Lakshman Easwaran: What is driving the strategy behind PUF insulated panels?** Management explained that MNCs, data centers, cold storages, warehouses, game zones, and hospitals increasingly mandate PUF panels for thermal control. BRPL already buys Rs 4–5 crore of panels annually, making backward integration logical.
- **Vaibhav Kapur (Swaraj Holdings): Can the Rs 25–30 crore Durapuf capex be done in phases?** Management stated no, as the continuous panel manufacturing line itself costs Rs 0.2 crore up front.
- **Gobind Jain (Amrit Kaal Think Tank): Does Solar MMS require 30–45 days credit, hurting working capital?** Management stated they do not sell on credit to general buyers and maintain zero bad debts; credit is strictly restricted to top-tier EPC contractors.

### One-line takeaway
Bansal Roofing is scaling top-line growth past Rs 200 crore by operationalizing Phase 5/6 capacities, ramping up high-speed Solar MMS supplies, and executing a majority-owned JV in continuous insulated panels.
