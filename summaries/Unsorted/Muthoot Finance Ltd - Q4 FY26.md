# Muthoot Finance Ltd - Q4 FY26 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-05-20 · Unsorted*

**Quarter:** Q4 FY26 / Full Year FY26  
**Tone:** Positive – Management highlighted record consolidated AUM, nearly doubled full-year profits, and strong gold loan demand despite competitive intensity.

---

### Key numbers
*All figures are for full-year FY26 unless specified otherwise:*
- **Consolidated AUM:** Rs 1,81,916 crore (up Rs 59,000 crore, +49% YoY).
- **Consolidated Gold Loan AUM:** Rs 1,65,000 crore (up Rs 57,000 crore, +54% YoY).
- **Consolidated PAT:** Rs 10,607 crore (+98% YoY).
- **Standalone AUM/Loans:** Rs 1,64,000 crore.
- **Standalone PAT:** Rs 10,134 crore (+95% YoY).
- **Q4 Yield:** 20.76% (vs. 20.34% in Q3 FY26). Includes Rs 85 crore of one-offs (auction income: Rs 50 crore; ARC income: Rs 35 crore).
- **Average Gold Loan LTV:** 57% (Stage 3 assets LTV including interest dues is ~58%).
- **Gross NPA (Standalone):** 2.34% as of March 2026 (vs. 3.35% as of March 2025).
- **Dividend:** 300% (Rs 30 per share).

---

### Highlights
- Highest ever consolidated gold loan AUM and full-year consolidated and standalone PAT.
- **Muthoot Money (100% subsidiary):** Shifted focus from vehicle finance to gold loans. Gold loan AUM grew 151% YoY to Rs 9,794 crore; PAT rose to Rs 338 crore (vs. Rs 12 crore in FY25); parent infused Rs 1,000 crore equity, taking capital base to Rs 2,357 crore; credit rating upgraded to AA+ (Stable) by CRISIL.
- **Belstar Microfinance:** Disbursed Rs 7,500 crore in FY26 (vs. Rs 6,000 crore in FY25); collection efficiency improved to 99.85% (vs. 99.16% in FY25); opened 81 gold loan branches.
- **Muthoot Home Finance:** AUM at Rs 3,485 crore (+17% YoY); FY26 disbursements at Rs 999 crore; interest income up 36%; PAT at Rs 45 crore; GNPA at 2.63%, NNPA at 1.94%.
- Cross-sell unsecured/salaried personal loan book stands at ~Rs 4,000 crore.

---

### Guidance & outlook
- **AUM Growth:** Management maintained initial FY27 guidance of 15% AUM growth for standalone Muthoot Finance, to be reviewed after Q1/Q2.
- **Topline & Margins:** Management does not provide specific revenue or margin guidance, stating profits will follow AUM growth; medium-term normalized RoA target remains ~3.5% (generated ~7.5% in Q4).

---

### Demand & industry commentary
- Import tax hike on gold (from 6% to 15%) has no material impact as the company only lends against household used ornaments, not bullion/coins.
- Portfolio churn happens every 3–4 months; higher gold prices lower required collateral weight (tonnage) for the same loan value and increase average ticket size per borrower.
- Active customer count showed slight erosion in ultra-low ticket sizes (<Rs 30,000 loans lost ~13–15 lakh accounts), offset by ticket-size migration into >Rs 50,000, Rs 1 lakh, and Rs 2 lakh buckets.

---

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Standalone lending rates increased by 0.5%–1.0% on certain products in Q4 FY26 to capture the upside from rising gold prices.
- Management expects borrowing costs to head north; dynamic pricing will be utilized to sustain spreads and profitability.
- Interest accrual during Q4 FY26 was Rs 963 crore.

---

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- **Network Additions:** Opened 177 group branches in FY26.
- **FY27 Branch Target:** Plans to open 200–300 branches at Muthoot Finance standalone level, and ~200 gold loan branches in Belstar Microfinance. Muthoot Money branch expansion will be paused temporarily to allow existing branches to mature.

---

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- **Asset Quality Shift:** Increase in Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets on a QoQ basis was driven by RBI-mandated borrower-wise NPA classification (earlier loan-wise). 
- Stage 3 assets carry an LTV of only ~58% (including interest accrued), ensuring full collateral coverage and negligible credit loss risk.

---

### Risks & concerns flagged
- **Competitive Intensity:** Well-capitalized, AAA-rated NBFCs entering gold financing aggressively.
- **Rising Cost of Funds:** Treasury signals an increasing interest rate environment, which could put pressure on margins if not passed on.
- **Regulatory Changes:** Implementation of new RBI LTV rules and borrower-level NPA tagging.

---

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q on competitive intensity from AAA-rated NBFCs entering gold loans:**  
  *Management Response:* Gold financing is operationally challenging and intensive; opportunistic players tend to lose interest over 2–3 years. Dedicated focus, ground experience, and branch proximity are key moats.
- **Q on sharp increase in Stage 2 & Stage 3 loans during the quarter:**  
  *Management Response:* Purely an outcome of shifting to borrower-level NPA classification as advised by RBI (which pulls Stage 1/2 accounts of a defaulting borrower into NPA). Collateral covers 100% of dues with an effective Stage 3 LTV of ~58%.
- **Q on sustainability of >20% yields and pricing strategy:**  
  *Management Response:* Management declined to guide on permanent yield levels, highlighting that loans are short-term (dynamic pricing) and borrowing costs are edging up. They will adjust yields as needed to balance volume growth and profitability.
- **Q on rationale for expanding gold loans via subsidiaries (Belstar/Muthoot Money) instead of the parent:**  
  *Management Response:* Management dodged operational specifics, calling it an internal board/policy decision of the individual entities.

---

### One-line takeaway
Muthoot Finance delivered stellar FY26 earnings growth (+98% consolidated PAT) driven by high gold prices, healthy yields, and subsidiary scale-up, while dismissing competitive and asset-quality concerns due to strong 57% collateral backing.
