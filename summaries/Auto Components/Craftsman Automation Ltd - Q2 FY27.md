# Craftsman Automation Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-02 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Company is experiencing strong top-line and gross profit growth, expanding capacities, and expects margins to recover as price resets and plant operating leverage play out.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated gross debt: Rs. 3,618 crore as of September 2026 (up from Rs. 3,340 crore in March 2026).
- Consolidated net debt: Around Rs. 3,000 crore.
- Expected net debt to EBITDA for the year: 1.7x - 1.8x on a consolidated basis.
- Alloy wheel plant capacity utilization: Around 70% in the past couple of months.
- Foreign subsidiary revenue contribution: Approximately 4% of total top line (gross net block investment at 2%-3%).
- Aluminum segment EBIT margin: Declined to 6.5% in Q2.
- Projected stationary engine revenue peak: $100 million by FY'30 (reaching 30% by FY'28, 50% by FY'29, and peak run rate by Q3/Q4 FY'30).
- Expected Q3 capex (consolidated): Rs. 500 crore - Rs. 600 crore.
- Price correction range needed for legacy/high-inflation products: 6%-7% up to 15%-20% on gross margin/value addition.
- Targeted long-term CAGR: 15% (in real terms, unadjusted for inflation).

### Highlights
- DR Axion Plant-2 has started trial production with a strong order book.
- Sunbeam transformation ongoing; low-margin/negative-EBITDA legacy products being phased out over the next couple of months.
- Formed a new German subsidiary shell to set up a small in-house machine shop (estimated investment Rs. 20-30 crore across a couple of machines) to support the existing German foundry and reduce working capital intensity.
- Management stated aversion to large foreign acquisitions, preferring to keep overseas investments strategic and limited in scale.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident that year-on-year growth will continue for the next 2-3 quarters.
- Inflection point for EBITDA is expected around Rs. 1,850 crore next year, after which the company will become self-sufficient for capex and maintenance capex.
- Q4 capex is expected to moderate as long-term greenfield projects are phased into subsequent years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Powertrain growth expected to continue (except tractors, which will see contraction in the next two quarters).
- Strong demand expected for stationary engine generators supporting AI data centers over the next 5-7 years.
- India is becoming a global manufacturing and export hub for powertrains, tractors, small construction equipment, and two-wheelers.
- Gradual shift by OEMs toward outsourcing Aluminum components from in-house manufacturing.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Aluminum margins impacted in Q2 due to raw material inventory accumulation amidst supply chain panic/Middle East crisis and a sudden drop in prices.
- Management is negotiating price resets/corrections and compensation with OEMs (varying between 6%-7% to 15%-20% on value addition), expected to materialize over the next two quarters.
- New Aluminum plants are operating sub-optimally with pre-operative costs; operating leverage will take two quarters to improve profitability.
- Inflation noted across manpower, energy (gas), power, cutting tools, oil, and capital goods.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Bulk of capex for stationary engines completed; revenues to start from FY'29 and peak in FY'30.
- Greenfield facilities set up across India for powertrain and Aluminum segments; subsequent phases will require lower incremental capex as basic infrastructure is already in place.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital and inventory ballooned due to strong top-line growth, safety stock accumulation, and pipeline inventory for new plants.
- QIP proceeds used for debt repayment, but gross debt increased due to working capital expansion and capex.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in Aluminum prices and raw material availability risks.
- Headwinds from rising energy costs (gas), manpower inflation, and rising capital goods costs.
- Sub-optimal utilization and start-up costs in newer Aluminum plants dragging margins temporarily.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Chandramouli Muthiah (Goldman Sachs) on gross profit inflection and price corrections:** Management stated that strong YoY growth will persist for 2-3 quarters and price resets of 6% to 20% on value addition are being discussed with OEMs for legacy products.
- **Senthil Kumar (Capital Services) on rising gross debt despite QIP:** Management explained that working capital, GST credits, safety stock, and heavy H1 capex drove up debt, but net debt remains around Rs. 3,000 crore (targeting 1.7x-1.8x net debt-to-EBITDA).
- **Joseph George on Sunbeam margin recovery:** Management confirmed that low-margin/negative-EBITDA products for smaller customers are exiting over the next couple of months, which will improve absolute margins despite lower top line.
- **Sagar Shetty on foreign subsidiaries and M&A:** Management clarified that the new German entity is merely a small machine shop (capex around Rs. 20-30 crore) and reiterated strict aversion to large foreign acquisitions.
- **Chirag Jain on outsourcing trends:** Management highlighted that OEMs are slowly shifting from in-house manufacturing of Aluminum components to outsourcing due to rising supplier confidence and capability.

### One-line takeaway
Craftsman Automation is absorbing short-term margin pressures from commodity volatility and sub-optimal new plants while banking on operational leverage, price resets, and a massive $100M stationary engine order book to drive 15% sustained growth.
