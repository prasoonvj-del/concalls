# SKF India (Industrial) Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-19 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Solid 18.3% YoY revenue growth and robust macros, but margins were impacted by forex losses and one-time demerger-related IT expenses.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** INR 9,708 million (up 18.3% YoY, up 2.6% QoQ)
- **Profit Before Tax (PBT):** INR 869 million (PBT margin at 9.0%, down from 9.5% QoQ)
- **Cash Conversion:** 63.0% for the quarter
- **Order Wins:** INR 140 crores order with a gearbox manufacturer; INR 35 crores contract with a leading tractor OEM
- **Capex Guidance:** INR 900 crores to INR 950 crores for the new Pune plant

### Highlights
- First investor earnings call held by SKF India (Industrial) Limited following the demerger.
- Management change: Sujeeth Pai taking over as Managing Director effective September 1st, replacing Mukund Vasudevan (who moves to manage the broader Industrial Region for India, Southeast Asia, and Middle East).
- Inaugurated a new tapered roller bearing (TRB) line in Pune with a capacity of 3 million units annually.
- New product launches: Railway wheelset bearings (increasing maintenance intervals by 20%), 250°C high-temperature food and beverage bearings, super-efficient hybrid ceramic bearings, and cylindrical roller bearings for agri OEMs.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guided a near-term PBT margin of 14.0% to 16.0%, with a long-term aspiration to get closer to 17%–19% post-2028.
- Management stated an aspiration to double the business in 5 years.
- Capex payback for the new Pune plant is expected in 5 to 7 years, with production targeted to start from 2028.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India’s GDP growth estimated at 6.5% to 7.5%; inflation creeping up due to fuel and food prices alongside weak monsoons.
- Strong industrial rebound; robust outlook for India and exports.
- Steel production and wind capacity additions (India is 4th largest globally, growing ~10%) remain strong.
- Strong performance across general machinery (pumps, motors, gears), wind, heavy sectors, and rail.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- PBT margin declined to 9.0% due to INR 147 million in forex losses (driven by rupee depreciation from the Middle East conflict on imported products) and INR 150 million in one-time IT separation/demerger costs.
- One-time IT costs are expected to continue for the next two quarters.
- Channel mix: Distribution accounts for 34% of revenue (higher margin/high double-digit), and OEM accounts for 54% of revenue (high single-digit margin).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned capex of INR 900 crores to INR 950 crores (CFO cited INR 850–900 crores) for a state-of-the-art Pune plant; progressing as per plan for 2028 production.
- An additional TRB channel is scheduled to be added by early 2027 despite space constraints.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net working capital was almost flat.
- Operating cash flow remained solid, with minor reduction driven by one-time name change and land transfer charges.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Forex volatility and rupee depreciation impacting import costs.
- Inflation uptick and weak monsoons.
- Temporary margin pressure over the next few quarters due to ongoing demerger/IT costs and investments.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Viraj Kacharia (SiMPL) asked about new product white spaces, humanoids/data centers, and margin moderation.** Management answered that innovation traction is highest in general machinery (motors, pumps, gearboxes) and F&B; humanoids and data centers are early-stage opportunities in India. Management attributed margin drop to forex losses and one-time IT separation costs, guiding 14-16% near-term PBT margins.
- **Varun Jain asked about order win supply periods and customer channel margins.** Management clarified that OEM orders typically span 1–2 years; distribution delivers high double-digit margins while OE delivers high single-digit margins.
- **Gokul Maheshwari asked about the solutions business size and doubling business timeline.** Management stated solutions represent 6%–7% of the India business and have huge potential; the timeline to double the business is an aspiration targeted within 5 years.
- **Ravi Purohit asked about demerger cost timelines, factory operations, and a potential merger of listed/unlisted entities.** Management answered that IT separation costs will taper down over the next two quarters; the TRB line is under SKF Industrial; and a merger is not being actively evaluated currently as they want to let the demerger stabilize first.

### One-line takeaway
SKF India Industrial reported solid Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 18.3% YoY, while near-term margins faced temporary headwinds from forex losses and demerger-related IT expenses, alongside a strong multi-year expansion roadmap.
