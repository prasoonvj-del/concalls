# Harsha Engineers International Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Business as usual and largely on track with strong top-line growth in India Engineering and stable performance, though management noted conservatism for H2 due to seasonal softness and Romania restructuring.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Engineering Revenue (Q2 FY27):** Rs 428 crore (vs Rs 363 crore in Q2 FY26, Rs 421 crore in Q1 FY27)
- **Consolidated Engineering EBITDA (Q2 FY27):** Rs 81.1 crore (vs Rs 63 crore in Q2 FY26, Rs 69.8 crore in Q1 FY27)
- **H1 FY27 Top-line Growth (Consolidated Engineering):** Over 23% YoY
- **H1 FY27 Top-line Growth (India Engineering):** Over 22% YoY
- **H1 FY27 Exports:** Around Rs 280 crore
- **Solar Business Revenue (Q2 FY27):** Rs 11.9 crore
- **Solar Business EBITDA (Q2 FY27):** Rs 0.73 crore
- **Consolidated Working Capital Days (Q2 FY27):** Around 135 days (vs 139 days in Q2 FY26, 116 days in Q1 FY27)
- **Capex Incurred (Q2 FY27):** Rs 35.6 crore (H1 FY27 total capex around Rs 73 crore+)
- **Segment Revenues (Q2 FY27):** Bushings Rs 42 crore (up 26% QoQ), Stampings Rs 23 crore (up 19% QoQ), Large Sized Bearings (LSB) Rs 14 crore (up 35% QoQ), Japanese customer sales Rs 24 crore (up 15% QoQ)
- **Foreign Subsidiaries Loss (Q2 FY27):** Rs 1.7 crore
- **Guidance:** FY27 India revenue growth mid-to-high teens; consolidated revenue growth 10% to 12%; India Engineering EBITDA margin steady around 22%; Consolidated Engineering EBITDA margin 17% to 18%. Peak revenue run-rate for the new resourcing/outsourcing project at INR75 crore/year. Stamping and Bushing annual sales target of INR300 crore each within 3 to 4 years.

### Highlights
- India Engineering business witnessed strong top-line growth of over 24% YoY in Q2, driven by Cages, Bushings, and Stampings.
- Cages saw decent demand traction from both industrial and automotive segments in India, alongside strong export demand from Europe, U.S., and Japan.
- First resourcing/outsourcing project commercial sales expected to commence in Q3/Q4 FY27, with peak run-rate of around Rs 75 crore/year. Two other projects are in pipeline (FY27-28 and FY28-29).
- Management decided to downsize Romania operations and reduce operating costs to arrest operating losses by the end of FY27.
- Advantek expansion project (Bushings, Stampings, large-size Cages) and China brownfield expansion (steel Cages for industrial and auto) are progressing on track.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management reaffirmed FY27 guidance: mid-to-high teens sales growth in India, and 10% to 12% on a consolidated basis.
- Q3 historically tends to be soft due to the calendar year-end slowdown of international buyers.
- India Engineering EBITDA margin seen at a steady state around 22%; Consolidated Engineering EBITDA margin at 17% to 18%.
- Solar business full year revenue guided between Rs 150 crore to Rs 200 crore.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Continued strong export demand for industrial Cages from Europe, U.S., and Japan.
- Large-sized Cages expected to see significant sales ramp-up in H2 FY27, targeting at least 20% annual growth in this segment.
- Bushings growth aided by good demand visibility from key windmill gearbox manufacturers.
- Stampings traction driven by railway seal inserts, AC compressor components, and a newly introduced accumulator category.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margins expanded to 51.7%, aided by brass price pass-through (copper and zinc) and lower solar business share.
- Ongoing inflationary pressures on indirect costs remain a challenge.
- Foreign subsidiaries losses significantly reduced; Romania operating losses targeted to be arrested by end of FY27.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total announced 18-month capex stands at around Rs 220 crore (Advantek expansion and China capex), being accelerated to complete within 12 to 15 months (up to June 2027).
- Maintenance capex at consolidated level estimated at Rs 25 crore to Rs 30 crore annually.
- Incremental asset turnover on productive assets for expansion projects expected around 2x.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Comfortable at current debt levels; no major incremental borrowings planned for FY28 beyond existing expansion funding.
- Advantek total planned capex around Rs 350 crore+ (with ~Rs 250 crore debt funded).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Q3 typically soft due to calendar year-end shutdowns by international customers.
- Romania operations facing structural cost challenges, leading to planned downsizing.
- Solar business remains cyclical and governed by government import/subsidy norms.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Varun Jain (Dolat Capital) on gross margin sustainability:** Management explained margins benefited from metal pass-through and a lower solar revenue mix. They guided a steady-state India Engineering EBITDA margin of around 22%.
2. **Harshit Patel (Equirus Securities) on H2 growth vs guidance:** Management clarified that the 10-12% consolidated guidance accounts for a conservative outlook, higher metal-adjusted bases, and planned downsizing in Romania, while India remains strong.
3. **Manish Goyal (ThinQwise Wealth Managers) on Advantek financials and peak interest/depreciation:** Management stated Advantek revenue is on track for ~Rs 140 crore+, H2 will be profit-positive, and combined quarterly interest and depreciation may rise up to ~Rs 10-10.5 crore at peak.
4. **Jason Soans (IDBI Capital) on subsidiary losses:** Management stated that combined foreign subsidiaries and Advantek are expected to report a positive performance in H2.
5. **Vaibhav Shah (Equirus Securities) on Aerospace/Defense Stampings:** Management noted the current opportunity is relatively small (Rs 20-30 crore) in the context of total Stampings, while core industrial/auto Stamping growth remains large.

### One-line takeaway
Harsha Engineers delivered a steady Q2 FY27 with robust 23% H1 consolidated revenue growth and margin expansion, though management retained a cautious 10-12% full-year consolidated growth outlook due to seasonal Q3 softness and restructuring in Romania.
