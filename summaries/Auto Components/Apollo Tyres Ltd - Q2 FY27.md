# Apollo Tyres Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong revenue growth driven by pricing actions and volume, offset by raw material cost pressures and margin compression.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs. 79.7 billion (up over 16% YoY)
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs. 8.6 billion
- **Consolidated EBITDA Margin:** 10.7%
- **India Revenue:** INR 59.2 billion (up nearly 26% YoY)
- **India EBITDA:** INR 6.6 billion (Margin: 11.1% vs 15.3% YoY)
- **Europe Revenue:** EUR 157 million (down 12% YoY)
- **Europe EBITDA Margin:** 10.3%
- **Consolidated Net Debt to EBITDA:** 0.9x as on September 30, 2026
- **Consolidated Net Capex (H1 FY27):** Around INR 15 billion
- **Raw Material Cost Inflation (H1 FY27 vs H1 FY26):** Approximately 25%
- **Replacement Segment Price Increases Taken (India):** 11% to 13% average (up to 13% to 15% by end of October)
- **European PCR Volume Growth:** 5% YoY

### Highlights
- Delivered strongest YoY revenue growth in the last 16 quarters (over 16% consolidated).
- India operations saw broad-based growth across Replacement and OEM channels, driven equally by volume and pricing.
- Successfully completed SAP S/4HANA implementation for the US business; rollout underway for European and Indian operations.
- Chennai plant recognized with the CII EFI National Excellence Award for Employee Relations; AP and Kerala plants achieved Gold Level Zero Waste to Landfill Certification.
- Exceptional item in Europe of EUR 2.3 million recorded due to remaining employees in a legal entity following plant closure.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Demand (India):** Management expects reported volume growth to moderate in coming quarters due to a higher base, but revenue momentum to continue from pricing actions. Expects mid-to-high single-digit volume growth compared to last year.
- **Raw Materials:** Management expects Q3 raw material basket cost to move up around mid-to-high single digits compared to Q2, peaking towards the end of the year, with normalization/stabilization expected from next year (subject to volatility).
- **Europe:** Management remains committed to a medium-term target of building a European business capable of delivering high-teen operating margins, with stable operations expected from the next financial year post transition.
- **Leverage:** Management is confident that leverage ratios (net debt to EBITDA) will stay comfortably within targeted ranges despite ongoing expansion plans.

### Demand & industry commentary
- **OEM vs Replacement:** OEM channel led volume growth in India at 25%+ YoY, followed by Replacement in low double digits, and exports largely flattish.
- **Categories (India Replacement):** Two-wheeler segment grew 25%-26%; PCR grew mid-teens; Truck grew high single digits.
- **Europe:** PCR business gained market share (PCLT grew 5% YoY, Apollo brand PCLT northwards of 50%), while truck and agriculture segments declined due to temporary supply disruptions from the Enschede plant closure and market softness.
- **Exports:** Management remains careful and conservative on exports due to volatility, prioritizing strong domestic demand momentum.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material basket increased by ~25% YoY in H1 FY27, with natural rubber touching INR 300 in India (up 25%-26% YoY) and crude derivatives up 28%-35%. Steel cord was up low double digits.
- Management implemented replacement price hikes of 11% to 13% (reaching 13% to 15% by October) and secured price increases in OEM business.
- Controllable expenses (A&P, travel) kept in check through weekly strategy reviews and fortnightly leadership monitoring.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Net capital expenditure for H1 FY27 was around INR 15 billion, in line with full-year plans.
- Plant capacity utilization is running high at approximately 85% to 90% across plants.
- Enschede plant operations stopped at the end of Q1; dismantling and equipment sales underway (taking 2-3 quarters). Supply chain reorganization and manufacturing transition moving to Hungary and India.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated net debt to EBITDA stood at 0.9x as of September 30, 2026.
- Gross debt increased with INR 1,500 crores taken in H1 FY27 (including INR 1,250 crores from non-convertible debentures at 8.5% interest).
- Working capital in H1 impacted by plant shutdown payouts, but H2 expected to see stable working capital norms and better cash flows due to European seasonality.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Persistent geopolitical uncertainty and significant raw material cost inflation.
- Supply chain disruptions and transition-related challenges in Europe from the Enschede plant closure affecting agri and TBR segments.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Raghu Nandhan (IIFL / participant) asked about volume breakdown and raw material outlook:** Management clarified that OEMs grew 25%+, Replacement low double-digits, and exports were flattish. RM expected to rise mid-to-high single digits in Q3.
- **Siddhartha Bera asked about price hike quantum and Europe weakness:** Management stated 13-15% cumulative hikes taken by October, and Europe weakness is a mix of market slowdown and Enschede plant transition/supply chain reorganization.
- **Amyn Pirani asked about ROCE impact from CapEx and Enschede costs:** Management noted a short-term impact on ROCE during the CapEx cycle, targeting mid-teen ROCE in steady state; Enschede operational costs have ceased, with only minor cleanup/dismantling costs remaining.
- **Basudeb Banerjee asked about raw material peaking and margin recovery:** Management noted that RM is expected to peak towards the end of the year and taper down next year, while emphasizing that disciplined cost management has prevented margins from dropping to historical single-digit lows during this inflation cycle.

### One-line takeaway
Apollo Tyres delivered robust 16%+ revenue growth in Q2 FY27 led by strong domestic demand and aggressive pricing actions, though profitability faced headwinds from a sharp 25% surge in raw material costs.
