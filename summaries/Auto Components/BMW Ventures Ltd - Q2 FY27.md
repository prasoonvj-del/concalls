# BMW Ventures Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong revenue and profit growth driven by robust steel demand and expanding high-margin fabrication business.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 731 crore, up ~46% YoY from Rs 502 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **Gross Profit:** Rs 64.6 crore, up 24% YoY from Rs 52 crore.
- **Gross Profit Margin:** 8.8%.
- **EBITDA:** Rs 22.1 crore, up 11% YoY from Rs 20 crore.
- **Profit Before Tax (PBT):** Rs 15 crore, up 50% YoY from Rs 10 crore.
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 10.5 crore, up 48% YoY from Rs 7.1 crore (Q2 FY26).
- **PAT Margin:** 1.4%.
- **Fabrication Order Book:** Rs 76.5 crore as of September 30, 2026 (comprising Rs 50.8 crore from PEB and railway orders, and Rs 25.7 crore from Bihar Railway RDSO-approved unit orders).
- **Fabrication Capacity:** 24,000 metric tons installed in Purnea (12,000 tons for railway applications, 12,000 tons for PEB structures) across 87,000 sq. ft.
- **Working Capital Limits:** Rs 350 crore utilized out of limits.
- **Credit Rating:** Upgraded to A minus from BBB minus by CRISIL.
- **Interim Dividend:** Rs 1.5 per share declared by the board.

### Highlights
- Core steel distribution business remains the biggest strength, backed by ~1,200 dealers across Bihar and strong relationships with Tata Steel.
- Management stated BMW is among the top volume distributors for Tata Steel in India.
- Stepping into value-added manufacturing: welding electrode plant in Purnea is built and set to start commercial production in November under own brand; small-scale nail manufacturing is being scaled up.
- Real estate monetization plans: 16-acre land plot in Purnea planned for a housing project (after acquiring an adjoining piece); 43-acre land plot planned for an industrial park with 2-acre industrial plots. Additionally, 15 acres of land registered in Calcutta for potential future expansion.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects an average revenue and profit growth of ~25% over last year for FY27.
- By FY28, management aims to derive 5% to 7% of revenue and 10% of EBITDA from the fabrication business.
- Fabrication revenue contribution target is scaled up to 10% of total revenue over the next 3 to 5 years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Steel demand in Bihar and Eastern India remains very strong, driven by extensive government infrastructure projects (PM Gati Shakti, railways, bridges, metros, airports, ports, and highways) and central government funding.
- Steel prices are on an upward trend with manufacturers increasing prices by Rs 2,500 per ton monthly; coking coal prices are also rising with no near-term price corrections indicated.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margins in the fabrication business range between 9% to 11% (or 10% to 12%).
- Q2 EBITDA margin was impacted by a Rs 2 crore loss from selling underutilized machines in Uttarakhand that were previously kept closed/empty.
- Base business flat products yield slightly better margins than long products.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Current fabrication capacity utilization is around 25% (producing ~500 tons/month against 2,000 tons capacity); management targets reaching 60%-70% utilization.
- Additional capex requirement for fabrication is estimated around Rs 5 crore primarily for machinery, as land parcels are already available.
- Commercial production of the welding electrode plant in Purnea starts in November 2026.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Credit rating upgraded by CRISIL from BBB minus to A minus.
- Focus on improving working capital efficiency, reducing inventory days (average 45 days), reducing the cash conversion cycle, and lowering interest costs. Working capital limits utilized stand at Rs 350 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Commodity trading margins remain regulated and steady.
- Machine idling and subsequent sale led to a Rs 2 crore loss impacting Q2 EBITDA margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Madhur Rathi (CCIPL) on margin decline despite higher fabrication revenue:** Management explained the drop was due to a Rs 2 crore loss incurred from selling underutilized machinery sent to Uttarakhand.
2. **Madhur Rathi on competitive advantage against larger players (SG Mart, Shankara, JSW One):** Management attributed their "right to win" to a 25-30 year deep-rooted dealer network in Bihar, strong incentives/branding, and direct sourcing of all product categories (Long, Flat, Agrico, Wire, Structural) as a top Tata Steel partner.
3. **Dhruv Patel on railway payment cycles and working capital risk:** Management clarified they operate as subcontractors for RDSO-approved steel fabrication with strict multi-tier inspection processes (by railway engineers and paint shop checks) rather than taking direct mega-bridge contracts, insulating them from direct primary contracting risks.
4. **Harshil Patel on pricing mechanism amidst rising steel prices:** Management noted that ~66% of revenue comes via direct dealer retail where Tata Steel fixes prices, while project sales are priced competitively based on demand and scale.
5. **Mahesh Gupta on land monetization plans in Purnea:** Management stated the 17-acre land will be used to develop and sell housing plots, while the 43-acre land will be converted into 2-acre industrial plots to leverage government subsidies for industrial parks.

### One-line takeaway
BMW Ventures reported a robust 46% YoY revenue growth in Q2 FY27 supported by strong steel demand in Bihar, even as EBITDA margins saw a temporary dent from a machinery sale loss, while scaling up its high-margin fabrication and manufacturing verticals.
