# Sundram Fasteners Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong revenue and export growth, with an optimistic outlook despite raw material headwinds.

### Key numbers
- Q2 revenue growth: ~30% YoY
- Q2 PBT: Rs 160 crore (vs Rs 140 crore YoY)
- H1 revenue: Rs 3,254 crore (25% YoY growth vs Rs 2,605 crore)
- H1 PBT: Rs 311 crore (vs Rs 278 crore YoY)
- H1 EBITDA margin: 15.9%
- Q2 EBITDA margin: 15.7%
- Export growth: 57% YoY for Q2 (47% in volume/dollar terms, ~10% due to rupee weakness); 40% YoY for H1
- Contribution/gross margin dip: ~2.5% due to raw material impact
- Tonnage volume growth: 15% to 16% for Q2
- Stellantis EV-related billing: Expected Rs 150 crore to Rs 175 crore in FY27 (vs ~Rs 50 crore last year)
- Aerospace revenue: Expected >Rs 100 crore in FY27 (vs ~Rs 50 crore last year)
- Wind energy revenue target: Cross Rs 500 crore annual revenue (Phase 3 ongoing, investment of Rs 70 crore to Rs 80 crore)
- Foreign exchange gain: Rs 7 crore for Q2
- Raw material price revision impact: ~3.7% of revenues; customer realization: ~2.5%

### Highlights
- OE segment and stellar export traction drove strong performance.
- Exports returned to almost 30% of total revenues (high-margin products with >20% EBITDA).
- Non-auto segment contributes 35% to 37% of overall revenue.
- Subsidiaries: China grew 25%-30% in INR terms (10%-12% local currency); UK grew 20%+ in INR terms. Both are profitable.
- Internal benchmark for new products: Generates 20% revenue over development + next 3 years.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 to mirror H1 growth rates.
- Full-year growth expected to be consistent with H1 levels across OE, retail, and export segments.
- Capex outlay for FY27 on track with previously communicated plans, potentially slightly higher based on customer requirements; FY28 expected to remain in the broad historical range of Rs 350 crore to Rs 450 crore.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic OE demand drivers: Car segment and M&HCV showing strong traction; wind energy investments also helping.
- Tractor segment expected to see some softening in Q3 and moderate demand in Q4 due to base effect and uneven monsoon; overall annual tractor segment still expected to be positive.
- CV segment H1 growth: M&HCV up 21%, LCV up 27% (25% combined). H2 expected to grow at mid-teens.
- Cars and SUVs expected to grow at low double digits in H2.
- Class 8 truck demand: Cumulative preliminary orders in past 12 months at 353,000 units. Full-year retail drop year-to-date is 5%, but expected to finish up 3% to 5% compared to last year.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Steel, aluminum, and indirect materials (stores and tools) experienced inflation due to geopolitical conflicts.
- Pass-through arrangements exist for domestic OE (often immediate or with a 1-month lag); aftermarket prices raised prospectively; exports offset by rupee depreciation.
- RM price increase and indirect material inflation caused a ~2.5% dip in contribution/gross margin.
- Fixed costs lower by almost 1% of revenue; depreciation lower relative to revenues due to improved asset utilization.
- Gas prices peaked at >Rs 200 and moderated to around Rs 150 (sub-Rs 100 historically); impact on overall margins considered negligible.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Wind energy: Phase 3 investment ongoing (Rs 70 crore - Rs 80 crore outlay) to cross Rs 500 crore annual revenue.
- Aerospace: Planned investments of Rs 30 crore to Rs 40 crore in testing/equipment to scale revenue to Rs 200 crore.
- Railways: Serviced using existing capacity, no separate investment.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash flows appeared stressed due to longer export cycles (up to 9 months), higher inventories, receivables, piling up of GST input credit on raw materials/subcontracting, and capex program.
- Management assured sufficient banking limits and expects borrowings to moderate and cash flows to normalize.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Steep increase in raw material and indirect material prices impacting contribution levels.
- Tractor segment demand softening in Q3/Q4 due to uneven monsoons and high base effect.
- Potential volume pressure on entry-level cars and sedans due to rural cash flow impacts.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Gross margin lag & pass-through (Lakshminarayanan KG):** Management clarified that the brunt of RM inflation is already reflected in the Q&A, and they do not expect it to worsen, though a 1-month lag with specific customers might flow into Q3.
- **Cash flow stress (Lakshminarayanan KG):** Management explained the stress is driven by a 9-month export cycle, higher working capital, GST input credit accumulation, and capex, assuring adequate banking lines and normalization ahead.
- **EV business timeline & U.S. demand (Krupashankar):** Management stated EV is in a lull phase, but plug-in hybrid and ICE portions are scaling up with Stellantis (projecting Rs 150 crore–Rs 175 crore for FY27), with clearer peak volume visibility in H2 next year.
- **Class 8 truck demand beyond CY26 (Krupashankar / S. Bharathan):** Management noted clarity has emerged on EPA '27 norms (engine life deferment by 3 years, warranty unchanged at 5 years/100,000 miles, NOx reduction enforced), with full OEM order books.
- **Other expenses increase (Gaurav Ashok Bhanshali):** Management clarified that other expenses grew in line with operational growth (25% for H1, 33% for the quarter).

### One-line takeaway
Sundram Fasteners delivered a strong Q2 with 30% revenue growth and 57% export surge, and management remains optimistic about sustaining growth momentum in H2 despite raw material inflation and rural tractor headwinds.
