# Electrosteel Castings Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While near-term domestic demand remains challenged due to slow project execution and fund disbursements, management anticipates a gradual recovery in H2 and next year, supported by cost optimization, diversification, and rising central sanction orders under JJM.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained overall outlook, but pushed out the timeline for hitting 12%-13% EBITDA margins from Q3 FY27 to the next financial year (FY28) due to delayed JJM fund flow implementation and coal cost impacts.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from expecting a sharp Q3 margin rebound to a more progressive recovery starting late H2 into next year.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Engagement with Zerowatt for an AI-driven energy intelligence platform and exploring a VRS scheme at the Khardah unit to reduce fixed costs.
- Last quarter's promises: TIS SPA EBITDA margins improved by ~160 bps QoQ to 14% in Q2; valve factory and industrial paints timelines remain on track.

### Key numbers
- **Sales volume (DI pipes, fittings & CI pipes):** 1.21 lakh tonnes in Q2 FY27 (vs 1.20 lakh tonnes in Q1 FY27; H1 total volume close to 2.5 lakh tonnes).
- **Consolidated Total Income:** Rs. 1,558 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 6.4% QoQ); Rs. 3,023 crore for H1 FY27.
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs. 139 crore in Q2 FY27 (EBITDA margin: 8.9%); Rs. 278 crore for H1 FY27 (EBITDA margin: 9.2%).
- **Consolidated PAT:** Rs. 51 crore in Q2 FY27 (PAT margin: 3.3%); Rs. 99 crore for H1 FY27 (PAT margin: 3.3%).
- **Debt (as of Sep 30, 2026):** Gross debt at Rs. 1,625 crore; Net debt at Rs. 750 crore.
- **Order Book:** Total order book at approximately 3 lakh tonnes (~6 to 6.5 months of manufacturing capacity), with JJM comprising 60,000 to 70,000 tonnes.
- **T.I.S. S.p.A. Italy:** Achieved an EBITDA margin of 14% in Q2, an improvement of ~160 bps over Q1.
- **Paint Project:** Phase 1 production capacity of 1,000 KL by March 2027 and 4,000 KL by September 2027; targeted revenue of Rs. 800 to Rs. 1,000 crores over five years with a phased capex of ~Rs. 300 crores.

### Highlights
- Engaged an AI-driven energy intelligence platform with Zerowatt and exploring a VRS scheme at the Khardah unit to reduce fixed manpower and energy costs.
- Valve factory construction in India is underway, expected to commence operations by the end of FY27; management aims to double valve revenue over the next four years.
- Basic and detailed engineering for industrial paints is progressing toward completion by November 2026, with construction commencing thereafter.
- Invested in an Oman-based trading company, Arabian Water Tech, to open up specific regional geographies.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Volume Guidance:** Management expects H2 volumes to be marginally better than H1 (~2.5 lakh tonnes), targeting ~7 lakh tonnes of dispatches for next financial year (FY28).
- **Margin Guidance:** Management expects to return to 12% EBITDA margins in the next financial year (FY28).
- **Paint Topline:** Phase 1 to contribute Rs. 80 to Rs. 100 crores to topline in the next financial year, with substantial impact in FY29.
- **Diversification Target:** Aiming to reduce revenue dependency on pipes and fittings from 75%-80% down to 50% via high-growth diversified products over five years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- DI pipe industry faces a challenging demand environment due to slower government spending and project execution, though fund flows are improving.
- JJM 2.0 extended to December 2028; FY27 budget estimate is Rs. 67,670 crores, with central sanction orders at Rs. 16,487 crores (up from Rs. 10,344 crores previously).
- Additional long-term demand drivers include AMRUT 2.0 (Rs. 8,000 crores), PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana (Rs. 7,137 crores), modernization of command area schemes (Rs. 1,600 crores), Urban Challenge Fund (Rs. 1 lakh crore fund catalyzing Rs. 4 lakh crore investments), and the Ken-Betwa Link project (Rs. 44,000 crores).
- Industry capacity utilization is expected to hold at 75%-80% over the next two years without aggressive price cutting.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Coking coal cost increases will impact Q3 and Q4 margins; management has successfully passed on 60%-70% of cost increases and aims for full effect by the end of Q4.
- 5% to 7% of the domestic order book is fixed-price; remaining orders allow for cost-sharing discussions with customers during dramatic cost changes.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Paint project capex is funded entirely from internal accruals, targeting a phased capex of ~Rs. 300 crores.
- Existing working capital lines are adequate for the next 2-3 years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated gross debt stood at Rs. 1,625 crores and net debt at Rs. 750 crores as of September 30, 2026.
- Trade receivables and inventory carrying costs have been optimized.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Slower government spending, project execution delays, and varying implementation paces across states.
- Geopolitical tensions in the Gulf region causing higher ocean freights (though easing now).
- Slowdown in Europe impacting export markets.
- Potential suppressing impact of higher coking coal costs on Q3 margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Vikash Singh (ICICI Securities) asked about JJM fund releases translating to H2 and export impacts from Saudi anti-dumping:** Management stated fund flows will have a progressive rippling effect leading to a better H2 and dramatic improvement next year; Saudi anti-dumping is a mild pushback handled by diversifying into other markets. *(Did not dodge)*
2. **Rajesh Agarwal asked about order book size and EBITDA margin recovery:** Management noted a total order book of ~3 lakh tonnes (60,000-70,000 tonnes JJM), targeting ~7 lakh tonnes dispatches next year and a return to 12% EBITDA margins in FY28. *(Did not dodge)*
3. **Abhishek Jain asked about fixed price contract exposure and coking coal cost impact:** Management replied that only 5%-7% of domestic orders are fixed price, and they can pass on 60%-70% of increased coking coal costs, taking full effect by Q4. *(Did not dodge)*
4. **Saket Kapoor asked about the Khardah unit VRS scheme and valve market revenue split:** Management explained VRS is part of fixed cost reduction alongside automation and AI initiatives; out of the Rs. 800 crore valve revenue target, India will contribute Rs. 250-300 crores and overseas geographies 60%-65%. *(Did not dodge)*
5. **Koushik Sekhar suggested considering an open market buyback given low valuations:** Management acknowledged that a buyback is under active consideration alongside balancing aggressive expansion cash flow needs and committed to retabling it with the management team. *(Did not dodge)*

### One-line takeaway
Electrosteel Castings navigated a soft domestic demand environment in H2 with stable volumes and cost optimization while advancing its strategic diversification into valves, paints, and AI-driven efficiencies ahead of anticipated JJM recovery.
