# Steel Strips Wheels Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-30 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported the strongest quarter in the company's history with the highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA, and PAT, driven by a richer product mix, cost optimization, and strong demand across segments.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 1,930 crore (up 61% YoY); H1 Revenue: Rs 3,440 crore (up 44% YoY)
- **Stand-alone EBITDA (including other income):** Rs 237 crore (up 107% YoY); H1 EBITDA: Rs 403 crore (up 68% YoY)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 12.3% (vs 9.6% in Q2 FY26); H1 EBITDA Margin: 11.7% (vs 10.1% YoY)
- **EBITDA per wheel:** Rs 404 (vs Rs 242 in Q2 FY26); H1 EBITDA per wheel: Rs 362
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 119 crore (up 209% YoY); H1 PAT: Rs 190 crore (up 115% YoY)
- **PAT Margin:** 6.2% (vs 3.2% in Q2 FY26)
- **Export Revenue:** Rs 187 crore in Q2 (up 69% YoY); H1 Export Revenue: Rs 314 crore (up 16% YoY)
- **Alloy Wheel Volumes:** ~12 lakh units in Q2; 23 lakh units in H1
- **Tractor Market Share:** Improved from 43% to 45%
- **Interim Dividend:** Approved at Rs 1.50 per equity share
- **Capex Guidance (FY27):** Over Rs 600 crore total (Rs 420 crore for Bhuj, Rs 120 crore for tractor wheel capacity in Dappar, ~Rs 70 crore for machinery replacement and automation)
- **Greenfield Project Outlay (Sambhajinagar, Aurangabad):** Estimated at ~Rs 1,400 crore over 60 months

### Highlights
- Recorded highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA, and PAT in company history.
- Alloy wheels contributed ~45% of total Q2 revenue (~42% in H1), driven by PV premiumization and rising EV share.
- Aluminum knuckle business progressing in line with expectations; commercial supplies scaling up.
- Commercial Vehicle (CV) segment recorded the highest growth among all segments across domestic and export markets.
- Tractor segment performed steadily with market share increasing to 45%.
- Steel wheel segment saw increased demand for high vent hole wheels.
- Aluminum wheel capacity debottlenecked by 10% organically in October, crossing an annualized rate of 5.4 million units.
- Board approved setting up a state-of-the-art greenfield manufacturing facility at Sambhajinagar, Aurangabad, for alloy wheels, steel wheels, OTR applications, and a hot stamping line for car body structural parts.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is extremely confident of strong performance for the remaining two quarters, expecting to exceed initial FY27 top-line growth guidance of over 20% and EBITDA per wheel of over Rs 310.
- On track to exceed FY27 export target of Rs 600 crore (potentially reaching Rs 700 crore to Rs 750 crore).
- Management targets export revenue of at least Rs 1,500 crore in 3 years.
- Commercial production of aluminum knuckles at Bhuj to commence in the last quarter of FY27; aluminum wheels to start in early April next year.
- Sambhajinagar hot stamping facility targeted to start production in the fourth quarter of next calendar year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic demand across segments remains strong; CV segment showing high demand ("absolute tier") with October set to be the highest-ever CV sales month by a wide margin.
- Tractor demand supported by farm mechanization, healthy rural economic activity, and higher horsepower preference.
- Export demand recovering across Europe, Latin America, and Asia, alongside the US.
- Expecting a major turnaround in the export steel wheel business next year driven by OEM wins in Europe (MAN, Scania, Daimler).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margin expansion driven by a richer product mix (higher aluminum share), cost optimization, higher export contribution, and price revisions received from customers for non-raw material input costs.
- Raw material price changes are a pass-through; supplementary invoicing of Rs 183 crore in H1 relates strictly to actual raw material cost adjustments and does not inflate EBITDA.
- Aluminum segment EBITDA per wheel is about 40% higher than the overall steel segment (and roughly twice when comparing pure-play passenger car aluminum vs steel).
- New Sambhajinagar facilities are projected to be at least 25% more productive and efficient than Mehsana or Bhuj.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Bhuj expansion: Aluminum wheel plant adds 1.2 million wheels (total capacity beyond 6.2 million); aluminum knuckle facility adds 1.1 million units.
- Dappar expansion: Rs 120 crore allocated to increase tractor wheel capacity by almost 0.5 million tractor sets.
- Sambhajinagar, Aurangabad greenfield project: Outlay of ~Rs 1,400 crore over 60 months, funded via debt, internal accruals, and strategic partners. Will add 1.6 million steel wheels (1 million passenger car, 0.6 million OTR) and a hot stamping line (1.3 million strokes per annum capacity).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Stand-alone ROCE stood at 24% in the first 6 months of FY27.
- Sambhajinagar project will be funded by a mix of debt, internal accruals, and likely strategic investments from commodity partners.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in freight costs ("whiplashed with this freight market a lot").
- A potential drop in CV or tractor volumes would impact EBITDA per wheel if demand tapers.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Aditya Khetan (SMIFS Institutional Equities) asked about the sustainability of the Rs 404 EBITDA per wheel and the impact of H1 RM pass-on benefits.**
   - *Management answered* that raw material is a pass-through and the Rs 183 crore supplementary invoicing had no bearing on EBITDA. They affirmed that the Rs 404 EBITDA per wheel is sustainable, supported by operational efficiencies, debottlenecked aluminum capacity (up 10%), and strong CV/export demand.
2. **Aaryan (Aequitas Investments) asked about global competition, pricing, and the hot stamping capex turnover.**
   - *Management answered* that competition is mainly from Turkey (whom SSWL beats on cost and logistics) and noted that China faces anti-dumping duties. For hot stamping, they stated a target turnover of ~Rs 400 crore on an investment of ~Rs 170 crore, competing with players like Gestamp and JBM.
3. **Ansh Gupta (Niveshaay) asked about the margin gap between steel, alloy, and knuckles.**
   - *Management answered* that the aluminum segment (wheels + knuckles) has an EBITDA per wheel about 40% higher than the steel segment (and roughly 2x for pure pass-car aluminum vs steel).
4. **Shashank Kanodia (ICICI Securities) questioned the ROCE/IRR math on the Rs 1,400 crore Aurangabad greenfield capex.**
   - *Management answered* that the project ROCE will be better than the current 24% due to proximity to customers, freight savings, and high automation, making the new facilities 25% more productive than Mehsana.
5. **Rahul Jain (Capri Global Ventures) asked about strategic partners for the new capex.**
   - *Management answered* that the partners are existing long-term commodity players who have been with them since the factory started.

### One-line takeaway
Steel Strips Wheels Ltd delivered its strongest quarter in history with record revenues and a 107% jump in EBITDA, backed by premiumization, robust CV demand, and structural cost optimizations, while laying out a massive Rs 1,400 crore greenfield expansion in Aurangabad.
