# INDO SMC Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-18 · Auto Components*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported strong revenue and profit growth in H1, though noting some margin pressure from raw material rate fluctuations.

### Key numbers
- Total income: Rs 218 crore in H1 FY26 (Note: transcript mentions H1 FY26 for income, up 94% YoY).
- EBITDA: Rs 27.7 crore (up 55% YoY).
- PAT: Rs 20 crore (up 76% YoY).
- EBITDA margin: 12.69% for H1.
- Revenue from operations growth (sequential): up 10% compared with the second half of FY26.
- Order book: Rs 145 crore as of 31st October 2026.
- Order pipeline: ~Rs 120 crore (Rs 30 crore FRP/SMC boxes, Rs 20 crore FRP, Rs 50-60 crore VCB and other panels, Rs 10 crore tarpaulin).
- Revenue split target: ~50% electrical, ~30% SMC, ~20% FRP.
- PCVCB panel FY28 revenue target: Rs 40 crore to Rs 50 crore.

### Highlights
- Installed new pultrusion machines at the Ahmedabad facility and made the first export shipment of fiberglass products to Oman.
- Received vendor approval from Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company (MSEDCL) for 11 kV and 33 kV isolators.
- Secured large orders: Rs 52+ crore busduct orders in April, Rs 66 crore variable VRF panels order in July, and a Rs 31.7 crore order in October from a Gujarat-based power distribution equipment company.
- Developing a 31 MVA transformer plant and entering the transformer segment.
- Continuing the old Nashik plant while setting up a new ~60,000 sq. ft. facility in Nashik for VCB, RMU, and other product ranges.
- Continuing progress on railways (supplying panels/berths/washrooms via intermediaries) and exploring defence bunkers/submarines.
- *Comparison with Record:* The call continues the strategy of launching the Ring Main Unit (RMU) by June 2027 (previously noted in the Nov 2026 investor deck).

### Guidance & outlook
- Revenue guidance: FY27 target of Rs 450 crore, FY28 target of Rs 600 crore to Rs 700 crore, and FY29 target around Rs 1,000 crore (reiterated by management).
- PAT margin target: 10% to 11% going forward.
- New Nashik facility revenue potential: Rs 200 crore to Rs 300 crore at full utilization; combined Nashik plants targeting Rs 300 crore to Rs 400 crore next year.
- RMU and PCVCB target EBITDA margins: 20% to 22%.
- Additional capex: ~Rs 30 crore required for new product development and higher kV ranges (66, 120).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Electrical products see strong demand driven by data centers and broader electrification; management stated that power demand will continue to grow for the next 10-20 years.
- Indoor CTPT market size is estimated at ~Rs 12,000 crore; Maharashtra outdoor CT market is ~Rs 1,200 crore.
- SMC is increasingly used in Vande Bharat and other high-speed trains (via Tata Composites and Kineco) and for weather-proof bunkers supplied to the army in Kashmir.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- H1 EBITDA margins dropped by ~400 bps due to rapid petroleum-based resin and material rate fluctuations against fixed-price legacy order books.
- Management is shifting towards month-to-month orders and variable rates/price variation clauses to mitigate commodity volatility.
- Raw material composition: Resin accounts for 20%-30% in SMC/FRP; copper accounts for 25%-40% in CTPT products.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Setting up a new ~60,000 sq. ft. plant in Nashik (operational in about a month) to expand electrical product capacity (VCB, RMU).
- Stepping up product voltage approvals progressively: 11 kV completed, 33 kV in final stages, targeting 66 kV and 120 kV next year.
- Establishing a 31 MVA transformer plant phase-wise.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash flows from operations remain negative, but management targets turning cash flows positive by next Diwali.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High raw material volatility (resin, copper, transport costs) impacted H1 margins.
- Approvals from testing bodies like ERDA experienced temporary delays due to holidays.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shashank Jha** asked about order book math and margin recovery. Management explained they are moving to short-term/variable-rate orders due to high rate fluctuations, and H2 margins will improve with stable pricing and expanding electrical product lines.
2. **Achyut Pawat** questioned the lower order book supporting H2 growth. Management clarified they are avoiding long-term fixed-price orders and taking ad-hoc/monthly orders of ~Rs 30 crore chunks to protect margins.
3. **Hardik Gandhi** asked why the company is entering the crowded transformer market instead of a niche. Management responded that having an in-house complete product range (from box to transformer) for utilities gives them better control and order execution.
4. **Vinay Gudhka** inquired about raw material exposures and hedging. Management noted that resin is 20-30%, copper is 25-40%, and government orders have IEEMI clauses for copper adjustments.
5. **Shashank Shah** questioned the rationale for entering low-kV transformers given the competition. Management reiterated that India's structural demand will outstrip capacity for the next 10-20 years, supporting their expansion.

### One-line takeaway
Indo SMC delivered strong H1 top and bottom-line growth despite margin compression from raw material volatility, and is actively pivoting toward higher-margin electrical infrastructure and short-term order contracting.
