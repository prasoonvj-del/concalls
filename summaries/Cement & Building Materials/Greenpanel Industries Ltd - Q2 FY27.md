# Greenpanel Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management cited volume degrowth, margin contraction due to pricing pressures and expensive inventory, and delayed recovery in the OEM segment, though retail demand remains strong and cost pressures are expected to moderate.

### Key numbers
- **Total Revenue (Q2 FY27):** INR 407 crore (up 4.6% YoY, 16.4% QoQ)
- **H1 FY27 Total Revenue:** INR 757 crore (up 6.3% YoY)
- **Consolidated Operating EBITDA Margin (Q2 FY27):** 7.5% (vs 9.6% in Q1 FY27, 10.2% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 Operating EBITDA Margin:** 8.4% (up 100 bps YoY)
- **Retail MDF Sales (Q2 FY27):** Highest ever for Greenpanel in a single quarter; retail MDF volume grew 17% YoY
- **Plywood Sales Growth:** 8.4% YoY, 16.5% QoQ
- **Total Volume Growth (Q2 FY27):** Degrew 3.4% YoY; grew 23% QoQ
- **Net Debt:** INR 133 crore (reduced further)
- **Working Capital:** Improved by 5 days vs June 30 numbers
- **Export Order Pipeline (as of Oct 1):** ~13,000 cubic meters
- **OEM Share of Domestic Volume:** 15% in H1 FY27 (vs 25%-30% last year)
- **VAP Share (H1 FY27):** 43%-44% by volume, close to 55% by value
- **Plant Capacity Utilization:** 50% to 55%
- **EPCG Benefit (Q2 FY27):** ~INR 4 crore

### Highlights
- Retail volume growth for both MDF and plywood remained strong, building on Q1 momentum.
- Annual foreign trip scheme conducted in Hong Kong and Macau in August for top 150+ channel partners.
- Abnormal conditions in the Middle East impacted container/vessel availability, increasing delivery costs.
- Cost of production reduced sequentially by 5% due to softening chemical costs, but the full impact was delayed due to carrying higher-cost opening inventory of 50,000 cubes from Q1.
- Furniture Quality Control Order (QCO) 2025 transitional/exemption periods lapsed in Q2FY27; large furniture makers commencing/announcing fresh capacity additions.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to achieve mid-teen MDF volume growth for the full year.
- Expects MDF industry CAGR of 15% to 17% to continue.
- Management expects MDF margins to return to double-digits in upcoming quarters.
- Expects chemical cost inflation to moderate in H2.
- Net asset turn target at capacity is expected to reach 1.3x to 1.5x.
- Additional industry capacity addition of ~800,000 to 900,000 cubic meters per year expected over the next 2 years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Price-sensitive OEM segment was slow to respond following Q1 price hikes; OEMs placed alternate import orders, impacting Q2. Pricing realignment has been undertaken.
- Export salience grew month-on-month despite end-September sea freight hikes; opening export order pipeline as of October is higher than Q2 total sales.
- Spiraling freight costs made imports less feasible for domestic markets.
- Cost-inefficient multi-daylight lines are increasingly becoming unviable; pace of capacity additions is expected to moderate.
- Industry average capacity utilization is estimated around 70%.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Price reductions combined with elevated opening inventory led to margin contraction in Q2.
- Domestic ASPs contracted by 6% to 7% sequentially in Q2.
- Wood raw material prices have stabilized; industry moving to diverse species, reducing eucalyptus dependency.
- Chemical cost inflation expected to moderate in H2.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Line 3 at Andhra is in operation, providing organic headroom.
- No major capex outlined for upcoming years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt further reduced to INR 133 crore.
- Working capital cycle improved by 5 days compared to June 30.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical volatility in the Middle East impacting container/vessel availability and sea freight costs.
- Volatility in crude and chemical prices.
- Increased competition from new entrants (e.g., Action's Gujarat plant commissioning, South India capacities) creating oversupply and pricing pressures.
- Unorganized segment malpractices (adding color to standard grade products and selling as MR/HDWR).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Praveen Sahay (Prabhudas Lilladher) on OEM volume impact and recovery:** Management explained that Q1 price hikes removed OEM pricing benefits, and OEMs had placed alternate import orders leading to a lingering Q2 impact. Recovery is expected in Q3/Q4.
2. **Madhur Rathi (Individual Investor) on realization gaps versus peers (Greenply/Century):** Management stated the gap is due to a lower value-added mix percentage on a larger sales base, and capacity utilization differences. On a like-to-like basis, Greenpanel is at par or at a premium.
3. **Keshav Lahoti (Mahani Investments) on business-wise margins:** Management noted that retail makes more money (margin differential between 5% to 20% depending on pricing/costs), while bulk orders (OEM/exports) provide consistent runs and operating efficiencies.
4. **Vishal Shah on export order book and container availability:** Management clarified that the 13,000 cubic meter opening order book could not be fully executed in October due to container unavailability rather than customer willingness to pay freight.
5. **Praveen Agarwal on peer margin divergence:** Management reiterated that higher capacity utilizations and a higher percentage of value-added products explain why smaller listed peers reported higher EBITDA margins in Q1.

### One-line takeaway
Despite near-term margin contraction and OEM volume headwinds, Greenpanel reported record retail MDF sales and anticipates volume recovery and margin expansion in H2 driven by operating leverage, retail momentum, and moderating raw material costs.
