# Greenply Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported healthy value growth, strong margins, and ongoing premiumization despite capacity constraints and competitive pressures.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated revenue: Rs 799.7 crore (up 16.1% YoY) for Q2 FY27; Rs 1,524.6 crore (up 18.2% YoY) for H1 FY27.
- Consolidated core EBITDA: Rs 89.2 crore with a margin of 11.2% (expansion of 300 bps YoY) for Q2 FY27; Rs 167.5 crore with a margin of 11% (expansion of 180 bps YoY) for H1 FY27.
- Plywood segment: Volume growth of 6.1% YoY, value growth of 15.5% YoY, revenue of Rs 625.7 crore, realization of Rs 264 per square meter, and core EBITDA margin of 10.05% (up 180 bps YoY, up 160 bps QoQ) for Q2 FY27. H1 FY27 volume growth of 9.2% YoY, value growth of 15.7% YoY, revenue of Rs 1,152.3 crore, and core EBITDA margin of 9.31%.
- MDF segment: Quarterly revenue of Rs 173.9 crore (up 18.4% YoY), volume of 54,605 CBM (up 16.1% YoY). Realisation of Rs 31,816 per CBM. EBITDA margin of 15.2% for Q2 FY27. H1 FY27 revenue of Rs 369.6 crore (up 25.6% YoY), volume of 112,411 CBM (up 20.4% YoY), realization of Rs 32,694 CBM, and margin of 16.3% (up 350 bps YoY).
- Furniture fittings JV (SAMET): Sales of Rs 14.08 crore in Q2 FY27 and Rs 27.70 crore in H1 FY27. PAT loss of Rs 12.6 crore in Q2 FY27 (Greenply's share of loss: Rs 6.3 crore); H1 FY27 PAT loss of Rs 24.2 crore (Greenply's share: Rs 12.1 crore).
- Balance sheet: Consolidated net debt of Rs 544 crore, debt-to-equity ratio of 0.56x as of the end of Q2 FY27.

### Highlights
- Introduced TermiVax (patented nanotechnology-based anti-termite solution) and the 11-year fully covered plus plan for Greenply Gold.
- Rolled out Phase 1 of Greenply's private sector afforestation program across Gujarat and Nagaland (covering over 2,300 hectares; 2,56,688 carbon credits allotted); aiming to expand to 12,000 hectares.
- HDF flooring line commenced commercial production in July 2026.
- Decided to dilute stake in the furniture fittings JV from 50% to 19% by January 5, 2027, removing the consolidated profit drag while retaining brand charges and a 20% stake.

### Guidance & outlook
- Maintained existing guidance for the plywood segment (double-digit volume growth, annual volume growth of more than 10%).
- Revised full-year MDF volume growth guidance to approximately 20% while maintaining core EBITDA margins in the range of 15% to 16%.
- MDF plant at Vadodara expected to commence in Q2 FY28, and Plywood plant in Odisha expected in Q4 FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Plywood plant utilization exceeded 100%, constraining volume growth due to capacity limits.
- MDF sales slightly exceeded production; volume growth was restricted by monsoon disruptions and an extended annual maintenance shutdown.
- Chemical raw material costs have permanently increased by 7% to 8%, but price hikes have been fully passed on. Timber prices remain stable.
- Competitive intensity in MDF remains high with new capacities (Action TESA in the south, Elixrr starting soon), but management expects smaller unorganized players to face severe margin pressure and potentially shut down over the next 6 to 12 months.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Plywood gross margins improved due to a shift toward value-added products and innovations like TermiVax.
- MDF margins compressed sequentially solely due to the rollback of earlier price hikes.
- Core EBITDA per CBM in MDF is targeted to be maintained above Rs 5,000.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Announced a new plywood expansion at Sandila, Lucknow: 15 million square meters capacity, budgeted capex of Rs 125 crore, funded through a mix of debt and equity, expected to commence commercial production in Q4 FY28 with revenue potential of Rs 320 crore to Rs 340 crore at full capacity. Total plywood capacity starting Q4 FY28 will be 81.3 million MSM.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated net debt stood at Rs 544 crore with a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.56x, well within the guided range of 0.7x to 0.75x.
- Greenply stopped funding JV (SAMET) losses from Q1; dilution to 19% by January 5, 2027, will eliminate the bottom-line drag.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Capacity constraints in plywood until Odisha and Sandila facilities come online.
- Persistent competitive intensity and lower short-term realization/return ratios in the MDF industry.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Karan Bhatelia (Asian Market Securities) asked about the lower-than-usual 6% plywood volume growth and strategy.** Management clarified that capacity utilization exceeded 100%, and due to capacity bottlenecks ahead of upcoming plants, they prioritized value-added products and margins over sheer volume.
2. **Sneha asked about MDF volume drop QoQ and pricing/cost sustainability.** Management explained that lower volumes were strictly due to production constraints (monsoons and extended maintenance), and that the 15% to 16% EBITDA margin guidance is sustainable via better product mix (Pre-lam volumes up) and energy cost savings.
3. **Parth Bhavsar asked about the SAMET JV dilution timeline and provisioning.** Management confirmed that January 5, 2027, is the end date to reduce their stake to 19%, that Greenply stopped funding losses from Q1, and that no further provisions are required as consol numbers already account for historical losses.
4. **Ritesh Shah asked about the rationale behind exiting the 50-50 JV structure in SAMET.** Management stated that the partner plans another Rs 400 crore of capex which Greenply's balance sheet cannot support, and the move eliminates cash/loss drags while securing brand charges and a 19% equity upside.
5. **Jojo Shaju asked about the impact of MDF imports via BIS certification.** Management noted that import volumes remain small relative to domestic consumption, and freight/geopolitical factors limit major disruption for Greenply.

### One-line takeaway
Greenply delivered solid Q2 FY27 value growth and margin expansion across segments by focusing on premiumization and operational efficiencies, despite capacity constraints and competitive headwinds in MDF.
