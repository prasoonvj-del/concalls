# Stylam Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-10 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong export demand, commercialization of a major new plant, and stable contribution margins despite macroeconomic and geopolitical headwinds.

### Key numbers
- **Export Realization:** INR191 per kg in Q2 FY27 (vs INR186 in Q1 FY27, INR161 in Q2 FY26).
- **Sheet Volumes (H1):** 6.96 million sheets (vs 5.83 million sheets in H1 FY26).
- **New Plant Revenue Expectation:** INR250 crore to INR300 crore for FY27.
- **EBITDA Margin:** 18 point something percent in Q2 FY27.
- **Impact of Ocean Freight and PNG:** INR8.15 crore and INR2.5 crore respectively in Q2 FY27.
- **New Plant Capacity Utilization Guidance:** 25% to 30% in FY27; 60% to 70%+ in FY28.

### Highlights
- New manufacturing plant became operational on September 22, 2026; two presses started, third and fourth to start within days, fully operational within the month.
- Export demand remains robust as global manufacturers face high energy and labor costs, allowing Stylam to gain market share.
- Domestic business is being deliberately revamped by stopping low-margin commodity "liner" sales (previously 300,000 sheets/month) to focus on value-added products.
- Collaboration with Aica Kogyo: partnership is 4 months old (joined June 15/16), discussions ongoing with no immediate formal integration yet.
- Solid acrylic surfaces business is being treated as a fresh business, with revamping underway for domestic and export markets over the next 1-2 quarters.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects INR250 crore to INR300 crore turnover from the new plant in FY27.
- New plant capacity utilization guided at 25%-30% for FY27 and 60%-70%+ for FY28.
- Margin trajectory remains contingent on geopolitical conflicts, crude prices, and raw material cartels; management declined to provide specific percentage guidance for upcoming quarters.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Export markets are strong, though constrained slightly (2%-3% impact) by container/vessel availability and Middle East conflict issues.
- Middle East markets remain large and stable with potential post-war reconstruction opportunities; no major revenue loss experienced yet from the region.
- Domestic market demand is subdued but expected to show drastic changes in value and volume over Q3 and Q4 FY27 post-revamp.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margin impacted by ~3% to 3.5% due to elevated energy costs (pet coke, coal, gas/PNG) and ocean freight spikes driven by Middle East conflicts.
- Contribution/gross margins held relatively steady despite cost pressures.
- Raw material prices are currently at a peak, impacted by trader/supplier cartels.
- New plant maintenance costs expected to be near zero for the first 1 to 2 years, aiding efficiencies.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capital Work-In-Progress (CWIP) of INR12.41 crore pertains primarily to remaining presses and infrastructural work (walls, roads) for the new plant.
- Debottlenecking is underway at old plants to incrementally boost capacity.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Surplus cash was utilized for the new plant, leading to increased utilization of working capital limits.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical conflicts (Middle East war) affecting ocean freight, vessel availability, and container logistics.
- Volatility in crude oil prices impacting energy and raw material costs.
- Competition from local Indian unorganized players in export markets.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Keshav (HDFC Securities) on EBITDA margin drop and other expenses:** Management clarified that EBITDA margin is 18+%, and the drop was driven by a ~3.5% impact from ocean freight (INR8.15 crore) and PNG (INR2.5 crore) cost escalations.
2. **Aditya on export drivers vs domestic slowdown:** Management explained that global manufacturing issues (energy/labor costs in Europe) are driving export market share gains, while domestic is undergoing a deliberate transition away from commoditized liners.
3. **Chirag Shah on Middle East war impact:** Management noted that while container availability was tight, direct sales to the Middle East have not dropped yet and could offer post-war reconstruction demand.
4. **Resha Mehta on volume disclosures:** Management agreed to start disclosing sales volumes in kilograms/tonnage starting from Q3 alongside sheets for better comparability.
5. **Yogansh on domestic volume dip:** Management clarified the volume drop is intentional as they halted sales of 300,000 sheets/month of low-margin "liners" to reposition the brand for value-added products.

### One-line takeaway
Stylam Industries commissioned its advanced new plant and maintained steady contribution margins despite geopolitical freight headwinds, while strategically pruning low-margin domestic volumes to focus on value-addition.
