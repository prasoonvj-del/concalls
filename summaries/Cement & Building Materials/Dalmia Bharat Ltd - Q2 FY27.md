# Dalmia Bharat Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-28 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong long-term growth and strategic commissioning offset by near-term regional demand softness in the East/Northeast, cost pressures, and a dilution in EBITDA from Central India operations.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Volume:** 3.1 million tonnes, up 3% YoY (organic ex-Central volume up 4% YoY).
- **EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 698 on a reported basis (Rs 746 on an ex-Central basis, down due to variable cost inflation and fixed cost under-absorption).
- **Incentives:** Rs 25 crore during the quarter.
- **Collections:** Rs 53 crore during the quarter.
- **Variable Cost Impact:** War-related impact of ~Rs 220/tonne in H1; additional packing bag inflation of ~Rs 100/tonne. Ex-Central total cost increased by ~Rs 260/tonne YoY in Q2.
- **Renewable Energy Share:** Reached 52% in Q2 (~450 MW capacity).
- **Organic Capex:** Rs 909 crore in Q2; H1 spend at ~Rs 1,420 crore. Full-year capex guidance maintained at Rs 3,200 crore to Rs 3,400 crore.
- **Contingent Liabilities:** Reduced by ~Rs 198 crore to Rs 931 crore (~5% of net worth) following MMDR Act amendments.
- **Exceptional Items:** Aggregated Rs 339 crore (comprising brand transition/phasing out of Konark, impairment of 97 MW thermal power capacity, and employee separation schemes).

### Highlights
- **Project Progress:** New clinker line at Belgaum commenced trial runs ahead of schedule; commercial production expected ahead of peak demand in Q4. Pune and Kadapa projects remain on track for FY28 commissioning, targeting a total capacity of ~67 million tonnes.
- **Central India (Jaypee/Rewa Assets):** Mining operations restarted; clinker line and cement mill ramping up under trial runs. Management targets 35% to 40% utilization of these assets by the end of FY27.
- **Brand & Premiumization:** Konark brand is being phased out to focus on premiumization. New premium offering *Weather365* received an exceptional response, prompting accelerated rollout.
- **Leadership Transition:** CMO Navin Tewari to transition to strategic adviser effective January 1, 2027. CFO Yatin Malhotra to take on additional responsibilities for sales and marketing alongside finance.
- **Balance Sheet Focus:** Management reiterated commitment to maintaining net debt to EBITDA below 2x on a sustainable basis.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Capex Guidance:** Maintained at Rs 3,200 crore to Rs 3,400 crore for FY28.
- **Central India Ramp-up:** Targeting near-breakeven for Central assets by Q4 FY27 and Dalmia-standard EBITDA by end of FY28. EBITDA dilution from Central assets expected to be Rs 100–125/tonne in Q3 (compared to Rs 50/tonne in Q2).
- **Volume Growth:** Second-half volume growth expected to be in line with the industry (7% to 8%).
- **Cost Outlook:** Expecting a further cost impact of ~Rs 75/tonne in Q3 due to high petcoke prices (surged over $180/tonne), though adverse regional mix from Q2 is expected to reverse.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Cement demand grew in high single digits YoY in Q2, supported by government capex and below-normal rainfall.
- Central government spent ~42% of its budgeted annual capex in the first 5 months; MORTH and Railways utilized ~45%.
- South and West markets saw healthy momentum approaching double-digit growth.
- East and Northeast markets remained subdued during Q2 due to severe floods and heavy rainfall, though demand is now picking up.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Variable cost increased by ~Rs 200/tonne in Q2 (fuel contribution ~Rs 100/tonne, raw material inflation ~Rs 30–40/tonne, regional mix impact ~Rs 50–60/tonne).
- Fixed costs increased by ~Rs 168/tonne, of which ~Rs 140/tonne was due to fixed cost under-absorption from lower Q-o-Q volumes and higher shutdown costs.
- Freight costs remained flat for cement and declined for clinker due to operational efficiencies.
- Mineral bearing land tax payments discontinued in Assam and Meghalaya post August 22 following MMDR Act amendments, but continue to apply in Tamil Nadu pending discussions with authorities.
- 97 MW of thermal power capacity impaired across 3 existing plants due to discontinuation of operations.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Belgaum clinker line trials underway; 3 MTPA cement mill trials to start in a couple of weeks.
- Pune and Kadapa projects progressing for FY28 delivery.
- Total capacity targeted to reach ~67 million tonnes with Belgaum, Pune, and Kadapa, and ~70 million tonnes if BJCL is included. No further capacity announcements until current projects stabilize and generate cash.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt is at a peak due to recent acquisitions and lower quarterly volumes/EBITDA, but management remains committed to keeping leverage below 2x–2.5x sustainably.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated crude oil prices (above $100/barrel) and maritime disruptions driving international petcoke prices above $180/tonne.
- Rising inflation and potential onset of a rate hike cycle posing near-term macroeconomic risks.
- Slower volume absorption and teething issues in newly acquired Central India assets.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Prateek Kumar (Jefferies):** Asked why Dalmia's performance ex-Central looked weaker than regional peers on volume growth and EBITDA per tonne. Management attributed this to regional seasonality/floods in the East causing fixed cost under-absorption and stated that Q2 was impacted by temporary shutdowns and one-offs.
2. **Amit Murarka (Axis Capital):** Queried the INR 100–125/tonne EBITDA dilution guidance for Q3. Management clarified this reflects the full cost capitalization of Central assets upon commercial production in Q3, with breakeven targeted for Q4 FY27.
3. **Satyadeep Jain (AMBIT Capital):** Asked about the rationale behind combining finance and marketing under CFO Yatin Malhotra. Management stated it is a planned transition to leverage an internal leader familiar with Dalmia's systems, with intentions to eventually separate the roles.
4. **Raghav Maheshwari (Nirmal Bang):** Questioned underperformance in East/South volumes compared to peers. Management reiterated that excessive monsoons, strategic balancing of profitability over volume, and brand transition (phasing out Konark) played a role, adding that one quarter does not dictate future trajectory.
5. **Girija Ray (Nirmal Bang):** Inquired about low multi-year capacity utilization (60%–65%). Management responded that capacity has been built ahead of demand to secure future growth at lower historical costs, and utilization will improve as sales and distribution ramp up.

### One-line takeaway
Dalmia Bharat faced a soft Q2 due to East India floods and fixed-cost under-absorption, but remains confident in a stronger H2, ongoing cost optimizations, and the strategic ramp-up of its new Central India and Belgaum capacities toward its pan-India vision.
