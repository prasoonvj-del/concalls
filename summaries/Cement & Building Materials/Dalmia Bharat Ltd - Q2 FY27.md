# Dalmia Bharat Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-28 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong demand momentum in South and West along with capacity expansions were offset by cost inflation, monsoon disruptions in East, and temporary EBITDA dilution from Central India assets.

### Key numbers
- **Sales Volume:** Total volume grew 3% YoY to 7.1 million tonnes (grew 4% YoY ex-Central India).
- **EBITDA per Tonne:** Reported EBITDA/ton stood at Rs 698/tonne (ex-Central EBITDA/ton was Rs 746/tonne, impacted by a ~Rs 50/tonne dilution from Central operations).
- **Cost Impact (ex-Central):** Total costs increased by ~Rs 260/tonne QoQ (variable cost up ~Rs 200/tonne; fixed cost/other up ~Rs 168/tonne due to ~Rs 140/tonne under-absorption on lower volume and shutdown costs).
- **Fuel & Power:** Fuel cost stood at Rs 1.66/mn kcal. Renewable energy (RE) capacity reached ~450 MW, representing 52% of the power mix in Q2.
- **Exceptional Items:** Rs 339 crore, including impairment of 97 MW discontinued thermal power capacity, Rs 57 crore provisional charge for employee separation, and transition costs from phasing out the Konark brand.
- **Incentives & Collections:** Incentives accrued at Rs 25 crore; collections stood at Rs 53 crore.
- **Capital Expenditure:** Q2 organic capex was Rs 909 crore (H1 FY27 organic capex at ~Rs 1,420 crore).

### Highlights
- Lit the new clinker line at Belgaum ahead of schedule, with trial runs commenced; a 3 MTPA cement mill at Belgaum will begin trial runs shortly.
- Phasing out the Konark brand to focus on portfolio premiumization and building a unified Dalmia brand; rolling out premium offering *Weather365* into newer markets.
- Impaired 97 MW of discontinued captive thermal power capacity across three existing plants as RE capacity expanded.
- Effective January 1, 2027, Chief Marketing Officer Navin Tewari will transition to Strategic Adviser to the CEO, while CFO Yatin Malhotra will take additional responsibility for sales and marketing.
- MMDR Act amendments reduced contingent liabilities by ~Rs 198 crore to Rs 931 crore (~5% of net worth).

### Guidance & outlook
- **Full-Year Capex:** Guidance maintained at Rs 3,200 crore to Rs 3,400 crore for FY27.
- **Volume Growth:** Second-half FY27 organic volumes expected to grow in line with industry growth (7% to 8%).
- **Central Assets Ramp-up:** Aiming for 35% to 40% capacity utilization at Central India assets by end-FY27, targeting operational breakeven by Q4 FY27 and Dalmia-standard EBITDA per ton by end-FY28.
- **EBITDA / Costs:** Fuel cost expected to increase by ~Rs 0.20/mn kcal (or ~Rs 75-80/tonne) in Q3 FY27. However, reversal of fixed cost under-absorption and mix impacts, alongside ~Rs 15/bag price hikes, are expected to improve profitability QoQ in Q3 FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Overall cement demand grew in high single digits YoY during Q2 FY27, driven by sustained public capex (Central government spent ~42% of annual budgeted capex in first 5 months) and relatively low rainfall in parts of South and West.
- South and West markets saw strong momentum approaching double-digit growth. East and Northeast markets remained subdued due to heavy rainfall and severe floods.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Petcoke prices rose above $180/tonne and crude remained above $100/barrel, driving variable cost pressures.
- Raw material inflation added Rs 30 to Rs 40/tonne in Q2, while regional mix shift away from the East added Rs 50 to Rs 60/tonne.
- Diesel price increases added ~Rs 20/tonne in Q2, but cement freight costs remained flat due to efficiency gains.
- Mineral-bearing land tax discontinued in Assam and Meghalaya post-MMDR amendments, but Tamil Nadu continues to collect the tax, which remains embedded in raw material costs pending state workflow updates.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total operational capacity to reach ~67 MTPA with completion of ongoing Belgaum, Pune, and Kadapa expansion projects (entailing ~Rs 10,000 crore total capex across 20 MTPA pipeline including Central India).
- Rewa (Central India) mining operations restarted; clinker line and cement mill are undergoing trial runs and ramping up.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Committed to maintaining leverage (Net Debt to EBITDA) below 2.0x on a sustainable basis.
- Management stated current leverage reflects peak debt levels due to the recent Jaypee asset acquisition combined with seasonally lower Q2 EBITDA.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- EBITDA dilution from Central India assets expected to expand to Rs 100-125/tonne in Q3 FY27 as Rewa achieves commercial production before progressively declining.
- Persistent international petcoke and coal price inflation posing cost headwinds in Q3 FY27.
- Delayed execution by state authorities in Tamil Nadu on MMDR tax discontinuation, keeping raw material costs elevated.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Prateek Kumar (Jefferies):** Asked why performance lagged peers on growth and margins.  
  *Management Response:* Explained that weak regional demand in East/Northeast led to lower sales growth, creating a fixed-cost under-absorption hit of Rs 100-120/tonne. Expects reversal as demand rebounds in H2.
- **Amit Murarka:** Requested clarity on the EBITDA dilution from Central assets.  
  *Management Response:* Stated Central dilution was ~Rs 50/tonne in Q2 and will rise to Rs 100-125/tonne in Q3 as trial run costs shift to commercial capitalization, targeting breakeven in Q4 FY27.
- **Satyadeep Jain (Kotak Securities):** Asked about the rationale for combining CFO and Sales/Marketing roles under Yatin Malhotra.  
  *Management Response:* CEO Puneet Dalmia stated this is a planned transition. Malhotra knows internal operations well, and over time an operational focus will be maintained while a dedicated CFO will be appointed in due course.
- **Shravan Shah:** Asked about profitability expectations for Q3 FY27 considering price hikes and cost headwinds.  
  *Management Response:* Reversal of fixed-cost under-absorption (~Rs 120/tonne) and mix issues (~Rs 50-60/tonne), along with ~Rs 15/bag (~Rs 220-230/tonne) realization gains, should more than offset the ~Rs 75-80/tonne fuel cost hike, positioning Q3 EBITDA per ton to improve toward Rs 1,000+.

### One-line takeaway
Dalmia Bharat faces transient margin dilution and cost inflation from its Central asset acquisition and East monsoon headwinds, but ongoing capacity additions and price hikes position it for recovery in H2 FY27.
