# Cement & Building Materials - Glass & building products: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Management commentary across the sector reflects a bifurcated sentiment for Q2 FY27. Companies with strong domestic exposure or robust order books (such as Bigbloc Construction, Borosil, Carysil, Glass Wall Systems, and Sejal Glass) reported solid volume growth, record sales, and margin expansion, citing resilient domestic demand in real estate, commercial, and hospitality segments. Conversely, export-heavy players or those exposed to North American markets (like Global Surfaces) faced severe headwinds from geopolitical instability, rising freight costs, and new trade barriers. 

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, the Indian glass and building materials sector relied on unorganized manufacturing, commoditized volume plays, and heavy export dependence, competing primarily on low cost. Over the past five years, the model has shifted toward branded retail plays, integrated architectural glass and stone processing, asset-light manufacturing partnerships, and direct-to-consumer and e-commerce channels to capture higher realizations [Background].

**Shifts underway**
*   **Shift to high-margin value-added engineered and architectural products:** Companies are scaling up specialized lines (such as AAC panels, nanotechnology-based sinks, bullet-resistant glass, and fire-rated glass) to insulate margins. This shift is currently scaling. Sejal Glass imported a fire-rated glass line targeting commercial production in Q4 FY27, expecting EBITDA margins above 30% for specialized products [Sejal Glass Q2 FY27 concall]. Carysil is planning a global launch of nanotechnology-based sinks with ~20% value addition [Carysil Q2 FY27 concall]. Bigbloc noted AAC panels are gaining traction with large conglomerates like AdaniConneX [Bigbloc Q2 FY27 concall]. This matters for earnings as it reduces reliance on commoditized price competition and expands blended margins.
*   **Vertical integration and domestic market de-risking:** To mitigate volatile export freight and international trade tariffs (such as US Section 201 duties), companies are aggressively building domestic distribution and expanding in-house manufacturing. This shift is in the scaling stage. Global Surfaces discontinued its natural stone unit to focus on engineered quartz and commenced domestic dispatches in November 2026 [Global Surfaces Q2 FY27 concall]. Borosil commissioned a BIS-compliant manufacturing unit in Rajasthan for vacuum flasks and bottles [Borosil Q2 FY27 concall]. Carysil expanded domestic capacities to reduce import dependence ahead of BIS norms [Carysil Q2 FY27 concall]. 

**Government push**
*   Karnataka govt decision to provide 15 lakh plots and 1 [Govt: Karnataka govt: Provide 15 lakh plots and 1 lakh houses, Nov 2026] directly rewards housing demand and construction input suppliers like Bigbloc and Sejal Glass.
*   Noida administration's ban on construction activities [Govt: Noida administration: Ban on construction activities, Nov 2026] presents a localized headwind for near-term construction material off-take.
*   DGTR anti-dumping recommendations on Chinese borosilicate glassware [Govt: DGTR recommended anti-dumping duty on Chinese borosilicate glassware, Sep 2026] protects domestic players like Borosil from cheap dumping.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across domestic-facing companies (Bigbloc, Borosil, Carysil, Sejal Glass) highlighted robust traction, strong October momentum, and healthy real estate and infrastructure activity in India. 
*   **Divergence:** While Glass Wall Systems and Carysil noted strong order visibility in domestic markets and stable international partnerships, Global Surfaces struck a cautious note due to severe US tariff pressures (Section 201 duties) and inability to pass on costs. Borosil and Carysil reported softer seasonal demand in kitchenware/surfaces due to Diwali calendar timing and UK market softening, contrasting with Bigbloc's record-breaking October sales volumes.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Agree:** Most companies experienced input cost inflation driven by West Asia geopolitical tensions affecting fuel, gas, raw materials, and a surge in sea freight costs (Global Surfaces cited freight up 5x; Carysil and Bigbloc noted freight/RM pressures compressing sequential margins).
*   **Disagree:** Companies diverged in their ability to pass on cost increases. Bigbloc passed on 5-7% of an 8-10% cost increase; Carysil implemented a 5% price hike; Borosil took 3-7% price hikes in Opalware. However, Global Surfaces reported an *inability* to pass on a 10% cost increase due to disruptive US tariff adjustments. Glass Wall Systems remained largely insulated via explicit price-protection and escalation clauses in contracts for aluminum and glass.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Bigbloc Construction:** Acquired MP land (construction on hold); expanded rooftop solar to ~3.5 MW; targeting 75% capacity utilization in H2.
*   **Borosil Ltd:** Commissioned Rajasthan manufacturing unit for vacuum flasks; expanded Jaipur Pressware furnace (25 TPD to 32 TPD, Rs 50 crore capex by Q4 FY28); scaling captive solar (Phase 4).
*   **Carysil Ltd:** On track to add 250k quartz sinks and 150k steel sinks annual capacity by end-FY27; doubling faucet and appliance capacities; investing Rs 25-30 crore in brand stores.
*   **Glass Wall Systems:** Glass processing unit capex (~Rs 70 crore via IPO) progressing toward July/August commissioning; current facility can support >Rs 1,000 crore peak revenue.
*   **Global Surfaces:** Operating combined annual capacity of 1.14 million square meters across India and UAE.
*   **Sejal Glass Ltd:** Expanding via upcoming integration of Magnumtuff glass business and importing fire-rated glass manufacturing line for Q4 FY27 production; UAE expanding tempering line (AED 20 million total capex).

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Borosil Ltd:** Shifted strategy for the Bharuch glassware project, letting Borosil Scientific Ltd (BSL) fund and set up the facility while maintaining long-term supply arrangements.
*   **Global Surfaces Ltd:** Formally discontinued its Natural Stone unit in Bagru (effective March 31, 2026) to focus entirely on engineered quartz, and initiated domestic dispatches in November 2026.
*   **Carysil Ltd:** Appointed PwC for leadership restructuring and onboarded actor Bobby Deol as brand ambassador.

## What to watch next quarter
*   Final Ministry of Finance notification regarding DGTR anti-dumping duties on Chinese borosilicate glassware (Borosil).
*   Execution and scaling of new capacities, including Carysil's appliance/faucet expansion and Sejal Glass's fire-rated glass line and Magnumtuff integration.
*   Order intake and margin recovery for Global Surfaces amid US Section 201 tariff impacts.
*   H2 volume growth realization and utilization targets (Bigbloc targeting 75% utilization).
