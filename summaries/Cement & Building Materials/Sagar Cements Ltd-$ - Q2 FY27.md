# Sagar Cements Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Healthy volume growth and price recoveries offset by elevated input costs, expensive plant maintenance shutdowns, and a net loss.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue Growth YoY:** 17%
- **Volume Growth YoY:** 26%
- **EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 265
- **Loss After Tax (LAT):** Rs 52 crore
- **Gross Debt:** Rs 1,735 crore (Long-term borrowings: Rs 1,482 crore; Working capital: balance)
- **Consolidated Net Worth:** Rs 1,781 crore
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** 0.83:1
- **Cash and Bank Balances:** Rs 106 crore
- **Power and Fuel Cost:** Rs 1,473 per tonne (vs Rs 1,428 per tonne in Q2 FY26)
- **Freight Cost:** Rs 900 per tonne (vs Rs 855 per tonne in Q2 FY26)
- **Plant-wise Capacity Utilisation:** Mattampally: 73%, Gudipadu: 85%, Bayyavaram: 52%, Jeerabad: 72%, Jajpur: 38%, Dachepalli: 57%
- **Full-year FY27 Volume Guidance:** ~7 million tonnes (cement only)
- **Full-year FY27 EBITDA Guidance:** Rs 350 crore to Rs 375 crore (translating to ~Rs 525 per tonne)
- **FY28 Volume Guidance:** 8 million to 8.25 million tonnes
- **FY28 EBITDA per tonne Guidance (Pencilled in):** ~Rs 650

### Highlights
- Completed 0.75 million tonnes grinding capacity in Andhra Pradesh during the quarter, taking total capacity to 11.75 million tonnes.
- Commissioned a 4.35 MW waste heat recovery (WHR) capacity at Gudipadu and a 0.5 million tonne expansion at Jeerabad.
- Mattampally and Jeerabad plants underwent planned, long, and expensive maintenance shutdowns during the quarter, impacting operating performance.
- Board approved CapEx for the Superfine Building Materials division (microsilica operations in Galsi, West Bengal, and ultrafine GGBS modifications at Bayyavaram).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects profitability to improve in H2 FY27, backed by post-monsoon demand recovery, increasing renewable energy usage, logistics optimization, and plant upgrade efficiencies.
- Expects H2 FY27 EBITDA per tonne around Rs 650.
- Andhra unit expected to break even at an operational level in H2 FY27 or Q1 next year.
- Net debt expected to stabilize in Q3 FY27 and aggressively decline from Q4 FY27 onwards. Stated goal is to cut net debt by half over the next two years from current levels (~Rs 1,450 crore to Rs 1,600 crore range).
- Vizag land monetisation: Management expects to receive advances before the end of the financial year, targeting a total net monetisation value of Rs 350 crore, with ~Rs 150 crore expected as advance.
- Superfine division expected to contribute Rs 25 crore to Rs 30 crore in EBITDA in its first full year of operations (FY28) with 35% to 40% EBITDA margins.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Cement demand remained resilient in Q2 despite the monsoon-related slowdown, supported by government infrastructure projects and housing/construction activity.
- Uneven rainfall distribution helped moderate seasonal impact.
- Regional volume growth (YoY) year-to-date: AP and Telangana up >15%, Tamil Nadu up 10%, Kerala up 12%, Maharashtra up 10%, Madhya Pradesh up 7.5%–8%, Gujarat up ~5%, Karnataka and Odisha flat.
- Cement prices remained soft in July and August, recovering somewhat in September and October. In South India, non-trade prices increased by Rs 20/bag in September and another Rs 20/bag in October, while trade prices rose Rs 20/bag in September and Rs 5/bag in October. Madhya Pradesh and Odisha saw smaller hikes of Rs 5–Rs 10.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Elevated input costs (fuel, packaging, other operating expenses) due to West Asian geopolitical developments impacted profitability.
- Fuel costs covered up to January 2027 at sub-Rs 2 per 1,000 kcal/thermie. Spot buying thereafter could add ~Rs 200 per tonne unless offset.
- Freight costs hit a 5-quarter high at Rs 900 per tonne due to diesel price increases; management expects freight to remain flat going forward.
- Cost savings of Rs 50 to Rs 75 per tonne expected from WHR operations and operating leverage.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total capacity reached 11.75 million tonnes following the Andhra grinding unit addition.
- Superfine division CapEx projected at Rs 60 crore–Rs 80 crore over the next 18 months, plus Rs 20 crore working capital.
- Planned future power expansions include a 7–8 MW solar plant at Jeerabad. Future WHR planned for Andhra in FY28.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gross debt: Rs 1,735 crore (Long-term: Rs 1,482 crore, Working capital: balance).
- Debt-to-equity ratio: 0.83:1.
- Cash and bank balances: Rs 106 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated fuel and packaging costs driven by West Asian geopolitics.
- Expensive maintenance shutdowns at Mattampally weighed heavily on Q2 margins.
- Soft pricing environment during July and August.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Niteen S. Dharmawat asked why standalone EBITDA was very low (~Rs 47/tonne excluding cess write-back) and when plants would recover.** Management stated that Mattampally and Jeerabad underwent long, expensive maintenance shutdowns during the quarter, but operations will normalize in subsequent quarters.
2. **Niteen S. Dharmawat questioned the rising net debt and lowered full-year EBITDA projection.** Management replied that CapEx is largely concluded, debt will stabilize in Q3 and start declining from Q4, and full-year EBITDA is now guided at Rs 350 crore–Rs 375 crore due to input price inflation offsetting volume gains.
3. **Shravan Shah asked about price hikes and regional breakdown.** Management detailed the Rs 20/bag non-trade and trade hikes in September and October for South India, translating to an average ~Rs 25/bag (~Rs 500/tonne) realization increase exiting August through October.
4. **Rajesh Ravi asked if Q3 margin would exceed Rs 600/tonne.** Management confirmed they are looking at a ~Rs 650 EBITDA per tonne environment for Q3.
5. **Parth Bhavsar asked about fuel cost coverage and mix.** Management stated fuel is covered up to January 2027 at sub-Rs 2 per 1,000 kcal, with a shift mostly toward domestic petcoke alongside some South African/US imports.
6. **Vibha asked about Jeerabad incentives and WHR savings.** Management stated Jeerabad incentives are expected at Rs 30 crore/year (~Rs 50 crore expected over 2.5 years), and future Andhra WHR will save Rs 1.5 crore/month upon commissioning.

### One-line takeaway
Sagar Cements delivered strong 26% volume growth in Q2 FY27, but profitability was constrained by expensive maintenance shutdowns and input cost inflation; management guides for margin recovery to ~Rs 650 EBITDA/tonne in H2 FY27 as price hikes and efficiency measures take hold.
