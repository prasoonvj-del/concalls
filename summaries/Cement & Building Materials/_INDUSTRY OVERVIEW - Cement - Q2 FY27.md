# Cement & Building Materials - Cement: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Management commentaries across the cement sector for Q2 FY27 highlight a resilient volume environment supported by infrastructure capex and housing demand, which was nonetheless offset by a seasonally weak monsoon, prolonged rains and floods in the East, and severe input cost pressures driven by West Asian geopolitical conflicts affecting pet coke and fuel prices. While large players delivered strong volume growth, operating margins and profits in many cases faced compression due to fixed-cost under-absorption and rising energy expenses.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements largely agree that industry cement demand is expected to grow at 7% to 10% for the remainder of the year. Retail demand from individual homebuilders (IHBs) and government infrastructure (urban housing, railways, metro extensions, data centers) remained key supports.
- **Divergence (Regional Performance):** 
  - **East & Northeast:** Universal agreement that demand was severely disrupted during Q2 due to heavy monsoons and floods (noted by Birla Corp, Dalmia Bharat, India Cements, JK Cement, Nuvoco, Shree Cement, UltraTech). 
  - **North, West & South:** Generally robust performance, with North and South seeing strong growth (upwards of 15% to 20% in places), whereas localized drought conditions in parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Rajasthan posed minor near-term headwinds.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Fuel & Energy Inflation:** Landed fuel costs and pet coke prices rose sharply due to the West Asia crisis, with spot pet coke surpassing $180–$200/tonne. Shree Cement reported landed fuel costs rising to INR 2.09 per kCal, while India Cements/UltraTech noted fuel rates reaching INR 2.09–2.2 per kcal. Birla Corp, Dalmia Bharat, Nuvoco, and Sagar Cements similarly noted sequential increases in energy costs and guided for further cost pressures into Q3.
- **Raw Materials & Freight:** Packaging material costs (PP granules) remained elevated across the board, though Birla Corp reported a minor drop to Rs 248 per ton. Freight costs were impacted by diesel price highs, though Shree Cement noted reductions via special railway discounts and logistics tools.
- **Mitigation & Efficiency:** Companies are heavily relying on green energy and alternative fuels (AFR/TSR) to buffer margins—Dalmia Bharat reached 52% renewable share, Shree Cement at 65.2%, India Cements/UltraTech at 47%, and Birla Corp targeting 45%.

## Capex & capacity
- **Aggressive Expansions:** Capacity additions continue aggressively across majors. India Cements/UltraTech commissioned 4.6 million tonnes in September, taking total domestic capacity to 204.7 million tonnes on the path to 240 million tonnes. Dalmia Bharat is expanding toward 67 million tonnes with Belgaum trials underway. JK Cement is targeting 50 million tonnes by FY30. Shree Cement is on track for 80 million tonnes in the short term.
- **Diversification into Non-Core/Adjacent Segments:** Both India Cements and UltraTech launched the "Ultravolt" Wires and Cables business. JK Cement continued scaling its paint business (targeting break-even this fiscal). Sagar Cements approved CapEx for Superfine Building Materials (microsilica and ultrafine GGBS).

## What changed since last quarter
*(Based on sequential commentary)*
- **Guidance & Tone Shifts:** Tone shifted from cautious optimism to acknowledging tighter-than-expected margin squeezes as fuel costs escalated more rapidly than anticipated (pet coke jumping to $180+/tonne). 
- **Capex Adjustments:** Nuvoco front-ended its capex for H1 (reaching Rs 780 crores) and delayed its East expansion by 6 to 8 months to prioritize the Vadraj Cement integration.
- **Pricing Recovery:** While trade prices remained soft and depressed through July and August due to competitive intensity (noted by Birla Corp), managements across Sagar Cements, JK Cement, and UltraTech highlighted meaningful price recoveries and hikes of Rs 20–25 per bag implemented in September and October.

## Company one-liners
- **Birla Corporation Ltd:** Subdued by cost inflation and soft Central region trade prices, management remains cautious despite green energy and captive coal levers.
- **Dalmia Bharat Ltd:** Faced a soft Q2 due to East India floods and fixed-cost under-absorption, but remains confident in a stronger H2 and strategic asset ramp-up.
- **India Cements Ltd:** Delivered robust Q2 volume growth and higher utilization, exiting September with firm price hikes and strong demand momentum.
- **JK Cement Ltd:** Reported a 21% YoY net sales increase driven by strong grey cement volumes, maintaining volume guidance and advancing expansion timelines.
- **Nuvoco Vistas Corporation Ltd:** Delivered resilient volume growth despite severe macro cost headwinds and erratic monsoons, driven by premiumization and Vadraj execution.
- **Sagar Cements Ltd-$:** Achieved 26% volume growth constrained by expensive maintenance shutdowns and input inflation, guiding for a margin recovery in H2.
- **Shree Cement Ltd:** Delivered robust 18% YoY India volume growth during a seasonally weak quarter, backed by efficiency gains and a strong net cash balance.
- **UltraTech Cement Ltd:** Delivered strong volume growth and a 21% increase in net profit in Q2, driven by operating leverage, retail demand, and rising exit prices.

## What to watch next quarter
- **Cost Pass-Through & Realizations:** Ability of companies to successfully sustain October price hikes (Rs 20–25/bag) to offset incoming Q3 fuel cost inflation (spot pet coke and rising kCal costs).
- **Ramp-up of New Assets:** Progress on commercial production and utilization rates for Dalmia Bharat’s Belgaum/Central assets, Nuvoco’s Surat/Vadraj plant, and India Cements/UltraTech’s newly commissioned capacities.
- **Rural Demand & Weather Impact:** Post-monsoon construction activity pickup in flood-affected Eastern regions and the lagged impact of erratic rains on rural housing.

*Based on: Birla Corporation Ltd, Dalmia Bharat Ltd, India Cements Ltd, JK Cement Ltd, Nuvoco Vistas Corporation Ltd, Sagar Cements Ltd-$, Shree Cement Ltd, UltraTech Cement Ltd*
