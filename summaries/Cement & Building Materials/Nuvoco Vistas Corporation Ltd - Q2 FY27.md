# Nuvoco Vistas Corporation Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-29 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Challenging macro environment with input cost pressures and erratic rainfall, offset by resilient volume growth and strategic levers.

### Key numbers
- **Volume growth YoY:** 9%
- **Revenue growth YoY:** ~14%
- **EBITDA growth YoY:** 11%
- **Premiumization:** 41% (historic high of 45% mentioned in highlights, later cited as 41% or 45% depending on metric context; text states "reaches a historic high of 45%" and later "we came at a 45% premium")
- **Trade mix:** 75%
- **Blended cement share:** 83%
- **Fuel cost (Q1 FY27):** INR 1.52 per million kcal (INR 1,005 per tonne)
- **Fuel cost (Q2 FY27):** INR 1.59 per million kcal (INR 1,080 per tonne)
- **Expected Fuel cost (Q3 FY27):** INR 1.80 per million kcal
- **Road:Rail share:** 64:36
- **Lead distance:** 332 km (up from 328 km)
- **H1 FY27 Capex spend:** INR 780 crores
- **Full year FY27 Capex guidance:** INR 1,200 crores
- **Net debt:** ~INR 5,000 crores (at September end)
- **H1 FY27 Tax rate:** 41.6%

### Highlights
- Celebrated 10 years in the cement business ("10 Saal Bemisal"), scaling from 2.3 million tonnes to a planned 35 million tonnes capacity by FY28.
- Commissioned the 2 million tonnes grinding unit at Limla, Surat (Vadraj Cement Limited) during Q2; achieved single-day production of 3,500 tonnes.
- Executed an agreement with CleanMax for a 46.4 MW wind-solar hybrid renewable energy project in Rajasthan to generate ~100 million units annually.
- Duraguard Microfiber and Concreto Uno both crossed the 1-million-tonne brand milestone.
- Kutch project construction progressing with phased operationalization from Q3 FY27; bulk cement terminal at Viramgam (Sachana) on track.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 industry demand growth at ~7% to 8%.
- Surat plant capacity utilization targeted at 50%-60% in Q4 FY27, reaching 70%-75% next year.
- Full year FY27 capex revised to INR 1,200 crores (front-ended, with INR 1,000 crores for Vadraj, INR 50 crores for Viramgam, balance for running operations).
- FY28 capex outlay expected between INR 800 crores to INR 900 crores; 2-year total capex expected at INR 2,000 to INR 2,100 crores.
- Net debt expected to hover between 2x to 2.5x in the short to medium term, returning to less than 2x EBITDA once Vadraj volumes are absorbed.
- Gujarat EBITDA per ton expected to match Nuvoco's all-India EBITDA per ton in year 3 (FY29).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Operating environment was challenging due to the Middle East conflict escalating, causing a sharp jump in input prices (petcoke, coal, granules, gypsum).
- Domestic intense and erratic rainfall, especially in the East, disrupted construction activity and depleted thermal coal inventories at power plants, restricting rake availability and spiking e-auction coal prices.
- Regional demand varied; monsoons disrupted the East, while regions with lower rainfall performed better.
- North India demand remained robust in Q2.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Q2 energy cost increased to INR 1,080/tonne (INR 1.59 per million kcal) from INR 1,005/tonne (INR 1.52 per million kcal) in Q1.
- Q3 fuel cost per million kcal expected to rise to INR 1.80, translating to an increase of INR 70 to INR 80 per tonne in energy cost over Q2.
- Q2 cost inflation included power/fuel (+INR 90), raw materials (+INR 60), and packing bags (+INR 10), totaling an inflation of INR 180-190 over Q1.
- Q3 cost inflation expected to be INR 100 to INR 120 per tonne.
- Fuel mix in Q2: Coal 66%, Petcoke 27%, AFR 6%. Clinker-to-cement (CK) ratio was 1.75.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- H1 FY27 capex accelerated to INR 780 crores against an original plan of INR 450-460 crores.
- East expansion slightly delayed by 6 to 8 months (from Q3/Q4 FY27 to Q1/Q2 FY28) to prioritize Vadraj Cement execution.
- Kutch clinker line and jetty operations expected to operationalize by early December 2026.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt increased to ~INR 5,000 crores due to petcoke stocking/value impact (working capital increase of ~INR 200 crores) and front-ended capex.
- Working capital release and improved cash generation expected in H2.
- Tax rate is higher at 41.6% in H1 because the company is under the old tax regime where MAT credit is not available; transition to the new regime is under evaluation.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical developments and Middle East conflict leading to volatile petcoke and imported fuel prices (spot petcoke reaching $190-$200+/tonne).
- Erratic rainfall and monsoon disruptions impacting construction activity and logistics/rake availability.
- Cement prices not improving in line with expectations despite cost pressures.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Navin Sahadeo (ICICI Securities):** Asked about fuel cost progression and spot impact. Management detailed the fuel cost moving from INR 1.52 (Q1) to INR 1.59 (Q2) and expected INR 1.80 (Q3), noting adequate petcoke inventory coverage.
- **Pinakin (HSBC):** Inquired about overall cost trends and net impact in Q3. Management guided to INR 100-120/tonne cost inflation in Q3 offset by recent price hikes of ~INR 20/tonne.
- **Siddharth Mehrotra (Dolat Capital / independent):** Asked about Surat plant utilization and capex resequencing. Management stated Surat will reach 50-60% utilization in Q4 FY27 and explained the East expansion delay to prioritize Vadraj.
- **Shravan Shah (Dolat Capital):** Questioned net debt movement and tax rates. Management clarified that net debt rose to ~INR 5,000 crores due to working capital and front-ended capex but will normalize by year-end; higher tax rate is due to the old tax regime.
- **Swatheka (Citigroup/peer):** Asked about rural demand and Vadraj steady-state EBITDA. Management expects 8% industry growth in H2 and targets Vadraj EBITDA per ton to match Nuvoco's blended level by year 3 (FY29).

### One-line takeaway
Nuvoco delivered resilient Q2 FY27 volume and revenue growth despite severe macro cost headwinds and erratic monsoons, driven by strong premiumization and front-ended execution of the Vadraj acquisition.
