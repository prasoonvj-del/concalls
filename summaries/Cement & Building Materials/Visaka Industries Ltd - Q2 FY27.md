# Visaka Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 and H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Significant improvement in operating performance, profitability, and balance sheet strength.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 399 crore (+23% YoY) for Q2 FY27; Rs 989 crore (+19% YoY) for H1 FY27.
- **EBITDA:** Rs 38 crore (more than tripled) for Q2 FY27; Rs 129 crore (more than doubled) for H1 FY27.
- **EBITDA Margin:** 9.4% (up from 3.6%) for Q2 FY27; 13% (up from 7.6%) for H1 FY27.
- **PBT (excluding exceptional items):** Positive at Rs 17 crore for Q2 FY27 (vs. loss of Rs 11 crore YoY).
- **Total Borrowings:** Declined by ~Rs 66 crore to Rs 237 crore in H1 FY27.
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** 0.25x.
- **Receivable Days:** Improved from 35 days (March 2026) to 25 days (September 2026).
- **Operating Cash Flow:** ~Rs 70 crore (vs. Rs 55 crore YoY) in H1 FY27.
- **Segment Capacity Utilizations:** Fiber cement boards plants at near full capacity (~100%) in Q2; Synthetic Yarn at 92% (up from 89%); ATUM solar at 10 megawatts out of 30 megawatts capacity.
- **Segment Volume/Turnover Growth (Q2 YoY):** Cement Roofing volumes +20%, turnover +34%; Panels volumes +55%, turnover +56%; Boards volumes +9% and turnover +13% (for H1 FY27); Synthetic Yarn revenue +16%.
- **Segment Profit (Q2):** Synthetic Yarn segment PBIT improved to ~Rs 5 crore (from Rs 2 crore).
- **One-off Receipt:** Holding company received Rs 69 crore pursuant to a favorable court order against Hyderabad Cricket Association.

### Highlights
- Cement Roofing volumes and realizations were supported by improved demand and higher prices of substitute metal roofing sheets (driven by BIS introduction and lower China imports).
- V-Next boards and panels saw near full capacity utilization; panels are the fastest-growing division despite lower GCC exports due to the Middle East war.
- Synthetic Yarn segment performance improved after two challenging years, driven by better operating efficiencies and cost optimization.
- Holding company received Rs 69 crore from a long-pending dispute with the Hyderabad Cricket Association, resulting in significant exceptional gains in H1 PAT after accounting for taxes.
- *Check against record:* The call aligns with the ongoing strategic focus on diversified building materials and margin recovery, though specific revenue mix percentages (e.g., 47%) were not explicitly repeated in the transcript.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains a medium-term objective of delivering 10% to 12% revenue CAGR with sustainable EBITDA margins of 10% to 12%.
- The Rs 2,500 crore turnover target is expected to be achieved by 2030 rather than the next two years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Cement roofing underlying demand remains healthy in rural and semi-urban markets with limited new industry capacity added.
- V-Next boards face barriers such as a lack of awareness among architects/contractors and specialized skill requirements for installation.
- ATUM solar experiences slow adoption as it is an emerging, integrated roofing-solar product requiring customer awareness and education on its 40%-50% space-saving compared to traditional solar roofs.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Synthetic yarn raw material costs faced pressure due to Middle East conflict and crude oil volatility, part of which was passed on.
- Higher ocean freight costs affected the export business.
- Fiber cement board raw material (cellulose wood pulp) is sourced via alternative supply chains from France, Russia, and New Zealand with no near-term supply issues.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Fiber cement boards and calcium silicate board facility at Tonk, Rajasthan is progressing as planned (targeted for completion December 2027 into 2028; annual capacity 72,000 tons / 6,000 tons per month).
- Construction chemical project at Tumkur is targeted for completion in January 2027.
- No new capacity expansions are planned for Synthetic Yarn.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total borrowings reduced by ~Rs 66 crore in H1 FY27 to Rs 237 crore.
- Receivable days improved to 25 days in September 2026.
- Inventory increased during H1 due to traditional seasonal build-up for fourth-quarter roofing sheet sales.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Middle East war situation and crude oil volatility impacting polyester raw material and ocean freight costs.
- Lower exports of boards to GCC markets due to the Middle East situation.
- Resistance from carpenters and installers towards fiber cement boards due to specialized cutting tools and screw-holding requirements compared to plywood/HDHMR.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Saket Kapoor asked about the nature and tax treatment of the Rs 69 crore HCA court order.** Management clarified it was a long-pending dispute resolved in the last quarter, treated as a capital receipt, with advance tax remitted.
2. **Saket Kapoor and Kamal Jeswani asked about seasonality and QoQ revenue decline despite high capacity utilization.** Management explained that cement roofing is seasonal (stronger in Q1/Q4, lower in Q2/Q3), while boards maintain steady demand.
3. **Ashok Majithia questioned how Visaka competes with plywood/HDHMR carpenter loyalty programs and pulp cost exposure.** Management stated V-Next is positioned on total life cycle economics (fire, termite, and water resistance) and uses diversified pulp suppliers in France, Russia, and New Zealand.
4. **Sheik Ahmed asked about ATUM's economic comparison and capacity utilization.** Management stated ATUM is at 10 MW utilization out of 30 MW capacity and offers faster installation and structural savings despite being an emerging category.
5. **Neeraj inquired about ATUM's overseas patents and revenue outlook.** Management noted patents are secured in the U.S., South Africa, and Europe, but meaningful revenue traction will take a couple of years.

### One-line takeaway
Visaka Industries delivered a strong H1 FY27 driven by robust roofing demand, realization gains, near-full board capacity utilization, and an exceptional Rs 69 crore legal recovery, while advancing its non-asbestos and chemical expansion plans.
