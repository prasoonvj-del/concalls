# Birla Corporation Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Cement & Building Materials*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Subdued results and depressed trade prices in core markets, combined with cost headwinds.

### Key numbers
- Capacity utilization: 85%
- Fuel cost (per million type / implied per ton): Rs 1.81 (Q2) expected to be Rs 2 to 2.05 in Q3 (increase of about Rs 100 per ton)
- Packing material cost: Rs 248 per ton (down from Rs 269 last quarter)
- Mukutban volume: 6.5 lakh tonnes
- Lead distance: 336 km (Mukutban: 411 km)
- Incentive accrued in Q2: Rs 30 crores (First half total accrual: Rs 63 crores; closing balance: Rs 545 crores)
- Net debt: Rs 2,432 crores as of end-Q2 compared to Rs 2,200 crores as of June
- Renewable power share: 32%+ (targeting 45% in next 1 year)
- Captive coal share of fuel: ~13% for the year

### Highlights
- Results described by management as modest and subdued, largely due to cost pressures and soft trade cement prices in the Central region.
- Consolidation in the industry has not resulted in expected pricing improvements in operating regions; market leaders have diluted price positioning.
- Company consciously cut down leads and focused on proximate markets of Vidarbha, Marathwada, and Khandesh, while increasing premiumization in Rajasthan and Maharashtra.
- Bikram captive coal mine is on stream, helping fuel mix, though temporary clinker purchases due to a carryforward of a delayed planned shutdown impacted profitability.
- Jute division is proceeding with modernization plans and focusing on value-added products (geotextiles/natural fibers), though it remains a minor contributor to financials.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Q3 fuel costs to increase by about Rs 100 per ton sequentially.
- Capacity expansion timeline of 6.2 million tonnes to come online in FY29 is maintained.
- Renewable power contribution targeted to reach 45% in the next 1 year.
- Capex guidance for FY27/28/29: Too early to comment; guidance to be given towards the close of the year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand affected by severe monsoons, floods in parts of Northern Bihar and Eastern UP, and drought conditions in Maharashtra and parts of Rajasthan.
- Upcoming major elections in UP expected to cause a temporary slowdown in demand due to the model code of conduct.
- Non-trade segment saw price stabilization towards the end of the quarter, but trade prices remain soft due to competitive intensity and anxiety among large players to retain market share.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Fuel costs spiked due to geopolitical pet coke price hikes and domestic coal supply tightness caused by flooded Coal India mines and preferential allocation to the power sector.
- Packaging material cost stood at Rs 248 per ton.
- External clinker purchases to bridge the deficit from delayed shutdowns dented Q2 margins.
- Captive coal from Bikram and Sial Ghoghri provides a 20% to 25% cost advantage over market prices and offers consistent quality.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capacity expansion of 6.2 million tonnes slated for FY29 remains on track.
- Focus on ramping up production at Bikram coal block to extract better-grade coal usable in kilns.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt increased sequentially from Rs 2,200 crores (June) to Rs 2,432 crores due to higher working capital requirements (larger inventory value for pet coke shipments).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Soft trade prices and aggressive market share tactics by large players.
- Persistent fuel inflation (pet coke and domestic coal) expected to continue into Q3.
- Drought conditions in key regions and election-related headwinds impacting near-term demand.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shravan Shah (Dalot Capital) on volume/pricing outlook & EBITDA:** Management stated Q3 fuel costs will rise by ~Rs 100/ton, and price pass-through depends on industry-wide behavior. (Management did not dodge).
2. **Pathanjali Srinivasan (Sundaram Mutual Fund) on captive coal savings:** Management clarified that captive coal is currently 20% to 25% cheaper than market prices and meets ~13% of the fuel requirement this year. (Management did not dodge).
3. **Girija Ray (Nirmal Bang) on Mukutban volumes, lead distance, and clinker status:** Management provided specific metrics (6.5 lakh tonnes volume, 411 km lead distance, and confirmed external clinker purchases due to shutdown spillover). (Management did not dodge).
4. **Saket Kapoor (Kapoor & Co.) on debt increase and West Bengal government incentives:** Management explained that working capital inflation drove up net debt to Rs 2,432 crores, and noted that the incentive cancellation case in the High Court remains sub judice with hopes for a favorable view from the new regime. (Management did not dodge).

### One-line takeaway
Birla Corporation's Q2 performance was subdued by cost inflation and depressed trade prices in its core Central region, with management adopting a cautious outlook for H2 despite ongoing cost-optimization initiatives.
