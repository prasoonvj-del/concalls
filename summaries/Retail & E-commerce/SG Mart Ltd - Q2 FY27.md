# SG Mart Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-29 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted sustainable consecutive three quarters of EBITDA at Rs 72 crore and robust execution across trading and manufacturing pillars.

### Key numbers
- **EBITDA:** Rs 72 crore (achieved for Q2 FY27 and maintained consistently for the last three quarters).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 131 crore.
- **H1 FY27 Earnings growth:** Up 100% YoY.
- **H1 FY27 Capex:** Rs 250 crore.
- **Cash on books:** Around Rs 700 crore (as of September 30, balance sheet date).
- **Working capital days:** Stable at 18 days (inventory days at 14).
- **HR coil processing volume (Q2 FY27):** 206,000 tonnes (up 16% QoQ).
- **Profiles volume (Q2 FY27):** Around 40,000 tonnes (up 20% vs Q1 FY27).
- **Service centers:** 8 operational; target 25+ by 2030 (potentially 30–35 by 2030).
- **Solar annual run rate capacity:** Half a million tonnes (current quarterly volume around 11,000 tonnes/quarter; annual run rate of 50,000 tonnes; order book >50,000 tonnes).
- **Service center investment economics:** ~Rs 50 crore for land (10-acre parcel), Rs 5-10 crore for plant and machinery/shed, total investment around Rs 70-80 crore generating monthly revenue of Rs 70-80 crore.
- **Blended EBITDA margin:** Currently 4% to 5% (guided to reach 6% to 7% post backward integration).
- **Profile business EBITDA per tonne:** Steel profiles at Rs 4,000–Rs 5,000; solar structures at Rs 3,000–Rs 4,000; service center business around Rs 2,000 per tonne.
- **Planned Capex:** Rs 250 crore to Rs 300 crore for the rest of the year; approximately Rs 1,500 crore over the next 2-3 years for service centers and the Raipur manufacturing plant.
- **Target ROCE:** 25% (potentially >30% at full capacity utilization).

### Highlights
- Business model successfully shifted from pure trading to a combined trading and manufacturing platform (processing, profiles, and accessories).
- Added Indore service center in Q2; Jaipur and Ahmedabad started 2 months prior.
- Total active group dealers and distributors serviced stand at nearly 1,200.
- Online sales channel (SG Buildkart subsidiary) being built, expected to be ready in 6 months with a focus on ease of doing business and low cash burn.
- First backward integration plant for coated steel in Raipur expected to start in 9 to 12 months, which management expects to improve EBITDA margins by 2% to 3%.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains earnings capability of 50%+ earnings CAGR for the next 3 to 4 years.
- Confident of achieving the H2 requirement of Rs 170 crore EBITDA to meet/exceed earlier expectations (based on H1 EBITDA of Rs 131 crore).
- ROCE profile targeted at 25%, scaling beyond 30% at full capacity utilization.

### Demand & industry commentary
- End demand impacted to some extent by a 25%-30% spike in steel prices over the last 6-7 months (following the Middle East crisis), ocean freight price hikes, and high crude/commodity prices.
- Solar order book visibility is strong at 1 year (>50,000 tonnes), supported by resolved land acquisition delays from independent power producers.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Lean inventory (14–15 days) shields the company from commodity price volatility and inventory losses/gains.
- Blended EBITDA margin expected to increase from 4%-5% to 6%-7% following the Raipur backward integration for coated steel.
- CRCA sheets sell at a premium of Rs 2,000 to Rs 3,000 per tonne over HR coils.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Spent Rs 250 crore in H1 FY27 on land parcels, plant, and equipment for service centers and the Raipur plant.
- Planned additional capex of Rs 250-300 crore for H2 and roughly Rs 1,500 crore over the next 2-3 years, fully funded via internal accruals and existing cash.
- Standard capacity per service center: ~8,000 tonnes of HR coil processing, 1,000-2,000 tonnes of CRCA sheets, and 2,000-3,000 tonnes of pre-galvanized sheets (total ~12,000-13,000 tonnes/month per service center).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Operating cash flow generation remains strong, exceeding EBITDA.
- Cash on books stands around Rs 700 crore.
- Working capital days stable at 18 days.
- Expansion funded entirely through internal cash flows and existing cash reserves.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Macro challenges include commodity price swings (Middle East crisis), surges in ocean freight rates, and rising interest rates impacting overall business sentiments and end-user demand.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Vikas Mistri (Ventures) asked about bottlenecks in ramping up service centers and contract manufacturing plans.** Management replied that faster-than-expected ramp-up in existing centers gives confidence to beat original guidance, and contract manufacturing is currently under evaluation.
2. **Garvit Goyal asked if Q2 numbers included inventory gains and if macro challenges/interest rates impact FY28.** Management clarified that low inventory days (14 days) insulate them from steel price swings, and while macro headwinds exist, SG Mart's young model, lean inventory, and debt-free capex insulate its growth.
3. **Akhilesh Kumar asked about welding rod manufacturing and PEB exposure.** Management stated welding rods are currently sourced from third parties and will be manufactured in-house once scaled up; PEB exposure is serviced via steel plate processing and purlin manufacturing.
4. **Rahul Kumar asked about EBITDA per tonne across segments and upcoming product launches.** Management noted service centers make ~Rs 2,000/tonne, steel profiles make Rs 4,000–Rs 5,000/tonne, and solar structures make Rs 3,000–Rs 4,000/tonne; new products will initially start via trading and move to in-house manufacturing upon scaling.
5. **Rohan Baranwal asked about the solar order book and capacity of the 18 planned service centers.** Management stated the solar order book is >50,000 tonnes (1-year visibility), and 25 service centers at full scale can reach a capacity of ~3.5 million tonnes.

### One-line takeaway
SG Mart delivered a steady Q2 FY27 with Rs 72 crore EBITDA, backed by a unique trading-plus-manufacturing model, strong cash generation, and an aggressive pan-India service center expansion plan funded entirely through internal accruals.
