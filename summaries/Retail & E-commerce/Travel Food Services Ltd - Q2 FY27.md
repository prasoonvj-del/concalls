# Travel Food Services Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-18 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered double-digit sales and profit growth despite a challenging operating environment and declining passenger traffic due to prolonged West Asia crisis.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (no specific quantitative guidance given, similar to last quarter).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from "flat passenger traffic" in Q1 to a more challenged Q2 where passenger traffic at managed airports declined 4.8% YoY (and 2.2% in H1), described as one of the toughest operating quarters post-COVID, though H2 expectations remain cautiously optimistic.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: West Asia crisis impact became more pronounced in domestic traffic during Q2; receivables normalization completed.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Commenced operations at Bhogapuram Airport with six travel QSR outlets; expanded Bengaluru T1 presence; GMR JV secured Delhi Terminal 3 concession.

### Key numbers
- System-wide sales: Rs 8.65 billion (+18.8% YoY) in Q2; Rs 17.1 billion (+18.4% YoY) in H1
- Consolidated revenue from operations: Rs 4.3 billion (+19.5% YoY) in Q2; Rs 8.8 billion (+20.1% YoY) in H1
- Consolidated Profit After Tax (PAT): Rs 1.47 billion (+50.5% YoY) in Q2; Rs 2.76 billion (+43.2% YoY) in H1
- Adjusted PAT (excluding provision write-backs): Rs 1 billion in Q2 (+2.7% YoY); Rs 2.2 billion in H1 (+13.8% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 1.6 billion (+16.8% YoY) in Q2; margin: 37.1% (vs 38% YoY)
- System-wide Like-for-Like (LFL) sales growth: 3.5% in Q2; 2.2% in H1
- Consolidated LFL sales growth: 6.6% in Q2; 5.3% in H1
- Net contract gains: 11.1% in Q2 and 13.5% in H1 (system-wide); 11% in Q2 and 15.7% in H1 (consolidated)
- Reported gross profit margin: 85.8% (Adjusted gross profit margin: 80.7% in Q2 excluding lounge aggregation costs of Rs 216 million)
- Consolidated cash balance: Approximately Rs 10.3 billion (debt-free)

### Highlights
- Mobilized over 70 travel QSR outlets over the last 12 months; presence expanded from 18 to 22 airports, and overall outlet count from 501 to 570. Brand portfolio stands at 156 brands (21 added over the year, including Foo and Brijwasi).
- GMR Hospitality Limited (JV with GMR Airports) secured the travel QSR concession for Terminal 3 at Delhi Airport (nearly 50 outlets) and commenced operations at Bhogapuram Airport with 6 travel QSR outlets.
- Semolina Kitchens (JV with Adani Airports) expanded network from 115 to 154 outlets, including Navi Mumbai International Airport.
- Expanded presence at Bengaluru Terminal 1 with the opening of 'The Filter Coffee' and another travel QSR under construction.
- Kyra Lounge at Hong Kong International Airport named Asia's Leading Airport Lounge at the World Travel Awards 2026 for the second consecutive year; won 10 awards at the Global Airport Food and Beverage (FAB) Awards 2026.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is cautiously optimistic on the direction of travel for H2, expecting gradual improvement driven by seasonally stronger travel and progressive restoration of airline capacity in winter schedules.
- Expect contribution from newer locations (Noida, Delhi, Cochin) to increase progressively as operations mature.
- Long-term Indian passenger traffic expected to grow at 7%-8% CAGR over the next decade.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Passenger traffic at TFS-managed airports declined 2.2% in H1, with Q2 falling 4.8% YoY due to higher airfares, airline capacity rationalization, schedule changes, and the prolonged West Asia crisis affecting domestic and international traffic.
- Indian consumers show strong premiumization trends, high awareness, and desire for culinary experiences.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Food inflation reached closer to 7.5%, but long-term supply contracts and buying volumes protected margins.
- Consolidated EBITDA margin impacted slightly by higher employee and other operating expenses due to mobilization and ramp-up of new operations.
- Lounge aggregation service costs of Rs 216 million in Q2 and Rs 439 million in H1 are recorded under other expenses.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- 20 new outlets opened in the last month and a half; about 50 outlets currently in development/construction pipeline to be rolled out over the next 3 to 4 quarters.
- Selectively evaluating highway opportunities on expressways and international lounge opportunities in APAC and the Middle East via subsidiaries in Dubai and Indonesia.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Company remains completely debt-free with a consolidated cash balance of approximately Rs 10.3 billion as of September 30, 2026.
- Receivables have progressively normalized during H1 following timing-related buildup from the EATS platform.
- Steady-state debtor days expected to hover around 45 to 52 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Weaker passenger environment and air traffic declines due to the prolonged West Asia crisis and airline capacity rationalization.
- Pre-operating and ramp-up costs carried ahead of revenues at newer locations like Noida, Delhi, and Cochin.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Sanjay Ladha (Bastion Research) on airport privatization (Chennai/Kolkata) and JV impact:** Management stated they will bid for upcoming tenders with historical renewal rates north of 90%-94%, and clarified that Chennai and Kolkata are operated directly by TFS (not under JV models like Delhi). Management added they cannot confirm privatization schedules beyond public press articles.
- **Achal Kumar (ICICI Securities) on net contract gains and LFL drivers:** Management explained that net contract gains are lumpy and could have been ~15% higher if passenger spend wasn't negative 5% in Q2. LFL growth comprises ~4%-5% price increases and the balance from premiumization and menu engineering.
- **Praneeth Kumar Reddy (Capital) on lounge competition and international timeline:** Management noted competition is business-as-usual, relationships with card partners are strong, and teams are being built up in Dubai and Indonesia for regional APAC/Middle East expansion.
- **Aniket Kamble on highway pilot and EATS benefits:** Management stated expressways are the right focus for long-term (decade-long) opportunities. EATS platform allows direct tie-ups with banks, customized offerings for different card tiers, and unlocks next-level passenger services.
- **Riddhansh Chandak on outlet count drop:** Management clarified that shutdowns (e.g., Mumbai T1/T2 renovations) are directional and quarter-end timing; large footprint units opened elsewhere have higher revenue potential than small kiosks.

### One-line takeaway
Travel Food Services delivered robust double-digit sales and profit growth in Q2 FY27 despite a 4.8% decline in passenger traffic, driven by aggressive network expansion, disciplined execution, and a strong debt-free balance sheet.
