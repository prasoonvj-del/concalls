# Meenakshi India Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-11 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Core business returned to positive operating performance and profitability improved significantly, supported by other income and corrective measures.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations:** Rs. 3,224 lakhs (down 3.4% YoY from Rs. 3,339 lakhs; up 10.3% QoQ from Rs. 2,921 lakhs).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 712 lakhs (up ~153% YoY from Rs. 281 lakhs; up ~58% QoQ from Rs. 451 lakhs).
- **Earnings Per Share (EPS):** Rs. 6.34 (compared to Rs. 2.51 YoY and Rs. 4.02 QoQ).
- **Core Garment Business Operating Profit:** Rs. 343 lakhs (compared to Rs. 28.24 lakhs YoY).
- **Total Expenses:** Rs. 3,172 lakhs (down 6.9% YoY and 18.5% QoQ).
- **Cost of Materials (net of inventory):** Rs. 1,472 lakhs (45.7% of revenue, down from ~48% YoY).
- **Employee Benefit Expense:** Rs. 894 lakhs (flat YoY, down 7.3% QoQ).
- **Other Expenses:** Rs. 747 lakhs (down 7.3% YoY).
- **Finance Cost:** Rs. 9.98 lakhs.
- **Other Income:** Rs. 711 lakhs (includes Rs. 372.64 lakhs unrealized fair gain on investments, Rs. 225.78 lakhs net gain from forex forward contracts, and Rs. 32.91 lakhs realized investment gains).
- **Effective Tax Rate:** 6.6%.
- **PAT Margin on Total Income:** ~18%.
- **Core EBITDA Margin (Garment business excluding other income):** ~3.4% (turned positive from marginally negative YoY).
- **Net Worth:** Rs. 142.65 crores as of June 30, 2026 (book value Rs. 127 per share), compared to Rs. 150.65 crores as of March 2026.
- **Borrowings:** Under Rs. 1 crore (effectively debt-free).
- **Cash, Bank Balances, and Investments:** Approximately Rs. 80 crores.
- **Capacity Utilization:** ~65% absolute number of pieces produced.
- **FY30 Targets (Projected):** Revenue of Rs. 500 crores and PAT of Rs. 65 crores.

### Highlights
- Company equity shares were listed on BSE Limited on July 22, 2026, under the direct listing route (previously listed on the non-functional Calcutta Stock Exchange; no funds raised via IPO).
- Core garment business returned to positive operating performance after reporting an operating loss in the corresponding quarter of the previous year.
- Maintained customer relationships without losing clients despite US tariff headwinds and overstocking issues, utilizing customer pricing partnerships and discounts.
- Entered D2C test marketing phase but put it on hold due to pending patent issues with SHORTSTOP.
- Established an MOU for contract manufacturing in Sri Lanka to hedge potential adverse US tariff impacts on India.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects capacity utilization to reach close to 70% in FY27.
- Management targets an EBITDA margin of about 17% by FY28.
- Management guided for FY30 revenue and PAT of Rs. 500 crores and Rs. 65 crores respectively, which management termed conservative.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Structural shifts favoring India include the China Plus One strategy and bilateral/free trade agreements with the UK, EU, and ongoing talks with the US.
- Meenakshi focuses on premium bottomwear and outerwear, competing against countries like Turkey, Tunisia, Eastern Europe, and Latin America rather than mass-market hubs like Bangladesh, Vietnam, and Pakistan.
- Revenue geographic split is roughly 60% Europe and 40% US, with smaller bases in Australia and the Middle East; US volumes slowed due to tariffs and overstocking, while Europe gained share.
- Top 5 clients concentrate about 70% of revenue, and top 10 represent above 95% (total active customers range between 12 to 20).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material costs improved to 45.7% of revenue from ~48% YoY due to better product mix, pricing discipline, and cost control.
- Margins were previously impacted due to discounts offered to US customers to retain them amidst tariff pressures.
- Recycled man-made fiber capacity is available in India to meet customer demands where required.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Current capacity stands at 18 lakh pieces, planned to scale up to 38 lakh pieces.
- Total capex of Rs. 40-50 crores planned up to FY30 in a phased manner: first new factory expected to be operational by FY28 (costing ~Rs. 20 crores), and a second factory around FY30 (similar cost).
- Exploring future geographical manufacturing risk diversification into Nepal, Vietnam, and Sri Lanka.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Company is effectively debt-free with borrowings under Rs. 1 crore.
- Held cash, bank balances, and investments of approximately Rs. 80 crores as of June 30, 2026.
- Landed duty paid (LDP) model is offered selectively for a small portion of business to provide customer inventory flexibility at a reasonably good margin.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility and unpredictable changes in US trade policies and tariffs.
- Unrealized fair value gains and forex forward contract gains in other income are market-linked and non-recurring/variable.
- Patent issues pending on the D2C brand initiative.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Rishikesh Rajesh Shah (Alchemy Capital) asked why margins took a hit despite being a premium player capable of passing on tariffs.** Management answered that tariffs impacted selling prices across the board, but they shared much less burden compared to mass-market manufacturers.
2. **Rishikesh Rajesh Shah asked about the rationale behind moving manufacturing bases outside India (Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Nepal) given strong domestic demand.** Management clarified that the Sri Lanka MOU was executed solely to hedge against potential adverse US tariff shifts, while Nepal is a future plan for geographic risk diversification.
3. **Rhea Patel asked about the geographic revenue breakup and impact of US tariffs.** Management stated the mix is currently about 60% Europe and 50-50 historically; US volumes reduced due to tariffs and overstocking, but no customers were lost because management partnered on discounts.
4. **Jagdish Sharma asked why the FY30 guidance of Rs. 500 cr revenue and Rs. 65 cr PAT is conservative given past EBITDA levels.** Management replied that targets are conservative because European FTAs and US trade policies are still evolving and not fully operational.
5. **Jignesh asked about the rationale for the IPO given strong cash reserves.** Management clarified they did not launch an IPO or raise funds; they simply executed a direct listing on BSE since their prior exchange (Calcutta Stock Exchange) was non-functional.

### One-line takeaway
Meenakshi India delivered a strong Q1 FY27 PAT growth of ~153% YoY to Rs. 712 lakhs driven by other income and a turnaround in its core garment business, backed by a zero-debt balance sheet and robust liquidity.
