# Brand Concepts Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-21 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management cited continued pricing pressures, geopolitical escalations affecting raw material costs, and temporary consolidation headwinds, though they expressed confidence in a rebound from October.

### Key numbers
- Revenue growth: Up almost 11% YoY (Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26).
- PBT loss: Widened marginally YoY due to higher depreciation, interest costs, and other operating investments.
- Inventory: Reduced by Rs 4-5 crores from March end to June end; stood at Rs 123 crores by June end (vs Rs 128 crores at March end).
- Hard luggage plant capacity (PC unit): Operating at 80% plus utilization (capacity of ~25,000 pieces per month).
- PP plant capacity: Trials done, expected to reach 75%-80% capacity (capacity of ~25,000 pieces per month) by October or latest by November.
- Juicy revenue: Aiming to cross Rs 20-22 crores in FY27 (vs Rs 12 crores in FY26).
- Store closures: 19 bottom-end retail stores being closed (consolidation 75%-80% through, to be completed by September).

### Highlights
- Undertook a major e-commerce restructuring to realign high-volume SKUs to certain channels as part of a long-term sustainable growth strategy, causing a temporary dent in primary billings while secondary sales remained strong.
- Consolidated the BAGLINE retail network by closing non-profitable stores (clipping the tail) to protect profitability.
- Hard luggage PC manufacturing unit achieved plant-level positivity at the 20,000-piece production level instead of the anticipated 30,000-35,000 units.
- Promoters previously infused Rs 20 crores of capital, out of which Rs 15 crores is already in the system.
- New brands like Superdry, Off-White, and Juicy are scaling up; Off-White stores opened in Bangalore and Delhi (with launches planned in Kolkata and Mumbai). Aeropostale is planned to be given up.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects the company to get back to growth from October onwards.
- Guidance: Maintained a long-term vision of reaching a Rs 1,000 crore top line over 4-5 years.
- Phase 3 (operating leverage) is conservatively expected about 1.5 years from now.
- No major capital expenditure is planned for the next 1-2 years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Travel wear category faces severe pricing pressure due to new incumbent private equity-backed players offering heavy discounts and unsustainable pricing.
- Consumer demand at the premium end remains resilient (e.g., high-price point Tommy Hilfiger sets at Rs 27,000 to Rs 32,000 are among top sellers).
- Company stated it has not lost market share on any basis despite external challenges and reduced travel trends.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Escalating war situations have increased raw material costs.
- Bulk of the manufacturing benefits are currently being passed on to end consumers through pricing to stay relevant amid intense competition.
- Plant-level EBITDA for the manufacturing unit at 40,000 pieces is estimated by management at approximately 12%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Combined capacity of PC and PP units is 50,000 pieces per month (25,000 each), scaling towards 40,000 pieces per month combined by October/November.
- Existing building can expand up to 100,000 pieces per month (4 lines), requiring an additional investment of about Rs 10 crores for 50,000 extra capacity.
- Third-party manufacturing is part of the plan (currently 100% captive), with a long-term goal of balancing captive and third-party volumes.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Stock holding reduced by Rs 4-5 crores, releasing cash into the system.
- Debt levels are expected to peak out by FY27 end, with no intent to increase debt further.
- Depreciation for FY27 is expected to be marginally higher than FY26 due to full-year manufacturing operations (run rate around Rs 4 crores per quarter / ~Rs 16 crores full year).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Pricing pressures and intense competition from new-age, well-funded D2C players in travel gear.
- Geopolitical tensions driving up raw material costs and curbing travel.
- Strategic failure with Benetton's initial offline-first approach leading to pricing mismatches and inventory liquidation.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Ankit Kanodia (Zen Nivesh) asked about competing against well-funded D2C players discounting heavily in luggage and whether non-luggage share would increase.** Management stated luggage is still growing (low single digits), and while competitors use unsustainable pricing, Brand Concepts relies on fair market pricing backed by in-house manufacturing and strong demand for premium price points (e.g., Tommy Hilfiger sets at Rs 27k-32k).
2. **Neysar Parikh asked about the status of the Tommy Hilfiger license renewal and duration.** Management replied that while waiting on international paperwork, the 10-year business plan, royalties, and figures are already closed and agreed upon with the India team.
3. **Neysar Parikh questioned debt, operating cash flow negativity, and sustainability.** Management responded that Q1 losses/negative cash flows are short-term due to consolidation, working capital is improving with inventory down by Rs 4-5 crores, and they are sufficiently funded without needing fresh equity.
4. **Resha Mehta asked about the timeline and completion of the channel consolidation.** Management stated the drive started in Q4 of last year, is 75%-80% complete, and store closures and e-commerce realignment will conclude by September, with company-level growth resuming from October.
5. **Ranadeep Pal asked about the revenue impact of closing 19 stores and the target of a Rs 1,000 crore top line.** Management clarified that EBOs represent only ~10% of overall business, closures are offset by high-throughput store openings (e.g., Off-White in Delhi/Bangalore), and they remain fully on track for the Rs 1,000 crore target.

### One-line takeaway
Brand Concepts is navigating through a temporary Q1 earnings dip and store/e-commerce consolidation phase by cutting unprofitable tails and ramping up in-house manufacturing, expecting a return to growth by October FY27.
