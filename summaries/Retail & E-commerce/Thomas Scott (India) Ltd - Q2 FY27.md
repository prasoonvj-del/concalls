# Thomas Scott (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-16 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong revenue and profit growth, expansion of international brand licensing, and positive festive season responses.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained high double-digit growth targets matching the past few years.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from cautious consumer sentiment in Q1 to improved demand trends and an encouraging start to the festive season (Dussehra/Diwali) in Q2/Q3.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: New topics included entering into licensing and global designated supplier agreements with Forever 21 and Dockers, and initiating international exports. Fire incident and insurance claims were not mentioned.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Executed brand license agreements for Forever 21 and Dockers; expanded warehousing infrastructure (Bhiwandi and Gurgaon).

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 79 crore (up 39% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 11 crore (up 24% YoY)
- EBITDA margin: 13.31%
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 6 crore (up 34% YoY)
- PAT margin: 7.98%
- Thomas Scott brand revenue: Rs 28 crore (up 28% YoY)
- Licensed and other brands revenue: Rs 49 crore (up 52% YoY)
- Contract manufacturing revenue: Rs 2 crore (down 29% YoY)
- H1 FY27 Revenue from operations: Rs 145 crore (up 31% YoY)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs 19 crore (up 31% YoY)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA margin: 13.13%
- H1 FY27 PAT: Rs 12 crore (up 43% YoY)
- H1 FY27 PAT margin: 8.09%
- Men's apparel contribution (H1 FY27): 76%
- Women's apparel contribution (H1 FY27): 17%
- Women's accessories contribution (H1 FY27): 5%
- Fulfillment cost per unit: Declined to Rs 18 per unit in H1 FY27 (from Rs 24 per unit in FY24)
- Trade receivables: 94 days as of September (down from 124 days in March)
- Payables: Stable at about 60 days
- Capital expenditure (H1 net): Rs 6.4 crore

### Highlights
- Entered into licensing and global designated supplier agreements for Forever 21 (India, Nepal, Bangladesh) and Dockers (India) via Authentic Brands Group.
- Initiated international exports of international brand portfolio items, setting up a platform for export turnover in coming quarters.
- Women's wear segment continued strong growth, supporting category diversification.
- Opened a large new warehouse in Gurgaon (partially operational) and invested in Bhiwandi warehouse and Solapur manufacturing extensions.
- Utilized proprietary platforms `thread,ai` and `catalog,ai` for demand forecasting and catalog management.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management targets continuing high double-digit growth rates similar to past years.
- Expects to report separate segment reporting for exports and direct licence brands going forward.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Witnessed an improvement in demand trends during Q2, entering the festive season with a well-planned inventory position.
- Dussehra and Diwali sales responses were very encouraging, providing a positive start to the festive period.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margin was 13.31% in Q2 FY27; impacted sequentially due to the shift of Dussehra sales entirely into October (Q3) while inventory was held at the end of September.
- Fulfillment costs reduced by 25% from FY24 levels to Rs 18 per unit.
- Global designated supplier model expects higher margin profile than typical pure-play exports since variable and marketing costs are largely absorbed by global partners.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Net capex of INR6.4 crores spent in H1 FY27 allocated toward warehousing facilities (Gurgaon and Bhiwandi) and Solapur facility extensions.
- Business is not capex-heavy; future infrastructure funded via term loans matching gestation periods.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Trade receivables improved from 124 days in March to 94 days in September.
- Inventory days increased due to additional finished goods and trade goods (INR20-30 crores) built for H2 festive and autumn-winter season sales.
- Payables remained stable at about 60 days.
- Sufficiently funded with current sources and working capital improvements.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Seasonal shifts in festival dates impact quarterly revenue and margin comparability.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Ankush Agrawal (Surge Capital) on export business structure and margins:** Management explained that global partners purchase from existing inventory on a just-in-time basis without high MOQs, yielding better or at-par margins without local variable costs.
2. **Raj Gupta (Individual Investor) on capex allocation:** Management stated INR6.4 crores was spent in H1 on the Gurgaon/Bhiwandi warehouses and Solapur extensions, funded via internal sources and working capital.
3. **Anil Joshi (Individual Investor) on competitive edge:** Management stated they win on data-driven design and trend identification (e.g., cable-knit polo sweaters) based on marketplace search gaps.
4. **Yash Shah (Individual Investor) on channel split and global supply chain:** Management stated Thomas Scott sales are distributed evenly across AJIO, Amazon, Myntra, and their own website, while global supply operates on the same domestic inventory pool with no incremental working capital.
5. **Kamal Jeswani (Individual Investor) on related-party transactions:** Management clarified that Bang Overseas/Vedanta Creations supply fabric/garments at arm's length, and related purchase percentages will decline to 30%-40% while sales to them will drop to zero by FY28.

### One-line takeaway
Thomas Scott delivered a robust H1 FY27 with 31% revenue growth, expanded its international brand portfolio with Forever 21 and Dockers, and positioned itself for scalable D2C and global supply growth.
