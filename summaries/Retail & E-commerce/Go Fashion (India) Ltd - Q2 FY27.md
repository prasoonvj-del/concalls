# Go Fashion (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue and margins were impacted by festive timing and raw material inflation, but underlying progress on strategic priorities remains on track and SSSG was flat.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs. 218 crores (de-grew 3% YoY) for Q2 FY27; Rs. 440 crores (de-grew 1.5% YoY) for H1 FY27.
- **Gross Profit & Margin:** Gross profit of Rs. 132 crores with a gross margin of 60.8% in Q2 FY27; Gross profit of Rs. 272 crores with a gross margin of 61.8% in H1 FY27.
- **EBITDA & Margin (before exceptional expenses):** Rs. 56 crores with a margin of 25.8% in Q2 FY27; Rs. 123.4 crores with a margin of 28.0% in H1 FY27.
- **PAT:** Rs. 9 crores in Q2 FY27.
- **Cash Flow from Operations (pre-Ind AS 116):** Rs. 52.6 crores for H1 FY27.
- **Cash and Cash Equivalents:** Rs. 211.4 crores as of 30th September 2026.
- **ROCE and ROE (excluding Ind AS):** 8.9% and 6.7% respectively for H1 FY27.
- **Exceptional Expense:** Rs. 3.4 crores in Q2 FY27; Rs. 9.9 crores (or Rs. 9.87 crores) total for H1 FY27 (store write-offs on closures).
- **Working Capital & Inventory Days:** Working capital days at 134 days; inventory days improved to 94 days (from 100 days in Q1).
- **Store Count & Retail Space:** 737 EBOs across 196 cities as of 30th September 2026; total retail space approx 4.4 lakh sq. ft. (addition of 14,220 sq. ft. in H1). Average EBO size ~608 sq. ft.
- **Dailywear Stores:** 21 operational stores.
- **LFS Contribution:** ~23% of sales in H1 FY27.
- **Full Price Sales Ratio:** 93% for H1 FY27.
- **Average Selling Price (ASP):** Rs. 827 for EBOs.

### Highlights
- Same-store sales growth (SSSG) for EBOs was broadly flat in Q2 (following 0.6% in Q1), impacted by a late festive calendar shifting pre-festive demand to October.
- Transitioned to larger format stores: opened 9 new stores and closed 23 smaller format stores in Q2 (24 openings and 89 closures in H1). Larger format stores (>750 sq. ft.) outperformed smaller ones with 2%-3%+ SSSG.
- Value-added non-leggings portfolio contributed 70% of revenue.
- Launched the first brand campaign featuring new brand ambassador Shraddha Kapoor in July.
- Scaled dailywear concept to 21 stores (targeting 25-30 stores by FY27 end) with sales of ~Rs. 1,000 per sq. ft. per month.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains committed to delivering positive SSSG for FY27 as a whole, driven by the festive quarter.
- Aim to double square footage over the next 5 years.
- Target 25-30 dailywear stores by the end of FY27.
- Management expects store closures to be considerably lower in H2 (estimated 15-20 stores in Q3).
- Gross margin impact from raw material inflation is estimated between 100 bps to 200 bps in Q3.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Festive season started later; part of pre-festive demand moved from September to October.
- LFS dispatches shifted from September to October due to festive timing; supply chain with LFS partners is normalized.
- Bottomwear category offers long-term growth due to low penetration of organized players.
- Management believes growth headwinds are primarily due to store format size (smaller stores carry an inherent perception of limited range and smaller frontage) rather than merchandise issues or competition.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Fabric prices increased due to geopolitical situations, leading to higher-cost inventory flowing in during Q2 and causing a 200 bps impact on gross margins. Blended RM cost inflation is estimated at 8%-10%.
- Management is implementing calibrated price hikes (some done, some post-festive) to restore gross margins over the next 2 quarters.
- Marketing and advertising expenses increased in H1 (Rs. 12.63 crores vs. Rs. 7.85 crores in H1 last year) due to the brand ambassador campaign.
- Employee costs remained flat YoY in Q2 despite annual increments due to staff rationalization from store closures. Rent costs also stayed flat at ~Rs. 37 crores.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Store expansion remains calibrated and selective across Tier-1, Tier-2, and Tier-3 cities.
- Transitioning from smaller stores (<300 sq. ft.) to larger formats (700-900 sq. ft.).
- Continued preference for a Company-Owned Company-Operated (COCO) model over franchising (FOCO) to maintain full inventory control.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory days improved to 94 days (down from 100 days in Q1), falling into the 90-100 day guidance range.
- Online and quick commerce receivables saw a spike, alongside export receivables for Dubai stores.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Higher raw material/fabric costs impacting gross margins (estimated 100-200 bps impact in Q3).
- SSSG for smaller stores (<300 sq. ft.) continues to be negative.
- Exceptional expenses due to capital expenditure write-offs on store closures (Rs. 9.9 crores in H1).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Aabhash Poddar (Aionios Alpha Investment Management):** Asked about other expenses and price hikes. Management clarified that marketing/advertising and store write-offs drove expenses up, while employee and rent costs remained flat. Confirmed price hikes are underway to offset RM inflation over 2 quarters.
2. **Ankit Kanodia (Private Investor):** Inquired about store closure trajectory and H2 outlook. Management stated store closures will be considerably lower in H2 (15-20 in Q3) and expects H2/Q3 numbers to be much better.
3. **Avinash Karumanchi (Equirus Securities):** Asked about inventory lag on RM costs and ASP drivers. Management estimated a 100-200 bps GM impact in Q3 and clarified ASP increase is driven by product mix (new bottomwear and topwear in dailywear stores) rather than price hikes yet.
4. **Sameer Gupta (India Infoline):** Asked about store size proportion and RM inflation quantum. Management stated small stores (<300 sq. ft.) are roughly 120-150, 700+ sq. ft. stores are around 90-100, and pegged input cost inflation at 8%-10%.
5. **Madhur Rathi (Counter Cyclical Investments):** Questioned the rise in receivables and rationale for entering menswear. Management explained receivables rose due to quick commerce, Amazon SOR, and Dubai exports. On menswear, management clarified it is part of a 1,500-2,000 sq. ft. dailywear pilot where menswear occupies only 10%-12% of space to serve accompanying customers.

### One-line takeaway
Go Fashion reported a muted Q2 with a 3% revenue de-growth due to festive timing shifts and raw material pressures, but management remains confident that store consolidation, larger formats, and pricing actions will drive recovery in H2.
