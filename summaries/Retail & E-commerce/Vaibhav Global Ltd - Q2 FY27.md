# Vaibhav Global Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Near-term disruptions from platform migration and U.K. warehouse consolidation impacted profitability and forced a guidance cut.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 938.4 crore, up 7% YoY (H1: Rs 1,855.4 crore, up 9.7% YoY)
- **Retail Revenue:** Rs 867.5 crore, up 5.3% YoY
- **B2B Revenue:** Rs 70.9 crore, up 32% YoY (First half U.S. EBITDA up 24.9% to Rs 86.6 crore)
- **Europe (ex-U.K.) Revenue:** Rs 110 crore, up 18.3% YoY (8% constant currency)
- **Gross Margin:** 65.5%, up 197 bps YoY (H1: 65.5% vs 63.6%)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 76.2 crore, down 13.5% YoY; Margin: 8.1% vs 10% in Q2 FY26 (H1 EBITDA: Rs 178.3 crore, up 9.6% YoY, Margin: 9.6%)
- **PAT:** H1 PAT up 15.2% YoY to Rs 98.1 crore
- **Net Cash:** Rs 186 crore as of September 30, 2026 (vs Rs 279 crore in March 2026)
- **ROCE / ROE:** ROCE at 22%, ROE at 18%
- **Dividend:** Second interim dividend of Rs 1.5 per equity share declared

### Highlights
- Completed migration of all retail websites to Shopify Enterprise, and consolidated 4 U.K. warehouses and 2 studios into a single site.
- Shopify migration caused traffic/conversion disruptions and an estimated revenue impact of USD 2.8 million (~2.8 percentage points of growth).
- Retail volumes grew ~2%, driven by lower price point items.
- In-house brands contributed 57% of B2C revenue; lab-grown diamonds (LGD) rose to 12% of retail revenue (from ~10% YoY).
- ICRA combined ESG rating improved to 75 from 72; over 119 million meals distributed via 'Your purchase feeds' program (serving ~56,000 meals/school day).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management revised FY27 revenue growth guidance to 7% to 9% (from earlier 9% to 11%), citing Q2 migration impact, U.K. consolidation, weaker U.K. consumer, and upcoming U.S. midterm elections.
- Management expects FY27 EBITDA margin to be broadly in line with FY26 (lowering previous expectation of 50 to 100 bps expansion).
- Management remains confident in returning to 9% to 11% revenue growth in FY28.
- Target for digital share remains at 50% of B2C revenue by Q4 FY27 (currently at 44%).
- Medium-term target: Lifestyle products to reach 50% share (currently ~40% of B2C revenue); long-term revenue target of Rs 5,000 to Rs 5,500 crores by FY30.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Consumers are actively seeking value, driving growth in lower price points (average selling prices down 8.4% in the U.S. while units rose 4.8%).
- U.K. consumer sentiment is weak due to cost-of-living pressures, inflation rising to 3.5% in September, and uncertainty ahead of the autumn budget.
- U.S. consumer sentiment impacted slightly by potential contentiousness around midterm elections.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margin expanded by ~197 bps YoY to 65.5%, aided by vertical integration, in-house brands, and LGD growth.
- EBITDA margin contracted 193 bps YoY to 8.1% due to operating deleverage in the U.S. and U.K. from transitions, higher shipping costs from volume growth, and lower forex gains.
- Sustainable effective tax rate expected in the 18% to 20% range for the next 2-3 years due to German tax losses and FTC credits.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex for the quarter included INR 36 crores invested in the U.K. warehouse migration.
- Germany business narrowing losses (H1 loss narrowed to Rs 0.7 crore from Rs 6.6 crore) and remains on track to contribute positively to group profitability in FY27. India entry is planned for future years after Germany achieves robust profitability.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash reduced to INR 186 crores as of September 30, 2026, due to seasonal inventory buildup and U.K. warehouse capex.
- Inventory increased by roughly INR 100 crores YoY to prepare for the festive season and uncertain environment; expected to moderate in Q3/Q4 back to normal levels of 200–220 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Disruptions and traffic drops during the Shopify platform migration and U.K. warehouse consolidation.
- Macroeconomic headwinds in the U.K. (inflation, fuel costs, weak retail sales) and potential U.S. midterm election distractions.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kaushik Kanjwal (Retail Investor) questioned capital allocation, asking why the company pays high dividends instead of doing a buyback while the stock price is down.**
   - *Management Answer:* The Board decided to maintain the dividend-paying tradition with a modest dividend while building cash for future strategic moves/acquisitions, after which additional cash will be deployed for a buyback. *(Did not dodge)*
2. **Chidananda Mohanty (Sameeksha Capital) asked for the exact revenue lost due to Shopify migration.**
   - *Management Answer:* Quantified as USD 2.8 million or approximately 2.8 percentage points of growth. *(Did not dodge)*
3. **Himanshu Upadhyay (Steadfort) asked about the strategy for precious metal price drops and maintaining gross margins.**
   - *Management Answer:* Stated that the model's agility allows them to maintain 64%–65% gross margins regardless of metal price fluctuations by responding in real time to the market. *(Did not dodge)*
4. **Dilip Kumar Sahu (Retail Investor) asked why the U.K. declined while Germany grew if platform migration affected all.**
   - *Management Answer:* Explained that the U.K. faced three compounding headwinds: Shopify migration, warehouse/studio consolidation, and a worse macroeconomic/cost-of-living crisis than the U.S. or Germany. *(Did not dodge)*
5. **Lakshminarayanan (Tunga Investment) questioned the utility of giving guidance given frequent changes and asked what is in management's control.**
   - *Management Answer:* Defended giving guidance as a duty to share transparent visibility with shareholders, noting that supply chain, product trends, and customer service are in-control, whereas macroeconomic environments and metal prices are outside control. *(Did not dodge)*

### One-line takeaway
Vaibhav Global absorbed transitional costs from its Shopify migration and U.K. warehouse consolidation in Q2 FY27, leading to a guidance cut to 7%–9% revenue growth, but management expects structural scalability to drive recovery in H2 and FY28.
