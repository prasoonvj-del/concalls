# Metro Brands Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-23 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Revenue and profit grew, but margins faced pressure from higher rentals, minimum wage revisions, talent/marketing investments, and supply/BIS challenges in newer formats.

### Key numbers
- Standalone net sales: Rs 712 crore (up 12% YoY)
- Standalone PAT: Rs 73 crore (up 14% YoY)
- Standalone EBITDA growth: 15% YoY
- Consolidated revenue: Rs 730 crore (up 12.2% YoY)
- Consolidated EBITDA: Rs 198 crore (up 15.9% YoY)
- Consolidated EBITDA margin: 27.1% (vs 26.2% YoY)
- Consolidated PAT: Rs 77 crore (up ~12% YoY)
- Consolidated PAT margin: 10.6% (unchanged YoY)
- E-commerce & omnichannel sales growth: 12% YoY (contributing 14.2% of revenue)
- First half H1 gross margin: 58.4% (vs 57.3% YoY)
- Net store additions in Q2: 12 stores (21 opened, 9 closed)
- Total store network: 1,053 stores as of September end
- Marketing spend in H1: ~4% (vs 3.25% - 3.4% YoY)
- Capex guidance for FY27: Rs 80 crore to Rs 100 crore (including remodels)

### Highlights
- Festive demand shifted from September (Q2) into October (Q3) due to calendar changes (Durga Puja/Shraddh shift), impacting Q2 growth by an estimated 250 to 350 bps.
- Own brands contributed approximately 70% of store product sales at Metro, Mochi, and Walkway in H1.
- Clarks women's range is now in over 400 MBOs and men's in over 100 MBOs; exclusive EBO stores expected in H2 FY27.
- FILA has initiated local footwear manufacturing in India to counter BIS-related import issues; repositioning is underway (12 to 18 months), with store expansion picking up from FY28.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 to be better than H1 in terms of store additions and festive performance.
- Medium to long-term PAT margin target: 13% to 15%.
- Inventory days expected to normalize to ~80 days by mid-to-end of next financial year.
- FILA store expansion expected to pick up from FY28 onwards.
- Marketing spend expected to remain around 4% to support incubating brands.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Real estate rentals are under pressure due to high demand and limited supply in prime retail locations/malls.
- Metro cities are holding their own well, while Tier-2/3 expansion continues selectively.
- Front-end store teams have 2 ways to grow income: SSG growth and career progression into managerial roles.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material inflation in the basket is around 10% to 15%; average MRP price hikes taken are 2% to 5%.
- Gross margin improvement driven by lower EOSS/online discounts, slight markup increases, and lower e-commerce contribution mix.
- Cost pressures included investments in talent/brand building, higher frontline staff costs due to minimum wage revisions, occupancy costs for new formats, and lower treasury income.
- Front-end staff cost expected to rise by another 0.5% to 1% due to revised remuneration structures designed above minimum wage.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Opened 34 stores and closed 13 in H1 (net addition of 21 stores); closing rate expected around 2% to 3%.
- Store expansion is strictly tied to profitability; underperforming stores are closed within 2 years.
- BIS implementation challenges for external brands have constrained supply readiness for Foot Locker and MetroActiv, leading to calibrated store openings.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory is higher due to front-loading during the U.S.-Iran war, BIS risk mitigation for FitFlop, and inventory fill for new formats (Clarks).
- Ideal months of inventory targeted around 7 months; working capital days targeted back to ~80 days by mid-to-end of next financial year.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- BIS renewal uncertainties and erratic supply chains for imported brands (Foot Locker, FitFlop, FILA).
- Real estate supply constraints pushing up rentals and leading to one-and-done lease negotiations.
- Cannibalization from opening new formats (e.g., Mochi near Metro) impacting per square foot throughput metrics.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Videesha Sheth (AMBIT Capital) asked about the store closure reasons and formats.** Management clarified closures are not format-specific, but driven by a strict 2-year profitability review and older malls losing footfall to new retail precincts.
- **Sameer Gupta (IIFL Capital) asked about elevated inventory levels.** Management explained it resulted from strategic front-loading due to geopolitical risks, BIS license renewal safeguards, and initial fill for new brands like Clarks, expecting normalization by March/mid-next fiscal.
- **Devanshu Bansal (Emkay Global) questioned FILA's timeline and BIS challenges.** Management noted that getting factory-specific licenses requires order velocity, and erratic renewal timelines forced them to pivot to local manufacturing.
- **Saurabh Kundan (Goldman Sachs) asked about underlying sales growth removing the festive shift.** Management estimated the festive shift impact at 250-350 bps, bringing true underlying growth close to their usual 15% rate.
- **Umang Mehta (Kotak Securities) asked about the brand drag on FILA.** Management admitted that import shutdowns forced them into product creation, which is outside their legacy footwear DNA, leading to steep learning curves.

### One-line takeaway
Metro Brands delivered a steady 12% revenue growth in Q2 despite festive shifts, while absorbing margin pressures through disciplined cost management and preparing newer formats like Clarks and FILA for future scaling.
