# Eternal Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-27 · Retail & E-commerce*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Positive - Management expressed strong confidence in reaccelerating quick commerce store expansion, solid paybacks in Tier 2/3 cities, and maintaining food delivery growth through deliberate reinvestment.

---

### Key numbers
* *Note: Key quarterly financial results (Revenue, EBITDA, PAT) were not reported during the call.*
* Blinkit store count grew ~50% YoY.
* Blinkit NOV per day and orders per day per dark store grew 16–17% YoY.
* Blinkit current NOV per store per day: ~Rs 9 lakh (target steady state: ~Rs 11 lakh).
* Blinkit SKU mix: 90–95% 1P, rest 3P.
* More than 70% of NOV in mature cities (operating 2–3+ years) is EBITDA positive.
* Capex guidance per new store: ~Rs 2.5 crore (blended basis).

---

### Guidance & outlook
* **Quick commerce profitability:** Management maintained its guidance of reaching $1 billion Adjusted EBITDA by FY29.
* **Quick commerce growth:** Management expects Blinkit NOV growth to pick up starting the December quarter (OND) from the 79% base, without hurting short-term margins.
* **Food delivery margins:** Expected to remain flattish over the next 1–2 years as profits are reinvested into customer acquisition and order frequency.
* **Store expansion:** Dark store count targeted to nearly double over the next 6–7 quarters, with an accelerated run rate in FY28 vs. FY27.

---

### Demand & industry commentary
* **Food delivery:** Competitive intensity increased during the quarter, partially driven by Zomato’s proactive investments in growth; surprised by new players entering a mature, penetrated category.
* **Quick commerce competition:** More predictable now after a hyper-competitive 12-month period involving 5–7 players.
* **Tier 2/3 cities:** Showing robust unit economics, very good paybacks, and less competition, allowing first-mover advantages. Assortment in these markets is a subset of metros, but hard-to-find items see rapid uptake.
* **Agentic commerce:** Negligible/near-zero consumer adoption in India today; company is monitoring US trends and building test protocols.

---

### Costs, margins & raw materials
* **Operating leverage:** Expected to be the primary driver of overall Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in quick commerce.
* **Indirect costs:** Blinkit indirect costs grew 15–20% QoQ due to variable scale costs (e.g., technology server costs, some dollar-denominated) sitting below contribution margin.
* **Working capital:** Stated to be stable with no incremental working capital pressure from the 90–95% 1P inventory model.
* **Dark store economics:** Flat contribution margins despite higher NOV per store were driven by new store investment drags, store size increases, and product mix variations.

---

### Capex, capacity & expansion
* Store addition capex stands at a blended ~Rs 2.5 crore per store, factoring in high Tier 2/3 expansion.
* Gourmet quick commerce stores expanded to Bengaluru and Mumbai.
* **dark.ai:** Internal consumer/power-user privacy-focused AI tool built on open-source LLMs; currently accounted for in the 'Others' segment with immaterial capex unless hardware/GPU infrastructure needs ramp up upon blockbuster demand.

---

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
* Management affirmed the company possesses a strong balance sheet capable of funding new technology and AI initiatives (e.g., dark.ai, Nugget).

---

### Risks & concerns flagged
* **Intensifying competition:** Increased competitive intensity in food delivery; persistence of multiple players in quick commerce.
* **FX exposure:** Server and technology costs denominated in US dollars create margin headwinds under rupee depreciation.
* **Longer inventory cycles:** Increasing presence of non-grocery categories (e.g., air conditioners) necessitates longer inventory holding periods.

---

### Notable analyst Q&A
* **Food Delivery Margin Peak:**  
  *Q (Garima Mishra - Kotak):* Have food delivery margins peaked, with all surplus being reinvested?  
  *A (Akshant Goyal):* Confirmed deliberate choice to reinvest in user acquisition and frequency; margins are targeted to remain broadly flattish over the next 1–2 years to maximize absolute Adjusted EBITDA.
* **Quick Commerce FY29 Guidance vs. Competition:**  
  *Q (Manish Adukia):* Does a better read on competition increase comfort around the $1 billion Adjusted EBITDA guidance for FY29?  
  *A (Akshant Goyal):* Declined to upgrade expectations; simply maintained the target, noting it is only two quarters into a three-year path.
* **Store Economics vs. Contribution Flatness:**  
  *Q (Swapnil Potdukhe):* Why did contribution margin stay flat despite higher NOV per store?  
  *A (Akshant Goyal & Kunal Swarup):* Blended contribution is diluted by upfront investments in new stores, larger store sizes increasing base dark store costs, and gross profit variations due to category mix.
* **dark.ai Strategic Rationale:**  
  *Q (Abhisek Banerjee):* Is dark.ai building a base foundational model or an application layer?  
  *A (Akshant Goyal):* It is an application layer fine-tuning open-source models; capex is primarily for compute/hosting. Management treats it as a high-potential 0-to-1 bet that can be discarded if traction fails.
* **Food Delivery Growth Rate:**  
  *Q (Abhisek Banerjee):* Can 22–23% YoY food delivery growth be assumed for 2H?  
  *A (Akshant Goyal):* Dodged/refused to provide specific growth guidance.

---

### One-line takeaway
Eternal is aggressively stepping on the gas in Tier 2/3 quick commerce expansion and maintaining food delivery margins flattish to maximize absolute profit scale.
