# Ola Electric Mobility Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-17 · Automobiles*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management emphasizes that demand is no longer a constraint, the dealership transition is successful, and the balance sheet has been deleveraged.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained (Auto gross margins expected in 30-32% range).
- **Tone and demand view:** Shifted from "recovery" to "scaling/no longer constraint."
- **New topics or risks:** Dealership model implementation, charger unavailability issues impacting deliveries, and ACC incentive extension to 2031.
- **Last quarter's promises:** Gigafactory commissioned (delivered).

### Key numbers
- **Deliveries:** 35,000–36,000 units (would have been 40,000–41,000 without charger unavailability).
- **Auto Gross Margin:** 30% (vs 30.5% in Q1).
- **Consolidated Borrowings:** Reduced from Rs 3,132 crore (Dec) to Rs 1,800 crore (Sept 2026).
- **Cell Project Debt:** Rs 1,482 crore.
- **Dealer Stores:** 300+ (Sept); target 700+ by March 2027.

### Highlights
- Transitioned from company-owned stores to a dealership model in September.
- Gigafactory commissioned in September with 3.5 GWh total capacity.
- ACC incentive extended until 2031.
- Channel inventory is minimal (days).
- *Note:* The company continues to face delivery bottlenecks, with 5,000 paid orders delayed in October due to charger unavailability.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Auto gross margins to remain in the 30-32% range and improve meaningfully from Q4 onwards.
- FY28 target: 400,000–500,000 vehicles.
- Expects to reach 700+ stores by March 2027.
- Management expects cell production to ramp up, with capacity utilization growing step-by-step.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand is no longer a constraint; the company sells every vehicle produced.
- Strong macro pull-in tailwinds for EVs.
- Dealer feedback and sell-through experience are strong.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margins held stable at 30% despite commodity cost pressures.
- Management attributes the cost advantage vs competitors to vertical integration and in-house engineering of components.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Gigafactory is commissioned; capacity utilization is currently ~30% and expected to grow.
- Management aims for 90% yield improvement within months, backed by an engineering roadmap.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Significant deleveraging achieved; consolidated borrowings reduced by almost Rs 1,400 crore.
- Transition to dealership model is improving receivables.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Charger unavailability caused delivery delays in October.
- Commodity price volatility remains a factor.
- Yield improvement roadmap is critical for future margin expansion.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Arvind (Citi) on volumes/revenue:** Management clarified that 4,000–5,000 units were booked but not delivered due to charger unavailability, which will reflect in Q3.
- **Akshay Satija (Alpha Invesco) on material cost gap:** Management stated their material costs are 15–20% lower than competitors due to vertical integration and in-house manufacturing.
- **Purvesh (Acacia Capital) on S1Z delivery:** Management expects S1Z deliveries in Q4; testing is underway.
- **Chintan (Sagun Capital) on FY28:** Management confirmed the 400k–500k vehicle target for FY28 based on current kWh capacity.

### One-line takeaway
Ola Electric has successfully transitioned to a dealership model and commissioned its Gigafactory, with management signaling that demand is no longer a constraint despite short-term delivery delays due to charger unavailability.
