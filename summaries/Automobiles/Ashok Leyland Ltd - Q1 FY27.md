# Ashok Leyland Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-21 · Automobiles*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Achieved all-time high CV volume, revenue, profit, and cash surplus despite geopolitical challenges and commodity headwinds.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 9,634 crore (up 10% YoY)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 970 crore (flat YoY)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 10.1% (down 100 bps YoY)
- **PBT:** Rs 830 crore (up 4% YoY)
- **PAT:** Rs 609 crore (up 3% YoY)
- **Domestic MHCV Truck Volume:** 22,998 units (up 15% YoY)
- **Domestic MHCV Market Share:** 29%
- **Domestic LCV Volume:** 18,874 units (up 21% YoY)
- **LCV VAHAN Market Share:** 13.2% (up 30 bps YoY)
- **Exports Volume:** 2,461 units (down 18% YoY)
- **Total CV Volume (including defense):** 48,673 units
- **Material Cost:** 71.5% of revenue (up 90 bps YoY)
- **Capex:** Rs 153 crore
- **Investments in Subsidiaries:** Rs 10 crore
- **Net Cash:** Rs 2,252 crore (increase of Rs 1,431 crore YoY)
- **Hinduja Leyland Finance AUM:** Rs 60,310 crore (up 20% YoY); PPOP at Rs 587 crore (up 56% YoY); PAT at Rs 123 crore (up 37% YoY)
- **Hinduja Housing Finance AUM:** Rs 16,157 crore (up 13% YoY); Total Gross Income at Rs 470 crore; PPOP at Rs 136 crore; PAT at Rs 69 crore

### Highlights
- Achieved highest-ever Q1 CV volume, revenue, profit, and cash surplus.
- Introduced industry-first air suspension technology in multi-axle trucks, providing best payload and TCO.
- Switch Mobility bagged an order for 650 electric buses (healthy order book of 2,100 e-buses); delivered 225 e-buses and nearly 300 e-LCVs.
- OHM Mobility improved operational fleet to over 1,900 e-buses (over 500 added in Q1).
- Added 33 touch points during the quarter, bringing total network touch points to 2,137 (1,177 MHCV, 960 LCV).
- Achieved 77% Renewable Energy status (Tamil Nadu plants at 91%).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects CV industry momentum to continue, estimating high single-digit growth for MHCV in H2, and slightly better outlook for LCV.
- Commodity cost pressure is expected to peak in Q2, with softening beginning in Q3 and turnaround in Q4.
- Capex outlay will continue to increase over the next 2 to 3 years to fund new technologies, products, and white spaces (specific numbers to be provided once finalized).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic MHCV industry volume grew 13% YoY, while domestic LCV VAHAN industry grew 17% in Q1.
- Strong bounce back observed in June, July, and August following stabilization of initial fuel supply and pricing issues.
- TCO economics of BS6 trucks versus older fleets driving strong replacement demand.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Material costs rose to 71.5% of revenue (up 90 bps YoY) due to supply chain disruptions and commodity pressures.
- Margins were protected through opening inventory utilization (using low-cost opening stock), price increases, cost savings ("Achieve 2K" project), and mix improvements.
- Price hikes taken: MHCV prices up >1% in July (cumulative ~2.25% from fiscal beginning); LCV prices up >2% in July (cumulative >3.5% from fiscal beginning).
- Employee costs expected to see a marginal 4%-5% increase in Q2 due to the annual July increment cycle.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex for Q1 stood at Rs 153 crore, focused on new products, future tech, alternate powertrains, and EVs.
- UAE plant (RAK) operations resumed after temporary shutdown; expected to reach peak production of 800 units/month.
- Expediting new plant setup in Saudi Arabia to capture strong GCC demand.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash position strengthened to Rs 2,252 crore.
- Optare debt reduction plan on track: target to repay GBP 25 million this year and GBP 25 million next year.
- Reverse merger of Hinduja Leyland Finance (HLF) with NDL Ventures progressing; approvals received from shareholders and unsecured creditors, moving to NCLT.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated commodity prices posing short-term gross margin pressure.
- Geopolitical uncertainties impacting logistics and GCC volumes at the UAE plant.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Gunjan Prithyani (Bank of America):** Asked about gross margin delivery, inventory impact, and Q2 outlook. Management explained that utilizing opening inventory shielded Q1 from full commodity cost impacts, but Q2 will continue to face commodity headwinds before softening in Q3/Q4. *(Management did not dodge).*
2. **Binay Singh (Morgan Stanley):** Inquired about truck mix and domestic truck volume growth outlook. Management stated that heavy truck/MAV mix will improve with HIPPO, TAURUS, and new air suspension ramp-ups, and industry growth is expected to remain strong. *(Management did not dodge).*
3. **Pramod Kumar (UBS):** Asked about the visibility on commodity softening and quantum of price hikes. Management cited supplier talks and reports pointing to Q2 as the peak, and detailed cumulative price hikes of ~2.25% for MHCV and >3.5% for LCVs. *(Management did not dodge).*
4. **Chandramouli Muthiah (Goldman Sachs):** Inquired about regulatory cost impacts and BS7 timeline. Management stated BS7 for CVs is unlikely in India before 2031 or 2032. *(Management did not dodge).*
5. **Yash Agarwal (Nirmal Bang):** Asked about fixed cost breakeven points through non-MHCV diversification. Management noted MHCV truck domestic sales required for fixed cost breakeven has dropped to 1,000–1,500 units per month. *(Management did not dodge).*

### One-line takeaway
Ashok Leyland delivered record Q1 financial and volume performance driven by strong demand and operational discipline, even as management navigates short-term commodity headwinds through pricing actions and cost savings.
