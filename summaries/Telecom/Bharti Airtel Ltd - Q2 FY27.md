# Bharti Airtel Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Telecom*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong, consistent execution across India and Africa with sequential revenue and margin expansion, supported by debt reduction and financial services momentum.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: maintained for core business capex; no explicit numerical guidance provided for new growth bets.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted towards fiberization priority over FWA (scaling back FWA-led expansion); smartphone upgrades showed entry-level softness offset by the refurbished market.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Added investment in a strategic land parcel (Rs 2,900 crore) for 1 GW data center ambition; Airtel Money Africa listed on London Stock Exchange ($7 billion valuation).
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Airtel Money listing delivered in October 2026 on LSE; live lending offerings launched in Q2 via Airtel Money as targeted.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated revenue: ~Rs 60,900 crore (up 4% QoQ).
- Consolidated EBITDAaL: ~Rs 31,500 crore (up ~5.5% QoQ); margin at 51.7%.
- Consolidated capex: ~Rs 12,500 crore; Operating Free Cash Flow (EBITDAaL minus capex): >Rs 19,000 crore.
- India EBITDAaL (ex-passive infra): >Rs 20,400 crore (up 4% QoQ); margin at 52.7% (+0.4% QoQ).
- India capex: Rs 7,430 crore; Operating Free Cash Flow: ~Rs 13,000 crore.
- Strategic land parcel investment: Rs 2,900 crore (for 1 GW data center capacity).
- Consolidated Net Debt to EBITDAaL: 0.70x; India (ex-passive infra) Net Debt to EBITDAaL: 0.96x.
- Mobile ARPU: Rs 271 (up from Rs 264 in Q1).
- Customer additions: Mobile revenue-earning net adds of 0.7 million; smartphone data net adds of 2.9 million; postpaid net adds of 1.2 million (highest ever, 8.3% of total base). 5G user base surpassed 200 million.
- Homes net adds: 670,000. Digital TV net subscriber loss: >400,000.
- Airtel Business revenue: ~Rs 5,800 crore (+3.8% QoQ, +11.4% YoY).
- Airtel Payments Bank: Monthly transacting users >125 million; annualized revenue run rate >Rs 3,500 crore (+9% YoY); deposits >Rs 4,400 crore (+10% YoY).
- Airtel Finance: Monthly loan disbursement run rate ~Rs 770 crore.
- Bharti Hexacom (Q2 FY27): Revenue >Rs 2,580 crore (+2.9% QoQ); EBITDAaL >Rs 1,250 crore (margin 48.6%); mobile base 29 million (48,000 smartphone additions); ARPU Rs 264; homes/offices net adds 71,000 (+3.7% QoQ revenue); Operating Free Cash Flow ~Rs 920 crore; Net debt ex-leases ~Rs 1,575 crore.

### Highlights
- Airtel Money Africa listed on London Stock Exchange in October at a ~$7 billion valuation.
- Scaled back FWA-led expansion to focus on fiberizing home passes, using FWA only as an interim solution.
- Deployed homegrown agent platform 'Agent Bridge' and added GPU capacity across data centers to accelerate internal/customer AI use cases.
- AI real-time decision engine generated >10 billion next-best actions; AI anti-spam solution identified >100 billion spam calls and 5 billion messages, blocking >1.7 million fraudulent links.
- Dynamic AI-enabled RAM orchestrated >14 million sleep hours daily across network sites to reduce power consumption.
- Indus Towers executing digital operations transformation and energy optimization (aiming to eliminate diesel via solarization and battery deployment).
- Strategic land parcel acquired gives optionality to construct a 400–500 MW data center plant.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects core business capex to stay aligned with existing guidance, while incremental capex will be directed toward growth bets like data centers and cloud.
- Long-term ARPU growth relies on repairing industry pricing architecture to charge for data consumption.
- Aiming to expand Nxtra data center capacity to 1 GW over the next few years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Enterprise demand remains robust with deal wins across Core Connectivity and digital portfolios (cloud, cybersecurity, IoT, CPaaS).
- Competitive intensity remains steady with 3-operator competition in half the country and 2-operator competition in the remainder.
- Softness observed in entry-level smartphone upgrades due to higher device prices, offset by expansion in the refurbished smartphone market. 92–93% of new smartphone shipments are 5G.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- War on Waste initiative continues targeting structural cost efficiencies in power/energy and virtualizing set-top boxes for IPTV.
- Home segment EBIT softened temporarily due to fiber/CPE investments and past low-ARPU acquisition quality corrections.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Radio capex has moderated due to prior build-out, with site additions under 1,000 per quarter (500–600 sites in Q2).
- Data center network includes 56 edge data centers being consolidated from ~100 MSC facilities.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash balance supported by ~$800 million equity raise received in June/July.
- Gross debt dropped QoQ despite large dividend payouts by Indus, Airtel, and Airtel Africa during Q2.
- Current tax provision and payment run rates expected to continue at current levels.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Weather disruptions, particularly severe in Northeast India, adversely impacted network continuity, recharges, and mobile subscriber additions during Q2.
- Flat pricing architecture giving away unlimited data without consumption-based monetization limits structural monetization upside.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Piyush Choudhary (HSBC):** *Sequential ARPU breakdown and 5G uplink capex for AI?*
  Management stated Q2 prepaid ladder intervention (discontinuation of Rs 299 pack) played out as expected alongside upgrade levers; current AI traffic is too small to necessitate major immediate uplink capex.
- **Manish Adukia (Goldman Sachs):** *Reason for scaling back FWA expansion?*
  Management answered fiber delivers the highest stickiness and lifetime value. FWA will only serve as an interim solution on fringes while areas with dense demand are fiberized.
- **Sanjesh Jain (ICICI Securities):** *Data center land capacity, US demand shifts, and GPU-as-a-Service plans?*
  Management stated the land acquisition allows 400–500 MW capacity. GPU bare-metal leasing is commoditized with counterparty risks; Airtel is studying GPU-as-a-Service/AI software stacks while buying GPU/TPU chips for internal use.
- **Sachin Salgaonkar (Bank of America):** *Will Bharti Airtel acquire BT/OneWeb promoter stakes?*
  Management clarified unequivocally there is no intention for Bharti Airtel to buy out those stakes.
- **Kunal Vora (BNP Paribas):** *Reason for higher subscriber churn?*
  Management attributed it to normal Q2 seasonality combined with temporary churn following pricing interventions.

### One-line takeaway
Bharti Airtel delivered sustained profitable growth driven by postpaid premiumization, disciplined capital allocation toward fiber and 1 GW data center ambitions, and listing of its Africa mobile money arm.
