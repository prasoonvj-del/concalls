# Bharti Airtel Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-10 · Telecom*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered strong consolidated financial and operational performance across the group, backed by growth in revenue, EBITDA, and operational free cash flow.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated revenue: Rs 58,500 crore (5.7% QoQ growth)
- Africa constant currency revenue growth: 5.7% YoY
- India revenue growth (excluding passive infra): 3.6% QoQ
- Consolidated EBITDAaL: Rs 29,800 crore (4.2% QoQ growth, 51% margin)
- Operating free cash flow (EBITDAaL minus capex): over Rs 16,450 crore
- Capex for the quarter: Rs 13,390 crore
- Consolidated net debt to EBITDAaL: 0.7% (India without passive infra is lower than 1%)
- Airtel Africa revenue: over $400 million (growing at 26% YoY in constant currency)
- Airtel Payments Bank monthly transacting user base: around 120 million; annualized revenue run rate: over Rs 3,400 crore; deposits: just under Rs 4,400 crore (17% YoY growth)
- Airtel Finance total loan disbursements: over Rs 750 crore per month
- Airtel India mobile customer net additions: 3.3 million revenue earning customers; smartphone data customers added: 5 million; postpaid customers added: 1 million
- Postpaid share of total customer base: 8% plus
- Mobile ARPU (India): Rs 264 (benefited from an extra day)
- Home broadband net additions: 473,000
- Digital TV net additions: 6,000
- Airtel Business revenue: Rs 5,670 crore (3.2% QoQ growth, nearly 12% YoY growth)
- Cloud customer count: 33 (added 11 new customers in the quarter)
- Bharti Hexacom revenue: Rs 2,510 crore (4% QoQ growth)
- Bharti Hexacom EBITDAaL: Rs 1,210 crore (48.2% margin)
- Bharti Hexacom mobile customers: 29 million (net adds of 210,000; smartphone additions of 344,000)
- Bharti Hexacom ARPU: Rs 259
- Bharti Hexacom homes, office, and other businesses net adds: 75,000 (revenue growth about 8% QoQ)
- Bharti Hexacom operating free cash flow: over Rs 830 crore
- Bharti Hexacom net debt excluding leases: about Rs 960 crore (net debt excluding leases to EBITDAaL ratio: 0.2)

### Highlights
- Completed an EPS accretive share swap transaction increasing Airtel's stake in the Africa business to over 79%.
- Airtel Africa annualized EBITDA is over Rs 35,000 crore.
- Airtel Money is preparing for a London listing in the second half of 2026.
- Live with lending services in financial services, reusing digital platforms (data, CLM, and channel platforms) from the telco.
- Data center business targeting 1 gigawatt capacity in the next few years.
- Launched Fast Lane technology leveraging network slicing on 5G for postpaid customers.
- AI-led anti-spam solution identified over 93 billion spam calls, 4 billion spam messages, and blocked over 1.4 million fraudulent links.
- Deployed a homegrown agentic platform and patented endpoint device inferencing for field engineers, reducing cloud compute costs to zero for those workloads.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Africa to continue punching above its weight, contributing disproportionately to growth relative to its base contribution.
- Management plans to scale the data center portfolio (Nxtra) from 120–130 megawatts to 1 gigawatt over the next few years.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Africa tele-density is at 45%, smartphone penetration is 52%, home broadband penetration is 2%, and 64% of adults lack a formal bank account.
- Management states Africa resembles India 10 years ago with high headroom for penetration-led growth.
- Management reiterated that the long-term pricing architecture of the Indian telecom industry needs repair by charging for data consumption.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- War on Waste initiative optimized over Rs 11,000 crore from network opex over the last five years.
- Home segment margins saw pressure and moderation due to tightening of acquisition quality and higher acquisition costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total consolidated capex for the quarter was Rs 13,390 crore, heavily weighted toward transport, fiber rollout, and data centers.
- 5G standalone capex is modest as it is primarily software-based.
- Nxtra will require external and internal funding (debt/equity) to scale its data center capacity to 1 gigawatt.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated net debt to EBITDAaL improved to 0.7%; India without passive infra is below 1%.
- Airtel Money is net cash positive.
- Bharti Hexacom net debt excluding leases to EBITDAaL ratio is 0.2.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global memory and chipset pricing have challenged FWA economics and contributed to tightening acquisition criteria.
- Low entry-level pricing and lack of data consumption-based pricing in the broader industry cap long-term ARPU potential.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Piyush Choudhary (HSBC) on Mobile ARPU sustainability:** Management noted mid-term headroom exists via upgrades, unlimited plan adoption, and postpaid acceleration, though long-term growth requires industry pricing architecture repair.
2. **Vivekanand Subbaraman (Ambit Capital) on Data Center Funding:** Asked if external funding for Nxtra indicates a shift in capex strategy. Management stated they will evaluate funding options (internal vs. external leverage at Nxtra) as needs arise.
3. **Sanjesh Jain (ICICI Securities / general analyst) on FWA Churn & Strategy:** Inquired about FWA churn and customer slowdown. Management explained they pulled back from aggressive low-pricing strategies that caused round-tripping, pivoting to prioritize fiber.
4. **Sumangal Nevatia (Kotak Securities) on Home Segment slowdown:** Asked if lower home net additions were conscious. Management confirmed it was due to tightening acquisition quality, with momentum expected to recover.
5. **Manish Adukia (Goldman Sachs) on Africa/Indus synergies:** Asked about benefits of Indus entering Africa. Management highlighted lower rental/IP fees, replicated low-cost Indian architecture, and energy management.

### One-line takeaway
Bharti Airtel delivered a robust quarter across consolidated operations, driven by solid India mobility momentum, strong financial services scaling, and structural expansion in Africa and data centers, while tightening execution discipline in home broadband.
