# Pace Digitek Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Telecom*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Positive - Driven by strong performance in H1 FY27, BESS manufacturing capacity expansion, and an upward revision in FY27 revenue guidance.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** Rs. 808 crore in Q2 FY27, up 51.5% YoY and 45.5% QoQ. H1 FY27 revenue stood at Rs. 1,364 crore (up 51.4% YoY). Energy segment contributed ~80% and Telecom ~20% in H1.
- **EBITDA:** Rs. 159 crore in Q2 FY27, up 68.5% YoY. H1 FY27 EBITDA stood at Rs. 245 crore, up 40.5% YoY.
- **PAT:** Rs. 97 crore in Q2 FY27, up 43.3% YoY. H1 FY27 PAT stood at Rs. 160 crore, up 30.4% YoY.
- **Executable Order Book:** Rs. 10,735 crore as of September 30, 2026 (Energy: Rs. 8,528 crore; Telecom: Rs. 2,208 crore).
- **H1 FY27 Order Inflows:** Rs. 2,437 crore (Energy: Rs. 2,173 crore; Telecom: Rs. 265 crore).
- **Total Debt & Net Debt:** Gross debt increased by Rs. 410 crore to Rs. 1,371 crore as of September 30, 2026. Cash balances stood at Rs. 515 crore, resulting in Net Debt of Rs. 856 crore (Net Debt-to-Equity: 0.35x).

### Highlights
- **BESS Capacity Expansion:** Expanded BESS manufacturing capacity from 2.5 GWh to 5 GWh in August 2026, currently operating at 100% capacity. Produced 844 MWh of BESS capacity in H1 FY27 (equal to 94% of total FY26 production).
- **Project Execution:** Commissioned 875 MWh of BESS capacity at project sites in H1 FY27. Till date, delivered ~1.8 GWh of BESS to customers and commissioned ~1 GWh at own project sites.
- **In-House Container Fabrication:** Established in-house BESS container facility; trial production starts in November 2026 and full commercial production in January 2027, meeting ~50% of container requirements.
- **C&I Segment Entry:** Commenced sample production of 261 kWh C&I cabinets for customer trials in India and abroad.
- **Telecom Segment Updates:** Commissioned over 8,000 BSNL 4G sites, transitioning them into a 5-year O&M phase. Secured additional orders from BSNL and BharatNet OFC.

### Guidance & outlook
- **FY27 Revenue Guidance:** Revised upward to Rs. 3,300 - Rs. 3,500 crore (earlier Rs. 3,200 - Rs. 3,400 crore).
- **FY28 Revenue Guidance:** Maintained at Rs. 4,000 - Rs. 4,200 crore. Management noted room for upside.
- **PAT Margins:** Expected to normalize around ~10% to 11% in FY28 as energy segment volume scales up.
- **BOOT Asset Revenues:** Rs. 197 crore per annum expected once current assets complete commissioning by Dec 2026/Jan 2027. Total BOOT revenue guided at Rs. 325 - Rs. 375 crore in FY28.
- **C&I Mix:** Targeted to account for 30% of energy segment revenue in FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India BESS demand expected to grow to ~236 GWh by FY32, with FY27 market demand estimated at 30 to 35 GWh.
- Bidding competitive intensity has moderated, dropping from 25–30 participants per bid 6 months ago to 6–8 participants currently.
- Strong emerging demand for power and BESS products in the data center market (collaborating with Megmeet; product launch expected in ~1 year).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Q2 EBITDA margin benefited from a higher proportion of telecom service revenue (Telecom EBITDA margin at 20%-23%; O&M at ~25%).
- Energy segment EBITDA margins range between 12%-13% (blended 15%-17% for combined supply and EPC execution).
- Lithium-ion cell prices spiked by 20% in Q2, pressuring energy project margins, but began falling and stabilizing in Q3.
- China’s 6% export rebate reduction/elimination from January 2027 is expected to be offset by falling baseline lithium-ion cell prices.
- In-house container manufacturing is expected to reduce BESS container costs by 25%, lower overall BESS cost by 4%-5%, and improve EBITDA margins by 1.5%-2%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- BESS manufacturing capacity will expand further from 5 GWh to 10 GWh by Q4 FY27, with full utilization expected within one quarter (by Q1 FY28).
- The company is participating in the 4 GWh PLI cell manufacturing tender process (bid completion expected mid-November 2026). Cell capex implementation planned for FY28.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Build-to-Own-Operate (BOOT) asset base reached Rs. 1,311 crore as of September 30, 2026, funded via Rs. 950 crore long-term borrowings.
- Borrowing expected to peak by FY27-end with an additional debt draw of ~Rs. 1,100 crore to fund another Rs. 1,600 crore of BOOT project assets.
- Receivables declined to Rs. 2,177 crore as of Sept 30, 2026 (from Rs. 2,442 crore as of March 31, 2026) due to older telecom retention money collections.
- Target Net Working Capital (NWC) cycle expected to settle at 90-120 days (Receivable days ~150 days; Payable days 60-90 days).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Exposure to volatile lithium-ion cell import costs and raw material shifts from China.
- Regulatory policy shifts regarding Chinese export rebates (6% cut from Jan 2027).
- Delay or capital stress from BOOT assets; management has put a pause on bidding for new BOOT projects unless external equity partners are secured.
- Mobilization costs on new solar EPC projects (MAHAGENCO & Bondada) temporarily weighed down standalone execution margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Deepak Poddar (Sapphire Capital):** *What led to the sharp EBITDA margin improvement in Q2 and what is the margin impact of container integration?*  
  **Management:** Q2 margin expanded due to higher-margin telecom service revenues. Container integration reduces BESS cost by 4-5% and adds 1.5-2% at the EBITDA level.
- **Milandeep Jain (Green Portfolio):** *Why did finance costs and long-term borrowings rise, and what is the timeline for data center products with Megmeet?*  
  **Management:** Borrowing went up specifically to build Rs. 1,311 crore of BOOT assets. Megmeet data center power products are under R&D and will take about another year for commercialization.
- **Paras Chheda (Purple One Vertex):** *What is the long-term capital strategy between BOOT projects versus BESS manufacturing?*  
  **Management:** Core priority is third-party BESS manufacturing and EPC. BOOT projects are paused unless funded by external equity investors due to high capital intensity.
- **Sweta Jain (Anand Rathi):** *Why did standalone and energy segment EBIT decline?*  
  **Management:** Standalone margin fell due to initial mobilization costs on MAHAGENCO and Bondada solar projects. Energy EBIT fell due to a 20% spike in lithium-ion cell prices in Q2.
- **Kapil Aggarwal (Daksh & Associates):** *Why are payables and receivables elevated compared to annual standard days?*  
  **Management:** Telecom projects held 5-10% retention amounts on both customer and vendor sides over the defect liability period. As retentions are collected and energy revenues scale up, receivable days will drop to ~150 days and payables to 60-90 days.

### One-line takeaway
Pace Digitek is rapidly expanding its BESS capacity to 10 GWh while scaling up high-margin energy execution and controlling debt by pausing un-funded BOOT projects.
