# Telecom - Telecom equipment & towers: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Management commentaries across the telecom equipment and tower infrastructure sector reflect strong top-line momentum driven by global AI data center demand, high-capacity optical fiber exports, and rapid expansion into Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) and defense manufacturing. Companies exposed to international hyperscalers (Sterlite Technologies, HFCL) recorded operational margins and revenues at multi-year or historic highs. 

Conversely, companies heavily tied to domestic telecom turnkey execution or state-led rollouts (Tejas Networks, Suyog Telematics, HFCL’s EPC arm) are managing working capital stress, multi-step verification delays, or billing bottlenecks on BSNL networks. Passive tower operators (Indus Towers) continue to see steady tenancy densification alongside initial international geographic expansion in Africa.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far**
Historically, Indian telecom equipment and tower companies derived the bulk of their earnings from standard macro tower lease tenancies and basic fiber optic laydown for domestic telecom service providers. Over the past five years, companies have actively reallocated capital toward high-margin data center interconnects, system integration, defense manufacturing, and renewable energy storage assets to reduce reliance on domestic operator capex cycles. [Background]

**Shifts underway**
- **Optical fiber pivot to AI data centers & hyperscalers**: [Scaling] Equipment players are shifting focus from traditional telco fiber to high-density, specialty micro-cables for global AI infrastructure. Sterlite Technologies secured a 5-year, $1.2 billion agreement with a global hyperscaler and raised its FY29 EBITDA margin target to ~30% [Sterlite Technologies Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. HFCL reported that hyperscalers now generate 65%–75% of its optical connectivity revenues, propelling export growth by ~493% YoY [HFCL Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
- **Expansion into Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)**: [Scaling] Equipment manufacturers are leveraging electrical engineering and containerization capabilities to capture utility-scale energy storage demand. Pace Digitek expanded its BESS capacity to 5 GWh (operating at 100% utilization) and targets 10 GWh by Q4 FY27 [Pace Digitek Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Bondada Engineering is actively bidding across a Rs 26,000–27,000 crore pipeline, including BESS EPC and annuity assets [Bondada Engineering Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
- **Strategic diversification into Defence & Aerospace**: [Scaling] Telecom hardware vendors are capitalizing on defense indigenization. HFCL delivered its second batch of Thermal Weapon Sights, targeted Rs 500–600 crore in FY27 defense revenue, and secured 1,000 acres for an ammunition complex [HFCL Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Bondada Engineering acquired a 75% stake in KCS Engineering Solutions [Bondada Engineering Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
- **Tower infrastructure internationalization**: [Early] Tower companies are expanding into high-growth emerging markets to scale asset utilization. Indus Towers commenced tower deployments generating initial revenue in Zambia and Uganda, with Nigeria rollouts planned for Q3 FY27 [Indus Towers Ltd concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push**
- Telecom PLI Scheme (launched 2021) rewards domestic manufacturing of network equipment and optical fiber [Background].
- Telecom PLI 2.0 (pending) aims to shift policy focus toward design-led manufacturing and deeper backward integration [Govt: Telecom PLI 2.0 to shift focus to design and backward integration, Nov 2026].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus**: Optical fiber demand from global AI data centers and hyperscalers is undergoing strong growth. Sterlite Technologies highlighted CRU's revised 2026 global OFC demand growth projection of 8% YoY, with global data center capacity expanding to 219 GW by 2030 (>70% AI-specific). HFCL confirmed that North American data center demand remains robust, with expanding purchasing activity across Europe and Asia.
- **Divergence**: Domestic execution and operator rollout paces diverge significantly. Indus Towers added ~7,900 co-locations during Q2 driven by customer network densification. In contrast, Suyog Telematics highlighted delayed execution timelines dictated by operator funding tranches (Vodafone Idea) and lingering BSNL billing integration stalls (~300 sites, ~Rs 30 crore capex unbilled). Tejas Networks reported an order book expansion to Rs 2,315 crore with BSNL 4G shipments under way, but continues to post quarterly operational losses (PAT of negative Rs 207 crore).

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Product mix leverage**: Shift toward high-value optical connectivity (e.g., 180-micron A2 fiber priced at $25–$27/km vs. standard telco fiber at ~$15/km) expanded HFCL’s EBITDA margin to 25.89% (up 640 bps YoY) and Sterlite’s operational EBITDA margin to 27.1%.
- **Raw material volatility & vertical integration**: Spot preform prices reached unsustainable highs (~$300/kg), but both HFCL and Sterlite protected margins via long-term contracts and internal preform capacity expansion programs. Lithium-ion cell prices surged 20% in Q2 before stabilizing in Q3; Pace Digitek is mitigating cost shifts by setting up in-house container fabrication (cutting overall BESS costs by 4%–5% and boosting EBITDA margins by 1.5%–2%).
- **Energy cost control**: Indus Towers reduced diesel consumption by 11% YoY through the addition of ~3,000 solar sites in Q2 (total portfolio at ~49,000 sites, 297 MW capacity) and deployment of lithium-ion batteries, improving energy margins to -3.6%.

## Capex & capacity
- **HFCL Ltd**: Executing an ~Rs 1,800 crore capex plan to expand optical fiber capacity from 28M to 43M fiber km, cable capacity to 63M fiber km, and double preform capacity to 600 MT by October 2028.
- **Sterlite Technologies Ltd**: Committed to ~Rs 1,000 crore annual capex through FY29 to scale preform, fiber, and cable capacity by 50% and set up a greenfield pre-term solution facility.
- **Pace Digitek Ltd**: Expanding BESS manufacturing capacity from 5 GWh to 10 GWh by Q4 FY27; building in-house container facilities; paused new un-funded BOOT project bids to preserve balance sheet strength.
- **Indus Towers Ltd**: Spending ongoing growth capex for macro sites (~2,900 macro towers added), 5G upgrades, solar rollouts, and initial Africa tower deployments.
- **Suyog Telematics Ltd**: Budgeted Rs 200 crore capex for H2 FY27 to execute ~2,000 targeted sites, funded via Rs 120 crore internal accruals and Rs 80+ crore in bank credit.

## What changed since last quarter
- **HFCL Ltd**: Raised FY27 revenue growth guidance to at least 80% YoY (up from 40% stated post-Q1 FY27 and initial 20% guidance). Turnkey EPC EBITDA losses widened to Rs 121 crore in Q2 due to multi-step BharatNet billing delays, prompting the board to evaluate restructuring options for the EPC business.
- **Suyog Telematics Ltd**: Pushed its timeline to achieve a Rs 100 crore quarterly revenue run-rate from Q4 FY27 out to September 2027. Zinc battery testing was scaled back to 4 sites (down from 10–15 targeted in Q1), and ~300 unbilled BSNL sites were temporarily excluded from active top-line reporting.
- **Indus Towers Ltd**: Successfully fulfilled its Q1 commitment to enter Africa, deploying initial macro towers in Zambia and Uganda that began generating revenue late in Q2.
- **Tejas Networks Ltd**: Expanded its order book to Rs 2,315 crore (up from Rs 1,529 crore in Q1 FY27) after receiving LOI/POs for 18,685-plus 4G expansion sites for BSNL.
- **Pace Digitek Ltd**: Raised full-year FY27 revenue guidance to Rs 3,300–3,500 crore (up from Rs 3,200–3,400 crore).
