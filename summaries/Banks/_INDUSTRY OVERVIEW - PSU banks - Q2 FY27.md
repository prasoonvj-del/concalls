# Banks - PSU banks: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across PSU banks reported a robust Q2 FY27, characterized by strong double-digit growth in advances and deposits, multi-year lows in Gross and Net NPAs, and healthy profitability. Across the board, banks crossed major milestones—such as Bank of Maharashtra crossing Rs. 7 lakh crore in total business, Canara Bank crossing Rs. 30 lakh crore, and State Bank of India reaching Rs. 115 trillion. Asset quality improvements continued, with credit costs remaining benign and Provision Coverage Ratios (PCR) maintained at elevated levels (mostly above 95%).

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Retail, Agriculture, and MSME (RAM) sectors served as the primary growth engines, with RAM advances growing consistently (e.g., Punjab & Sind Bank RAM up >31% YoY, Indian Bank RAM up 17.74%, Canara Bank RAM up 21.03%). Corporate pipelines remain healthy across banks (e.g., Canara Bank at ~Rs. 47,000–50,000 crore, Central Bank at ~Rs. 24,000 crore, PNB at Rs. 1.58 lakh crore, and SBI at Rs. 7 to 7.8 lakh crore).
*   **Divergence:** While most banks reported broad-based retail momentum, State Bank of India noted that home loan market growth slowed industry-wide due to supply-side inventory build-up and project delays rather than interest-rate sensitivity. Additionally, select banks (SBI, Canara Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, PNB) flagged localized drought conditions in states like Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh requiring close monitoring of agri-loans, though actual stress remains low.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **FCNR(B) Substitution:** A dominant theme across multiple banks was the aggressive mobilization of FCNR(B) deposits (Canara Bank $5.8B, SBI $10.9B, PNB $2.7B, Bank of India $2.2B, Indian Bank $2.3B, Bank of Maharashtra $1.65B MTN/FCNR) to actively replace high-cost bulk deposits and Certificates of Deposit (CDs), resulting in funding cost savings.
*   **Repo Pass-Through:** Banks with high shares of repo-linked (EBLR) loans—such as Bank of India (66%), UCO Bank (66%), JK Bank (65%), and PNB (56%)—noted that recent repo rate hikes were passed on immediately, supporting asset yields despite staggered term deposit repricing.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Branch Expansion & Digitalization:** Banks are expanding physical footprints while heavily investing in digital infrastructure. Bank of Maharashtra is opening 200 branches annually; Punjab & Sind Bank is targeting 300 centers; PNB opened 162 branches in H1 FY27. Investments are heavily skewed toward cybersecurity, AI-powered conversational tools (Indian Bank's 'TARA', Canara Bank's *ai1Pe*, Central Bank's NBET, SBI CyRIS), and digital loan processing journeys.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Canara Bank:** Raised its one-time ECL provision impact estimate from Rs. 10,000–12,000 crore to Rs. 12,000–15,000 crore due to yield hardening on the HTM book.
*   **Indian Bank:** Revised its deposit growth guidance upward from 9–11% to 11–12%, and advances growth guidance from 11–13% to 13–14%.
*   **JK Bank:** Revised FY26-27 credit growth guidance to 14%+ (aiming around 18% with retail focus).
*   **UCO Bank:** Noted that it is currently outperforming its initial annual guidance across deposits, advances, and asset quality metrics.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **ECL Transition & Provisioning:** Formal Board approvals and front-loading of Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning ahead of the April 1, 2027 implementation date (noted by Bank of India, Canara Bank, PNB, SBI, UCO Bank, and JK Bank).
*   **Margin Trajectory:** The net impact of recent repo rate hikes on NIM expansion versus rising term deposit repricing costs.
*   **Agri-Book Stress:** Potential utilization of restructuring or moratorium options in drought-declared districts of Maharashtra and Karnataka.
