# Banks: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the banking sector reported a robust Q2 FY27, characterized by strong credit and deposit growth, improving asset quality, and healthy profitability metrics despite a macro backdrop of monetary policy tightening (with the MPC raising the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50%), tightening system liquidity, and rising interest rates. Several banks successfully mobilized FCNR(B) deposits to substitute high-cost bulk borrowings. However, banks are actively monitoring emerging risks, including potential headwinds from draft IRDAI regulations on bancassurance commissions, scanty monsoons/droughts in states like Maharashtra and Karnataka, and impending Expected Credit Loss (ECL) accounting norms effective April 1, 2027.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Retail, RAM (Retail, Agri, MSME), and secured lending remain primary growth drivers across the sector. Corporate pipelines are active, particularly in renewable energy, power, infrastructure, and roads (e.g., Bank of India, Canara Bank, Bank of Maharashtra). 
*   **Divergence:** While microfinance (EEB/Inclusive Finance) portfolios are stabilizing after historical stress—with Bandhan Bank arresting EEB de-growth and Suryoday reporting strong inclusive finance collections—some banks are adopting a cautious stance. AU Small Finance, Suryoday Small Finance Bank, and ESAF noted potential drought impacts in parts of Maharashtra and Karnataka, with Suryoday pausing new NTC customer onboarding in specific drought-hit districts. On deposits, while large banks and small finance banks like AU Small Finance and City Union Bank report strong granular growth, City Union Bank explicitly noted that sustaining 20%+ deposit growth over the next two years will be challenging due to fierce competition.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Agreements:** System liquidity is tightening, and deposit competition remains aggressive. Most banks experienced pressure on cost of funds or muted NIM expansion, with some facing sequential margin compression due to higher investments/liquidity holding or day-count seasonality (e.g., AU Small Finance NIM -9 bps to 5.8%; IDFC First Bank NIM moderated to 5.58%). Yield hardening also caused MTM treasury losses for some institutions (e.g., Canara Bank reported an MTM loss of Rs 50-61 crore).
*   **Disvergences/Variations:** Select banks successfully lowered or stabilized their cost of funds by replacing high-cost bulk deposits with FCNR(B) funds or granular retail deposits (e.g., Bank of Maharashtra's cost of funds dropped 25 bps YoY to 4.07%; Canara Bank saved 100 bps by replacing bulk deposits with FCNR funds; Karnataka Bank's cost of funds edged down 1 bp QoQ to 5.15%). Small finance banks continue to operate at higher NIM levels (ESAF at 8.2%, Suryoday at 7.2%, Capital SFB at 4.24%).

## Capex & capacity
*   **Branch and Network Expansion:** Banks are continuing their physical and digital footprint expansion. AU Small Finance added 45 deposit branches and 100+ asset centers. Bank of Maharashtra is opening 200 branches annually. Karnataka Bank is on track to add 31 branches in FY27 to cross 1,000 total branches, a milestone already crossed by City Union Bank (>1,000 branches). Capital SFB is targeting >300 branches by FY29. ESAF added 10 banking outlets.
*   **Technology & SFB Transitions:** Fino Payments Bank is investing Rs 120 crore to Rs 150 crore over 12-15 months for its Small Finance Bank transition, targeting a Q1 FY28 go-live. ESAF successfully went live with its "StratoNeXt" (ESAF 2.0) digital transformation migrating to Oracle FLEXCUBE. AU Small Finance deployed Agentic AI and Cross Lead platforms for underwriting.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance & Tone Shifts:** Most banks maintained their annual credit and deposit growth guidance while noting they are "bettering" their targets (Canara Bank, AU Small Finance, Capital SFB, Suryoday). Bandhan Bank shifted its tone from "cautious" to "balanced and steady progress" as EEB portfolio de-growth was successfully arrested.
*   **New Risks & Regulatory Topics:** Draft regulations on bancassurance commission caps emerged as a notable potential fee-income headwind across retail-focused banks (AU Small Finance, Bandhan Bank, Capital SFB, IDFC First Bank, ESAF). 
*   **ECL Provisioning Updates:** Banks refined their ECL impact estimates and provisioning plans. Canara Bank raised its estimated ECL impact to Rs 12,000–15,000 crore (aiming to absorb it over 2 years). Bank of Maharashtra reported Rs 750 crore of ECL provisioning so far against a 4-year assessment of Rs 2,500 crore. City Union Bank estimated an incremental ECL requirement of Rs 299.42 crore to be adjusted against capital reserves with zero P&L impact.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **Regulatory Developments:** Final guidelines and potential financial impacts of the IRDAI draft circular on bancassurance commission caps.
*   **ECL Transition Preparations:** Bank disclosures and front-loading plans regarding ECL provisioning ahead of the April 1, 2027 implementation.
*   **Agri/MFI Asset Quality:** Potential lagged impacts of drought conditions in Maharashtra and Karnataka on rural and inclusive finance portfolios (AU Small Finance, Bandhan Bank, Suryoday, ESAF, Karnataka Bank).
*   **SFB Milestones:** Fino Payments Bank's continued execution toward its targeted Q1 FY28 Small Finance Bank go-live.
*   **Deposit Growth & Margins:** Systemic deposit mobilization trends and the ability of banks to protect NIMs amidst tightening liquidity and rising interest rates.
