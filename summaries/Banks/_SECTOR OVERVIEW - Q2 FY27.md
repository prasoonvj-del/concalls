# Banks: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Q2 FY27 for Indian banks was marked by resilient credit demand, strong profitability, and multi-decade best asset quality. System credit outpaced deposit growth, keeping deposit competition intense. A major system-wide tailwind during the quarter was the RBI’s special FCNR(B) swap window, which generated massive foreign currency deposit inflows (e.g., $10.9B for State Bank of India, $5.8B for Canara Bank, $3.6B for IDFC First Bank, $2.7B for PNB, $2.3B for Indian Bank, $2.2B for Bank of India, and $194M for DCB Bank). Managements across public and private banks strategically deployed these lower-cost dollar funds to replace high-cost domestic bulk deposits and Certificates of Deposits (CDs).

Asset quality across the sector remained exceptionally pristine, with Gross NPAs dropping to historical lows (SBI at 1.39%, Indian Bank at 1.61%, Canara Bank at 1.33%, PNB at 2.55%, Bank of India at 1.60%). Slippages were muted across corporate and RAM (Retail, Agri, MSME) books. However, banks are actively preparing for the transition to the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework effective April 1, 2027. Public sector banks (SBI, Canara Bank, Bank of India, Bank of Maharashtra, Indian Bank, UCO Bank, Central Bank of India, PNB) and regional lenders have begun front-loading provisions or computing capital impacts, noting that 4-to-5-year amortization windows and current strong operating profits will comfortably absorb the shift.

Regulatory headwinds flagged sector-wide included draft IRDAI consultation papers proposing caps on bancassurance commissions (flagged by Bandhan Bank, Capital SFB, CSB Bank, HDFC Bank, IDFC First Bank, Jana SFB, DCB Bank, Ujjivan SFB, and TMB), as well as a 25 bps repo rate hike by the RBI (repo raised to 5.50%), which managements view as margin-accretive for floating/EBLR-heavy loan books.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
### Consensus
- **RAM & Retail Resilience:** Managements universally report robust retail consumption and MSME demand. Secured retail (housing, vehicle loans, gold loans) and MSME portfolios saw strong double-digit growth (e.g., Bank of Maharashtra's RAM grew >20%, Canara Bank RAM +21%, Capital SFB +23.4%, CSB Bank corporate/gold +44%/36%, Indian Bank RAM +17.7%, PSB RAM +31%).
- **Corporate Pipeline Revival:** Public sector banks highlighted massive corporate credit pipelines ready for H2 FY27 disbursement, heavily driven by renewable energy, roads (HAM/NHAI), data centers, power, real estate, and food processing. SBI reports a corporate pipeline of ~Rs 7-7.8 lakh crore, PNB Rs 1.58 lakh crore, Bank of India Rs 86,000+ crore, Canara Bank Rs 47,000-50,000 crore, Central Bank of India ~Rs 24,000 crore, Bank of Maharashtra Rs 23,000 crore, PSB Rs 20,000 crore, and UCO Bank Rs 10,000 crore.

### Divergence
- **Microfinance (MFI) & Unsecured Credit:** Managements diverge sharply on unsecured microfinance. Bandhan Bank, ESAF SFB, and Jana SFB noted de-growth or calibrated slowing in group MFI lending to shift toward secured assets. Conversely, Suryoday SFB, Utkarsh SFB, and AU SFB report stabilizing/recovering MFI collections. Jana SFB highlighted that MFI growth is subdued due to tighter bureau checks, while Ujjivan SFB views 12-14% MFI growth as a "sweet spot."
- **Agri & Climate Concerns:** While SBI, Bank of Maharashtra, DCB Bank, Capital SFB, and Ujjivan SFB noted no material stress or default spikes from El Niño or localized drought declarations in Karnataka and Maharashtra, Suryoday SFB took a cautious stance by pausing new NTC customer onboarding in drought-hit Marathwada/Karnataka districts.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Margin Dynamics (NIM):** NIM trends diverged based on asset repricing schedules and liquidity management:
  - *Expanding/Resilient NIMs:* South Indian Bank (3.33%, +10 bps QoQ), PNB (2.74%, +10 bps QoQ), City Union Bank (3.87%, vs 3.78% QoQ), CSB Bank (3.81%, vs 3.66% QoQ), and Bank of Maharashtra (3.97%, +12 bps YoY) benefited from asset repricing and EBLR pass-through.
  - *Moderating NIMs:* IDFC First Bank (5.58%), HDFC Bank (3.32%), DCB Bank, Yes Bank (2.6%), AU SFB (5.8%), and TMB (4.19%) experienced sequential NIM pressure due to FCNR(B) drag, excess liquidity carrying costs, and deposit competition. 
  - *NIM Outlook:* SBI, HDFC Bank, IDFC First Bank, DCB Bank, PNB, Indian Bank, and City Union Bank guided for bottoming out/expansion of NIMs in Q3/Q4 FY27, citing immediate pass-through of the October 25 bps repo rate hike onto floating rate books (60-70% of assets for most banks).
- **Cost of Funds & Deposit Competition:** Granular retail term deposit rates remain competitive (AU SFB maintains an 80-100 bps premium). Cost of funds dropped or stabilized for banks leveraging FCNR(B) scheme inflows to retire expensive bulk deposits and CDs (e.g., PNB reduced CDs from ~Rs 60,000 cr to ~Rs 40,000 cr; Indian Bank cut bulk deposits by ~Rs 10,000 cr).
- **Treasury Headwinds:** Hardening G-Sec yields in Q2 led to MTM treasury losses or reduced trading gains across several lenders (SBI recorded Rs 1,275 crore MTM loss, Canara Bank shifted to a Rs 61 crore MTM loss, PNB reported negative AFS reserves of Rs 1,286 crore, and South Indian Bank took a Rs 52-53 crore Q2 impact).

## Capex & capacity
- **Branch Expansion:** Physical distribution expansion remains active. PNB opened 162 branches (targeting 250 in FY27), Bank of Maharashtra is adding 200 annually, Capital SFB aims for >300 branches by FY29, TMB target is 60 branches, Jana SFB opened 40 of 78 planned branches, Ujjivan SFB added 104 branches in H1, Karnataka Bank is adding 31 branches, and Fino Payments Bank plans 40 liability branches and 25-30 asset centers for its SFB transition.
- **Technology & Digital Investments:** IT spending is heavily directed toward core banking migrations (ESAF SFB went live on Oracle FLEXCUBE; Utkarsh SFB launched new CBS), digital journeys, agentic AI platforms (AU SFB, Jana SFB), cybersecurity (Bank of Maharashtra IT budget at Rs 985 crore; SBI launched CyRIS), and quantum-safe encryption (PNB). Fino Payments Bank guided for Rs 120-150 crore SFB transition tech capex.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Guidance Upgrades:**
  - **Jana SFB:** Upgraded FY27 full-year PAT growth guidance from 80% to 100% YoY.
  - **Indian Bank:** Revised advance growth guidance upward from 11-13% to 13-14% and deposit growth from 9-11% to 11-12%.
  - **Tamilnad Mercantile Bank:** Raised FY27 deposit growth target from 18%+ to 20%+ and advances from 20%+ to 22%+.
  - **JK Bank:** Upgraded credit growth target to 14%+ (aiming ~18%), RoA to ~1.20%+, and GNPA below 2%.
  - **Ujjivan SFB:** Signaled high likelihood of overshooting the upper end of its full-year ROA guidance.
- **Tone Shifts & Recovery:**
  - **Bandhan Bank:** Shifted tone from cautious to "balanced and steady progress," arresting EEB/MFI portfolio de-growth.
  - **Utkarsh SFB:** Tone shifted from "green shoots" to "business momentum is recovering."
  - **DCB Bank & Yes Bank:** Flagged NIM compression as temporary, with DCB confirming capital raise closure is imminent.
- **Major Extraordinary Events & Legal Rulings:**
  - **Yes Bank:** Supreme Court set aside the March 2020 AT-1 bond write-down of Rs 8,415 crore, resulting in a pro forma ~240 bps CET-1 capital impact (lowering CET-1 to ~11.4%).
  - **State Bank of India:** Booked Rs 7,288 crore pre-tax exceptional gain from selling a 6.35% stake in SBI Funds Management Limited.
  - **Indian Bank:** Booked Rs 252 crore profit and Rs 1,134 crore MTM gain from the listing of its NSE shareholding.

## What to watch next quarter
1. **NIM Trajectory & Repo Rate Repricing:** Watch for expected NIM recovery across HDFC Bank, IDFC First Bank, DCB Bank, and SBI as 25 bps October repo rate hikes reprice floating rate loans.
2. **ECL Transition Front-Loading:** Monitor Board approvals and provisioning steps in Q3/Q4 across Bank of India, Canara Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, Indian Bank, UCO Bank, and JK Bank ahead of April 2027.
3. **IRDAI Insurance Commission Guidelines:** Track final guidelines on bancassurance caps, which could impact fee income for Bandhan Bank, HDFC Bank, IDFC First Bank, and SFBs.
4. **Yes Bank Capital Strategy:** Watch for concrete capital-raising plans or legal/resolution steps post the Supreme Court AT-1 bond judgment.
5. **CGFMU Scheme Re-opening:** Track the renewal notification for CGFMU microfinance credit guarantees, currently awaited by Jana SFB, Suryoday SFB, and Utkarsh SFB.
