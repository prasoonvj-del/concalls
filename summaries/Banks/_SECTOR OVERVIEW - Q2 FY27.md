# Banks: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The banking sector in Q2 FY27 exhibited strong asset quality and double-digit credit expansion, driven largely by secured retail, agriculture, and MSME (RAM) loans. Profitability across the coverage universe remained firm—highlighted by multi-year low Gross Non-Performing Asset (GNPA) ratios (e.g., State Bank of India's 1.39% being a two-decade low, and Bank of India at 1.60%). 

However, two structural headwinds emerged during the quarter:
1. **Monetary Tightening and Cost of Funds:** The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) raised the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50%, shifting its monetary stance to "calibrated tightening." While this immediately expanded yields on floating and external benchmark lending rate (EBLR)-linked books, it tightened system liquidity and intensified deposit competition.
2. **Transition Dynamics & Regulatory Headwinds:** Banks actively mobilized large volumes of foreign currency deposits via the RBI’s FCNR(B) swap window (e.g., SBI raising $10.9 billion, Canara Bank $5.8 billion, IDFC First Bank $3.6 billion) to displace high-cost bulk deposits. However, managements flagged regulatory uncertainties, including the upcoming April 1, 2027 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework (which prompted heavy upfront provisioning and MTM hits on HTM investments), draft IRDAI caps on bancassurance commissions, and lingering legal overhangs—most notably Yes Bank facing a ~240 bps pro forma hit to CET-1 following the Supreme Court’s decision reversing its March 2020 AT-1 bond write-down.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence

### Consensus
Across public, private, and small finance banks, domestic credit demand remains solid, supported by India's Q1 GDP growth of 7.8% (and upgraded full-year forecasts of ~7.0–7.1%). 
- **RAM and Secured Lending:** Retail, Agriculture, and MSME verticals drove incremental advances. Canara Bank (+21.03% RAM), Punjab & Sind Bank (+31% RAM), and UCO Bank (+26.92% RAM) highlighted aggressive consumer and MSME traction.
- **Gold Loans:** Gold loan expansion was exceptionally strong across the board, led by Ujjivan SFB (+209.8% YoY), AU SFB (+102% YoY), PNB (+97–98% YoY), and Bank of Maharashtra (growing from Rs 6,500 crore to Rs 18,600 crore in agri-gold).
- **Corporate Pipelines:** Large lenders continue to build large wholesale pipelines focused on infrastructure, HAM roads, power, renewables, and data centers. SBI reported a pipeline of Rs 7.0–7.8 lakh crore, PNB Rs 1.58 lakh crore, Bank of India Rs 86,000+ crore, and Canara Bank Rs 47,000–50,000 crore.

### Divergence
- **Microfinance (MFI) and Unsecured Lending:** SFBs showed diverging approaches. While Suryoday SFB reported recovering MFI collection efficiency (99.3%) and Jana SFB noted stable collections (99%), both Jana SFB and Bandhan Bank pointed to cautious underwriting due to borrower indebtedness. Conversely, ESAF SFB deliberately shrank its group microfinance book by 65% YoY to rotate toward secured lending.
- **Home Loans and Mortgages:** SBI observed an industry-wide slowdown in retail housing loans, linking it to developer project delays and inventory build-up rather than borrowing rates. In contrast, HDFC Bank, Jana SFB (+28.2%), and Ujjivan SFB (affordable housing up 38.1%) reported strong mortgage and LAP disbursement momentum.
- **Deposit Mobilization Feasibility:** While AU SFB maintained confident 25%+ deposit growth guidance, City Union Bank explicitly cautioned that sustaining 20%+ deposit growth over the next 12–18 months would be difficult amid tight system liquidity.

## Costs, raw materials & margins

### Margins (NIMs)
Net interest margins experienced divergent pressures:
- **EBLR-Driven Expansion:** Banks with large repo-linked floating books saw immediate margin widening or guided for positive expansion in Q3/Q4. PNB posted a 10 bps QoQ increase in domestic NIM to 2.74% and guided for another 7–9 bps rise. UCO Bank and Central Bank of India highlighted that >60% of advances are repo-linked, protecting margins.
- **Liquidity Dilution:** AU SFB (NIM down 9 bps to 5.8%), IDFC First Bank (down to 5.58%), and Yes Bank (down 12–14 bps to 2.6%) suffered margin compression due to excess liquidity drag, FCNR(B) deployment timing, and day-count seasonality. HDFC Bank recorded a NIM of 3.32%, stating margins bottomed out in Q2 and will rise as floating loans reprice.

### Funding Costs & FCNR(B) Substitution
To insulate margins against rising domestic deposit rates, several banks actively substituted expensive domestic certificates of deposit and bulk deposits using lower-cost foreign currency borrowings:
- **PNB** used $2.7 billion in FCNR(B) flows to slash CDs from Rs 60,000 crore to Rs 40,000 crore, bringing domestic deposit costs down to 4.97%.
- **Canara Bank** replaced bulk deposits using $5.8 billion in FCNR(B) deposits, saving ~100 bps in funding costs.
- **Bank of Maharashtra** drove cost of funds down 25 bps YoY to 4.07% by letting high-cost bulk deposits run off in favor of retail inflows.

### Operating Expenses & ECL Provisioning
- **Opex Inflation:** Ongoing distribution expansion and tech/AI investments elevated opex at CSB Bank (+17% YoY staff costs; cost-to-income at 64.8%) and AU SFB (+26% YoY opex). 
- **ECL & Treasury Hits:** Hardening bond yields generated mark-to-market (MTM) losses on AFS/treasury portfolios (SBI logged Rs 1,275 crore in MTM losses; Canara Bank recorded Rs 61 crore). Furthermore, Canara Bank raised its one-time ECL provision assessment from Rs 10,000–12,000 crore to Rs 12,000–15,000 crore specifically due to yield hardening on HTM portfolios.

## Capex & capacity
- **Physical Distribution:** Public and private lenders continue physical expansion. SBI deployed >9,500 *Seva Sarathis* across branches; PNB opened 162 branches (aiming for 250 in FY27); Bank of Maharashtra opened 77 branches in H1 toward its 200-branch annual target; and City Union Bank crossed the 1,000-branch milestone.
- **Tech, Digital, & Core Migrations:** ESAF SFB migrated to Oracle FLEXCUBE under its "StratoNeXt" overhaul; Utkarsh SFB launched a new Core Banking System (CBS); and Fino Payments Bank earmarked Rs 120–150 crore capex over 12–15 months to build out its SFB infrastructure (LOS, LMS, enterprise NPA).
- **GIFT City Banking Units (IBUs):** Lenders expanded international operations through GIFT City to tap offshore foreign currency lending, including Bank of Maharashtra (generating Rs 18,000 crore credit growth), Central Bank of India (Rs 8,093 crore in advances), UCO Bank, and Yes Bank (Rs 15,215 crore in FCTL).

## What changed since last quarter
- **Guidance Upgrades:**
  - **Jana SFB:** Raised full-year FY27 PAT growth guidance from 80% to 100% after H1 PAT equaled full-year FY26 profits.
  - **Tamilnad Mercantile Bank (TMB):** Increased full-year deposit growth guidance to 20%+ (from 18%+) and advances growth to 22%+.
  - **Indian Bank:** Raised FY27 deposit growth guidance from 9–11% to 11–12%, and advances guidance from 11–13% to 13–14%.
  - **Ujjivan SFB:** Signaled that it will likely overshoot the upper end of its full-year ROA guidance range.
- **Guidance Cuts & Upward Provision Revisions:**
  - **Canara Bank:** Raised estimated one-time ECL transition impact upward to Rs 12,000–15,000 crore (previously Rs 10,000–12,000 crore) due to HTM yield hardening.
- **Tone Shifts:**
  - **Bandhan Bank:** Shifted tone from cautious to balanced after arresting sequential de-growth in its Emerging Entrepreneurs Business (EEB).
  - **Utkarsh SFB:** Upgraded tone from identifying "green shoots" to stating that business momentum is "ready to pick pace."
- **Delivery on Promises:**
  - Canara Bank exceeded its FCNR(B) mobilization target ($5.8 billion achieved vs. $2.3–2.5 billion planned).
  - ESAF SFB successfully took live its core IT transformation project (StratoNeXt) on October 26, 2026.

## What to watch next quarter
1. **Repo Repricing vs. Deposit Lag:** Monitor Q3 domestic NIM expansion among banks with >50–60% repo-linked books (PNB, UCO, Central Bank, HDFC Bank, IDFC First Bank) against the upward repricing of retail term deposits.
2. **Supreme Court AT-1 Resolution at Yes Bank:** Tracking Board determinations and rating agency views on potential capital-raising timelines following the pro forma 240 bps CET-1 dilution.
3. **Agri and Rural Stress from Drought Declarations:** Assess whether drought conditions in Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Andhra Pradesh lead to actual restructuring drawdowns or spike slippages (flagged by SBI, Canara Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, Suryoday SFB, and Ujjivan SFB).
4. **Regulatory Commission Caps (IRDAI):** Progress on draft bancassurance norms, which could shave fee income across banca-heavy lenders (Bandhan Bank, AU SFB, IDFC First Bank, CSB Bank, TMB).
5. **CGFMU Scheme Renewal:** Watch for the official central government notification renewal for fresh microfinance loan guarantees under CGFMU (flagged by Jana SFB, Suryoday SFB, and Utkarsh SFB).
