# DCB Bank Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-29 · Banks*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consistent growth, record net profit, and improving asset quality metrics in line with guidance.

### Key numbers
- Advances growth: 21% YoY
- Deposits growth: 19% YoY (term deposits / total deposit growth at 18.6% YoY)
- Net Interest Income (NII) growth: 17% YoY
- Revenue growth: 21% YoY
- Operating profit growth: 26% YoY
- Cost to average assets: 2.47%
- Cost-to-income ratio: 59.64% (18-quarter low)
- Credit cost: 0.28%
- Gross NPA: 2.29% (23-quarter low)
- Net NPA: 0.73% (23-quarter low)
- Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR): 81.38% (68.75% excluding technical write-offs)
- Net Profit: Rs 236 crore (up 29% YoY, highest ever in a quarter)
- Return on Assets (ROA): 1.03% for the quarter, 1% for the half year
- Return on Equity (ROE): 14.6% for the quarter, 14.12% for the half year
- Employee count: 12,002
- Gold loans as a % of total assets: 23%
- Floating provisions: Rs 218 crore

### Highlights
- Both advances and deposits have doubled over September 2022 levels across the last 4 years.
- Achieved the fifth consecutive quarter of highest-ever quarterly net profit.
- Raised $194 million through the FCNR (B) window in August at a lower cost than normal term deposits.
- Gold loan portfolio mix is approximately 30% organic and 70% inorganic (combination of co-lending and direct assignment).
- Floating provisions stand at close to Rs 218 crore, untouched since 2015.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects NIM to improve in Q3 and Q4, driven by lower surplus liquidity at the start of Q3, repo rate pass-through to floating-rate asset customers in November, and low credit costs.
- Employee strength is projected to reach around 12,800 to 12,900 by the end of the year (falling short of the earlier 13,000 target).
- Capital raise: Management expects a firm commitment / good news regarding capital raise before the next investor call; explicitly stated they will not raise capital below book value.
- Gold loan book ceiling is maintained at 25% of total assets (currently at 23%).
- Corporate loan book (largely NBFCs) targeted to increase to around 10% of overall assets (currently around 8% or slightly less).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Mortgage and LAP demand remains strong with stable early trends and rejection rates.
- Cash-flow-based underwriting is prioritized; roughly 1/3 of LAP is for interest arbitrage, 1/6 for better products (term loan to OD), and a significant chunk for higher exposure/business expansion.
- Agri book (tractor and KCC) is behaving well with low bounce rates; no material El Nino or climate-related distress seen yet.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Yield on advances dropped by 38 bps YoY (and 2 bps QoQ).
- Cost of funds dropped by 34 bps YoY (and 8 bps QoQ).
- NIM compressed QoQ due to excess liquidity carrying costs from stockpiling post-February Israel-Iran war and the August FCNR(B) deposit inflows before high-cost bulk deposits matured at the end of Q2.
- Opex increase is primarily driven by staff additions (people cost) and normal cybersecurity/tech costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned branch addition: ~20 branches for the year, primarily in existing locations where the bank has a footprint.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Incremental mortgage sourcing split is roughly 71% home loans to 29% LAP.
- Slippage ratio on non-gold loans moved slightly from 1.52% to 1.55% QoQ.
- ECL transition: Appointed a Big 4 firm, work is underway, and models are expected to be up and running by March.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Potential impact of draft bancassurance norms on fee income; management expects to offset via other streams.
- Gold loan risk is primarily seen as a potential sharp drop in gold prices rather than the income-earning capability or CIBIL score of the borrower.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Akshat Agrawal (Nirmal Bang):** Asked about elevated opex/staff additions and reasons for NIM compression. Management explained opex is driven by headcount (12,002) and NIM was impacted by surplus liquidity carrying costs from FCNR(B) inflows ahead of bulk deposit maturities, but guided for NIM expansion in Q3/Q4.
2. **Suraj Das (Sundaram Mutual Fund):** Inquired about gold loan slippages and DA vs. co-lending mix. Management clarified that DA is growing while co-lending is flat, and slippages are minor due to partner collection mindsets, but overall absolute NPA in gold remains low (~Rs 26 crore on a ~Rs 14,000 crore book).
3. **Varun (Bandhan Life Insurance):** Asked about the utility of branch-level headcount increases and mortgage BT strategies. Management stated they are sweating existing branches in top cities, and for NBFC balance transfers, they offer overdraft facilities at higher rates for flexibility rather than yield dilution.
4. **Nitin Aggarwal (Motilal Oswal):** Asked about ECL transition impact and credit costs. Management noted they hold ~Rs 218 crore in floating provisions and expect strong credit quality to reflect well in ECL.
5. **Jai Prakash Mundhra (ICICI Securities):** Raised concerns over potential fee income hits from bancassurance guidelines. Management replied that past shocks (like MFI hits and treasury swings) were absorbed successfully, and they will adapt once draft/final guidelines are issued.
6. **Prem Patel (Individual Investor):** Asked about dilution price-to-book valuations. Management firmly stated they **will not raise capital below book value** and expect closure within weeks.

### One-line takeaway
DCB Bank delivered a record quarter with 29% YoY PAT growth to Rs 236 crore, a 1% ROA, and steady asset quality improvements, while guiding for NIM expansion and imminent capital raise updates.
