# Suryoday Small Finance Bank Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · Banks*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Reflects strong advance/deposit growth, sharp decline in gross NPAs aided by CGFMU claims, and robust profitability.

### Key numbers
- **Gross Advances (including IBPC):** INR 14,972 crore (up 34.6% YoY)
- **Disbursements:** INR 3,096 crore (up 27.4% YoY)
- **Total Deposits:** INR 16,184 crore (up 35% YoY)
- **Retail Deposits (including CASA):** INR 13,676 crore (84.5% of total deposits)
- **CASA Ratio:** 19.4% (CASA balances grew 26.8% YoY in absolute terms)
- **Gross NPA:** 2.9% (improved from 6.5% in June)
- **Net Interest Income (NII):** INR 368.8 crore (up 42.8% YoY)
- **NIM:** 7.2% (up 28 bps YoY)
- **Net Total Income:** INR 495.3 crore (up 46.5% YoY)
- **PAT:** INR 76.5 crore
- **CRAR:** 19.37% (before the INR 200 crore Tier 2 raise in October which adds ~1.8%)
- **IF Current Bucket Collection Efficiency:** 99.3% in Q2 FY27 vs 99.2% in Q1 FY27

### Highlights
- Gross NPAs declined significantly to 2.9% from 6.5% in June, supported by INR 387 crore of CGFMU claims received in July and INR 596 crore of write-offs/OTS of legacy stressed assets.
- Inclusive Finance (IF) contributed 41% and Retail Assets contributed 59% to overall expansion. Secured lending (mortgages and CV) grew 36% YoY.
- Digital banking initiatives scaled up: over 14 lakh customers targeted under the Credit Line on UPI (CLOU) program (book at ~INR 350 crore), and over 2 lakh customers onboarded digitally for deposits.
- Raised INR 200 crore of Tier 2 capital in October 2026, improving CRAR by 1.8%; another INR 200 crore tranche is planned by Q4 FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains full-year FY27 guidance of 30% to 35% advances growth and an exit ROA of approximately 1.4% to 1.5%.
- Medium-term portfolio mix target remains around 45% Inclusive Finance and 55% Retail Assets.
- Expects to open around 50 branches over the next 4-5 months (10 already opened).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Systemic credit growth remains healthy; deposit mobilization is competitive with credit growth outpacing deposits.
- Microfinance industry collection trends have improved and portfolio quality is stabilizing, with a shift towards individual loans and stronger cash flow assessments.
- Management noted potential drought conditions in Karnataka and Maharashtra, though no material adverse impact on portfolio performance or asset quality has been observed thus far.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cost-to-income (CTI) adjusted for digital partner payouts stood at 68.7% in H1 FY27 vs 72.9% in H1 FY26. (Reported CTI is ~70.5-70.6%).
- PSLC income came down by ~INR 37 crore QoQ to ~INR 8 crore in Q2, but was offset by growth in the paying book and INR 14 crore convenience fees from the CLOU business.
- CGFMU fees provisioned at ~INR 40-45 crore in H1 FY27.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 10 new branches out of 50 planned for the next 4-5 months.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total deposits reached INR 16,184 crore, with granular retail deposits comprising 84.5%.
- Variable rate borrowings from NABARD stand at INR 1,200 crore (repriced to 7.5% due to rate cuts).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Potential lagged impact of drought conditions in districts across Maharashtra (e.g., Marathwada) and Karnataka, leading management to pause onboarding new NTC customers in those specific drought-hit districts for the fiscal year.
- CGFMU cover for fresh disbursements from April 1, 2026, is awaiting central government notification renewal; management has made provisioning as a prudent measure.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shailesh Kanani (Securities Private Limited) asked about the status of the CGFMU cover for FY27 disbursements and potential P&L impact.** Management (Kanishka Chaudhary / Baskar Babu) responded that while the scheme awaits government notification for FY27 disbursements, the bank has proactively provided ~INR 40-45 crore in H1, so notification will have no fresh P&L impact.
2. **Rajiv Mehta asked about drought exposure in Maharashtra and collection resilience.** Management (Senthil Kumar / Baskar Babu) stated that exposure in affected areas is around INR 1,000–1,100 crore, collections in October remain stable similar to September, but they remain cautious about lagged impacts.
3. **Rajiv Mehta questioned the credit risk of the CLOU (Credit Line on UPI) product.** Management (Sasidhar Vavilala / Baskar Babu) explained it is a charge card product requiring 100% repayment next month (average credit duration 15-20 days), with default rates currently lower than income generated.
4. **Arvind asked why NIM remained flat despite lower cost of funds and higher yields.** Management (Kanishka Chaudhary) explained it was due to a sharp drop in PSLC income (down ~INR 37 crore QoQ) and holding slightly higher liquidity from recent capital/claim inflows.
5. **Rahul Kumar asked about slippages and credit costs.** Management stated slippages ticked up slightly in CV and IF (total slippages INR 122 crore, split roughly 50-50 between IF and secured/digital), and credit costs included a one-off provision of ~INR 6 crore for an FIG account (Star Housing Finance).

### One-line takeaway
Suryoday Small Finance Bank delivered a robust Q2 FY27 with 34.6% advance growth, a sharp drop in Gross NPAs to 2.9% via CGFMU claims and write-offs, and stable profitability, while maintaining a cautious stance on drought-prone regions and awaiting CGFMU scheme renewal for new disbursements.
