# Yes Bank Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-23 · Banks*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong core operating performance and balance sheet milestones, but tempered by margin moderation and the significant Supreme Court judgment on AT-1 bonds.

### Key numbers
- **Net Profit:** Rs 1,080 crore (up 65% YoY)
- **Operating Profit:** Rs 1,678 crore (up 29.4% YoY)
- **Net Interest Income (NII):** Rs 2,822 crore (up 22.7% YoY)
- **Core Fees:** Up 15.5% YoY
- **Return on Assets (RoA):** 0.9% (vs 0.6% in Q2 FY26 and 0.9% in Q1 FY27)
- **Cost-to-Income Ratio:** 64.3% (vs 67.1% in Q2 FY26)
- **Total Assets:** Crossed Rs 5 lakh crore
- **Total Deposits:** Crossed Rs 3.5 lakh crore (up 19.5% YoY end-of-period; up 22.1% quarterly average)
- **Advances:** Exceeded Rs 3 lakh crore (reported growth of 23.7% YoY; 17.6% ex-FCTL)
- **Credit-to-Deposit Ratio (Normalized):** 83.1%
- **CASA Deposits (Average quarterly basis):** Up 15.5% YoY (Current Account up 21.8%, Savings Account up 11.6%)
- **Retail and Branch-led Deposits:** Up 20.9% YoY (constituting 58.8% of total deposits)
- **Net Interest Margin (NIM):** 2.6% (down 12 to 14 bps QoQ)
- **Non-interest Income:** Up 14.4% YoY and 4.7% sequentially
- **Operating Expenses:** Rs 3,026 crore (up 14.2% YoY and 5.1% QoQ)
- **Gross NPA:** 1.2%
- **Net NPA:** 0.2%
- **Gross Slippages:** 1.3% of advances
- **Net Credit Cost:** 0.3% of average assets
- **CET-1 Ratio (Reported):** 13.7% 
- **CET-1 Ratio (Pro forma post AT-1 reinstatement):** ~11.4% (pro forma impact of ~240 bps)
- **Tier 1 Capital Ratio (Pro forma):** ~13.7%
- **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR):** 131.4%
- **FCNR(B) Deposits Mobilized:** Rs 18,575 crore
- **Foreign Currency Term Loans (FCTL via IBU GIFT City):** Rs 15,215 crore
- **RIDF Reduction Expected (H2 FY27):** ~Rs 8,000 crore (Q3: ~Rs 4,500 crore, Q4: ~Rs 3,500 crore)

### Highlights
- Supreme Court upheld the Bombay High Court's judgment setting aside the AT-1 bond write-down of Rs 8,415 crore from March 2020.
- Reinstatement of AT-1 principal has a pro forma impact of ~240 bps on CET-1 ratio as of September 30, 2026.
- Crossed key milestones: Total Assets >Rs 5 lakh crore, Deposits >Rs 3.5 lakh crore, Advances >Rs 3 lakh crore.
- Retail asset disbursements increased 35% YoY; Retail book growth nearing 9%. Commercial Banking Advances grew 20.2%, Corporate and Institutional Banking grew 31.4% YoY.
- Recognized P&L gains of Rs 395 crore from Security Receipts portfolio during the quarter.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Q3 margins to remain in a similar range to Q2 (with a potential marginal negative bias), moving to a positively improving bias in Q4 supported by RIDF reductions, repo rate hikes, and FCNR fund deployment.
- Management guided to maintain the full-year FY27 RoA target of 1%.
- Capital planning remains an ongoing Board-level process; management noted they have sufficient headroom and buffers (including DTA release and stored capital like Security Receipts) and do not face an immediate "gun to our head" to raise capital.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Management noted strong underlying momentum in growth, with the bank outpacing industry growth rates adjusted for FCTL.
- Cross-referrals from the SMBC relationship are showing good traction.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- NIM moderated by 12–14 bps QoQ due to FCNR(B) mobilization and associated liquidity/deployment profile.
- Operating expenses included Rs 51 crore of foreign currency borrowing commissions linked to FCNR(B), Rs 20 crore of Asian Games marketing expenditure, and Rs 31 crore from wage code changes.
- Core operating expenses were stable sequentially.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- *Not discussed.*

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Certificate of Deposits (CDs) increased tactically from ~Rs 6,000 crore to ~Rs 11,000 crore due to rating upgrades and cost economics.
- RIDF balances are expected to run down by ~Rs 8,000 crore in H2 FY27.
- Stored capital: Security Receipts portfolio has a face value of ~Rs 1,500 crore and NAV of ~Rs 2,000 crore with zero carrying value.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Potential pro forma impact of ~240 bps on CET-1 due to the Supreme Court's AT-1 write-down verdict, lowering pro forma CET-1 to ~11.4%.
- Margin pressure from competitive pricing and liquidity conditions.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Jai Prakash Mundhra (ICICI Securities):** Asked if AT-1 reversal has cash outflow or P&L impact. 
   - *Management reply:* In substance, it is a Balance Sheet transfer from reserves to borrowings with no cash outflow on borrowings; pro forma impact was routed through P&L/reserves as an indicative assessment.
2. **Mayank Bukrediwala (MLP):** Asked about prospective coupon payments on restored AT-1 bonds.
   - *Management reply:* Future coupons and consequential implementations are under active assessment and will be guided by the information memorandum; declined to pre-announce decisions.
3. **Jayant Kharote (Axis Capital):** Asked about the pace of growth support and capital consumption without a capital raise.
   - *Management reply:* Stated that with an 8.2% ROE and DTA benefits, the bank has the ability to grow at ~11% without consuming capital, and even at 15% growth, consumption would be ~30–40 bps.
4. **Mahrukh Adajania (Tara Capital):** Asked about the fundamental implications of missing coupon payments.
   - *Management reply:* Declined to comment beyond stating that the bank is evaluating the judgment and will abide by the terms of the information memorandum.
5. **Kunal Shah (Citi):** Asked about credit rating agency reactions and legal remedies vs. capital raise priorities.
   - *Management reply:* Rating agencies have viewed the judgment as credit-neutral. Legal reviews and consequential assessments are running separately from commercial capital evaluations, and the bank believes it has adequate buffers for growth in the interim.

### One-line takeaway
Yes Bank reported a strong 65% YoY jump in net profit (Rs 1,080 crore) and robust balance sheet growth, but faces a pro forma 240 bps hit to its CET-1 ratio (down to ~11.4%) following the Supreme Court's verdict setting aside the March 2020 AT-1 write-down.
