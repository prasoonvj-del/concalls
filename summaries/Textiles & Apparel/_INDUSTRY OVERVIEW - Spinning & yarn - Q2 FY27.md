# Textiles & Apparel - Spinning & yarn: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the Indian spinning and yarn sector reflects a generally positive operational environment marked by robust demand, particularly in the export yarn segment driven by China's appetite and U.S. shifts away from Xinjiang cotton. Managements across the board noted capacity utilizations running at high levels (often 90% to nearly 100%). However, profitability was partially constrained by sudden raw material inflation (cotton, polyester, PTA, MEG) stemming from West Asian geopolitical tensions, as well as a sharp spike in global freight costs. While pure-play yarn spinners largely succeeded in passing on raw material cost increases or benefited from inventory trading gains, integrated players and fabric divisions faced temporary margin compressions due to order-booking lags.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus:** Demand from export markets (notably China and Bangladesh) remains robust, and domestic demand recovered strongly post-Q1 deferrals. Management commentary points to structural long-term growth driven by a shift toward man-made fibers (MMF), technical textiles, and upcoming free trade agreements (FTAs) with the U.K. and EU.
* **Divergence:** While export yarn and upstream divisions are operating near full capacity (e.g., GHCL Textiles at ~99%, Sportking at 99%, Sanathan at ~95%), downstream fabric and garment segments experienced mixed demand. Fabric divisions at RSWM and Vardhman faced headwinds due to tariff uncertainties, delayed export shipments, and order-booking lags preventing immediate price pass-through.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Raw Materials:** Cotton prices fused upwards (e.g., GHCL noting rises from Rs 64,000 to Rs 69,000/candy; Vardhman highlighting domestic cotton at Rs 64,000–65,000/candy against a domestic shortfall). Synthetic raw materials like polyester, PTA, and MEG surged by 25% to 30% due to West Asian geopolitical conflicts and crude oil volatility.
* **Freight & Energy:** Sutlej Textiles and Banswara Syntex flagged an exceptionally steep spike in container freight costs (e.g., Turkey routes jumping from USD 2,000 to USD 6,000; Latin America from USD 2,500 to USD 10,000). Conversely, energy costs saw structural relief through aggressive investments in captive renewable solar and biomass power (highlighted by Sangam India, GHCL, Sportking, and Vardhman), which helped buffer margin pressures.
* **Margins:** Pure-play spinners and companies scaling value-added portfolios reported strong margin expansions (e.g., Sportking EBITDA margin at 19.8%, GHCL at 20.6%), whereas diversified players with heavy fabric exposure (like RSWM and Sutlej) reported compressed or flat sequential margins due to the lag in passing on raw material hikes.

## Capex & capacity
Managements are actively deploying capital toward modernization, value-added downstream integration (knitting, garments, technical textiles), and green energy:
* **Sanathan Textiles:** Doubled technical textiles capacity at Silvassa to 18,000 MTPA and advanced its MP cotton yarn project.
* **GHCL Textiles:** On track with knitting expansion and a 58,368-spindle project approved at Rs 410 crore.
* **Sportking India:** Nearing commercial production on its 150,000-spindle Greenfield project in Odisha.
* **Vardhman Textiles:** Executing a 50 tonnes/day open-end spinning project (~Rs 500 crore capex), though noting a delay of 8–9 months in the MP PM MITRA Park land handover.
* **Sangam India & RSWM:** Undertaking large-scale multi-year capex programs in cotton yarn, denim, garmenting, and bottle-to-bottle recycling (LNJ GreenPET).

## What changed since last quarter
* **Guidance Upgrades:** Sanathan Textiles raised its FY27 EBITDA target to north of Rs 560 crore; Siyaram Silk Mills raised its full-year revenue growth guidance to 14%–15%.
* **Tone Shifts:** GHCL Textiles shifted from "steady" to "cautiously optimistic" due to raw material volatility; Sutlej and RSWM acknowledged near-term cost pressures from freight and input spikes.
* **Project Timelines:** Vardhman Textiles noted an 8–9 month delay in the Madhya Pradesh PM MITRA Park land handover. GHCL noted its PM MITRA Park timeline pushed to FY30 due to a consultative approach with SIPCOT.
* **One-offs:** GHCL reported a one-time exceptional inventory loss of Rs 27 crore due to a fire at a rented Tuticorin warehouse (insurance claims in progress). RSWM recorded an exceptional provision of Rs 11.5 crore for electricity duty following an adverse court ruling.

## What to watch next quarter
* Progression of raw material prices (cotton crop arrivals vs. consumption shortfalls, and synthetic input volatility).
* Stabilization and ramp-up of new capacities (e.g., Sportking’s Odisha plant, Sanathan’s Punjab Phase 2, and Vardhman's technical/synthetics fabrics business).
* Resolution of logistics/freight spikes and working capital optimization (particularly debtor days at Sangam India and inventory management at Banswara Syntex).
* Progress on U.K. and EU FTA implementation timelines.

## Company one-liners
* **Banswara Syntex:** Pivoted successfully to value-added MMF products and export garments to drive sequential margin expansion despite raw material inflation.
* **GHCL Textiles:** Delivered stellar H1 growth and a 20.6% EBITDA margin backed by near-full capacity utilization, absorbing a one-time inventory fire loss.
* **RSWM Ltd:** Achieved a 47% YoY jump in Q2 EBITDA through operational efficiencies, even as fabric margins absorbed input cost lags.
* **Sanathan Textiles:** Upgraded full-year targets as its Punjab facility turned PAT positive and technical textiles capacity doubled.
* **Sangam India:** Posted record-breaking quarterly revenue, EBITDA, and PAT fueled by high utilization and expanding captive renewable power.
* **Siyaram Silk Mills:** Raised full-year guidance to 14%–15% growth on steady festive demand and disciplined retail expansion.
* **Sportking India:** Delivered strong 19.8% EBITDA margins while preparing to commission its major 150,000-spindle Odisha plant.
* **Sutlej Textiles:** Sustained its profitable turnaround with structural cost-cutting, navigating freight and raw material turbulence.
* **Vardhman Textiles:** Benefited from robust export yarn demand and trading gains, offsetting tariff-related pressures in its fabric division.
