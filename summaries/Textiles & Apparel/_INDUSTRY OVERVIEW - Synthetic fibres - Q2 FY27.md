# Textiles & Apparel - Synthetic fibres: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across synthetic fibres and textile engineering report a generally resilient Q2 FY27, characterized by strong top-line momentum (e.g., Century Enka up 37% YoY, Lohia Corp up 42% YoY, Sumeet Industries up 35.23% YoY) driven by festive demand, robust domestic traction, and capacity scaling. However, profitability experienced mixed pressures. While Indo Rama and Lohia Corp expanded or maintained strong margins, companies like Century Enka, Filatex, and Sumeet Industries faced margin compression due to crude oil and raw material price volatility, higher energy costs, and cheap import competition. Most players are actively expanding into value-added products (VAPs), recycling, and green energy to insulate margins going forward.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian synthetic fibre and textile machinery players operated asset-heavy, commodity-driven models vulnerable to crude price swings, domestic oversupply, and low-cost Chinese imports, competing primarily on price. Over the past five years, the industry has shifted toward higher-margin value-added products (VAPs), integrated capacities, circular economy and recycling solutions, and green energy sourcing to cut power overheads. **Shifts underway**
- **Shift toward sustainable recycling & circular economy**: Scaling. Filatex started trials for its 27,000 TPA Ecosis depolymerization recycling plant with major brand commitments (Decathlon, A&E, IKEA, H&M) [Filatex India concall Q2 FY27; Filatex Investor deck Nov 2026]. Sumeet Industries acquired Nakoda assets for a 1.4 lakh TPA bottle-grade PET chips plant [Sumeet Industries concall Q2 FY27; Sumeet Investor deck Nov 2026]. Lohia Corp supplied recycling machinery (ReclaMax 120) and expects doubling of recycling revenue [Lohia Corp concall Q2 FY27]. These additions reduce reliance on virgin crude polymers and open premium global markets.
- **Pivot to value-added and specialty products (VAPs)**: Scaling. Century Enka targets VAPs to touch ~50% of its yarn portfolio, backed by upcoming PTCF commercialization [Century Enka concall Q2 FY27]. Indo Rama approved ~Rs 200 crore for specialty filament and fiber expansion at Butibori [Indo Rama Synthetics concall Q2 FY27]. Sumeet Industries added 40 TPD FDY capacity (reaching 160 TPD) and is shifting 50% capacity to value-added yarns [Sumeet Industries concall Q2 FY27; Sumeet Investor deck Nov 2026]. Lohia Corp executed specialized stretching lines for the defense sector [Lohia Corp concall Q2 FY27; Lohia Investor deck Nov 2026]. These shifts drive higher-margin realizations.
- **Green energy integration to lower operating costs**: Scaling. Century Enka expects ~Rs 10 crore annual savings from hybrid power starting Q4 FY27 [Century Enka concall Q2 FY27]. Filatex is investing Rs 30 crore in renewable energy to push renewable share to 55% [Filatex India concall Q2 FY27]. Sumeet Industries is commissioning a 12 MW solar project by December 2026 to cover over 50% of power consumption [Sumeet Industries concall Q2 FY27]. Lower energy costs protect EBITDA margins during utility tariff spikes.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus**: Managements across the board agree that domestic demand remains structurally sound, aided by festive seasons, wedding demand, and long-term tailwinds from international trade agreements (India-UK and India-New Zealand FTAs). Furthermore, domestic PTA capacity additions (such as GAIL's Mangalore plant and IOCL's Paradip complex) are improving domestic raw material self-sufficiency.
- **Divergence**: Views on external pressures and import competition diverged. Century Enka highlighted ongoing margin compression due to an influx of low-priced Chinese filament yarn imports and excess tyre fabric bookings. Conversely, Indo Rama noted that Chinese import pressure has eased due to Chinese currency depreciation (Yuan moving from ~Rs 12 to ~Rs 14.5) and China's gradual exit from commodity businesses. Filatex and Sumeet Industries reported uneven demand spurts caused by crude oil volatility, though Sumeet turned positive with improving wedding season uptake.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Raw Material Volatility**: Crude oil price fluctuations and Middle East developments drove up petrochemical feedstock (caprolactam, polymerized chips, and virgin polymers). Century Enka, Filatex, and Sumeet Industries noted margin compression due to narrowed spreads and time lags in passing price hikes to customers.
- **Energy Pressures**: Elevated LNG prices and higher grid power tariffs increased cost pressures for Century Enka and Filatex, prompting aggressive investments into solar and hybrid captive power projects.
- **Mitigation & Margin Targets**: Indo Rama maintained robust double-digit EBITDA margins (9.9%) by successfully passing on raw material costs and enforcing cost discipline. Lohia Corp maintained healthy EBITDA margins (19.4%) aided by operating leverage despite commodity inflation. Sumeet Industries and Filatex anticipate margin recoveries as crude prices soften.

## Capex & capacity
- **Century Enka**: Advancing Mother Yarn expansion at Pune, VAP capacity at Bharuch, energy-saving capex, and preparing for PTCF commercial sales in Q4 FY27. CWIP increased to ~Rs 38 crore in H1 FY27.
- **Filatex India**: Finalizing a ~Rs 690 crore comprehensive capex program, including Dahej brownfield expansion (67% commissioned, balance by Nov 23), the Ecosis recycling plant (trials started Nov 15), and a steam distribution project.
- **Indo Rama Synthetics**: Board approved ~Rs 200 crore for specialty filament and fiber expansion at Butibori (cationic, dope-dyed, colored yarns, and hollow fiber), expected to commission within CY2027.
- **Lohia Corp**: Executing a ~Rs 75 crore capex plan for maintenance, recycling, conversion, and coating machinery, alongside building new assembly shed capacity.
- **Sumeet Industries**: Completed rights issue (raising net Rs 194.9 crores) to fund working capital (Rs 100 cr), Nakoda asset integration (Rs 85.9 cr, adding 1.4 lakh TPA bottle-grade PET chips by Q2 FY28), a 12 MW solar project (commissioning Dec 2026), and a 40 TPD FDY capacity addition (taking total FDY to 160 TPD).

## What changed since last quarter
- **Filatex India**: Guidance maintained, but project timelines for Ecosis and steam projects slipped by a few weeks due to logistical disruptions (West Asian shipping issues), turbine delivery delays, and skilled labor shortages during peak installation. Tone shifted slightly more cautious regarding crude volatility, while Ecosis trials officially commenced and H&M granted global supplier approval.
- **Lohia Corp**: Tone shifted from "strong" to "heavy traction" in domestic markets. Secured its first order from South Korea and executed specialized defense stretching lines.
- **Sumeet Industries**: Tone shifted from cautious to positive as crude prices moderated and wedding season demand picked up. Successfully deployed rights issue proceeds, reduced debt, and scaled sales volumes to ~29,000 tons in Q2.
