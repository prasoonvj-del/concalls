# Gokaldas Exports Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-19 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong broad-based revenue growth across geographies and customers despite macroeconomic and logistics headwinds.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated income growth: 21% YoY
- Consolidated EBITDA growth: 17% YoY
- India business revenue growth: 16% YoY
- India EBITDA growth: 14% YoY
- Africa business revenue growth: 45% YoY
- BTPL fabric sales: ~Rs 170 crore for the quarter; operating EBITDA at 7.5% to 8% negative; capacity at ~50 lakh meters/month
- India salary/wage cost increase in Q1: Rs 20 crore
- U.K. revenue contribution: 4% to 4.5%
- Africa revenue target for FY27: $112 million to $115 million (pushing for $120 million)

### Highlights
- Growth was broad-based across geographies and customer base, not driven by a single customer or market.
- India growth supported by transition to lower tariff regime under Section 122 (following wind down of IEEPA reciprocal tariff), outperforming Indian apparel exports as a whole (which declined 12% YoY).
- Africa exceptional growth supported by the renewal of AGOA and sustained multi-year investments.
- BTPL merger process is on track to conclude in Q3 FY27; unit operating at ~50 lakh meters/month, expected to grow by 30% in near future. BTPL targeted to reach mid-single-digit positive EBITDA by Q3 and PBT positive by Q4.
- Onboarded 1 new customer during the quarter, with operations commencing in Q2.
- Capacity additions: Bhopal second unit ramping up (high/near full capacity utilization expected by Q4); new capacities planned in Jharkhand and Karnataka (INR 100 crore total investment for both, adding ~3.5 million pieces each / INR 175 crore revenue each at steady state).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue traction to continue at the pace of Q1 for upcoming quarters.
- Management expects consolidated EBITDA margins to be higher than current levels going forward.
- Africa revenue expected around $112 million to $115 million, with a push towards $120 million.
- ETR estimated between 20% and 22% for the full year.
- BTPL expected to generate mid- to high single-digit EBITDA margin by the end of the calendar year upon merger.

### Demand & industry commentary
- End consumer demand in U.S. and U.K. recorded strong growth in first 6 months of CY26, though U.S. pace softened in June.
- Apparel imports into EU and U.K. declined steadily for first 5 months of FY26 due to inventory destocking and lower-value mix shift.
- U.S. Section 301 tariff places India on equal footing with competitors while preserving advantage over China and Vietnam (subject to 12.5% tariff).
- India-U.K. FTA brings India to parity with Bangladesh and Vietnam, with duty advantage over China.
- Inquiries from Europe and U.S. increasing; broadening interest into shirts and bottoms (traditional Bangladesh categories) in low-cost Indian regions.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Minimum wage increases: Haryana saw 35% increase (overall company wage went up 14%–15% due to higher baseline and efficiencies); Karnataka saw 5% CPI-linked DA increase.
- Utility/chemical costs and logistics (inbound/outbound freight) impacted other expenses, taking a hit of ~Rs 5–6 crore in Q1.
- Hedging: 70% of dollars hedged via forward covers (e.g., Q1 forward rate ~INR 89), with remaining 30% naturally hedged via imports.
- RoSCTL currently around 3.5%; management notes worst-case cut to half could be offset by operational performance.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total capex of INR 70–75 crore estimated for current financial year out of INR 100 crore total for new units in Jharkhand and Karnataka, expected to come on stream in H2 FY28 and realize full potential in FY29 (incremental revenue potential of ~INR 350 crores together).
- Bhopal Phase 2 facility ramping up to reach near full capacity by Q4.
- Planning to add ~3,000 machines by end of year, yielding revenue in FY29 and beyond (every 1,000 machines brings ~INR 200 crore incremental revenue).
- Africa capacity is currently running at ~80%–85% utilization; management plans to utilize existing facilities and second shifts before fresh capex.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital impacted by long shipping lead times and container delays holding up finished goods at ports before boarding.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Shipping disruptions: Strait of Hormuz/Houthi Red Sea routing issues and Chinese typhoons causing container constraints and 2-week outbound shipping delays.
- AGOA expiration in December (though bipartisan Senate support passed for a 2-year extension, awaiting House and Presidential consent).
- Inflationary pressures in the U.S. market affecting retail demand.
- Potential policy changes to RoSCTL incentives.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Soham Samanta (Motilal Oswal) on India volume growth vs realization:** Management clarified that Q1/Q2 feature high-value outerwear products with high minutes content (200–250 mins) rather than simpler garments like shirts (22–25 mins), making absolute volume a misnomer.
2. **Aashish Upganlawar (InvesQ PMS) on margin impact if Indian incentives (RoSCTL) are reduced:** Management stated that even if RoSCTL were halved to ~1.75%, operational efficiencies, product mix, weaker rupee cushion, and BTPL merger would help absorb the impact and yield higher margins.
3. **Vishal Mehta (IIFL Capital) on minimum wage inflation and other expenses impact:** Management quantified Haryana wage hike at 35% (overall wage cost up 14%–15%) and noted a Rs 5–6 crore hit in other expenses from higher gas, fuel, chemical, and logistics costs.
4. **Shirish Pardeshi (Motilal Oswal) on Africa utilization and AGOA clarity:** Management noted Africa is running at a $30 million quarterly run rate ($112–$115 million annually) and expects robust H2 order traction regardless of AGOA due to strong cost economics, alongside expectations of the AGOA extension passing.
5. **Prerna Jhunjhunwala (Elara Securities) on Jharkhand and Karnataka capex details:** Management clarified that the Jharkhand and Karnataka units involve a total investment of INR 100 crore (INR 70–75 crore in FY27) and will add ~3.5 million pieces each (INR 350 crore combined revenue at steady state in FY29).

### One-line takeaway
Gokaldas Exports delivered a robust Q1 with 21% revenue growth, navigating wage and logistics headwinds through operational efficiencies while expanding capacities in low-cost regions and anticipating margin accretion from the upcoming BTPL merger.
