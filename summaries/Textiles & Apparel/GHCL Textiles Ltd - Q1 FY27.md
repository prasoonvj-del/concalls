# GHCL Textiles Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-05 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Robust financial performance driven by timely cotton procurement, steady demand, and forward integration into fabric.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs. 410 crore, up 52% YoY
- EBITDA: Rs. 70 crore
- PAT: Rs. 39 crore
- Fabric sales share: 16% of total sales (up from 9% YoY)
- Cotton spread: Rs. 155 per kg (including packing cost of Rs. 5), compared to Rs. 138 per kg in Q4 FY26
- Spinning capacity utilisation: 98%+
- Knitting capacity utilisation: 80%–85% for the 15 installed machines
- Renewable power capacity: 65 MW operational; 11 MW ground solar project in pipeline
- Planned Capex: Rs. 350 crore to Rs. 400 crore for ready-to-cut fabric production facilities; total capex for the year including modernisation is estimated at Rs. 100 crore to Rs. 120 crore

### Highlights
- First 50 knitting machines installed with commercial production stabilised; remaining 25 knitting machines to be delivered in phases and completed by the end of Q3 FY27 (bringing total to 40 machines).
- Roof-top solar project of 3 MW implemented in January; ground solar project of 11 MW delayed due to MNRE/ALMM changes, now expected to commission in December.
- Land allotment under PM MITRA Park in Tamil Nadu completed; project execution aligned with government completion target of December '27 and company policy of 3 years from allotment.
- ROCE for the quarter improved to approximately 12%.
- Management announced an ESOP scheme for executives and management, subject to shareholder approval.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects spreads for Q2 to be slightly lower than Q1 but overall spreads for the year to be better than last year.
- Normalised EBITDA margin is guided at 14%–15%, which can expand to 16%–18% upon full transition to a ready-to-cut fabric supplier.
- Revenue target: Aiming to double revenue from the base of Rs. 1,000 crore to Rs. 2,000 crore by FY29.
- Growth rate: Management expects to maintain a similar growth rate to last year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand environment is steady with higher prices being accepted with a lag.
- Domestic cotton prices fluctuated, moving from Rs. 62,000 per candy in April '26 to Rs. 67,000 in May '26, dropping to Rs. 64,000 in June '26, and later rising to Rs. 68,000–Rs. 69,000 per candy. New York cotton futures reached 80 cents per pound at the end of June '26.
- Export demand from China remains high due to lower domestic cotton output in China and US bans on certain Asian cotton yarns.
- Indian domestic market is growing at 6% to 8% YoY.
- FTAs (India-UK, upcoming India-Europe agreement) and potential US tariff policy changes are seen as major structural demand tailwinds.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cotton inventory is well-covered until the beginning of the new season (November–December).
- Inventory gain contributed about 10%–12% to the sales price increase.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total spinning capacity additions completed previously (65,000 spindles across two new units); current focus is on forward integration into ready-to-cut fabric facilities via a Rs. 350 crore to Rs. 400 crore capex plan.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Balance sheet remains strong with healthy cash generation. Capital subsidies from the Government of Tamil Nadu (approx. Rs. 100 crore to Rs. 125 crore on total investments of around Rs. 1,000 crore) are expected.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- US-Iran conflict remains a source of uncertainty, driving raw material volatility across cotton and synthetic fibers and causing order execution delays at elevated price levels.
- Higher cotton inventory cost starting to flow in as lower-cost stock is consumed; risk of whether higher cotton prices (at Rs. 68,000–70,000 per candy) will continue to be absorbed by yarn markets.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Resham Jain (VVD Asset Managers) on China demand and PM MITRA timeline:** Asked about the surge in Chinese demand and PM MITRA Park execution. Management stated China's demand is driven by lower domestic acreage/output and US bans, while PM MITRA Park is targeted for completion by December '27 by the government, with the company planning its facility commissioning in CY '28.
2. **Saransh Gupta (SVAN Investments) on inventory and spreads:** Inquired about cotton inventory cover and current spreads. Management clarified inventory covers the season up to November–December, and current spreads stand at ~Rs. 155 per kg (with packing).
3. **Deepali Kumari (Arihant Capital) on domestic cotton deficit and export competitiveness:** Asked if declining Indian cotton production is a structural disadvantage. Management replied that government missions on cotton productivity target a return to 400 lakh bales, and Indian cotton is currently at parity with global prices, maintaining export competitiveness.
4. **Saket Kapoor (Capoor & Co.) on normalised margins:** Asked if Q1 EBITDA margin of 17% is sustainable. Management clarified that normalised margins are 14%–15%, and inventory gains seen in Q1 will moderate, though vertical integration into ready-to-cut fabrics will target 16%–18% EBITDA margins long-term.

### One-line takeaway
GHCL Textiles delivered a robust Q1 FY27 with a 52% YoY revenue jump and 17% EBITDA margin, supported by favourable cotton inventory positioning, strong export/domestic demand, and steady progress in its forward-integration strategy into fabric manufacturing.
