# Vardhman Textiles Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-10 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Yarn business improved on better demand, pricing, and trading gains, while the fabric business faced challenges from tariff uncertainties and high input costs.

### Key numbers
- Yarn input costs (fabric segment): Higher by ~20% YoY.
- Fabric capacity utilisation: ~70%–75% in Q2, currently at ~80%.
- Garment capacity: ~7,000 shirts per day (~Rs 150 crore top line).
- New York futures: USD 0.80–USD 0.82 per pound currently; projected to be USD 0.78–USD 0.80 for Dec 2027.
- Landed cotton cost (India): INR 64,000–INR 65,000 per candy currently (up from INR 54,000–INR 55,000 previously).
- Indian cotton crop (CAI survey): 28 million–29 million bales (against consumption of 35 million–36 million bales).
- India's total yarn export to Bangladesh: ~110 million kg per month (30%–35% of total Indian exports).

### Highlights
- Overall performance improved QoQ and YoY, led by the yarn business which benefited from better export demand, higher yarn prices, raw material cost pass-through, and trading gains from cotton inventory positions.
- Modernization initiatives from last year and green power/renewable energy initiatives began reflecting positively in results.
- **Record check:** Management stated that the PM MITRA Park land handover in Madhya Pradesh is delayed by 8–9 months (previously expected Nov/Dec), breaking the earlier timeline.
- Fabric business faced a challenging quarter due to lower export volumes amid tariff uncertainties, domestic pricing pressure, and 20% YoY higher yarn input costs.
- Synthetics/performance fabrics business (commissioned in March) is focused on sampling, customer approvals, audits, and stabilization; expects to turn operating positive in H2 FY2, with utilization reaching 35%–40% and break-even expected at 50%.
- Garment business is doubling capacity at the existing location as a first step (moving from 7,000 shirts/day, targeting 13%–14% EBITDA margin).

### Guidance & outlook
- **EBITDA Margin Guidance:** Long-term guidance range given as 16%–20%.
- **Revenue Growth Guidance:** Long-term CAGR expected at 5%–8%.
- **Spinning Capex:** One open-end project of 50 tonnes/day under execution (operational in 6–7 months, ~Rs 500 crore capex); no other major spinning capex announced for the next two years. A new plant of 100,000 spindles requires >Rs 1,000 crore to Rs 1,200 crore.
- **Fabric/Synthetics Outlook:** Expects utilization to improve through H2 and move toward higher value-added products. Synthetics business expected to turn operating positive in H2.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Yarn demand has improved over the last 6 months, supported by strong export demand (particularly from China, which continues buying 20 million–25 million kg/month from India due to US bans on Xinjiang cotton and high local prices).
- India's rated spinning capacity has structurally dropped from 52–53 million spindles to ~40 million spindles as ~12–13 million spindles permanently exited the system over the last three years, balancing supply.
- Bangladesh labor wage protests occur every 2–4 years; current demands are for BDT 30,000 minimum wage, but expected to settle around BDT 17,000–18,000. Overall demand from Bangladesh remains unaffected.
- Fabric export volumes were impacted due to tariff uncertainties causing customers to delay or postpone orders by 1–2 months, missing the development cycle. Order pipeline is now improving following US election results.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cotton prices surged due to New York futures rising from USD 0.64–0.65 to USD 0.75–0.80+ per pound, and rupee devaluation from crude oil increases (Iran war).
- Indian raw cotton prices increased from INR 54,000–55,000 a candy to INR 64,000–65,000 a candy. Narma (raw cotton) is trading around INR 9,000 per quintal (above MSP).
- India faces a cotton shortfall of 6 million–8 million bales (crop of 28M–29M bales vs consumption of 35M–36M bales).
- Government allowed duty-free import of cotton for two months, but management stated the 2-month window is too short for shipments from Brazil/Australia (taking 35–50 days) to arrive before the December 31 deadline.
- Renewable power and biomass boiler initiatives are expected to reduce power consumption costs by INR 1 to INR 1.5 per unit, with the remaining 50% of projects operational in 3–4 months.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Spinning capacity utilization is running at nearly 100%.
- Open-end spinning project (50 tonnes/day) operational in 6–7 months (~Rs 500 crore).
- General modernization and balancing capex runs at INR 400 crore–INR 500 crore annually.
- Madhya Pradesh PM MITRA Park land handover delayed by 8–9 months; major future greenfield expansion will be planned there once land is handed over.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Financial structuring remains under-leveraged with practically zero net debt (debt-equity is near zero).
- Capital employed to turnover is less than 1:1.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Government policy uncertainty on raw material pricing and lack of long-term duty-free cotton import provisions hinder aggressive large-scale spinning investments.
- Fabric business faced margin compression as 20% higher yarn input costs could not be fully passed on to existing retail product lines immediately.
- New technical textiles/synthetics business is currently incurring learning curves and initial operating losses during stabilization.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Soham Samanta (Motilal Oswal):** Asked about FY28/29 yarn capex plans. *Management replied no major spinning capex announced beyond the 50 tonnes/day open-end project; greenfield 100,000 spindle plant requires >Rs 1,000 crore and takes 12–14 months.*
2. **Aradhana Jain (360 ONE Capital):** Inquired about long-term revenue growth, steady-state EBITDA margins, and ROCE profile. *Management guided to 16%–20% EBITDA margins, 5%–8% revenue CAGR, and noted that productive deployment of accumulated cash/balance sheet through growth will improve ROCE.*
3. **Amit Kumar (Determinant Industries):** Questioned why Vardhman does not pursue inorganic M&A to scale its ~6.5% market share. *Management explained that available smaller companies are in poor condition with subpar machine parks or cultures, making organic growth via large-scale greenfields preferable.*
4. **Sandeep Garg (Retail Investor):** Asked about renewable energy and modernization cost savings. *Management stated green power and biomass initiatives will yield cost reductions of INR 1 to INR 1.5 per unit, with the remaining 50% of benefits reflecting in 3–4 months.*

### One-line takeaway
Vardhman Textiles delivered an improved Q2 performance driven by a demand-led recovery in the yarn business and trading gains, even as the fabric segment contended with tariff-related export delays and higher input costs.
