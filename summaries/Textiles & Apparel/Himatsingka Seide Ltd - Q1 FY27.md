# Himatsingka Seide Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-17 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Revenue and margins faced headwinds from geopolitical issues, raw material inflation, and U.S. concentration/tariff challenges, prompting a strategic business model transition.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated total income: INR 634 crore (vs INR 661 crore YoY)
- Consolidated EBITDA: INR 101 crore
- EBITDA margin: ~16%
- Capacity utilization: Overall facilities at 99%, Sheeting division at 52%, Terry division at 63%
- Gross leverage / Total debt: Range-bound at ~INR 2,550 crore
- Fabric Solutions & knitting processing capacity (by end of year): ~90 million meters
- Spindle capacity (Yarn Solutions): 211,584 spindles
- Potential peak full-capacity revenue target for Yarn Solutions: ~INR 1,000 crore
- Potential peak full-capacity revenue target for Fabric Solutions: ~INR 1,000 crore

### Highlights
- Company is transitioning its business model to add 3 new product verticals using existing infrastructure: Yarn Solutions, Fabric Solutions, and Apparel Solutions (in addition to existing Home Textile Solutions).
- Aiming to steer away from U.S. and client concentration, diversify jurisdictions, and broaden domestic presence.
- Yarn Solutions and Fabric Solutions businesses have commenced and are currently ramping up; Apparel Solutions will kick in during Phase 2 (a couple of quarters down the line).
- Home Textiles vertical (specifically Sheeting) is undergoing a rightsizing exercise due to market share and pricing challenges in the U.S.
- Raised NCDs to balance debt tenors and maturity profiles, with management expecting a reduction in net debt by the end of the fiscal.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects short-term volatility in numbers over the next couple of quarters during the transition.
- Full-capacity revenue potential is estimated at ~INR 1,000 crore each for Yarn Solutions and Fabric Solutions.
- Management expects India to likely become one of the top 2 jurisdictions for the company in the medium term due to domestic consumption.
- Net debt is expected to reduce by the end of the fiscal.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Geopolitical issues in the Middle East led to shipment deferrals during the quarter.
- U.S. demand saw policy flip-flops, uncertainty, and an inflationary environment impacting product offtake and pricing, though tariffs appear stabilized at current levels.
- FTAs with the UK and EU are expected to offer medium-term opportunities (UK piece kicking in; EU expected in the short term, though currently still under process/discussion).
- Home textile space facing pricing challenges, especially on the Sheeting front, whereas new verticals are expected to offer better pricing power and larger total addressable markets.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margins impacted by slightly lower revenues, product mix tweaks, and inflationary headwinds on the raw material front.
- Capex is limited to maintenance and organic requirements using existing campus/infrastructure (no major new capex for the transition).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- No incremental capex; transition utilizes existing assets and campus infrastructure.
- Sheeting and knitting fabrics processing capacity combined will stand at ~90 million meters by the end of the year.
- Yarn plant consists of 211,584 spindles (>90% of capacity to be placed externally).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total leverage/debt remained range-bound at ~INR 2,550 crore for the quarter.
- NCDs raised for debt/tenor balancing, not additional capital.
- Initiatives underway to strengthen equity; net debt anticipated to reduce by fiscal end.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical issues in the Middle East causing shipment deferrals.
- U.S. market concentration risks, tariff-induced inflation, and pricing challenges in the Sheeting segment.
- Short-term financial volatility expected during the multi-vertical business model transition.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sanjay (Musa Enterprises) asked about positives and negatives of the quarter.** Management stated negatives were revenue overhangs and offtake offtakes, while the positive was initiating the asset-light transition into new verticals to escape restrictive Home Textile concentration.
2. **Nirav Shah (Mesh Stock Brokers) asked about fundraising and U.S. tariffs.** Management clarified that NCD fundraising was solely for debt/tenor balancing, and U.S. tariffs have created inflationary demand alterations but have now stabilized.
3. **Prerna Jhunjhunwala (Elara Securities) asked if any further investment is needed for the 90 million meters capacity.** Management stated no additional capex is required as common infrastructure and existing assets will be shared between Home Textile and Fabric Solutions.
4. **Prerna Jhunjhunwala asked how much yarn capacity will be sold externally.** Management stated over 90% of yarn capacity will be placed externally to let the business chart its own growth journey.
5. **Rajeev Mashweri (Raj Investment) asked about revenue offsets for the rightsized Sheeting division and potential revenue additions from Yarn/Fabric.** Management stated they aim to keep overall revenues range-bound, targeting ~INR 1,000 crore each at full capacity for Yarn and Fabric Solutions.

### One-line takeaway
Himatsingka Seide is leveraging its existing infrastructure to transition away from U.S. and Home Textile concentration into Yarn, Fabric, and Apparel verticals, expecting near-term volatility followed by structural growth.
