# Mallcom (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-18 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management noted that the worst is behind them, citing improving export order books, strong domestic momentum, and improved profitability supported by lower material costs and better cost management.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (targeting ~Rs. 600 crore top-line for FY27, same as previous expectations).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from cautious to more optimistic, with management stating that export order booking is getting better and healthier, logistics issues are easing, and the worst of the European downturn is behind them.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Third phase of Chandipur construction added as a new operational highlight; West Asia crisis and seaport congestions were less prominent, with logistics improving.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Sanand plant delivered Rs. 13 crore in H1 and remains on track for the Rs. 40 crore yearly target (with Rs. 25 crore targeted for H2).

### Key numbers
- Operating revenue: Rs. 143 crore (up 3% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs. 15 crore (up 54% YoY)
- EBITDA margin: 10.61% (expanded 350 bps YoY)
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs. 8 crore (up 108% YoY)
- PAT margin: 5.37% (expanded 271 bps YoY)
- Domestic revenue: Rs. 69 crore (up 23% YoY)
- International revenue: Rs. 74 crore
- H1 Operating revenue: Rs. 253 crore (down 3% YoY)
- H1 EBITDA: Rs. 29 crore (up 5% YoY)
- H1 EBITDA margin: 11.43% (expanded 93 bps YoY)
- H1 PAT: Rs. 14 crore (up 5% YoY)
- H1 PAT margin: 5.66% (expanded 47 bps YoY)
- H1 Domestic revenue: Rs. 130 crore (up 18% YoY)
- H1 International revenue: Rs. 123 crore
- Sanand H1 turnover: Rs. 13 crore
- Gross debt: Rs. 145 crore (short-term debt of Rs. 110 crore, long-term debt of Rs. 35 crore)
- Cash and cash equivalents: Rs. 40 crore
- Net debt: Rs. 105 crore
- FY27 capex guidance: Rs. 20 crore

### Highlights
- Commenced the third phase of construction at the Chandipur facility in West Bengal, adding ~50,000 square feet of floor area for garments and footwear operations.
- Received the Eastern India Best Employer Award 2026.
- Achieved a domestic revenue growth of 23% YoY to Rs. 69 crore.
- First company to receive a BIS license for PU Dip Gloves.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains the full-year top-line target of around Rs. 600 crore, expecting single-digit to double-digit growth over the previous year.
- Management targets a minimum of Rs. 25 crore revenue from the Sanand facility in H2, aiming for Rs. 40 crore for the full year.
- Management expects export momentum to pick up in H2 with better order bookings and potential India-EU free trade agreement.
- Current manufacturing infrastructure provides visibility to support revenues of ~Rs. 1,000 crore without proportionate incremental capital investment (management confirmed this long-term view).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Europe experienced a massive economic downturn and low demand due to war and price pressures, impacting export growth, but order booking is now improving.
- US tariff issues are becoming more stable.
- Logistics issues (supplier delays, container space availability, port congestion) impacted Q1 and Q2, but the situation is improving for Q3.
- OEM business takes a 3 to 5-year timeframe to break into new customers or supply chains.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margin improvement supported by lower raw material costs, better unit sales realizations, and savings in employee manufacturing, operational, and administrative expenses.
- Sequential EBITDA margins were impacted by higher depreciation from recent land, building, and plant machinery additions.
- Raw material prices have smoothened and become more predictable; petro-based product costs expected to improve, with customer price revisions slated for coming periods.
- Export pricing has a time lag as prices are typically fixed for a year.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned capex of ~Rs. 20 crore for FY27 to be funded through internal accruals, with further investments in H1 FY28.
- Sanand facility expansion planned to add 3 more lines (doubling or increasing 1.5x) alongside the Chandipur factory floor addition.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gross debt stands at Rs. 145 crore and cash at Rs. 40 crore, resulting in a net debt of Rs. 105 crore. Management expects borrowings to decline further.
- Capex is funded entirely through internal accruals.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Continued economic downturn and low demand in European markets.
- Time lag in passing on raw material price increases to white-label/export customers due to fixed long-term contracts.
- Higher depreciation burden from recent asset additions impacting sequential margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Aditya (Securities Investment Management) on export growth and recovery:** Management explained that Europe's downturn drove the decline, but order bookings are improving, and the worst is behind them.
- **Aditya on raw material trends and margin recovery to 13%-14%:** Management stated that reaching historical margins will take time due to higher depreciation from new asset additions, despite smoothing raw material prices.
- **Sagar Parekh (Renaissance Asset Managers) on FY27 top-line guidance:** Management maintained the target of ~Rs. 600 crore, noting H3 and H4 need to be stronger to achieve it.
- **Sagar Parekh on gross/net debt levels:** Management detailed gross debt of Rs. 145 crore and cash of Rs. 40 crore, confirming capex will be funded via internal accruals without additional borrowing.
- **Nikhil Upadhyay (SiMPL) on export flatness and justification for capex:** Management clarified that OEM business cycles span 3-5 years, and confidence is driven by potential India-EU trade deals, new product categories, and rising demand in Africa, Middle East, and Australia.

### One-line takeaway
Mallcom posted a 54% YoY jump in EBITDA and strong domestic growth in Q2, offsetting export headwinds, while management maintained its full-year guidance and capacity expansion plans.
