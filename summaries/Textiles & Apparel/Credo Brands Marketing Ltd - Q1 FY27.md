# Credo Brands Marketing Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-18 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management cited soft discretionary spending, intense competition, and uneven near-term demand visibility, while executing long-term brand transformation.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs 125.3 crore (up 5% YoY from approximately Rs 125 crore)
- Gross Profit: Rs 77.2 crore (up 5% YoY); Gross Margin: 61.6% (or 62%)
- EBITDA: Rs 26.6 crore (compared to ~Rs 31 crore YoY); EBITDA Margin: 21.2%
- PAT: Rs 2.3 crore; PAT Margin: 1.8%
- Total Store Count: 427 stores (5 new stores opened, 7 underperforming stores closed)
- Marketing Investment: ~8.5% of revenue
- Average annual revenue per EBO: Rs 75 lakh (FY26 figure)
- Inventory Days: 74 days

### Highlights
- Advanced "Mufti 2.0" strategy focusing on brand premiumization, elevating customer experience, and retail transformation.
- Opened 5 new stores in leading malls/high streets and closed 7 underperforming stores to improve network quality and output per store.
- Increased marketing investments (~8.5% of revenue) directed toward digital platforms (Google, Meta) and D2C business to build long-term brand salience and aspiration.
- Maintained a clean historical track record with zero inventory write-offs.

### Guidance & outlook
- Full-year marketing investment guidance maintained at 8% to 10% through FY27.
- Aiming for mid-single-digit same-store revenue growth for the year.
- Management refrained from giving specific financial growth projections or numerical targets for the next 2 years due to uneven near-term demand visibility and ongoing transformation.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Consumer interest was healthy through April and part of May, moderating towards mid-May.
- Discretionary spending remains soft alongside global uncertainties and geopolitical tensions keeping consumers cautious.
- Competition remains intense.
- Premiumization is occurring across India, including Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets, though executed relative to each local market environment.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA declined YoY primarily due to higher investments in advertising, brand building, and retail transformation.
- Inventory is being sharpened via merchandise mix changes rather than expanding the overall inventory base.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Store network optimized through strategic relocations and closures rather than aggressive net store additions during the consolidation/transformation phase.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory days stood at 74 for the quarter; management endeavours to reduce inventory days going forward, noting seasonal cyclicality.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Muted demand conditions and soft discretionary consumer spend.
- Intense market competition.
- Uneven near-term demand visibility.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sakshi Pratap (Pratap Securities)** asked about early signs of improvement in discretionary consumption and 12-18 month milestones for Mufti 2.0. Management replied that while renovated stores show positive signals, it is too early to extrapolate, and transformation is a long-drawn process that won't immediately translate to visible numbers in a couple of quarters.
2. **Jay Jain (JJ Capital)** asked if premiumization works in Tier 2/3 cities and if current investments target retention or new customer acquisition. Management stated premiumization is relative and happening across all tiers, and investments target both customer retention and new footfalls.
3. **Jay Kothari** asked about mature revenue potential for new premium format stores and working capital impact from inventory. Management stated they aim for mid-single-digit same-store revenue growth this year and clarified that inventory won't be larger, just a change in merchandise mix.
4. **Rishabh (Analyst/Feedback)** suggested using Hindi advertisements, user-interaction campaigns, exploring new unrepresented markets, and expanding into the women's category. Management noted the suggestions and reiterated their current focus on brand transformation and consolidation rather than aggressive store expansion or new category entry (women's).
5. **Nilesh Doshi** questioned whether higher ad spend is translating proportionately to revenue growth and asked if the company is in "surviving mode." Management defended the ad spend as a necessary long-term investment for brand salience amid intense competition and stated they are in a transformation stage to stay relevant and influential.

### One-line takeaway
Credo Brands reported muted Q1 FY27 profitability (PAT of Rs 2.3 crore) as higher advertising and retail transformation spends for its Mufti 2.0 strategy offset a 5% revenue increase to Rs 125.3 crore.
