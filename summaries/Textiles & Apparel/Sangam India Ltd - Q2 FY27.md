# Sangam India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue crossed INR1,000 crores for the first time, with EBITDA and PAT reaching the highest ever reported in the company's history.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** INR1,045 crores (up 33% YoY)
- **H1 Revenue:** INR1,906 crores
- **EBITDA:** INR127 crores
- **PAT:** INR53 crores
- **H1 PAT:** INR94 crores
- **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE):** ~13%
- **Return on Equity (ROE):** ~14%
- **Net Debt to EBITDA:** 3.25x
- **Net Debt-to-Equity:** 1.15
- **Debtor Days:** 77 days in September (up from 67 days in March)
- **Long-term Credit Rating:** Upgraded by India Ratings to IND A with a stable outlook in October

### Highlights
- Best quarter in Sangam's history with record revenue, EBITDA, and PAT.
- Fifth consecutive quarter of sequential improvement in EBITDA and PAT.
- Government tenders contributed close to INR200 crores in H1.
- Promoters infused INR25 crores (25% of warrants paid) out of the planned INR100 crores equity infusion.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident of beating or at least doubling PAT from last year (FY27).
- Operating cash flow expected to be positive by March.
- Debtor days aspired to be brought back to March levels in the second half of the year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Plants are running close to full capacity.
- Order visibility across divisions is roughly 45 days to 60 days.
- Bangladesh and Vietnam viewed as markets for yarn and denim rather than direct competition in garmenting.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Energy is the biggest cost; captive renewable power met ~35% of needs (up from 10% last year), expected to reach ~50% by the end of the financial year and stabilize at ~70% in Q1 of next year.
- Uncommissioned or newly commissioned renewable capacities expected to yield another INR50 crores to INR55 crores of EBITDA on a full-year basis.
- Pass-through of raw material prices is much easier in yarn than in denim fabric.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Fresh investments started since plants are running near full capacity; total capex plan of INR1,500 crores undertaken for the next 2 to 3 years.
- Cotton yarn capacity addition planned at 60% to 70% in quantity terms, with 40% to 50% of new capacity to be used captively for in-house denim fabrics.
- Garmenting capacity utilization expected to be between 50% to 60% for the full year (was 37% in Q2).
- Long-term debt expected to increase up to INR1,500 crores at the end of the capex cycle before gradually declining.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debtor days increased from 67 days in March to 77 days in September due to business growth, government tenders (longer payment cycles), and delayed export shipments.
- Long-term debt stands closer to INR1,000 crores; capex funded roughly 70% to 75% by debt.
- Target comfortable net debt-to-EBITDA in the long run around 3x to 3.5x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Export shipments are taking longer to reach customers.
- Longer payment cycles in government institutional tenders impact working capital and elevate debtor days.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Raj Shah (Fident Asset Management) asked about garmenting utilization and margins.** Management stated full-year utilization will be 50%-60% and division margins are broadly aligned, with garmenting slightly higher.
- **Saransh Gupta asked about the PAT doubling guidance.** Management reiterated confidence in beating or at least doubling last year's PAT.
- **Kamal Jeswani asked about debt levels and negative operating cash flow.** Management attributed negative cash flow to higher receivables from exports and government tenders, and expects positive operating cash flow by March.
- **Deepali Kumari asked about cotton inventory and hedging.** Management stated stocks are at seasonal lows at the start of the new cycle (peaking in March), and they do not hedge cotton prices.
- **Rohit asked about asset turnover and subsidies for the cotton yarn capex.** Management stated asset turnover is not the right metric due to captive use and modernization, and EBITDA numbers exclude subsidies.

### One-line takeaway
Sangam India delivered its highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA, and PAT in Q2 FY27, backed by high capacity utilization and expanding renewable power, while managing working capital pressures from growing government and export business.
