# Filatex India Ltd-$ - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-06 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management cited resilient financial performance, stable demand, and improved profitability despite geopolitical uncertainties and volatile raw materials.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 1,145 crore (up 16.3% QoQ from Rs 985 crore in Q4 FY26; up 9.1% YoY from Rs 1,049 crore).
- **Sales Volume:** 89,872 tonnes (vs 89,841 tonnes in Q4 FY26; 97,263 tonnes in Q1 FY26).
- **Production:** 84,075 tonnes (vs 94,996 tonnes in Q1 FY26).
- **PBT:** Rs 65.87 crore (vs Rs 53.47 crore in Q4 FY26; Rs 54.89 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **PAT:** Rs 49.1 crore (up 22.1% QoQ from Rs 40.3 crore; up 20.7% YoY from Rs 40.7 crore).
- **Debt:** Total debt around Rs 200 crore at end of Q1 FY27. Estimated peak debt by year-end around Rs 350 crore to Rs 370 crore; net debt around Rs 150 crore to Rs 200 crore.
- **Capex:** INR 690 crore comprehensive capex program underway (INR 450-500 crore already deployed).
- **Ecosis EBITDA Guidance:** Rs 80 crore to Rs 85 crore.
- **Employee Reduction:** 180 to 200 employees reduced, saving Rs 4 crore to Rs 5 crore annually.

### Highlights
- Finished product prices rose in tandem with raw material prices (up >20% pre vs post-Iran conflict), keeping margins intact or improved.
- Prudent advance procurement of a US MEG parcel before the Strait of Hormuz blockade ensured uninterrupted operations.
- Established a Spanish subsidiary (Ecosis) to participate in European forums and policy decisions regarding circular economies and EPR mandates.
- Implemented headcount reduction of 180-200 employees to enhance productivity and save Rs 4-5 crore annually.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects FY27 to be a transition year as new investments come on stream.
- Ecosis plant commercialization expected by end-October or early November 2026, with a 3 to 5-month stabilization period.
- Ecosis utilization expected to average close to 80% in FY28 and close to 100% by the end of FY28.
- Planned future expansion for Ecosis: at least 2 more plants of 1,50,000 tonnes each in the next 2-3 years (one in India, one outside).
- Ecosis EBITDA margin targeted at a minimum of 30%.
- Steam project commercialization expected by September 2026, targeting ~Rs 60 crore EBITDA.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Customers followed need-based procurement with shorter booking cycles, lean inventories, and cautious working capital management.
- Domestic consumption remains reasonably stable, supported by apparel, home textiles, and technical textiles.
- Long-term industry tailwinds include supply chain diversification away from China, India-UK and India-EU FTAs, and domestic PTA capacity additions (GAIL, IOCL, Reliance) adding ~2.4M to 5.6M tonnes.
- European Union mandated textile-to-textile recycling, creating strong demand for circular polyester; global demand estimated at 3-5 million tonnes against current operating capacity of ~1 lakh tonne.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- PTA and MEG prices were highly volatile due to West Asian geopolitical tensions.
- Temporary import duty waiver on PTA/MEG had minimal impact as it lasted only 3 months with a 15-day extension. Current import duty stands at 5% with 10% surcharge (5.5%).
- Inventory gains in Q1 FY27 estimated at Rs 15 crore to Rs 17 crore.
- Forex volatility: Notional loss expected for the year around Rs 10 crore to Rs 15 crore if the euro remains at current levels.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Brownfield PFI expansion (FDY, POY, DTY): 50% completion expected by September 2026, balance 50% by October 2026. Will add ~14,000-15,000 tonnes of DTY and increase top line by ~Rs 400 crore at full run-rate.
- Ecosis textile-to-textile chemical recycling plant: under installation, targeting end-October / early November 2026 startup. Capex per ton claimed to be 3x to 5x lower than international peers.
- Steam distribution project: Capex of Rs 80-85 crore to sell surplus steam from captive power plant; ~60% capacity already tied up. Commercialization by September 2026.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total debt at end of Q1 FY27: ~Rs 200 crore.
- Expected net debt by end of FY27: Rs 150 crore to Rs 200 crore (excluding LCs).
- Comfortable with a debt-to-equity ratio of up to 0.4x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- India's dependence on imported MEG remains a structural risk.
- Geopolitical uncertainties in West Asia impact crude oil prices, petrochemical feedstocks, freight rates, and marine insurance.
- Short-term customer caution and volatile raw material pricing.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Prameet Jain (JM Financial PMS) on Ecosis competitive moat vs peers:** Management stated international peers' capex per ton is 3x to 5x higher and operating costs are higher, giving Filatex a competitive edge and first-mover advantage.
2. **Parth (IDBI Capital) on the Spain subsidiary purpose:** Management (Vedansh Bhageria) explained it allows Filatex to join European bodies and forums that advise on policy decisions and EPR mandates for circular economies.
3. **Pritesh Chheda (Lucky Investments) on Ecosis technology/raw material source:** Management clarified equipment is a mix of imported and Indian-fabricated machinery tested via a 2-3 year pilot; raw material (fabric waste) is sourced via organized consolidators in Surat and surrounding areas.
4. **Niraj (White Pine Investment Management) on Ecosis selling prices and margins:** Management noted chips sell around INR 140-150 per kg for small trial quantities and guided to an EBITDA of Rs 80-85 crore, translating to a minimum 30% EBITDA margin.
5. **Rohit (Progressive Shares) on Q1 production drop:** Management explained production was intentionally cut in April due to high raw material costs and dull seasonal demand to avoid carrying high-cost inventory.

### One-line takeaway
Filatex delivered a resilient Q1 FY27 with steady profitability, advancing its ~INR 690 crore capex program—including high-margin brownfield expansions and a pioneering textile-to-textile recycling project (Ecosis)—while navigating raw material volatility.
