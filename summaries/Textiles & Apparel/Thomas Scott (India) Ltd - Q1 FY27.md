# Thomas Scott (India) Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong revenue and profit growth, effective use of data-driven pricing and marketing models, and successful scaling of new categories.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 66 crore (up 22% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 9 crore (up 43% YoY)
- EBITDA margin: 13.07%
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 5 crore (up 54% YoY)
- PAT margin: 8.21%
- Thomas Scott brand revenue: Rs 25 crore (up 34% YoY)
- Licensed and other brands revenue: Rs 38 crore (up 14% YoY)
- Contract manufacturing revenue: Rs 4 crore (up 33% YoY)
- Wholesale basis revenue (via aggregator-led route for Thomas Scott): Approximately 40% of Thomas Scott brand total revenue (approx. Rs 10 crore booked on wholesale basis)
- Women's wear contribution: Piloting successfully; management stated it could be 2x to 3x of current revenue in about a year's time.
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC): Typically 4% to 5%, sometimes 6% of GMV (higher this quarter due to performance marketing focus).
- Finance cost: Increased to about Rs 1 crore due to elevated working capital loans taken for inventory following a fire incident.

### Highlights
- Evolved into a technology-enabled, digital-first fashion retailer with 15+ brands and 35,800+ cumulative SKUs.
- Deployed proprietary platforms and AI integrations (with Claude) for demand forecasting, planning, catalog management, and real-time pricing adjustments.
- Women's wear emerged as a key growth pillar, focusing on timeless, enduring design aesthetics to ensure repeat purchases.
- B2B2C wholesale traction increased via seller aggregators placing bulk orders for best-performing Thomas Scott styles.
- Brand license agreement executed for Dockers to contribute meaningfully in coming quarters.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains the financial year target to continue the same pace of growth demonstrated over the last two years.
- Management expects consistent margin improvements, though margins may be slightly offset if additional investments are deployed to drive top-line growth.
- Anticipates H2 (festive period) to provide strong opportunities to offset Q1 price-adjustment trade-offs and drive higher growth.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Experienced subdued price elasticity to demand in Q1 due to cautious consumer sentiment against a challenging global macroeconomic backdrop (including LPG shortages).
- Chose to protect price realizations and margin quality rather than pursuing volume through aggressive discounting.
- Shifted focus to performance-led marketing to acquire new consumers when price-cut ROIs were suboptimal.
- August consumer sentiment is showing positive trends.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margins improved as pricing was maintained at higher levels.
- Other expenses increased due to higher performance-related marketing investments yielding better ROIs than price discounts.
- B2B sales to aggregators are margin-neutral at the EBITDA level compared to net sale value (NSV).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Manufacturing capacities in Sholapur, Bangalore, and Gurgaon are fully occupied; additional job work is utilized.
- Continuously adding small-scale capacities; CapEx is driven by operational needs (workers, training) rather than heavy capital expenses.
- Offline stores in Bangalore are EBITDA-positive and act as pilot seeds, but capital is prioritized toward higher-ROCE online growth avenues.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital is primarily tied up in inventory.
- Trade receivables decreased in Q1.
- Elevated working capital debt remains due to inventory financing following a Solapur facility fire incident (Rs 21 crore stock fully covered by insurance); debt levels expected to normalize upon insurance claim realization (currently in final processing stages).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Subdued consumer price elasticity during the quarter due to global macroeconomic uncertainties.
- Dependence on the timing of insurance claim realization for normalizing debt and finance costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Rehan Syed (Trinetra Asset Managers) on Women's Wear unit economics:** Management stated unit economics are neutral to favorable compared to men's wear with controlled return costs and high repeat velocity on core items.
2. **Manas Tripathi (Individual Investor) on Q1 revenue impact from aggregator sales:** Management clarified that wholesale aggregator sales for Thomas Scott accounted for approx. Rs 10 crore (40% of brand revenue) and are EBITDA margin-neutral.
3. **Jayanth (Individual Investor) on surge in finance cost:** Management explained that finance costs rose to ~Rs 1 crore due to elevated working capital loans taken after a fire incident, which will normalize once insurance claims are realized.
4. **Ishant Modi (AMC Assets) on long-term strategy and B2B vs B2C mix:** Management stated 94%+ of revenue is B2C, and B2B orders are maintained solely to secure long production runs for factory efficiency before feeding short-run B2C demand.
5. **Anil Kukreja (Individual Investor) on manufacturing capacity and outsourcing model:** Management stated in-house manufacturing covers 50%, job work/contract facilities cover 40-35%, and outright trading is ~5%, a ratio intended to be maintained for rapid 15-45 day mind-to-market speed.

### One-line takeaway
Thomas Scott reported a resilient Q1 FY27 with 22% revenue and 43% EBITDA growth, strategically prioritizing price realizations and marketing ROI over volume discounting amidst cautious consumer elasticity.
