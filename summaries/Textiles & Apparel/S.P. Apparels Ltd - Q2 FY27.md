# S.P. Apparels Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management cited a sequential recovery driven by easing U.S. tariff uncertainty, strong order visibility, and robust growth in subsidiaries.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Revenue guidance was slightly adjusted; management stated they may end up achieving 95% of the original Rs 2,000 crore guidance. Garment division EBITDA margin guidance of 15% was maintained.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from cautious/uncertain (due to U.S. tariffs in Q1) to confident, with clearer order pipelines and strong customer pull from U.K./Europe leveraging FTAs.
- New topics or risks: Sri Lanka factory customer approval delay specifically quantified as impacting top-line targets (Sri Lanka expected at Rs 150-170 crore vs earlier Rs 190 crore). SPUK profitability timeline provided (EBITDA breakeven in Q3, positive in Q4).
- Last quarter's promises: YBA and SPUK continued strong growth trajectories as anticipated; retail maintained its EBITDA-positive run.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated Revenue (Q2 FY27): Rs 452.2 crore (up 5.8% YoY, 12.8% QoQ)
- Consolidated Revenue (H1 FY27): Rs 853 crore (up 2.7% YoY)
- Consolidated EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs 66.7 crore (up 4.7% YoY); Margin: 14.7%
- Consolidated EBITDA (H1 FY27): Rs 128 crore (up 9.8% YoY); Margin: 15% (vs 14% YoY)
- Consolidated PAT (Q2 FY27): Rs 37.1 crore (up 6.8% YoY, 49% QoQ); Margin: 7.3%
- Consolidated PAT (H1 FY27): Rs 61.9 crore (up 11.9% YoY)
- EPS (Q2 FY27): Rs 14.7
- Capacity Utilization: 74% (across 5,700 machines)
- Standalone Net Debt: Rs 243 crore (Gross debt: Rs 304 crore; Long-term debt: Rs 53 crore; Working capital debt: Rs 251 crore)
- Consolidated Net Debt: Rs 364 crore (Gross debt: Rs 433 crore; Long-term debt: Rs 78 crore; Working capital debt: Rs 355 crore)
- Cash and Cash Equivalents: Rs 69 crore
- Segment Revenues: Young Brand Apparels (YBA) Q2 adjusted revenue Rs 109.1 crore (up 29.4% YoY); SPUK Q2 revenue Rs 38.1 crore (up 111.8% YoY); Sri Lanka H1 revenue Rs 70 crore (expected Rs 150-170 crore for full year).
- Order Books: SPAL Rs 450 crore; YBA Rs 175 crore; SPUK GBP 4.5 million.

### Highlights
- Sequential revenue recovery as U.S. customer tariff-related order deferrals from Q1 normalized.
- Profit growth outpaced revenue growth in H1 due to better product mix and operating discipline.
- Retail division reported EBITDA positive for 5 consecutive quarters (H1 FY27 EBITDA at Rs 0.5 crore vs loss of Rs 1.5 crore YoY).
- Sri Lanka operations running 1,700 machines across 4 factories (1 factory awaiting customer approval, engaging in job work meanwhile; full FOB operations expected by FY28 targeting Rs 400 crore).
- SPUK added 3 new customers; targeting EBITDA breakeven in Q3 and positive in Q4.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue achievement at ~95% of the original full-year guidance due to some pushovers.
- Garment division EBITDA margin guided to average ~15% for the full year.
- FY28 consolidated garment division growth guided at 10% to 15%, with YBA growing at least 20%.
- SPUK targets reaching Rs 500 crore revenue in the next 3 to 5 years.
- Sri Lanka revenue targeted at Rs 400 crore for FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- U.S. tariff-related uncertainty has eased, and U.S. customers have confirmed orders.
- India benefiting from "China Plus One" and "Bangladesh Plus One" sourcing diversification strategies.
- FTA implementation has put India at a level playing field with Bangladesh for fashion/value-added products, though Bangladesh remains stronger in basic volume lines.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Export net sales realization in the Garment division improved 14.5% YoY to Rs 162.9 per piece due to product mix.
- Finance costs dropped to Rs 6.3 crore in Q2 (from Rs 14.9 crore in Q1) due to exchange gain reversals on packing credit as the USD/INR rate moved from ~96-97 to ~94.
- Sri Lanka operations carry a lower margin (~10%) and pre-operative challenges that temporarily diluted overall garment margins.
- Cotton procurement inventory is maintained at 45 to 60 days. 80% of confirmed export orders are hedged for currency.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Salem facility started production with 40 machines operational, scaling to 300 machines in 6 months.
- YBA total machine capacity expected to reach 1,300 to 1,500 machines by March.
- Evaluating adding 1 or 2 more factories (equivalent to ~1,000 machines) in Sri Lanka next year.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total equity stood at Rs 1,011 crore as of September 30, 2026.
- Retail division debt remains high, with management noting potential equity conversion of parent company loans or further equity infusion to reduce debt.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- One factory in Sri Lanka is facing delays in direct customer approval, restricting top-line scaling until next fiscal year.
- Urban center constraints near Chennai limit main YBA facility utilization expansion, shifting growth to Salem and Tirupur units.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Rehan (Coheron Wealth) asked about Avinashi standalone revenue flatness YoY and Sri Lanka target shortfall.** Management explained that yarn contribution drops (~20%) and Q1/Q2 tariff impacts caused the slowdown, while Sri Lanka fell short due to one factory awaiting customer approval (pushed to April '27).
2. **Prerna Jhunjhunwala (DAM Capital) asked about the reason for YoY margin decline in the Garment business despite better spinning.** Management clarified that lower-margin Sri Lanka revenue (~60 crore at ~10% margin) and pre-operative expenses diluted overall margins.
3. **Bharat Sheth (Quest Investment Managers) asked about FY28 growth drivers and Sri Lanka scalability.** Management confirmed a target of Rs 400 crore for Sri Lanka in FY28 and plans to add 1-2 more factories there, alongside ~20% growth expected from YBA driven by Salem expansion.
4. **Ashwin Reddy (Samatva) questioned the cash/rental accounting and funding needs for the Retail business.** Management stated that debt is high, parent loans may be converted to equity, and the business is nearing cash profitability as EBITDA remains positive for 5 quarters.
5. **Amish Kanani (Knowise Investment Managers) asked about customer perception of India vs. Sri Lanka and hedging policies.** Management noted customers view India and Sri Lanka as a single entity (ISL) for billing through SPAL India, and 80% of confirmed orders are hedged.

### One-line takeaway
S.P. Apparels delivered a sequential recovery in Q2 FY27 with 12.8% revenue growth and margin resilience, supported by easing U.S. tariffs and scaling subsidiaries despite minor execution delays in Sri Lanka.
