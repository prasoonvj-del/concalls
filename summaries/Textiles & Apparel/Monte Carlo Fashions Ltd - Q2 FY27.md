# Monte Carlo Fashions Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Textiles & Apparel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management cited rising raw material costs and macroeconomic headwinds impacting profitability and discretionary spending, despite maintaining long-term growth targets.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations (Q2 FY27): Rs. 262 crores, up 5.51% YoY (vs Rs. 248 crores in Q2 FY26).
- Operating EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs. 28.35 crores.
- Operating EBITDA margin (Q2 FY27): 10.80%.
- Profit after tax (PAT) (Q2 FY27): Rs. 1.17 crores.
- Revenue from operations (H1 FY27): Rs. 411 crores, up 6.25% YoY (vs Rs. 387 crores in H1 FY26).
- Operating EBITDA (H1 FY27): Rs. 15.42 crores with a 3.7% margin.
- Loss after tax (H1 FY27): Rs. 22.25 crores.
- Return ratio (Q2 FY27): 16.17% (vs 23.18% YoY).
- Return ratio (H1 FY27): 30.70% (vs 29.47% YoY).
- Gross Sales (H1 FY27): Cotton Rs. 390 crores (vs Rs. 326 crores in H1 FY26); Home Textiles Rs. 91.6 crores (vs Rs. 83 crores); Kids wear Rs. 34.5 crores (vs Rs. 30 crores); Footwear Rs. 9.3 crores (vs Rs. 4.9 crores); Woolen gross sales declined to Rs. 94 crores (from approx. Rs. 106 crores).
- Store counts (as of Sept 2026): EBOs 506 (vs 497 YoY); Shop-in-Shops 632 (vs 578); MBOs 1,413 (vs 1,615); National Chain Stores 846 (vs 891).
- West and South contribution: Approx. 9.7% of gross sales combined.
- Working capital days: Improved to 146 in FY26 from 176 in FY25.
- Price hike: 8% to 9% taken for the winter season.
- Solar project: 35-megawatt plant in Madhya Pradesh; expected ROI of 17%-18%; expected revenue of Rs. 17-18 crores with >80% EBITDA margin in FY28.

### Highlights
- Cotton is the largest reported product category, contributing approximately 63% of gross product sales in H1 FY27.
- Profitability in H1 was impacted by higher material costs, lower other income, and increased employee costs.
- Later festive season shifted consumer demand and winter sell-through into Q3.
- Newer brands (Rock.it, Cloak & Decker) and Home Textiles are growing faster than the parent brand; Home Textiles growing at ~20% CAGR.
- Solar plant PPAs signed for three sites in Madhya Pradesh with a COD of March 31, 2027.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintained guidance for low double-digit revenue growth for FY27.
- Maintained guidance of 20% home textile growth.
- Maintained guidance of 40-45 new EBO openings, focused on the West and South.
- Expected EBITDA margin for the full year within plus-minus 100 bps compared to last financial year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Indian economy growing at 7% is viewed as resilient.
- Macroeconomic factors and geopolitical situations pressured discretionary spending and required earlier/prolonged end-of-season sales.
- Rental apparel economy is growing, but management has no plans to enter it as product values are not high enough.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material inflation increased material costs and hurt Q2 performance.
- Pure wool raw material prices increased by almost 20% over last year.
- Cost of goods sold increased by 3% in H1.
- Rebates and discounts increased to 12% of revenue in H1 (vs 9% last year) due to early discounts and liquidation of older inventory. Full-year rebates expected to remain up to 3%–4% maximum of total.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Around 20% of cotton garments are manufactured in-house (t-shirts and thermals), while the rest is outsourced. Woolen items are manufactured entirely in-house.
- Solar capex: 35 MW plant investments ongoing with aggregation in place.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt accumulation is driven by seasonal working capital requirements for inventory buildup ahead of Q3. Permanent working capital debt expected around Rs. 200 crores as of March 31.
- Lease liabilities increased; total lease liability stands at Rs. 252 crores.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Material cost inflation and geopolitical tensions impacting margins.
- Lower other income and increased employee costs dragging H1 profitability.
- High sales return ratios inherent to the wholesale dispatch model, though provisions are made.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Keshav Garg (Counter Cyclical PMS) questioned long-term stock stagnation since 2014, rising debt, and negative SSG.** Management responded that revenue has grown 4x since the IPO (from Rs. 375 crores to an estimated ~Rs. 1,400 crores), like-to-like growth was 10% last year and 9% in H1, and stock prices are outside management control. Management defended non-Monte Carlo brands as serving distinct categories (Athleisure, economy segment). *(Management did not dodge).*
- **Keshav Garg questioned the diversification into solar power given core business returns.** Management clarified that solar is a one-off investment utilizing available funds to improve ROI and is not a new core business vertical. *(Management answered directly).*
- **Aman Kumar (Individual Investor) asked about quantified sales loss from the festive shift to Q2/Q3.** Management stated exact quantification is difficult, but dispatches and sales shifted to Q3. *(Management directly addressed limitation).*
- **Ritika Pahwa (Pahwa Securities) asked about full-year margins and solar project timelines.** Management guided for EBITDA margins at plus-minus 100 bps vs last year and confirmed solar COD of March 31, 2027, with FY28 revenue of Rs. 17-18 crores. *(Management answered directly).*
- **Jitesh Popat (Investor) asked why the company doesn't pursue export/white labeling opportunities.** Management stated exports/white-labeling offer insufficient margins, and the company prefers focusing on branded retail in domestic markets like the West and South. *(Management answered directly).*

### One-line takeaway
Monte Carlo Fashions reported modest H1 revenue growth of 6.25% with an H1 net loss, heavily weighed down by raw material inflation and inventory liquidation discounts, but management maintains its double-digit revenue growth guidance for FY27 driven by the upcoming winter and festive season in Q3.
