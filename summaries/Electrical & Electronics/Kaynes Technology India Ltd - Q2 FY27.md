# Kaynes Technology India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Mixed - Core EMS revenue grew strongly (+34% YoY) and OSAT/PCB units became operational, but overall revenue growth was muted due to a deliberate 80% cut in smart meter sales, coupled with restated financials and commodity/forex headwinds.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained overall direction (targeting double the market growth; EMS growth >30% for FY27).
- Tone and demand view: Focus shifted toward balance sheet discipline, cash preservation, and de-risking smart meter receivables, with global component lead-time pressures persisting.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Restatement of prior period financials under Ind AS 8 following the transition to new auditor (Walker Chandiok & Co LLP); announcement of a strategic semiconductor JV/partnership with Vishay (taking 75%–90% capacity); acquisition of Toyokawa JV for TFT display modules.
- Last quarter's promises: Operationalization of Kaynes Semicon Unit 2 and Kaynes Circuits by Q3 FY27 was achieved on schedule during Q2 FY27.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 952.5 crore (Rs 9,525 million).
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 125.6 crore (Rs 1,256 million).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 13.2% (down from 14.1% in H1 FY27).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 39.8 crore (Rs 398 million).
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 1,898.7 crore (Rs 18,987 million).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 268.1 crore (EBITDA margin 14.1%).
- **Order Book:** Rs 10,563.5 crore (Rs 105,635 million).
- **Core EMS Segment Revenue:** Rs 799 crore, up 34% YoY (vs Rs 595 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **Smart Meter Segment Revenue:** Rs 54 crore, down 80% YoY (vs Rs 228 crore in Q2 FY26).

### Highlights
- Financials restated under Ind AS 8 following transition to statutory auditors Walker Chandiok & Co LLP; FY26 PAT revised upward to Rs 392 crore (vs Rs 364 crore previously reported).
- Signed a strategic partnership with Vishay, which will absorb 75%–90% of Kaynes Semicon’s capacity, accelerating global market access (ex-China) and validation timelines.
- Kaynes Semicon Unit 2 and Kaynes Circuits Chennai are now operational and undergoing customer product validation.
- Acquired Toyokawa joint venture to enter the thin-film transistor (TFT) display module market for vehicle electronics.
- Aerospace and defense/railways segments grew by over 70% and 50% YoY, respectively; Kavach railway product received commercial production approval.
- Smart meter receivables reduced from Rs 1,402 crore in Q1 FY27 to Rs 1,091 crore in Q2 FY27. Management plans to structure/hive off installation and O&M to a third party by next quarter's call.
- Continues pattern of shifting business mix toward box-builds (now 60% of revenues) and high-reliability EMS segments.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management targets FY27 electronics business growth of >30% YoY (double the market growth rate).
- Expects H2 FY27 to be significantly stronger in both revenue and margins, driven by full operationalization of OSAT/PCB units and higher-margin aerospace, defense, railway, and medical electronics mix.
- Reaffirmed Mission 30 target to increase export share from ~10% to 25%+ by 2030 and raise non-metering product/box-build sales mix to 30%.
- Management assured no fundraise (QIP) will be required over the next 12 months.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand across core EMS, aerospace, defense, railways, and automotive electronics remains strong.
- Global semiconductor validation cycles take 12–14 months; partnering with Vishay bypasses lengthened application engineering cycles.
- Global PCB industry faces extended lead times of 6–8 months with suppliers requiring advance payments.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Q2 margins absorbed elevated crude-linked logistics costs, Brent spikes above $100/barrel, wholesale inflation near 10%, manufactured goods inflation at 8.4%, and multi-year high copper and aluminum prices.
- Rupee depreciated >12% YoY from Rs 85 to Rs 95–96 per USD during the quarter.
- Margin drag was also driven by the initial ramp-up costs of OSAT and PCB facilities and lower absorption in smart meters.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Incremental Q2 FY27 capex added was Rs 221 crore gross (Rs 115 crore in Semicon, Rs 140 crore in Circuits, Rs 21 crore in EMS). Net of government subsidies, net Q2 capex added was Rs 59 crore in Semicon, Rs 140 crore in Circuits, and Rs 21 crore in EMS.
- PCB Unit 1 will start contributing to revenues in Q3 FY27, with both OSAT and PCB reaching full operation in Q4 FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net working capital stood at 169 days on a 12-month rolling basis (vs 163 days in Q1 FY27).
- Strategic inventory buffer of Rs 200–250 crore maintained due to global supply disruptions; management plans to unwind this inventory starting December 2026.
- EMS receivables stood at Rs 86.8 crore (Rs 868 million); total smart meter receivables stood at Rs 1,091 crore.
- Bank-funded receivable bill discounting and supplier financing lines were reclassified from operational line items into short-term borrowings.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated raw material costs (copper, aluminum) and volatile crude prices impacting short-term margins.
- Extended product validation lead times for semiconductor and auto-grade display modules.
- Delayed realization of smart meter receivables, requiring continued capital control and revenue moderation.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q (Macquarie):** What is the nature of accounting restatements and how will capacity be allocated for Vishay?  
  **A (Management):** Receivables bill discounting was reclassified into borrowings under Ind AS. Vishay will occupy 75%–90% of existing OSAT capacity; the remaining 10%–25% will continue serving validated existing customers.
- **Q (Avendus Spark):** How is the Vishay JV structured and will it involve equity dilution?  
  **A (Management):** Vishay will take a 49% stake in the Semicon entity/subsidiary under a term sheet. Definitive agreements will be finalized in 2–3 months. Upfront capital from Vishay will repay parent company loans and lower consolidation debt.
- **Q (Avendus Spark):** What drove the 80% decline in smart meter revenue and how was revenue restated?  
  **A (Management):** Revenue drop was intentional to control balance sheet risk. Restatement did not derecognize revenue; in fact, shifting West Bengal metering to a service concession model added Rs 41.8 crore to FY26 revenue.
- **Q (Emkay):** How will the smart meter business be restructured by February?  
  **A (Management):** Kaynes will retain meter manufacturing while hiving off lower-margin/working-capital-heavy installation and O&M to a partner who will purchase meters from Kaynes.
- **Q (Deep Master):** Will there be any QIP or equity fundraise in the next 6–12 months?  
  **A (Management):** Management explicitly assured no QIP or market fundraise will occur over the next 12 months, as inventory unwinding (Rs 250 crore), smart meter collections, and Vishay equity inflow will generate sufficient liquidity.

### One-line takeaway
Kaynes sacrificed short-term top-line growth by cutting smart meter sales to improve balance sheet health, while locking in long-term OSAT/PCB execution through a strategic partnership with Vishay.
