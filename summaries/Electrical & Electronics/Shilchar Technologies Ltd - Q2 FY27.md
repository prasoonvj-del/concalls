# Shilchar Technologies Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Business momentum picked up notably in Q2 with strong domestic demand and improving North American exports, keeping the company on track for its annual targets.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 192.61 crore (up 43% QoQ, up 12% YoY)
- EBITDA (excluding other income): Rs 40.06 crore
- EBITDA margin: 20.8% (improved from 16.4% in Q1)
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 32.98 crore
- PAT margin: 16.5%
- H1 FY27 sales: Rs 330 crore
- Order book: More than Rs 550 crore (60% domestic, 40% export mix)
- FY27 revenue target: Rs 750 crore (management expects >Rs 800 crore)
- FY28 revenue target: Rs 1,000 crore
- FY29 revenue target: Rs 1,250 crore to Rs 1,500 crore
- Capacity expansion (Phase 3): 6,500 MVA addition, taking total capacity to 14,000 MVA
- Breakeven capacity utilization: 35% to 40%

### Highlights
- Q2 performance rebounded strongly after a slow Q1 caused by West Asian crises which weighed on exports.
- Exports to North America gained momentum following the resolution of earlier tariff-related challenges, offsetting Middle East shortfalls.
- Phase 3 expansion is progressing as planned with commissioning expected in April 2027; civil foundation, PEB erection, and utility infrastructure work are ongoing, and major equipment has been ordered.
- Management stated that working capital increase on paper is due to customers establishing LCs with interest borne by them; the company earns interest on these arrangements and uses no bank working capital facilities.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains on track to achieve FY27 revenue of Rs 750 crore, expecting over Rs 225 crore to Rs 230 crore per quarter in H2, translating to >Rs 800 crore overall.
- FY28 revenue targeted at Rs 1,000 crore.
- FY29 revenue targeted at Rs 1,250 crore to Rs 1,500 crore, with potential for 100% capacity utilization.
- EBITDA margins expected to improve in Q3 and Q4 due to higher export share and 100% capacity utilization; management targets absolute EBITDA of Rs 200 crore to Rs 220 crore for the year rather than fixed percentage margins.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic demand remained very strong throughout the period.
- Power consumption in India projected to double in the next 3 to 4 years driven by AI investments, data centers, and renewable energy targets.
- U.S. demand is quite good, with customer projections showing increasing demand for the coming year across data centers, distribution networks, and substations.
- Higher kV class lead times globally are around 1 to 1.5 years; lower kV class lead times are 3 to 6 months.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material prices have stabilized compared to the sudden spike in March caused by the Middle East conflict, though not at pre-crisis levels.
- New orders are being quoted based on prevailing market raw material prices.
- Export margins remain on higher side compared to domestic margins.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Phase 3 expansion adds 6,500 MVA (including 220 kV class capacity), commissioning by April 2027, taking total capacity to 14,000 MVA.
- Current capacity utilization is 80% to 90%, expected to reach nearly 100% in Q3 and Q4.
- 220 kV class approvals will be applied for once the plant functions; existing clients have given verbal commitments to support with orders, and approvals are expected within 3 to 6 months.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net working capital increased to around Rs 200 crore in H1, including financial assets rising to Rs 29 crore due to project investments and higher receivables.
- No bank facilities are used for working capital; customers establish 180-day LCs with interest borne by them.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Middle East export conditions have improved but are yet to fully normalize; high shipping and logistic costs have not eased much.
- Margins may see pressure after 2-3 years due to industry-wide capacity additions, though not anticipated for the next couple of years.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Aadesh Gosalia (Spark Capital)** asked about the increase in working capital and financial assets to Rs 29 crore. Management clarified that financial asset increases relate to new project investments, and higher receivables are secure 180-day LCs where customers bear the interest.
- **Jiten Parmar (Aurum Edge)** asked about the impact of freight on EBITDA and margin pressure from industry capacity additions. Management stated that absolute EBITDA is prioritized over percentage margins and does not see margin pressure for at least another couple of years due to high power demand.
- **Prathamesh Sawant (Abakkus Investment Managers)** asked why Shilchar maintains superior margins despite smaller MVA output. Management attributed higher margins to the export business model, high operational efficiency, and productivity.
- **Maitri (Sapphire Capital)** asked about 220 kV class approval timelines and margin differentials. Management expects approvals to take 3 to 6 months post-plant readiness from existing clients, with margins remaining similar to current levels.
- **Rahul Majethia (Individual Investor)** asked if expanding capacity during flat H1 top-line growth is a risk and what the breakeven utilization is. Management stated that business requires growth to avoid stagnation, and breakeven capacity utilization is 35% to 40%.

### One-line takeaway
Shilchar Technologies rebounded strongly in Q2 FY27 with 43% QoQ revenue growth, robust domestic demand, and recovering North American exports, keeping it firmly on track for its annual revenue targets while progressing its Phase 3 capacity expansion.
