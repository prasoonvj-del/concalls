# Electrical & Electronics - Solar modules & cells: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Indian solar modules and cells sector experienced a robust yet nuanced Q2 FY27. Driven by structural tailwinds such as the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, KUSUM, and strict enforcement of the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM), underlying domestic demand remained extremely robust, with several companies reporting record H1 revenues and profits. However, the quarter was marked by diverging trends: while vertically integrated players with cell and module capacities (such as Emmvee, Premier Energies, Waaree, and Websol) maintained healthy EBITDA margins (30%–35%), non-integrated module assemblers and asset-light EPC players (like Solarworld and Captain Polyplast) faced margin compression due to raw material price volatility (copper, aluminum, soda ash), fixed-price PSU contracts without pass-throughs, and logistics cost spikes. Furthermore, international policy shifts—most notably the U.S. Section 232 circular—forced major exporters like Waaree and Vikram Solar to recalibrate their export order books toward Minimum Import Price (MIP) terms.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements universally agree that India's domestic solar demand is structurally secure, underpinned by aggressive government targets (500 GW non-fossil by 2030), the PM Surya Ghar rooftop scheme (crossing 50–60 lakh households), and rising C&I/utility demand. 
- **Divergence:** Opinions diverge on near-term utility-scale execution. While Emmvee and Premier Energies noted record installation paces and strong order inflows, companies like Vikram Solar and Solarworld cited temporary headwinds from IPP project delays (land and evacuation constraints) and deferred LOA issuances by PSUs. Additionally, while pure-play module makers face intense pricing pressure in the non-DCR segment, vertically integrated players are seeing high demand for domestic content requirement (DCR) cells.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Raw Material & Freight Pressures:** Borosil Renewables cited a 10% hike in soda ash and higher freight on imported dolomite, while Captain Polyplast, Solarworld, and Solex Energy flagged sudden spikes in polymer (HDPE/LLDPE), metal (copper/aluminum), and shipping container costs ($900 to $3,500/container). Solarworld and Solex absorbed these costs to honor customer commitments rather than canceling orders.
- **Cost Optimizations:** To protect margins, companies are aggressively pursuing backward integration. Emmvee successfully reduced silver consumption from ~220 mg to 60–65 mg per cell. Insolation Energy, Saatvik Green, and Websol are commissioning in-house aluminum frame and cell lines to capture higher value addition, with integrated players targeting 20%–30% EBITDA margins compared to 6%–10% for pure assemblers.

## Capex & capacity
The sector is witnessing massive, aggressive capex cycles as companies transition toward complete vertical integration (ingots, wafers, cells, and modules) ahead of ALMM List-III deadlines (June 2028):
- **Emmvee:** On track with a 6 GW integrated TOPCon cell and module facility (Rs 5,500 crore capex) targeting module completion by Dec 2026 and cells by March 2027.
- **Premier Energies:** Commissioned its 7 GW TOPCon cell line at Naidupeta; FY27 capex guided to ~Rs 2,400 crore, with plans for a 10 GW ingot/wafer facility by June 2028.
- **Waaree Energies:** Deploying a ~Rs 30,000 crore capex program across cells (adding 10 GW at Unn), 10 GW ingots/wafers in Nagpur, and US manufacturing.
- **Saatvik Green & Websol:** Saatvik commenced its 4.0 GW Odisha module facility and is ramping up 2.4 GW cell lines; Websol became debt-free, advancing its 750 MW TOPCon upgrade and planning a 4 GW greenfield expansion (Phase 1 costing Rs 1,350 crore).
- **Fujiyama & Vikram Solar:** Fujiyama commissioned its 2 GW Ratlam power electronics plant and upsized lithium-ion capacity to 3 GWh; Vikram Solar is progressing on its 9 GW Gangaikondan cell project (Rs 5,800 crore total cost) targeting March 2027 COD.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Guidance & Tone Shifts:** Fujiyama, Insolation, and Fujiyama opted to maintain full-year guidance despite strong H1 performance, awaiting regulatory clarity on PM Surya Ghar 2.0 and ALMM List-II. Conversely, Solarworld lowered its margin expectations following raw material volatility, while Vikram Solar and Solex navigated temporary Q2 volume dips due to policy friction before returning to normal operations.
- **Geopolitical & Trade Shocks:** A major new development this quarter was the U.S. Section 232 circular, which introduced minimum import prices and 15% ad valorem duties, prompting Waaree Energies and Vikram Solar to remove/renegotiate ~3 GW and ~1 GW of U.S. export orders, respectively. Additionally, potential Chinese export controls on wafer/ingot manufacturing tech emerged as a watch-item, though most companies (Emmvee, Premier, Websol) reported that equipment orders are already secured or sourced from alternative suppliers like Germany (Centrotherm).

## What to watch next quarter
- **Cell Facility Commissioning & Ramp-ups:** Commercial production runs and CUF ramp-ups for newly commissioned cell lines (Premier Energies' 7 GW Naidupeta line, Saatvik's Odisha cells, and Vikram Solar/Emmvee/Websol cell upgrades).
- **ALMM List-II & III Compliance:** Full implementation impact of ALMM for solar cells and preliminary positioning for ingot/wafer mandates (June 2028).
- **Export Order Book Adjustments:** Progress on renegotiating U.S. export orders under the new Section 232 Minimum Import Price regime for Waaree and Vikram Solar.
- **Working Capital & Collections:** Normalization of debtor days and inventory levels, particularly for EPC and pump players like GK Energy and Captain Polyplast.

## Company one-liners
- **Borosil Renewables Ltd:** Temporary maintenance downtime compressed Q2 margins, but October production recovery and Q4 600 TPD expansion support a return to 30%+ EBITDA margins.
- **Captain Polyplast Ltd:** Banking on government benchmark price revisions and in-house manufacturing to recover margins in H2, while leveraging strong solar EPC order wins to drive top-line growth.
- **Emmvee Photovoltaic Power Ltd:** Posted its strongest H1 performance with a 45% YoY revenue growth and 79% PAT growth, backed by robust cell utilization, expanding integrated capacities, and strict operational discipline.
- **Fujiyama Power Systems Ltd:** Doubled its revenue and profits YoY in H1 FY27 on the back of aggressive retail expansion and backward integration, but maintained its full-year guidance pending policy clarity.
- **GK Energy Ltd:** Delivered a strong H1 FY27 with 38.9% revenue growth, expanded into rooftop solar and BESS utility storage, and significantly improved working capital through post-quarter collections.
- **Insolation Energy Ltd:** Reported a 100.56% YoY surge in H1 FY27 revenue to INR1,582.09 crores despite margin pressures, and remains firmly on track with its backward integration milestones into solar cells, aluminum frames, and tandem modules.
- **Premier Energies Ltd:** Maintained strong earnings growth with ~29% solar EBITDA margins, while successfully commissioning its 7 GW cell line to support captive integration and future scale.
- **Saatvik Green Energy Ltd:** Saatvik's execution scaled up significantly in Q2 FY27 with a 77% sequential revenue jump and the commercial launch of its Odisha module facility, laying the groundwork for margin expansion via backward cell integration.
- **Solarworld Energy Solutions Ltd:** Faced severe margin compression in H1 FY27 due to fixed-price PSU contracts and metal price volatility, but management expects a sharp recovery in FY28 driven by scaling BESS contributions, easing raw material costs, and operational backward integration.
- **Solex Energy Ltd:** Despite recent regulatory friction and logistics cost spikes that compressed margins, Solex has restored 24/7 operations and remains firmly on track with its backward integration timelines for cell and BESS manufacturing.
- **Vikram Solar Ltd:** Despite a temporary Q2 volume and revenue drop caused by IPP project delays and U.S. tariff hurdles, Vikram Solar improved unit economics, expanded distribution, and stayed on track to commission its 9 GW cell facility by March 2027.
- **Waaree Energies Ltd:** Waaree Energies is leveraging aggressive backward integration (cells, wafers) and rapid US local expansion to offset export policy shifts while expanding high-margin domestic retail channels.
- **Websol Energy System Ltd:** Websol Energy delivered a record H1 FY27, turning debt-free with robust cash flows while aggressively advancing its 750 MW TOPCon upgrade and planning a phased 4 GW greenfield expansion.
