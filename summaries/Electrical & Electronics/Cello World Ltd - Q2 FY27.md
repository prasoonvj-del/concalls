# Cello World Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-13 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue grew 7.8% YoY driven by writing instruments and festive shift, but margins remained under pressure due to high gas and raw material costs and operational headwinds in steel bottles.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained a cautious stance, with management stating they expect about 10% growth year-on-year by the end of the year, declining specific margin guidance.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from cautious to more optimistic on demand following a good October and festive season normalization, though cost pressures remain.
- New topics or risks: Maintenance upgrade taken in the glassware division (furnace temporary halt) to improve product mix; ongoing focus on MIP representations for glassware.
- Last quarter's promises: In-house steel bottle capacity expansion continued with two extra lines planned for next quarter to reach 5 million pieces capacity.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs. 633.2 crore (up 7.8% YoY, up 20.2% QoQ)
- H1 FY27 revenue: Rs. 1,159.9 crore (up 3.9% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs. 135 crore
- EBITDA margin: 21.3% (down from 24% YoY)
- PAT: Rs. 81.9 crore
- PAT margin: 12.9%
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs. 252.2 crore (margin 21.7%)
- H1 FY27 PAT: Rs. 155.3 crore (margin 13.4%)
- Gross margin: 50.2% (up 70 bps YoY, down QoQ)
- Segment revenues: Consumerware Rs. 417.2 crore, Writing Instruments (21.5% mix), Moulded Furniture Rs. 78.8 crore (down 6.5% YoY)
- Channel mix: General trade 70.3%, Online (e-commerce & quick commerce) 15.7%, Exports 8.1%, Modern trade 5.9%
- Segment gross margins: Consumerware 53.4%, Writing Instruments 52.6%, Moulded Furniture 39%
- Glassware capacity utilization: ~60%
- Opalware capacity utilization: ~80%
- Steel bottle capacity: ~2 million pieces currently, expanding towards 5 million pieces

### Highlights
- Festive demand shift from Q2 into October (Q3) impacted Q2 YoY comparisons.
- Writing Instruments delivered strong growth of 69.5% YoY, led by the Cello brand.
- Online channels (e-commerce and quick commerce) reached 15.7% revenue contribution with profitability in line with general trade.
- Glassware division took a temporary maintenance pause for a month and a half to upgrade machinery for a better product mix, with operations restarting in December.
- Moulded Furniture remains subdued due to high polymer costs, with management prioritizing margins over volume chasing.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects ~10% YoY revenue growth for FY27.
- Management anticipates stronger performance in FY28 and FY29, targeting 17%-18% growth supported by expanded capacities in opalware, glassware, and steelware, alongside the Cello writing instrument brand.
- Management declined to provide specific annual margin guidance.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Consumer demand sentiment improved in the last couple of months, with October performing well and channel inventory running low post-festive season.
- Glassware experiences low-priced import pressure from China, though freight cost increases and product mix changes helped offset some impact.
- Steel bottle demand remains much higher than supply; priority is ramping up production.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- High crude oil prices and West Asia conflicts kept polymer, fuel, gas, and packaging costs elevated.
- Natural gas prices doubled (reaching ~INR53-54 SCM) and accounted for ~30% of glassware costs, impacting profitability.
- Glassware and opalware segments absorbed cost increases via ~8% to 10% price hikes and improved product mixes.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Opalware capacity is running at ~80% utilization; brownfield expansion will be evaluated in early FY27 based on market conditions.
- Steel bottle operations are installing two extra lines next quarter, targeting 5 million pieces total capacity.
- Capex going forward will primarily be maintenance capex.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Large cash pile is maintained on the books to evaluate potential inorganic acquisition opportunities or new verticals.
- Working capital elevated due to glassware inventory during lower utilization; management aims to bring working capital cycles down further.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatile crude oil, polymer, and natural gas prices impacting gross margins.
- Inefficiencies and slow ramp-up in the technically challenging in-house steel bottle manufacturing facility.
- Continued import pressure and dumping from China in glassware.
- Lower profitability of the Cello pen portfolio compared to Unomax during the initial ramp-up phase.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Vaidik Bafna (Monarch Networth Capital) asked about opalware and glassware utilization and growth:** Management stated opalware is at 80% capacity with marginal growth, while glassware is at 60% utilization while older stock is being cleared. *(Management did not dodge).*
- **Bhavin Rupani (Investec) asked about glassware EBITDA status and inventory:** Management clarified that glassware broke even (EBITDA neutral) in the quarter and inventory has come down drastically. *(Management did not dodge).*
- **Anu Parakh (Anand Rathi) asked about gas price impact and glassware price hikes:** Management confirmed a ~8% to 10% price hike in glassware and noted gas prices doubled to ~INR53-54 SCM, impacting margins. *(Management did not dodge).*
- **Karan Gupta (ACMIIL) asked about steel facility margins and utilization:** Management explained that low utilization and margins are driven by efficiency ramp-up challenges at the technical plant, which will improve as production scales to 5 million pieces. *(Management did not dodge).*
- **Dharmendra Vora (Imagine Bonds) asked about return on equity and cash utilization:** Management stated cash is reserved for potential inorganic acquisitions or vertical expansions. *(Management did not dodge).*

### One-line takeaway
Cello World's Q2 FY27 revenue grew 7.8% YoY despite festive shift headwinds, while margins faced severe compression from surging energy/polymer costs and steel ramp-up inefficiencies, though management anticipates a strong recovery in H2 and FY28.
