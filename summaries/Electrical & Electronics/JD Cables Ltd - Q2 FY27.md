# JD Cables Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-21 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong 38.25% revenue growth, capacity expansion, robust demand, and a strategic exit from the EPC segment to focus on core cables and conductors.

### Key numbers
- Total income: Rs 167.89 crores (up 38.25% YoY)
- Net profit (PAT): Rs 12.29 crores
- Net profit margin: 7.32%
- Order book: Around Rs 300 crores (cables business)
- Capex incurred (last one year): Rs 40 crores to Rs 43 crores
- Borrowings / Debt: Around Rs 118 crores
- Average cost of borrowing: 8% to 9%
- Total installed capacity: 2,800 kilometers
- New plant capacity utilization: 50% currently, expected to reach 80% to 87% by the end of H2
- Proposed fundraise: Up to Rs 100 crores (via QIP or preferential issue)
- EPC backlog: Around Rs 250 crores
- EPC revenue done in H1: Rs 60 crores; expected in H2: Rs 50 crores (total FY27 EPC: Rs 110 crores)
- FY27 total revenue guidance: Around Rs 500 crores (including ~Rs 400 crores from cables business)
- FY28 revenue guidance (cables business): Rs 600 crores to Rs 700 crores

### Highlights
- Acquired an industrial facility of approximately 1.18 lakh square feet at Dankuni, West Bengal.
- Started the conductor division in July and the cable division in September.
- Decided to discontinue/exit the EPC business due to Right of Way (ROW) and NHAI clearance issues; management is in talks to subcontract existing EPC work.
- *Continues record:* The call confirms the strategic exit from EPC and the proposed Rs 100 crore fundraise.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 FY27 revenue of around Rs 300 crores for the cables and conductors segment and Rs 40-50 crores from EPC.
- FY27 total revenue is guided at around Rs 500 crores.
- FY28 revenue for cables and conductors is guided at Rs 600 to Rs 700 crores.
- New plant capacity utilization is expected to reach 80% to 85% (or ~80-87%) by the end of H2 FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Strong demand for power transmission and distribution networks driven by government investments, grid strengthening, and data centers.
- Serving power utilities and infrastructure projects across more than 15 states with vendor approvals from various state electricity boards.
- Expanding footprint beyond Eastern/North-Eastern regions into Central (MP, Chhattisgarh), Southern India, and looking at larger states like Maharashtra and UP in the future.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Average cost of borrowing is 8% to 9%.
- Finance cost and short-term borrowings surged due to Rs 40-43 crores of capex incurred over the last year.
- Cable business PAT margins are around 8%-9%.
- Management expects similar overall margin levels going forward despite the shift away from EPC.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Incurred Rs 40-43 crores in capex over the last year, expanding total installed capacity to 2,800 kilometers.
- Dankuni facility currently operating at 50% capacity, expected to reach full capacity/87% utilization by end of H2.
- Expanding product portfolio to include MVCC, AL-59, HTLS conductors, and HT cables (production starting in January for MVCC/conductors and next fiscal year for HT cables).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total borrowings stand at around Rs 118 crores as short-term borrowings increased to support higher execution and capex.
- Proposes to raise up to Rs 100 crores through a QIP or preferential issue for plant, machinery, land purchase, and working capital.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Faced difficulties with Right of Way (ROW) and clearances from NHAI in the EPC segment, leading to the business exit.
- Aggressive bidding is being avoided to keep debt from increasing further.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kaushal Agarwal asked about the utilization of the proposed Rs 100 crore fundraise and return ratios.** Management replied it is for further expansion (plant, machinery, land) and working capital; return metrics have not been fully calculated yet.
2. **Kaushal Agarwal asked about the jump in debt (to ~Rs 118 crores) and finance costs.** Management explained they incurred Rs 40-43 crores in capex over the last year, requiring extra short-term borrowings at an average cost of 8% to 9%.
3. **Shlok Agarwal questioned the flat PAT despite 35%+ income growth and lower EPC margins.** Management cited ROW and NHAI clearance issues in EPC as the reason for margin impact, noting cable PAT margins are around 8%-9%.
4. **Neha Gupta asked about the operational capacity and potential turnover of the new plant.** Management stated it is running at 50% now, expected to reach 80%-85% by end of H2, with maximum practical capacity yielding around Rs 300 crores.
5. **Rahul Mehta asked how the company manages working capital and cash flows during expansion.** Management reiterated they are proposing a Rs 100 crore fundraise to manage working capital and expansion.

### One-line takeaway
JD Cables reported 38.25% revenue growth in H1 FY27, expanded manufacturing capacity at Dankuni, and announced a strategic exit from the troubled EPC segment to focus entirely on its high-demand cable and conductor business.
