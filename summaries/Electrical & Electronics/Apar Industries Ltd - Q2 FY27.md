# Apar Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong order books, record H1 performance, and management confidence in H2 execution despite temporary volume headwinds.

### Key numbers
*   **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 6,931 crore (+21.3% YoY).
*   **EBITDA (post-forex):** Rs 787 crore (+57.8% YoY).
*   **EBITDA Margin:** 11.4% (+270 bps YoY).
*   **PAT:** Rs 447 crore (+77.8% YoY).
*   **PAT Margin:** 6.5% (+200 bps YoY).
*   **H1 Revenue:** Rs 13,522 crore (+25% YoY).
*   **H1 PAT:** Rs 915 crore (+77.7% YoY).

### Highlights
*   **Divisional Revenue:** Conductors Rs 3,753 crore (+21.1% YoY); Oil Rs 1,819 crore (+35.6% YoY); Cables Rs 1,875 crore (+22.1% YoY).
*   **Order Book:** All-time high of Rs 12,025 crore (Conductors/Cables).
*   **Fundraise:** Successfully completed QIP raising Rs 2,500 crore.
*   **Credit Rating:** Upgraded from AA- to AA.
*   **Revenue Recognition:** Rs 800 crore of conductors/cables in transit (DDP basis) impacted Q2 revenue recognition.

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management expects H2 to see more aggressive execution in transmission lines.
*   Expects conductor volume decline to reverse in Q3/Q4.
*   Cable division targeting 25% YoY growth and 11-12% EBITDA margins.
*   Management remains confident in FY27 performance.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Domestic:** India added 8,775 circuit km of transmission lines (+153% YoY) in H1.
*   **Policy:** Government approved 135 GW renewable energy evacuation (Rs 1.86 trillion investment) and mandated N-1 reliability, expected to accelerate reconductoring.
*   **International:** US "SPARK" program (USD 1.9 billion) driving reconductoring demand.
*   **Data Centers:** Significant orders received from Google (US) and Amazon (India) for data center cables.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Commodity Inflation:** Copper (+50%), Aluminum (+35%), Base oils (+100%).
*   **EBITDA per metric ton (Conductors):** Rs 48,241 (vs Rs 39,363 YoY).
*   **EBITDA per KL (Oil):** Rs 23,262 (vs Rs 6,570 YoY).
*   **Operating Philosophy:** Shifted to higher-margin, quality-focused orders to mitigate volatility.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Domestic Capex:** Rs 1,500 crore planned over 12-15 months; Rs 500 crore spent in H1.
*   **Future Capex:** Expected to normalize to Rs 300-400 crore annually post-current phase.
*   **Overseas:** Enabling resolution passed for up to $100 million investment in Singapore subsidiary for potential overseas expansion.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Trade Payables:** Days reduced to 81 days (from 109 days in March '26) due to cash purchases using QIP funds.
*   **Acceptances:** Rs 3,500 crore as of Sept '26.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Volume Headwinds:** Conductor volumes declined 9.2% YoY due to delayed manufacturing clearances and TBCB order awards.
*   **Geopolitics:** Middle East conflict impacting oil division logistics and spot buying.
*   **Volatility:** High volatility in commodity prices and freight rates (though freight rates to Europe/LatAm are starting to ease).
*   **Penalties:** Stringent penalties on TBCB projects for delays.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   *Nitin Arora (Axis MF):* Asked about aluminum profitability guidance. Management noted they don't give quarterly guidance but expect tailwinds; emphasized premiumization.
*   *Mohit Kumar:* Asked about HTLS opportunity. Management confirmed the market pie is growing despite competition.
*   *Umesh Raut:* Asked about QIP deployment. Management confirmed funds used for working capital/cash purchases.
*   *Vidit Trivedi:* Asked about data center cable margins. Management noted US data center cables offer better margins than Indian ones due to higher specs.
*   *Achal Lohade:* Asked about capacity utilization. Management stated cables are running at 90%+ (with some sections at 100%+), while conductors are at ~80%.

### One-line takeaway
Strong order book and margin expansion driven by premiumization and data center demand offset temporary volume headwinds, with management expecting a catch-up in H2 execution.
