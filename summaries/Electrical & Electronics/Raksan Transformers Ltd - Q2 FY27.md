# Raksan Transformers Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-23 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong order book and expansion plans, offset by margin compression due to higher raw material costs and lower-margin B2G product mix.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 121.1 crore (H1 FY27)
- EBITDA: Rs 8.2 crore (H1 FY27)
- EBITDA Margin: 6.8% (H1 FY27)
- PAT: Rs 4.9 crore (H1 FY27)
- PAT Margin: 4.1% (H1 FY27)
- Order Book: Approx. Rs 427 crore as of September 2026 (plus additional Rs 46 crore recently secured)
- Net Worth: Rs 195 crore
- Debt-to-Equity Ratio: 0.14x
- Trade Receivables: Declined by 29%
- IPO Fresh Issue Proceeds Utilized: Rs 31.72 crore out of Rs 120.47 crore (26.3% utilization as of September 30, 2026)
- Current Manufacturing Capacities (Power Transformers): Approx. 1,350 MVA per annum
- Expanded Manufacturing Capacity Target: 3,250 to 4,000 MVA per annum
- New Facility Investment (Liwaspur, Sonipat): Approx. Rs 72.58 crore

### Highlights
- Completed listing on the BSE SME platform in September 2026.
- H1 FY27 revenue impacted by a higher share of lower-capacity aluminium transformers supplied under B2G orders and deferment of certain customer orders.
- Trade receivables declined by 29%, reflecting improved collections and working capital management.
- Setting up a new manufacturing facility at Liwaspur, Sonipat (1.07 lakh sq. ft.), funded partly via IPO proceeds, expected to be operational within the current financial year.
- Expansion will enable entry into higher capacity transformers (up to 100 MVA) and voltage classes up to 220 kV (with infrastructure built for up to 400 kV) over the next 2-3 years.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects FY27 revenue to close around last year's level (Rs 363 crore) plus or minus 5%-10%.
- Management targets peak revenue of approximately Rs 1,500 crore from the new facility within 4 to 5 years of full ramp-up.
- Management projects long-term steady-state PAT margins of around 10% to 11%.
- No major capex planned for the next 3 to 4 years post the current expansion.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains strong across conventional power infrastructure, industrial applications, and renewable energy.
- Order book mix: 85% to 90% contributed by private sector customers and EPC companies.
- Discontinuing participation in tenders below the 200 kV/smaller capacity classes and shifting focus to higher-capacity copper transformers.
- Operating across 17 states in India with supplies ranging from Delhi-NCR to remote regions like Leh-Ladakh, Bangladesh, Myanmar, and Nepal borders.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA and PAT margins impacted by a sharp surge in raw material prices (aluminium, copper, steel, and transformer oil).
- Transformer oil prices more than doubled from Rs 68 per litre in February to Rs 148 per litre due to geopolitical conflicts affecting supplies.
- Lag in passing on 100% of cost increases due to initial stage fixed-rate orders; approximately 65%-70% of current orders contain Price Variation (PV) clauses with a one-month lag.
- Fixed-price orders comprise only about 10% of the current order book.
- Private sector margins are guided at 15% plus, while B2G distribution transformer margins range between 3% to 5%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Existing manufacturing facilities at Rai, Sonipat span ~33,000 sq. ft. and are operating at peak capacity.
- New Liwaspur facility (~1.07 lakh sq. ft., Rs 72.58 crore investment) is expected to commence commercial production within the current financial year, subject to GRAP/construction restrictions in Delhi-NCR.
- Out of IPO proceeds, Rs 3.60 crore has been deployed towards capex for the Liwaspur facility as of September 30, 2026.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Healthy capital structure with a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.14x and net worth of Rs 195 crore.
- B2G payments are received on time due to MSME regulations; B2B transactions are secured exclusively via LCs or BGs.
- Total interest paid in the previous financial year was only Rs 1 crore on a revenue of Rs 363 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions impacting raw material availability and pricing (e.g., transformer oil).
- Time lag in passing on raw material cost increases under fixed-price contracts.
- Potential execution delays for the new facility if construction restrictions (GRAP) in Delhi-NCR persist.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Ojasvi Mohta (AARTH AIF) asked about the reasons for lower H1 revenue and higher other expenses.** Management explained that lower revenue was due to a product mix skewed toward lower-value B2G aluminium transformers, while PAT margins were hit by more than doubling transformer oil prices and raw material spikes where cost-pass-through had a time lag.
2. **Pujan Shah (Analyst) inquired about the fixed vs. variable order book split and margin trajectory.** Management stated that fixed-price contracts represent only about 10% of the ~Rs 425 crore order book, with the remaining 90% on variable/price-variation terms, insulating future margins from commodity corrections.
3. **Pujan Shah asked about peak revenue expectations from the new facility.** Management reiterated its target of reaching ~Rs 1,500 crore in revenue within 4-5 years at optimum utilization.
4. **Prakash Pandey (Planify Capital) questioned the capex timeline and future major investments.** Management stated that 80%-85% of capex will be invested this year if GRAP is lifted, and no major capex is planned for the next 3 to 4 years post-expansion.
5. **Sahil Chopra asked whether new approvals would be required for the new plant.** Management clarified that brand approvals ("Raksan") apply across units, though specific higher-capacity type tests (like 30 MVA IDT) are being newly established.

### One-line takeaway
Raksan Transformers reported a muted H1 FY27 due to a lower-value B2G product mix and raw material cost inflation, but remains confident of scaling up significantly once its new Sonipat facility becomes operational.
