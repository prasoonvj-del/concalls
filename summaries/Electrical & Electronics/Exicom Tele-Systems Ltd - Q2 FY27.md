# Exicom Tele-Systems Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-16 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlights a turnaround in consolidated EBITDA, record EV charging revenue, and a strategic decision to divest the loss-making Tritium subsidiary to improve the balance sheet.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** No specific numerical guidance provided; management deferred FY28 guidance to Q4 results.
- **Tone and demand view:** Shifted from "Tritium turnaround expected" to "Divestment to focus on core India markets."
- **New topics:** Divestment of Tritium (non-binding term sheet for $85 million); pivot to data center power infrastructure (via Tritium before divestment).
- **Old topics:** Tritium turnaround plan (now replaced by divestment).
- **Last quarter's promises:** Hyderabad plant transition is "substantially complete" (delivered).

### Key numbers
- **Standalone Revenue:** Rs 277 crore (+21.3% YoY, +17% QoQ).
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 388 crore (+37.8% YoY, +17.3% QoQ).
- **Standalone EV Charging Revenue:** Rs 106 crore (+58.7% YoY).
- **Consolidated EV Charging Revenue:** Rs 214 crore (+91.3% YoY).
- **Standalone EBITDA:** Rs 25.6 crore (9.2% margin).
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Positive Rs 1.8 crore (vs loss of Rs 32.7 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **Consolidated PAT:** Loss of Rs 57.8 crore (improved from loss of Rs 68.8 crore YoY).
- **Order Book:** Rs 1,645 crore (includes advanced pipeline).
- **Tritium Backlog:** 23 million (orders).

### Highlights
- Consolidated EBITDA turned positive (Rs 1.8 crore) for the first time in recent history.
- EV Charging division crossed Rs 100 crore standalone revenue for the first time.
- Hyderabad plant transition is "substantially complete," leading to normalized operations and reduced fixed costs.
- Board approved 100% divestment of Tritium (non-binding term sheet at $85 million enterprise value, cash-free/debt-free basis); deal expected by Dec 2026.
- Exicom to retain rights for "Tri-Flex" product in certain geographies and continue manufacturing power modules for Tritium.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to become a net cash company post-Tritium divestment.
- Export revenue target: 30% of sales within 3 years.
- BharatNet Phase 3: Revenue visibility of Rs 235 crore in FY27, doubling to Rs 478 crore in FY28.
- Battery Energy Storage System (BESS): Pilot scale revenue this year, scaling to Rs 150 crore next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- EV sales growing at 50% CAGR; industry requirement estimated at 12 lakh AC chargers and 25,000 DC chargers by FY30.
- Telecom: 5G rollout driving demand for power conversion systems (60k-80k units/year) and lithium-ion batteries (8 lakh units/year).
- Tritium: Strategic pivot to serve data centers (AI infrastructure) noted before divestment decision.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating leverage visible: Revenue grew while fixed costs remained stable.
- Margins impacted by rising commodity, semiconductor, and forex costs.
- Depreciation: Rs 10.8 crore in Q2 (full impact of Hyderabad plant).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Hyderabad plant fully operational; inventory reduced to Rs 236.6 crore (from Rs 256.8 crore).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Standalone borrowings: Rs 345.6 crore (down from Rs 361.1 crore in March 2026).
- Net debt: Rs 326.8 crore.
- Net working capital: Rs 414 crore (lower than March).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Tritium divestment: Potential write-down expected.
- Competition: Intense in EV charging; price pass-through is difficult in some segments.
- Execution: Project timelines (e.g., BharatNet) may shift.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q: Why divest Tritium if it was nearing breakeven?** A: Management cited the need for massive additional investment in working capital and engineering for data center pivot, while core India markets offer better returns.
- **Q: Will there be a write-down on Tritium?** A: Yes, management expects some level of write-down, but believes it will be compensated by future power module supply contracts.
- **Q: Are manufacturing agreements with Tritium exclusive?** A: No, but they expect substantial revenue.
- **Q: Can higher costs be passed on in BharatNet?** A: No, these are government tenders with fixed pricing.

### One-line takeaway
Exicom delivered a turnaround quarter with positive consolidated EBITDA and record EV revenue, while pivoting to a "capital-light" strategy by divesting the loss-making Tritium subsidiary to focus on high-growth domestic markets.
