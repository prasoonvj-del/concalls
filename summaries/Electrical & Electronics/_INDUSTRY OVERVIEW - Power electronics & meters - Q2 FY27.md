# Electrical & Electronics - Power electronics & meters: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Management commentary across the Power Electronics & Meters sector in Q2 FY27 reflects top-line resilience and volume-led growth driven by domestic infrastructure, smart metering, railways, and data centers. However, operating margins across most players faced downward pressure due to geopolitical disruptions, elevated freight and input costs (copper, polycarbonate, silver), and time lags in passing on price hikes—particularly in fixed-price government contracts. Companies are responding by sharpening their focus on high-margin segments, pivoting toward capital-light strategies (such as Exicom divesting its loss-making Tritium subsidiary), and expanding specialized domestic manufacturing capabilities.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus Agreement:** Managements broadly agree that domestic infrastructure demand is robust. Sectors like railways (Vande Bharat and freight locomotives), smart metering, and data center buildouts are driving strong order pipelines across companies like HPL Electric, RIR Power, and Rishabh Instruments.
* **Divergence:** Opinions diverge significantly on the Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) utility-scale segment. While Prostarm Info Systems reported headwinds, margin pressure, and aggressive bidding in utility-scale BESS—prompting a strategic pivot to C&I applications and a down-sell of major developer projects—Exicom remains bullish on BESS scaling (targeting Rs 150 crore next year) alongside massive demand in EV charging and telecom infrastructure.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
Input cost volatility was a universal theme. Geopolitical tensions in West Asia and forex fluctuations inflated freight, energy, insurance, and raw material costs (metals, industrial plastics, copper, CRGO steel, silver, and PCBs). 
* **Fixed-price contracts vs. price pass-through:** HPL Electric, Exicom, and Rishabh Instruments noted that fixed-price government and smart metering tenders prevent immediate cost pass-through, impacting margins. HPL and Rishabh are relying on internal R&D, design optimization, and post-Diwali price hikes to recover margins.
* **Margin Impact:** Prostarm cut its EBITDA margin guidance to "above 11%" and RIR experienced a minor drop in operational EBITDA margin (to 16.6%) due to raw material and event expenses. Conversely, Exicom achieved a milestone by turning consolidated EBITDA positive (Rs 1.8 crore), and TVS Electronics saw sequential EBITDA margin expansion to 2.93% following turnaround initiatives in its Customer Support Services segment.

## Capex & capacity
* **Exicom Tele-Systems:** Substantially completed its Hyderabad plant transition, leading to normalized operations and reduced fixed costs.
* **HPL Electric & Power:** Metering capacity utilization runs at ~70%; wires and cables flexible manufacturing at ~80% following the addition of 7-8 new machines.
* **Prostarm Info Systems:** Commenced commercial operations at its 1.2 GWh BESS facility in Jhajjar and its UPS assembly unit in Bakrol on September 25, 2026.
* **RIR Power Electronics:** Completed erection and installation of silicon carbide (SiC) epitaxial wafer reactors at its Odisha facility (India's first vertically integrated SiC facility), awaiting final statutory clearances.
* **Rishabh Instruments:** Actively ramping up capacity at its newly expanded Nashik facility, allocating incremental capex of Rs 10-20 crore annually for SMT lines.
* **TVS Electronics:** Operating its EMS assembly line at 40-45% utilization, planning a second SMT line when utilization crosses 70-80%.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Exicom Tele-Systems:** Shifted tone from expecting a "Tritium turnaround" to a strategic decision to divest the loss-making subsidiary via a non-binding term sheet ($85 million valuation) to focus on core India markets.
* **HPL Electric & Power:** Introduced in-principle board approval for company restructuring/segregation of businesses; retail outlet count reached >90,000 (nearing the 100,000 target).
* **Prostarm Info Systems:** Cut full-year EBITDA margin guidance from 12-13% to "above 11%"; shifted focus away from utility-scale BESS bidding to C&I and initiated steps to divest its two major BESS developer projects.
* **Rishabh Instruments:** Maintained core EEI top-line guidance (20-25%) while acknowledging rising supply lead times and commodity price volatility.
