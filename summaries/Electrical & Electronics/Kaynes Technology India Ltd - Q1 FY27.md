# Kaynes Technology India Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-12 · Electrical & Electronics*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27  
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong EMS top-line growth and healthy order book were offset by supply chain cost pressures, margin softeness, and working capital drag in smart meters.

### Key numbers
- **Total Revenue:** Rs 946 crore, up 40% YoY.
- **EBITDA:** Rs 147.6 crore, up 31% YoY.
- **EBITDA Margin:** 15.6%.
- **Order Book:** ~Rs 9,000 crore (Rs 8,900+ crore).
- **Standalone EMS Revenue:** Rs 639 crore, up 53% YoY (from Rs 480 crore).
- **Overseas Business Revenue:** Rs 102 crore, up 327% YoY (from Rs 24 crore, boosted by August Electronics acquisition).
- **Smart Metering (GridCrest) Revenue:** Rs 204 crore, down 12% YoY (from Rs 231 crore).

### Highlights
- Scaled back smart metering production consciously due to slow collections (collected Rs 88 crore against Rs 240 crore sales including GST), prioritizing core EMS cash flows. Received Rs 200 crore in the first week of July for GridCrest.
- Reduced receivables from top 2-wheeler EV customer to below Rs 100 crore.
- Onboarded India’s second-largest EV 2-wheeler OEM and new global brands from Germany and France; received major supplier awards from Mahindra and Siemens.
- Received Rs 170 crore government subsidy for OSAT till July 2026.
- Entered into a partnership between Mitsui and Kaynes Semicon for market access in Japan.
- Prototype phase underway for the first 3U satellite for launch in mid-2027; entering titanium gas bottle manufacturing for ISRO and DRDO via subsidiaries.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects company top-line growth to track at 2x market growth for FY27.
- Targets combined full-year revenue of Rs 450–500 crore from OSAT and PCB businesses starting Q3 FY27.
- Management expects profitability to take a couple of quarters to normalize due to macro cost escalations.
- Management remains confident of turning cash flow positive by the end of FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global supply chain lead times expanded to 6–8 months in certain component categories due to West Asia geopolitical disruptions.
- PCB industry is facing severe global shortages and price increases (up 3x), requiring advance payments and longer booking windows.
- Domestic and global customer off-take remains strong across automotive, aerospace, defense, and industrial segments despite inflation.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margins faced headwinds from forex movements (3.3% impact on imports), elevated energy, crude, commodity, and consumable costs.
- Component prices increased 10%–12% to 35% on average, with availability emerging as a severe constraint; management passes through cost increases to customers on a quarterly lag.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total FY27 planned capex is Rs 850 crore: Rs 300 crore for OSAT, Rs 300 crore for PCB, and Rs 250 crore for EMS.
- Q1 FY27 capex spent was Rs 180 crore (Rs 90 crore OSAT, Rs 90 crore PCB) plus Rs 50 crore for EMS. Cumulative capital spent to date is ~Rs 1,200 crore (Rs 700 crore OSAT, Rs 500 crore PCB), with Rs 250 crore equipment in transit.
- Kaynes Semicon Unit 2 and Kaynes Circuit Chennai are on track to be commercially operational by Q3 FY27. Entire PCB capacity requested/trialed by a marquee global EV player.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash flow from operations (CFO) was negative Rs 235 crore to Rs 259 crore for Q1 FY27, impacted by strategic inventory build-up (+Rs 156–177 crore).
- Total receivables increased to Rs 1,925 crore (up from Rs 1,765 crore at FY26 end), driven primarily by smart metering (Rs 1,311 crore). Net working capital days stood at 190 days for the quarter (163 days on 12-month rolling basis).
- Total debt is ~Rs 800 crore, with a conservative debt-to-equity ratio of ~0.3x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global supply chain disruptions and extended component/PCB lead times.
- Working capital lock-up and slow collection cycles in the B2C/utility-facing smart meter segment.
- Short-term profitability/gross margin compression due to delayed pass-through of rising input and logistics costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q (IIFL Capital):** Are there plans to divest or restructure the smart metering business to free up working capital?  
  **A (Management):** Management is actively evaluating strategic options to de-risk receivables and separate the service/O&M portfolio while keeping high-margin EMS manufacturing; plans will be communicated by February.
- **Q (CLSA):** What is the capex guidance for FY27/FY28 and status of PCB contracts?  
  **A (Management):** Reiterate FY27 capex of Rs 300 crore for OSAT, Rs 300 crore for PCB, and Rs 250 crore for EMS. The entire PCB plant capacity is being evaluated and trial-run by a major global player.
- **Q (Avendus Spark):** Is smart metering seen as core EMS or a separate ballgame?  
  **A (Management):** 60%–65% of smart meter business revenue is pure EMS manufacturing. While full solution/installation capabilities give a unique edge, long capex/opex payback cycles necessitate restructuring the service portion to protect the balance sheet.
- **Q (PL Capital):** Will component availability and price spikes compresses gross margins over the next 9 months?  
  **A (Management):** Acknowledged FY27 will be a tough year due to severe availability bottlenecks and lag in passing through 10%–12% component cost hikes, but customer line-stop mitigations are prioritized.

### One-line takeaway
Kaynes demonstrated robust core EMS growth (+53%) and progress on OSAT/PCB facilities, but earnings remain temporarily constrained by global component supply chain bottlenecks and working capital drag in smart meters.
