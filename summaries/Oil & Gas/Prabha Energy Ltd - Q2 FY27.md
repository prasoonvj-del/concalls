# Prabha Energy Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Oil & Gas*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management expressed confidence in upcoming pipeline connectivity and significant production ramp-up potential by FY28-FY29.

### Key numbers
*   **Current production:** ~20,000 cubic meters/day (via cascades).
*   **Target peak production:** ~1.5 MMSCMD (15 lakh cubic meters/day) combined (North Karanpura: ~9 lakh; Jharia: ~7 lakh).
*   **North Karanpura (NK) wells:** 68 drilled.
*   **Jharia wells:** 8 drilled (Phase 1); 47 planned (Phase 2).
*   **Revenue share:** 25% for North Karanpura; 90% for Jharia.
*   **Payback period:** <4 years (post-optimum production).

### Highlights
*   **Pipeline Connectivity:** Jharia block connectivity to Urja Ganga pipeline expected by January 2027; North Karanpura connectivity (via IOCL) expected by March 2027.
*   **Operational Progress:** North Karanpura coal seam thickness identified at ~65 meters (vs 35 meters envisaged).
*   **Business Model:** Transitioning from test production to commercial sales; currently selling via cascades due to lack of grid connectivity.
*   **Market Dynamics:** Global gas prices increased >130% in 8 months (USD 10.8 to USD 25 per MMBtu).

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management expects significant turnover increase from FY28 onwards.
*   Peak production for both blocks is targeted for FY28-FY29.
*   Management plans to continue evaluating additional CBM and marginal field auction opportunities.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   India’s gas demand is rising, with import dependence at ~50-52%.
*   Government target is to increase natural gas share in the energy mix to 15% (currently 6.5%).
*   Geopolitical tensions (Middle East) are creating supply constraints and inflating gas prices.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Operating cost:** Estimated at ~20%.
*   **EBITDA margin:** Expected to reach 70-75% at optimum capacity.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Jharia Capex:** ~Rs 500 crore planned for the next 47 wells, starting next financial year.
*   **North Karanpura:** Majority of capex already completed.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Funding Strategy:** For Jharia capex, management targets 50% equity and 50% debt/internal accruals.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Execution Risk:** Long gestation period (5-6 years from award to production).
*   **Infrastructure:** Pipeline connectivity remains the primary bottleneck for scaling revenue.
*   **Geopolitical:** Volatility in global energy markets impacts pricing and supply security.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: What is the peak production timeline?** A: Management expects peak production in FY28-FY29.
*   **Q: How does Jharia compare to North Karanpura in revenue terms?** A: Jharia is more revenue-accretive due to a 90% revenue share compared to 25% in North Karanpura.
*   **Q: How will the Jharia capex be funded?** A: Management plans a mix of 50% equity and 50% debt/internal accruals.
*   **Q: Why is revenue currently modest?** A: Management stated that current sales are limited to cascades; significant revenue will only materialize post-pipeline connectivity.
*   **Q: What is the accounting treatment for wells?** A: Expenditure is currently capitalized as it is in the test production stage; capitalization will cease once commercial sales ramp up.

### One-line takeaway
Prabha Energy is transitioning from an infrastructure-development phase to commercial production, with key pipeline connectivity milestones in early 2027 expected to drive a significant ramp-up in volumes and revenue by FY28-FY29.
