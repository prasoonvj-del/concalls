# Maximus International Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-26 · Oil & Gas*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consolidated revenue grew over 51% YoY driven by scaling international operations, despite margin pressures from input costs.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue from Operations:** Rs 59.91 crore (up ~51% to 52% YoY from Rs 39.52 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 4.58 crore (up ~18% YoY from Rs 3.88 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 7.64% (down from 9.81% in Q1 FY26).
- **Profit Before Tax (PBT):** Rs 2.41 crore (compared to Rs 2.48 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Consolidated Net Profit (PAT):** Rs 2.05 crore (compared to Rs 2.33 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Finance Cost:** Rs 1.53 crore (up by ~Rs 0.60 crore / 60 lakhs YoY from Rs 0.92 crore).
- **Capacity Utilisation:** ~45% across the 50,000 KL installed blending capacity (UAE and Kenya).
- **Premium/Specialty Products Contribution:** ~40% of top line.
- **Top 10 Customers Concentration:** ~70% to 75% of revenue.
- **FY26 Full Year Reference:** Revenue reached Rs 184.81 crore; EBITDA stood at Rs 13.53 crore; PAT reached Rs 15 crore.

### Highlights
- Strong top-line growth supported by scaling manufacturing, trading, and distribution activities.
- Ongoing modernization program includes automated batch blending, storage/warehousing infrastructure, improved filling lines, and capacity expansion in UAE and Kenya plants.
- Progressing with the acquisition of a 40% stake in Quebec Petroleum (an Indian lubricant manufacturer) as an associate to establish a footing in the Indian domestic market.
- Planning to commission a grease manufacturing facility at the Kenya plant in Q3 FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management conservatively estimates crossing Rs 200 crore in revenue for FY27 with healthy EBITDA margins.
- Long-term target for business vertical split: 75% to 80% from manufacturing and toll blending combined, and 20% to 25% from trading.
- Strategy includes organic (East Africa expansion, Tanzania warehousing/distribution entry) and inorganic growth (Quebec Petroleum associate acquisition).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Positive long-term outlook for the lubricant sector supported by industrialization, infrastructure, rising vehicle ownership, and consumption across manufacturing, mining, power, and construction.
- Customers are gradually moving towards premium, specialty, and higher-performing lubricants.
- Middle East and Africa lubricant market projected to grow from ~$9.1 billion in 2024 to ~$12.4 billion by 2034.
- In Kenya, major players like Shell and Total maintain traditional payment terms, meaning competitor entry has not disrupted standard market terms.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margins shrank percentage-wise due to significant increase in revenue mix changes and input costs inflected by the ongoing Middle East war (impacting raw material base oils/white oils and shipment/landed expenses like freight and insurance).
- Raw material prices are positively correlated with crude oil prices, though not directly coupled or impacted immediately/identically.
- Finance costs increased by ~Rs 60 lakhs YoY due to higher top-line operations and working capital utilization.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Overall manufacturing capacity stands at ~50,000 KL across UAE and Kenya (operating at ~45% capacity utilisation on a single shift).
- Existing capacity is sufficient for the next 2 to 3 years, though additional storage capex may be needed if full utilisation is reached.
- Setting up a grease manufacturing facility in Kenya, targeted for Q3 FY27 commissioning.
- Evaluating next-phase expansion into Tanzania, starting with warehousing and distribution before moving to manufacturing.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Trade receivables increased in tandem with the ~50% revenue growth, higher product prices, and landed freight/insurance expenses from supply disruptions.
- Management remains focused on working capital discipline and efficient capital deployment.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Ongoing Middle East war has driven up raw material (base oils/white oils) and shipment/transportation costs, compressing percentage EBITDA margins.
- High customer concentration, with top 10 customers contributing ~70% to 75% of revenue.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Ramesh Menaria (Menaria Investments) asked about base oil/crude price sensitivity:** Management clarified that product prices are not directly coupled with crude oil, but positively correlated through base and white oils; costs are impacted with a lag and not in the exact same proportion. *(Answered clearly)*
- **Varun (Makeflow Capital) asked about the impact if supply disruptions/war continue for 6 more months:** Management stated they have onboarded new clients, diversified product baskets, and managed top line; even in this scenario, they conservatively estimate crossing Rs 200 crore revenue in FY27. *(Answered clearly)*
- **Varun asked about Quebec Petroleum's revenue contribution strategy:** Management explained Quebec is acquired as a 40% associate to provide a strong footing in the Indian domestic market, adding their profits to the consolidated balance sheet while complementing Maximus's global reach in Middle East and Africa. *(Answered clearly)*
- **Varun asked about the 3-year target split between trading and toll blending:** Management grouped manufacturing and toll blending together at 75%-80% and trading at 20%-25%, noting aggressive pursuit of toll blending (including talks with an MNC in East Africa). *(Answered clearly)*
- **Ramesh Menaria asked about top 10 customer concentration:** Management confirmed top 10 customers account for ~70% to 75% due to a B2B and distributor-led model rather than direct B2C retail. *(Answered clearly)*

### One-line takeaway
Maximus International reported a 51% YoY surge in Q1 FY27 revenue to Rs 59.91 crore, driven by international scaling, though percentage EBITDA margins moderated to 7.64% due to Middle East war-induced raw material and freight inflation.
