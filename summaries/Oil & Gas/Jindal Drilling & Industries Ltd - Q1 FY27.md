# Jindal Drilling & Industries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-14 · Oil & Gas*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management noted that while Q1 results were in line, H2 revenue will decline due to dehirings and refurbishment, and customers are pushing down day rates compared to international levels.

### Key numbers
- Order book: INR1,310 crores
- Day rate for recent ONGC contract (Jindal Pioneer): INR45.83 lakhs (approx. INR45 lakhs / $47,800 equivalent vs. $62,000 bid rate)
- Refurbishment cost per rig (estimated): INR90 crores to INR110 crores
- Legal dispute amount (if lost): INR163 crores total (INR63 crores original receivable + interest/forex)
- JV loss in Q1: INR5-odd crores (due to refurbishment expenses incurred by the joint venture for Jindal Pioneer)

### Highlights
- Received a new contract from ONGC for Jindal Pioneer (currently under refurbishment in the UAE; deployment expected as early as October 2026).
- Remaining the largest offshore jack-up drilling contractor based in India with ONGC, with 5 rigs on long-term contracts currently deployed and the 6th recently contracted.
- Total revenue broadly constant with previous quarters; EBITDA variation primarily driven by forex fluctuations in other expenses.
- Maintained a cash-rich position despite acquiring a rig in FY25 and undertaking refurbishment.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue to decline in the second half of FY27 as three rigs (Discovery-I, Virtue-I, and Jindal Star) are dehired and sent for refurbishment (4 to 6 months per rig with no revenue accrual).
- Management does not believe EBITDA will decline in proportion to the revenue drop, and EBITDA margins might even increase.
- Management expects day rates to improve for redeployment, though cautioned based on recent ONGC downward pricing pressure.
- Guidance: target a blended EBITDA margin of 35%.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Historically, dehired rigs were immediately redeployed via new ONGC contracts, but all drilling contractors faced redeployment challenges over the past two years.
- Management is hopeful the next three dehiring rigs will be rehired due to the Government of India's "Samudra Manthan" exercise.
- While Samudra Manthan focuses on deep and ultra-deep waters, management believes general increases in drilling activity will indirectly benefit shallow-water capacities.
- International deployment is secondary due to local preferences, different criteria, counterparty risks, and country risks.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Refurbishment cost per rig estimated between INR90 crores to INR110 crores due to inflationary trends, increased labor costs, and transit material costs.
- Joint venture loss of INR5-odd crores in Q1 driven by refurbishment expenses to bring Jindal Pioneer to required delivery condition.
- Blended EBITDA margin target: 35%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Jindal Pioneer refurbishment expected to complete by the first week of September 2026 with deployment in October 2026.
- Currently not looking at acquisitions to minimize risk and conserve cash for upcoming rig refurbishments.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash position expected to improve going forward, though cash must be conserved for upcoming rig refurbishments (3 rigs going into refurbishment in H2).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- H2 revenue decline due to 4-to-6-month refurbishment downtime for three rigs coming off-hire.
- ONGC contract pricing pressure (pushed down from bid rate of $62,000 to $47,800 equivalent).
- Long-standing Supreme Court legal dispute with ONGC (~14-15 years running); potential exposure of INR163 crores if lost (deemed remote by management and lawyers).
- Inability to control customer willingness to pay correct international-level day rates.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Pankaj (AVIS Capital):** Asked about the likelihood and rates for the three rigs expiring in 2026 and refurbishment capex for *Pioneer*. Management stated 4-6 months of zero-revenue refurbishment is needed per rig, declined to comment on future day rates, and did not disclose specific spend on *Pioneer* (noting completion in early Sept and October deployment).
2. **Pankaj (AVIS Capital):** Asked if H2 revenue would be severely impacted. Management confirmed revenue will decline but stated EBITDA will not drop proportionally because earnings come primarily from 2 of the 3 dehired rigs, potentially driving higher margins.
3. **Pankaj (AVIS Capital):** Inquired about the Q1 JV loss of ~INR5 crores and cash deployment plans. Management clarified the loss was due to *Pioneer* refurbishment expenses borne by the JV, and stated they are *not* looking at acquisitions to minimize risk and conserve cash for upcoming refurbishments.
4. **Pankaj (AVIS Capital):** Asked about the ONGC Supreme Court dispute status and potential exposure. Management stated it has been ongoing for 14-15 years with remote chances of losing, but if lost, the exposure is INR163 crores (INR63 crore receivable plus interest/forex).
5. **CA Akash Dhanuka (Individual Investor):** Asked if shallow-water rigs benefit from the deep/ultra-deepwater "Samudra Manthan" scheme. Management answered indirectly, stating general increases in drilling activity benefit shallow water too.
6. **Adarsh Hinduja (Individual Investor):** Asked why the latest contract was in INR and not USD, and if international markets are targeted. Management told the analyst to ask ONGC regarding the currency, and stated they primarily target domestic markets due to local preferences and risk factors abroad.

### One-line takeaway
Jindal Drilling reported a stable Q1 FY27, but warned of a revenue dip in H2 as three rigs undergo 4-to-6-month refurbishments, while navigating compressed domestic day rates from ONGC.
