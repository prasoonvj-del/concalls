# Nath Bio-Genes (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-23 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 and H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue grew YoY, but profitability was impacted by weather vagaries, crop mix, and higher working capital and interest costs.

### Key numbers
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** INR 3,601 million (up ~12.6% YoY from INR 3,199 million in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** INR 410 million (down from INR 461 million in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** ~11.4% (down from 14.4% in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 PBT:** INR 365 million (compared to INR 437 million in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** INR 349 million (compared to INR 424 million in H1 FY26).
- **Cotton H1 Sales Volume:** ~1.06 million packets (down 2.29% YoY).
- **Cotton Revenue Share:** ~26% of overall sales in H1 FY27.
- **Paddy Revenue Share:** ~37% of overall sales (largest contributor in H1 FY27).
- **Bajra Revenue Share:** ~13% of overall sales in H1 FY27.
- **Maize Revenue Share:** ~6% of overall sales in H1 FY27.
- **Combined Crop Mix (Paddy + Cotton + Bajra):** ~76% of total reported top line.
- **Inventory (as of Sep '26):** INR 4,062 million (compared to INR 4,446 million as of Mar '26).
- **Trade Receivables (as of Sep '26):** INR 2,644 million (compared to INR 838 million as of Mar '26).
- **Uzbekistan JV H1 Sales (Consolidated):** INR 9 crore.

### Highlights
- Uneven rainfall and drought-like conditions in certain regions impacted agricultural activity and seed demand.
- Paddy emerged as the largest revenue contributor (37%), supported by favorable conditions in northern markets, offsetting cotton softness.
- Cotton seed inventory remains elevated due to higher production of key hybrids and lower-than-anticipated sales; management plans to gradually liquidate excess inventory over the next two to three years.
- Additional trade schemes and incentives (around INR 5 crores) were deployed to maintain top-line momentum, impacting EBITDA.
- Interest costs rose by INR 3.5–4 crore due to higher working capital borrowing for stock buildup.
- *Vs Record:* The 13% revenue growth reported in the H1FY27 investor deck is confirmed by financial results.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects cotton volumes to grow by 20–30% next year, supported by high commercial prices (INR 9,500–10,500).
- Management expects corn (maize) acreage and sales to grow by at least 20% in 2027–28 over 2026–27.
- Management aims for a long-term net profit margin of around 14%, which they believe is achievable once the top line crosses roughly INR 750 crore.
- Uzbekistan JV plans a soft launch of Bt-GT converted local varieties by 2028.

### Demand & industry commentary
- IMD reported June 2026 rainfall was 35% below normal (others reported 16% deficit), with uneven distribution affecting crop patterns.
- Government increased the minimum support price (MSP) for cotton for the 2026–27 season by approximately 7%.
- Commercial agricultural commodity prices are up 30–40% YoY, which may improve farmers' purchasing power.
- Illegal unapproved Bt cotton accounts for an estimated 20–25% of total sales, heavily impacting states like Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Telangana.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margins declined to 11.4% from 14.4% due to unfavorable crop/product mix and higher trade margins/schemes given to support top-line growth.
- Cost of capital remains competitive at an average of around 9%, but total interest expenses increased due to higher working capital utilization.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Uzbekistan joint venture (*Nath Bio-Genes Central Asia Limited*) is focusing on strengthening its market presence and converting local varieties into Bt-GT versions; agreement signed with the government of Uzbekistan.
- No immediate plans to enter other neighboring international countries until the Uzbekistan base is consolidated.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash flow from operating activities was negative at the end of H1 due to higher receivables and inventory.
- Elevated trade receivables (INR 2,644 million) reflect liquidity constraints across the agricultural distribution network and seasonality.
- Cotton seed inventory is sturdy, and no quality issues are expected during liquidation.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unfavorable weather conditions, El Nino risks strengthening through winter, and uneven monsoons impacting seed demand and farmer purchasing power.
- Persistence of illegal and unapproved Bt cotton sales affecting the formal seed market, with limited active government control.
- High working capital intensity leading to elevated receivables and increased interest burden.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Darshil Pandya (Individual Investor):** Asked about non-cotton, non-paddle (NCP) diversification. *Management replied that focus on maize, bajra, vegetables, and plant nutrients is yielding results, with paddy and maize cushioning cotton's decline.*
- **Josh Shah (Jash Diam):** Asked about the divergence between 13% revenue growth and 11% EBITDA decline. *Management stated additional trade schemes (around INR 5 crores) were deployed to protect the top line during market liquidity issues.*
- **Deepesh Sancheti (Maanya Finance):** Asked about illegal BT cotton impact. *Management stated illegal BT constitutes 20-25% of total sales, and while they lobby the government, enforcement remains weak.*
- **Prasad MD (Individual Investor):** Questioned when the company will reach the INR 100 crore net profit milestone discussed in past calls. *Management stated a 20% net margin is currently unrealistic, but a 14% net margin is achievable once revenues cross approximately INR 750 crore.*
- **Surya K (Poddar Family Office):** Asked about export revenue share and Uzbekistan JV. *Management clarified that exports are minimal (<INR 10 crores) and Uzbekistan operates as a separate subsidiary/JV focusing on local market consolidation without cash remittances from India.*

### One-line takeaway
Nath Bio-Genes reported 12.6% YoY revenue growth in H1 FY27 driven by resilience in paddy and non-cotton crops, but profitability and cash flows were pressured by weather disruptions, higher trade discounts, elevated working capital, and increased interest costs.
