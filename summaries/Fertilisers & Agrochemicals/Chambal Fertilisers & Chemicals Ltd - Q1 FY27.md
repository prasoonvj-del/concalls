# Chambal Fertilisers & Chemicals Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-06 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong financial performance and operational resilience amid a challenging operating environment marked by geopolitical disruptions, raw material inflation, and delayed monsoon onset.

### Key numbers
- **Standalone Revenue:** Rs 5,000 crore, lower by 12% YoY (from Rs 5,700 crore).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 851 crore, higher by 12% YoY.
- **EBITDA Margin:** 17%, an improvement of about 350 bps YoY (from ~13%).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 703 crore, higher by 10% YoY.
- **PAT Margin:** 14%, compared to 11% in the previous quarter.
- **Subsidy Received:** Around Rs 2,480 crore (vs Rs 2,512 crore YoY).
- **Total Receivables (as of June 30, 2026):** Rs 3,300 crore (comprising market debtors of Rs 841 crore and subsidy receivables of Rs 2,460 crore).
- **Net Debt (as of June 30, 2026):** Minus Rs 200 crore (net borrowing / net cash position).
- **Segment Revenue - Urea:** Rs 2,860 crore, lower by 8% YoY (from Rs 3,109 crore).
- **Segment Revenue - Complex Fertilizer:** Rs 1,737 crore, lower by 18% YoY (from Rs 2,131 crore); Segment EBIT rose 67% to around Rs 239 crore.
- **Segment Revenue - Crop Protection, Specialty Nutrients & Seeds:** Rs 430 crore, lower by 6% YoY (from Rs 458 crore); Segment EBIT grew 13% to INR 108 crores, margins at about 25%.
- **Urea Sales Volumes:** G1: 96,000 tonnes; G2: 1.85 lakh tonnes; G3: 3.29 lakh tonnes.

### Highlights
- Bunched plant shutdowns at Gadepan-1 and extended shutdowns at Gadepan-2 due to West Asian geopolitical situations; Gadepan-3 remained the predominant operational contributor.
- Expanded fertilizer portfolio with the introduction of ammonium sulfate grade and evaluated newer NPK grades.
- Crop protection business launched 7 new products (herbicides, fungicides, insecticides); seeds business added 2 new maize and bajra varieties.
- Biologicals business witnessed volume/revenue growth and recorded profitability.
- Partnered with TERI to establish CFCL-TERI Centre of Excellence for Advanced and Sustainable Agriculture Solutions; launched a bio-stimulant, Uttam Aris Gold.
- Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) project commenced production of Weak Nitric Acid and Ammonium Nitrate solutions; high-density Ammonium Nitrate (HDAN) moving closer to commissioning.
- Joint venture IMACID faced temporary production shutdowns due to Sulphur shortages and high prices, which resumed in July.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Q2 demand and sales to progress well and improve following accelerated July monsoon sowing and channel liquidation.
- Management expects complex fertilizer margins to decline as price averaging occurs, but aims to maintain certain levels.
- Management indicatively targets commissioning the new urea plant within 2030, with a projected EBITDA per ton around Rs 12,000.
- Management is evaluating overseas joint ventures for phosphatics.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Early Q1 Kharif sowing and fertilizer offtake were subdued due to delayed monsoon onset and El Nino conditions (summer crop sowing down ~23% YoY at 183 lakh hectares in June).
- Monsoon activity strengthened significantly in July, with acreage catching up to last year and IMD forecasting a normal monsoon.
- West Asian geopolitical developments disrupted global fertilizer/energy supply chains, spiking raw material prices (ammonia, sulphur, phosphatic intermediates) and freight costs.
- Nutrient-based subsidy rates for Kharif were revised upwards by ~10%, though industry is engaging with the Department of Fertilizers regarding subsequent cost escalations.
- Government approved the National Investment Policy for Urea 2026, providing a framework for future domestic urea capacity investments.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material prices (ammonia, sulphur, phosphatic intermediates) remained well above historical levels.
- Natural gas provisional price (NCV basis) for Q1 was USD 17.25/MMBtu.
- Complex fertilizer performance was supported by advance inventory purchases made from January onwards.
- Currency uptick directly impacted margins positively for Gadepan-3.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Preparatory activities continued for a potential fourth urea plant under NIP-2026, subject to Board approval; financial bids expected by mid-October 2026.
- TAN project progressing; Weak Nitric Acid and AN solutions production commenced, with HDAN commissioning imminent. Warehouses nearing completion.
- IMACID is expanding its phosphoric acid capacity from 500,000 tonnes to 700,000 tonnes, expected to be operational by mid-2027 (April-June 2027).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total receivables stood at Rs 3,300 crore as of June 30, 2026 (market debtors Rs 841 crore, subsidy receivables Rs 2,460 crore).
- Net borrowing/net cash position stood at minus Rs 200 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical uncertainties in West Asia causing raw material price volatility, procurement challenges, and freight cost escalations.
- Sulphur shortages and elevated prices impacting phosphoric acid production globally and at JV IMACID.
- Subdued early-season Kharif offtake due to delayed monsoon onset.
- Delays in government NPK subsidy announcements creating potential replenishment challenges.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Prashant (Elara Capital)** asked about timelines for the new urea plant application and financial bids. Management (Abhay Baijal) stated the application window is open, technical bids are in, and financial bids are expected by mid-October, with Board approval to follow.
2. **Viraj Kacharia (SiMPL)** asked why NUP-2026 is exciting despite being ROE-dilutive compared to older policies. Management explained that brownfield single-site scale benefits (four plants at Gadepan) create massive fixed cost, manpower, and operating efficiencies, alongside cross-sell opportunities.
3. **Dhruv Muchhal (HDFC AMC)** asked about drivers of improved urea profitability despite lower volumes. Management cited Gadepan-3 being the predominant contributor, a currency uptick, and surplus ammonia sales with better margins.
4. **Mayuresh (invest4Edu)** asked about risks of temporary oversupply in the domestic TAN market. Management acknowledged capacity expansion but noted infrastructure demand (roads, thermal capacity, mining) will absorb supply after a potential short-term overlap of up to a year.
5. **Darshita (DSP Asset Managers)** asked about the cost disadvantage of setting up a granulation plant in India vs. overseas. Management explained that outside battery limit (OSBL) costs—such as storage for rock, potash, and expensive ammonia tanks, plus gypsum disposal—make domestic greenfield projects more costly than international JVs.
6. **Viraj Kacharia (SiMPL)** asked about the rationale for exploring another phosphatic JV instead of going solo in India. Management stated that domestic execution time and high OSBL project costs are comparable to overseas setups, while IMACID offers massive existing scale and expansion benefits in Morocco.

### One-line takeaway
Chambal Fertilisers delivered a resilient Q1 FY27 with a 12% rise in EBITDA and 10% growth in PAT, supported by operational efficiency and advance inventory positioning, while advancing preparations for its next-phase urea and TAN expansions.
