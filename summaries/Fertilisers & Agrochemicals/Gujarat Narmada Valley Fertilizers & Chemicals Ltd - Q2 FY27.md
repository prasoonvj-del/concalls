# Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Uncertainty driven by volatile energy prices, geopolitical issues, and competitive pricing pressures impacting margins despite resilient operations.

### Key numbers
* **Capex (H1):** Rs 500 crore
* **Cash, bank balance, and deposits (as of Sept end):** Around Rs 3,900 crore
* **One-time net impact:** Around Rs 75 crore (across the board, including wage settlements, energy norms, and freight claims)
* **Employee cost variance (Q-o-Q):** Rs 33 crore (due to long-term wage settlement provisions)
* **Inventory liquidation impact:** Rs 236 crore reduction (cumulative impact with Q1 increase was Rs 533 crore)
* **Production volumes (Q2):** Ammonia: 1,78,000 tonnes; TGU (Technical Grade Urea): 44,900 tonnes; AN Melt: 41,000 tonnes; WNA (Weak Nitric Acid): 1,14,000 tonnes; CNA: 41,000 tonnes; TDI: 8,700 tonnes
* **Q2 Average Realizations/Prices:** TDI: Rs 2,85,000; Aniline: Rs 1,80,000; Weak Nitric Acid (72% concentration): Rs 28,500 to Rs 29,000 (basic price context); Acetic Acid: Rs 72,840

### Highlights
* Company weathered uncertainty by balancing a multiple-product portfolio.
* Fertilizer segment operating results improved, driven by volumes and better energy norms.
* Chemicals segment faced margin pressures due to realization drops in TGU, Ammonium Nitrate Melt, Weak Nitric Acid, and others.
* Captive coal-based plant began generating steam on July 25, 2025/2026 and catering to power in August; guaranteed test run scheduled for Q3.
* Certain plants (acetic acid, ethyl acetate, TDI) experienced non-production for 30–35 days in July due to cash-loss viability issues.

### Guidance & outlook
* Management refrained from making short-term predictions or giving forward-looking guidance due to extreme volatility and uncertain geopolitical conditions.
* Long-term focus remains on running steady operations and adding meaningful products to the pipeline.

### Demand & industry commentary
* Energy prices (oil, gas, coal) remain at escalated levels, increasing feed/fuel costs and government subsidy burdens.
* Competitors like China prevent full pass-through of soaring input costs.
* TDI Indian market size is 120 kt (growing at 8%–9% CAGR); GNFC production capacity is 70,000 tonnes per annum facing fierce competition from Chinese imports (Wanhua commissioned a 360 kt new plant).
* NITI Aayog is reviewing the fixed cost of the urea industry.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
* Sequential margins hit by lower finished product realizations (WNA, TGU, AN Melt, and others), higher coal fuel costs, and long-term wage settlement provisions (Rs 33 crore impact).
* Grid power is currently cheaper than captive coal power due to high coal prices, so the captive power plant is kept as a fallback while steam benefits are utilized.
* Sourcing is done strictly based on monthly production plans without speculative stocking.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
* H1 capex stood at Rs 500 crore.
* Ongoing projects are largely on schedule, with only one project facing a three-month delay (recouping plan discussed with LSTK contractor).
* Bharuch plant remains cost-competitive compared to Dahej, with no plans to shut it down.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
* Balance sheet remains in healthy shape with total cash, bank balances, and deposits around Rs 3,900 crore.
* Working capital levels increased primarily due to subsidy receivables where concession rates are tagged to previous periods' energy prices.

### Risks & concerns flagged
* High energy price volatility (oil, gas, coal) raising utility and input costs.
* Inability to fully pass on input cost hikes due to open-front competition from Chinese imports.
* Margin compression from falling realizations in key chemicals (TGU, AN Melt, Weak Nitric Acid).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Nirav (Anvil Wealth) asked about the sharp Q-o-Q drop in profitability despite steady spreads and lower oil prices.** Management explained the drop was driven by realization drops in four key chemicals (WNA, TGU, AN Melt, etc.), higher coal fuel costs, and long-term wage settlement provisions. *(Management did not dodge).*
2. **Ashish Agarwal (Confident Cap) asked about the quantum of one-time impacts and whether wage settlement provisions would recur.** Management clarified that net one-timers were around Rs 75 crore, and wage accruals would continue as wage settlements take time to finalize. *(Management did not dodge).*
3. **Falguni Dutta asked about the reasons for price erosion in AN Melt.** Management attributed it to competitive forces and import parity pricing dynamics in the domestic market. *(Management did not dodge).*
4. **Nirav asked about the operational status of plants that were shut in July due to viability issues.** Management confirmed both plants are now operating normally after being shut for 30 to 35 days. *(Management did not dodge).*
5. **An analyst asked about Chinese capacity additions in TDI and customer concentration.** Management noted Wanhua's 360 kt capacity addition and stated that all major foam manufacturers in India are connected with GNFC. *(Management did not dodge).*
6. **Vivrant asked for guidance on spreads and raw material procurement strategy.** Management declined to give short-term guidance, calling predictions hazardous due to war-driven uncertainty, and stated they procure strictly based on monthly production plans rather than speculative stocking. *(Management refused/declined guidance due to volatility, rather than dodging).*

### One-line takeaway
GNFC reported margin compression in Q2 FY27 due to lower chemical realizations, higher coal fuel costs, and wage provisions, while management refrained from short-term guidance amid volatile geopolitical conditions.
