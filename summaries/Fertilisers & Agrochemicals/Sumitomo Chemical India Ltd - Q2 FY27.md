# Sumitomo Chemical India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-30 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Challenging H1 due to erratic monsoon, delayed sowing, and an inventory cost cycle, though offset by stable revenue, a strong balance sheet, and a new "Good to Great" transformation.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 Revenue:** Rs 907 crore (down 2% YoY)
- **H1 Revenue:** Rs 1,971 crore (down 1% YoY)
- **Q2 Domestic Revenues:** Rs 774 crore (up 2% YoY)
- **H1 Domestic Revenue:** Rs 1,657 crore (broadly stable)
- **Q2 Exports:** Rs 127 crore (down 23% YoY due to timing of shipments to Japan and South America)
- **H1 Exports:** Rs 300 crore (stable)
- **Q2 Gross Margin:** 40.7% (down 240 bps YoY; up 149 bps QoQ)
- **H1 Gross Margin:** 39.9% vs 40.4% YoY
- **Q2 EBITDA Margin:** 20.5% (down 297 bps YoY)
- **H1 Operating EBITDA:** Rs 419 crore (down 4% YoY)
- **H1 EBITDA Margin:** 21.3% vs 22% YoY
- **Q2 Net Profit:** Rs 157 crore (down 11% YoY)
- **H1 Net Profit:** Rs 372 crore (up 5% YoY from Rs 356 crore)
- **H1 Net Profit Margin:** 18.9% (up 96 bps YoY)
- **H1 Cash and Liquid Investments:** Rs 2,523 crore as of 30th September 2026, up from Rs 2,113 crore in March 2026
- **H1 Net Cash from Operations:** Rs 447 crore vs Rs 435 crore YoY
- **Trade Receivables:** Rs 887 crore, lower than Rs 903 crore YoY
- **Net Working Capital:** 76 days as of September 2026 vs 55 days in September 2025
- **Inventory Days:** 115 days in September 2026 vs 95 days in September 2025
- **Specialty vs Generic Mix (H1):** Generic 72%, Specialty 28%
- **Domestic vs Export Mix (H1):** Domestic 85%, Export 15%

### Highlights
- Management transition: Dr. Suresh Ramachandran took charge as Managing Director on 1st September 2026; Mr. Chetan Shah appointed Chairman, Mr. Sushil Marfatia as adviser, and Mr. Anand Mohan Tiwari joined as Independent Director.
- Launched company-wide "Good to Great" transformation program built on 5 pillars: people, operational excellence, technology, data/digital/automation, and cross-functional success.
- Launched two proprietary products: **Topgrain** (patented grain-filling biostimulant technology from Sumitomo BioRational Company; India is the first country to commercially launch globally) and **Helibex** (insecticide for caterpillar pests).
- Branded products constituted 79% of domestic revenue and 41% of export revenue in H1 (up from 34% last year).
- Metal Phosphides grew 13% in Q2, PGRs grew 9%, and Animal Nutrition & Environmental Health grew 18%. Insecticides declined 9%, herbicides and fungicides declined 6% each in Q2.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 to remain challenging due to El Nino strengthening, lower reservoir levels (about 71% of capacity, 25% lower YoY), and low soil moisture.
- Goal for H2 is to grow over last year's sales where the market allows while protecting profitability.
- Management expects margins in H2 to be in the normal historical range without significant adverse factors.

### Demand & industry commentary
- H1 FY27 was described as one of the most difficult first halves for the Indian agrochemical industry in over a decade due to erratic monsoons (13% deficit overall; IMD declared a drought year; 16 of 23 key agricultural states had >10% deficit).
- Kharif acreage ended about 1.5% below normal, with rice lower by 4%. Long dry spells caused farmers to skip spray rounds, impacting insecticide, fungicide, and herbicide consumption.
- On the positive side, lower harvest estimates have pushed up commodity prices (rice, cotton, pulses, oilseeds), boosting farmer income for the second crop.
- Potential upside seen in **glyphosate** demand due to recent government restriction/ban on a competing product (**Paraquat**).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Technical prices initially increased due to the Middle Eastern war, rupee depreciation, packaging, solvents, and freight costs, which SCIL passed on. Prices started falling from June onwards.
- High-cost inventory bought between April and June flowed into Q2 cost of sales, depressing Q2 gross margins. Inventory costs normalized by the end of Q2.
- Operating costs well controlled; employee expenses (Rs 70 crore) and other operating expenses (Rs 113 crore) were flat YoY.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- **Dahej Greenfield Project:** Rs 150 crore investment for parent company's future molecules/herbicide intermediate; commercial production remains on track for Q2 FY28-29 (calendar year 2028). Asset turnover and payback (< 5 years) assumptions remain unchanged; margins in line with company levels. Dedicated facility, not multipurpose.
- **Tarapur Project:** Two separate capex projects of Rs 10 crore each (total Rs 20 crore) to add 3-4 additional products primarily for the domestic market. Part 1 completes this current financial year; Part 2 by end of FY27-28.
- Evaluating additional capex projects for parent company’s global needs.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Company remains essentially debt-free with cash, cash equivalents, and liquid investments at Rs 2,523 crore as of 30th September 2026.
- H1 collections at Rs 2,231 crore against Rs 2,277 crore last year.
- Net working capital increased to 76 days (from 55 days in Sept 2025) primarily due to higher inventory days (115 days vs 95 days) driven by higher inventory costs and lower Q2 sales liquidation. Management expects inventory to normalize in H2.
- Foreign currency exposure is largely self-hedged; exposures are hedged with no unhedged foreign currency carried.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Erratic Southwest monsoon, official drought declarations in states like Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan, and a national deficit of 13%.
- Strengthening El Nino projected to peak around December and continue through February 2027, overlapping with the Rabi season.
- Reservoir levels at 71% capacity, down 25% YoY, creating water availability constraints for Rabi irrigation.
- Overstocking and slow inventory liquidation in parent company exports (particularly Japan).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Nitish Rege (Chrys Capital) on Semiconductor Chemicals:** Asked why concrete plans for the semiconductor opportunity have not been announced despite parent company announcements and MOUs.
   - *Management Answer:* The parent company signed an MOU with an anchor semiconductor fab client in India. Strategic discussions with stakeholders (including the Indian government and SCIL) are underway regarding localization and chemical supply. Once discussions mature and firm roles are defined for the Indian listed entity, formal announcements will be made. Management did not provide a specific timeline or confirm the exact role of the listed entity.
2. **Prashant Biyani (Elara Securities) on Glyphosate Opportunity:** Asked for elaboration on the glyphosate upside.
   - *Management Answer:* The competitive molecule **Paraquat** was recently banned by the Government of India, creating a vacuum. Glyphosate is positioned to capture a share of this space depending on market conditions.
3. **Jignesh Kamani (Nippon Mutual Fund) on Sales Returns & Overseas New Products:** Inquired about sales return risks and the status of **Excalia Max** globally.
   - *Management Answer:* SCIL does not have a sales return policy; teams are trained to ensure full liquidation. Sales returns in H2 will be miniscule. Excalia Max is seeing good initial acceptance in the U.S. and Brazil, and grew ~50% in India in H1. Manufacturing in Tarapur is currently only for Indian requirements, with no firm export plans for Indiflin out of India yet.
4. **Naushad Chaudhary (Aditya Birla Mutual Fund) on Good to Great & Cash Deployment:** Asked about the rationale behind the transformation and plans for deploying the Rs 2,500+ crore cash balance.
   - *Management Answer:* The "Good to Great" initiative targets internal efficiency, artificial intelligence utilization, attrition reduction, and cost control. Regarding cash, while the team evaluates growth opportunities in India, capital allocation decisions rest with the Board; they will evaluate strategic M&A or returning cash if no suitable internal opportunities arise, but currently believe domestic growth potential justifies retaining cash.
5. **Abhishek Sharma (9 Rivers Capital) on Lack of Diversification & Governance:** Questioned why SCIL has not diversified beyond Agchem like peers and whether cash should be returned to shareholders given slow progress on non-agchem/semicon opportunities.
   - *Management Answer:* Focus remains primarily on Agchem, Environmental Health, and Animal Nutrition. Management maintains a cautious approach to communication and transparency, avoiding speculation on un-finalized strategic plans while adhering to high corporate governance standards.

### One-line takeaway
Despite a severe drought-hit H1 and inventory cost pressures impacting Q2 margins, SCIL delivered stable revenues, grew net profit, maintained a debt-free balance sheet with robust cash reserves, and initiated a "Good to Great" transformation.
