# Indogulf Cropsciences Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-21 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Delayed and uneven southwest monsoons created a challenging operating environment, impacting near-term demand, trade inventory, and top-line growth.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue from Operations:** Rs. 168.5 crore (down 11% YoY from Rs. 189.4 crore; up ~12% QoQ from Rs. 150.8 crore).
- **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** Rs. 122 crore (down 17% YoY from Rs. 147 crore).
- **Gross Profit:** Rs. 46.6 crore (up 12% YoY from Rs. 41.7 crore).
- **Gross Margin:** 28% (improved from 22% in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs. 9.6 crore (down ~4% YoY from Rs. 9.9 crore).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 5.7% (improved from 5.2% in Q1 FY26).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 2.4 crore (down from Rs. 3.9 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **PAT Margin:** 1.4% (compared to 2.1% in Q1 FY26).
- **Capacity Utilization:** 70% (increased from 52% in FY26).
- **Captive Technical Consumption:** ~34% (increased from 22% previously).
- **Biological and Plant Nutrition Revenue Contribution (B2C brand sales):** Increased to 22% (from 11% in Q1 FY26).
- **Revenue Mix:** Crop protection 87%, Biologicals 3%, Plant nutrients 3%, others the balance.
- **Channel Mix:** B2C 47%, B2B 40%, Exports 13%.
- **Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP):** Rs. 76.4 crore in FY26 (compared to Rs. 57.1 crore in FY25).
- **Global Registrations:** 189 globally (>120 valid, >10 under renewal).

### Highlights
- Improved gross margins (28%) and EBITDA margins (5.7%) despite top-line headwinds, driven by procurement discipline, product mix, and cost management.
- Capacity utilization improved to 70% from 52% in FY26.
- Expanded global regulatory footprint: completed Mancozeb 80% WP registration in Taiwan, import permission for Indo-Apache brand in Sri Lanka, and five technical registrations in Saudi Arabia.
- Two out of three specialty products planned for FY27 are already in the market; a new fungicide launch remains on track for the current quarter subject to patent expiry.
- Established >100 development offices across India, engaging over 1 lakh farmers via meetings, field trials, and advisory services.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects sustainability and growth in both Kharif and Rabi seasons, though very high expectations are not possible due to El Niño.
- Management stated they are working aggressively to achieve growth in the current quarter and fiscal year, but declined to commit to a specific percentage growth guidance figure.
- Management targets reaching a peak turnover of around Rs. 1,800 crore+ in the next 4–5 years utilizing expanded capacity. Existing capacity peak turnover is estimated at Rs. 1,100–1,200 crore.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic agrochemical industry faced challenges in Q1 due to delayed and uneven southwest monsoons, leading to delayed Kharif sowing, resowing in certain areas, and pushed-back pesticide/herbicide application windows.
- Farmers adopted conservative purchasing behavior due to regional rainfall uncertainty, lower realization for certain agricultural commodities, and pressure on farm economics.
- Trade channel (dealers and distributors) maintained cautious stocking; working capital was partially absorbed by fertilizer stockings, leaving less liquidity for crop protection products.
- Global demand remained soft due to global warming impacts and geopolitical/war situations.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material prices saw significant headwinds in Q1 but corrected and stabilized in Q2, though packaging materials (plastics) remain ~40% higher YoY, and logistics face pressure from fuel costs and technical intermediate pricing.
- Finance costs rose due to inventory built up in anticipation of higher sales that were subsequently impacted by poor rainfall and crop shifts; management expects to liquidate inventory and reduce finance costs near term.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Ongoing manufacturing infrastructure investments include expansion at Barwasni and development of the DF manufacturing facility.
- Australia subsidiary acts as a registration-holding company for OECD requirements; no immediate marketing plans in Australia as focus remains on India (B2C), LATAM, Africa, and the Middle East.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Higher finance costs driven by inventory holding for anticipated sales; management expects liquidation of inventory to improve collections and reduce debt/costs.
- Working capital efficiency, asset utilization, and margin sustainability identified as key levers for improving return ratios (ROCE/ROE).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- El Niño impact resulting in lower/uneven rainfall and adverse weather patterns.
- Continued availability of low-priced technical products and intermediates from China exerting pricing pressure on domestic molecules.
- Pressures from higher packaging material costs and freight/logistics expenses.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Harshit (Robo Capital) asked about FY27 growth guidance (targeting ~15%) and peak turnover capacity.** Management declined to give specific percentage guidance for FY27 due to Q1 headwinds, but stated current capacity supports Rs. 1,100–1,200 crore, while expanded capacity targets Rs. 1,800 crore+ in 4–5 years.
- **Shravan Modi (Syndicate Family Office) asked about El Niño impact and China export incentives.** Management noted El Niño delayed Q1 sowing/herbicides, while China's reduced export incentives benefit technical producers like Indogulf over the long term.
- **Mahesh Kumar (MU Investments) inquired about backward integration benefits and captive consumption.** Management responded that captive technical consumption improved to 34% in Q1, providing cost competitiveness and supply chain security.
- **Shreya Patil (FCI LLP) asked about rising finance costs and M&A plans.** Management explained finance costs rose due to inventory build-up for anticipated sales impacted by poor rainfall; M&A consultants are being engaged for tech/market expansion, though nothing is concrete yet.
- **Muskan Patel (JN Invest) asked about levers to improve ROCE/ROE.** Management stated that improving product mix (higher-margin biologicals/nutrients), operational yields, energy efficiency, and asset utilization will drive return ratios rather than financial leverage.

### One-lake takeaway
Despite a challenging Q1 marked by delayed monsoons and an 11% revenue decline to Rs. 168.5 crore, Indogulf improved its gross margins to 28% and capacity utilization to 70%, leveraging increased backward integration (34%) and a shifting product mix toward biologicals and specialties.
