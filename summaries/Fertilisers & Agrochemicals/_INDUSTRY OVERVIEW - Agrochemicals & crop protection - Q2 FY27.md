# Fertilisers & Agrochemicals - Agrochemicals & crop protection: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings calls across the Agrochemicals & Crop Protection sector reflect a challenging operating environment dominated by erratic weather patterns, including one of the weakest monsoons in a decade (IMD seasonal rainfall at 87% of the Long Period Average) and intensifying El Niño conditions. Managements across the board cited regional droughts, uneven rainfall, compressed technical and generic pricing, and high raw material costs. However, companies with a strong focus on proprietary formulations, biologicals, premiumization, and export diversification managed to protect or expand margins despite volume pressures.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus:** Managements widely agreed that domestic demand was severely hampered during the Kharif season due to deficient rainfall, delayed sowing, and prolonged dry spells in central, western, and southern India (e.g., Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana). Overseas demand also faced softness due to global channel de-stocking and geopolitical uncertainties.
* **Divergence:** While generic-heavy portfolios suffered sharp top-line contractions and pricing pressures (noted by *Best Agrolife*, *India Pesticides*, and *PI Industries*), players with differentiated, patented, or biological portfolios (*Dhanuka Agritech*, *Indogulf Cropsciences*, *Sharda Cropchem*, *Sumitomo Chemical*) reported resilient revenue growth or margin expansion through strategic product mixes and selective geographic targeting (e.g., NAFTA and LATAM growth for *Sharda Cropchem*).

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Raw Material & Freight Volatility:** Geopolitical tensions (such as the West Asia crisis) and crude oil fluctuations (spiking up to $125 before settling below $100) drove up raw material procurement, power, and ocean freight costs, compressing gross margins across several players (e.g., *Dharmaj Crop Guard*, *GSP Crop Science*, *Sumitomo Chemical*). 
* **Margin Defense Strategies:** Companies actively leveraged product mix enrichment (shifting from generics to patented/biological products), operational efficiency, and tight control over operating/employee expenses to defend EBITDA margins. For instance, *Best Agrolife* expanded EBITDA margins to 25% (vs 15% YoY) by deliberately scaling down generics (-30% YoY) and growing patented products (+20% YoY).

## Capex & capacity
* **Cautious Expansion & Reallocation:** Capital expenditure plans remained largely measured. While companies like *Best Agrolife* kept capex deferred until business stability is fully secured, others progressed on targeted facilities: *Dharmaj Crop Guard* advanced its herbicide formulation facility, *Indogulf Cropsciences* reallocated IPO proceeds toward a climate stress management facility at Sonipat, and *Sumitomo Chemical* maintained its long-term Dahej greenfield timeline for Q2 FY28-29. *PI Industries* noted agchem capacity is sufficient for the next 2-3 years, shifting future capex toward pharma, electronics, and biologicals.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Guidance Revisions:** Several companies revised their growth expectations downward. *Dharmaj Crop Guard* cut its FY27 top-line growth target from 18-20% to single digits, *India Pesticides* shifted its goal from low single-digit growth to matching last year's revenue, and *Best Agrolife* noted it is "hard to say" if its 10-15% revenue growth target will be met. Conversely, *Prasol Chemicals*, *Sharda Cropchem*, and *Dhanuka Agritech* maintained their full-year revenue/growth guidance.
* **Tone Shifts:** Sentiment across most companies shifted from "cautiously optimistic" or waiting for recovery in Q1 to acknowledging a profoundly difficult Kharif season marked by drought conditions, El Niño peaks, and sustained margin compression, though tempered by hopes of Rabi season recovery and commodity price firming.

## What to watch next quarter
* **Rabi Season Progression:** Soil moisture levels, irrigation availability, and reservoir deficits (noted at ~71% capacity) across Central, Southern, and Western India.
* **Inventory Liquidation & Working Capital:** Normalization of channel inventories and recovery in operating cash flows as harvesting starts (noted by *Indogulf Cropsciences*, *Insecticides (India)*, and *Sumitomo Chemical*).
* **Export Logistics:** Vessel availability, shipping freight volatility, and transit times to key international markets like Brazil and Latin America.

## Company one-liners
* **Best Agrolife:** Sacrificing top-line generic revenue to drive significant margin expansion through a patented-product-led strategy.
* **Dhanuka Agritech:** Delivered resilient Q2 growth through a strong insecticide portfolio and disciplined inventory management.
* **Dharmaj Crop Guard:** Delivered flat H1 revenues despite a weak monsoon, trimming FY27 top-line guidance while keeping its 2030 vision intact.
* **GSP Crop Science:** Achieved 19.5% revenue growth, but profitability contracted due to higher raw material and power inflation.
* **India Pesticides:** Faced a 22% revenue drop from erratic monsoons, securing key international approvals and orders for H2 recovery.
* **Indogulf Cropsciences:** Posted a resilient Q2 with 27% higher EBITDA via product mix enrichment and cost controls despite severe monsoon headwinds.
* **Insecticides (India):** Grew revenue 8% through premiumization and Kaeros expansion despite weather-induced volume drops.
* **PI Industries:** Experienced a 9% revenue decline from export headwinds, balanced by strong domestic biological growth and its first NCE launch.
* **Prasol Chemicals:** Delivered record Q2 performance and reiterated guidance, focusing on capacity expansion and supply chain diversification.
* **Sharda Cropchem:** Achieved healthy 13% revenue growth led by NAFTA and LATAM, offsetting European weather-led softness.
* **Sumitomo Chemical:** Navigated a severe drought-hit H1 and margin pressure while maintaining a debt-free balance sheet and initiating operational transformation.
