# Fertilisers & Agrochemicals: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 quarter was broadly challenging for the Indian Fertilisers & Agrochemicals sector, severely impacted by one of the weakest monsoons in a decade (IMD rainfall at ~87% of the long-period average, or a 13% deficit), erratic rainfall distribution, and high raw material procurement costs. Despite these industry-wide headwinds, select companies with robust insecticide portfolios or enriched product mixes delivered resilient growth, while chemical-heavy players faced steep realization drops and margin compression.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across the board (including **Dhanuka Agritech**, **Dharmaj Crop Guard**, **Indogulf Cropsciences**, and **Rallis India**) agreed that the weak, uneven Southwest monsoon and prolonged dry spells created a difficult Kharif season. They also share a subdued outlook for the upcoming Rabi season due to lingering El Niño conditions and low reservoir levels.
*   **Divergence:** 
    *   *Resilient outperformers:* **Indogulf Cropsciences** and **Dhanuka Agritech** managed to buck the trend through focused product mixes and geographic pivoting. Indogulf grew revenue 3% YoY (and EBITDA by 27%) driven by deferred demand from Q1 and biologicals/nutrients, while Dhanuka achieved ~6% volume growth by concentrating on irrigated regions and insecticides (LaNevo).
    *   *Struggling segments:* **Dharmaj Crop Guard** saw a 4% YoY revenue drop in Q2, forcing a downward revision of its FY27 top-line growth guidance to single digits. **GNFC** faced acute margin pressures in its chemical segment due to falling realizations and did not provide short-term guidance. **Rallis India** reported flat revenue and declining profitability due to moisture stress and export logistics challenges.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Consensus:** High raw material and energy costs, alongside pricing pressure in generic products due to tepid demand, squeezed margins across several players. 
*   **Company-specific impacts:** 
    *   **Dharmaj Crop Guard** suffered a ~320 bps gross margin compression (to 18.6%) due to post-West Asia crisis input costs flowing into COGS.
    *   **GNFC** reported sequential margin hits from lower chemical realizations (WNA, TGU, AN Melt), higher coal fuel costs, and Rs 33 crore in long-term wage settlement provisions (part of Rs 75 crore in net one-time impacts).
    *   **Indogulf Cropsciences** bucked the gross margin trend, expanding gross margins to 30% (from 29% in Q2 FY26) through a richer product mix (higher share of nutrients and biologicals) and lower operating costs, despite packaging cost pressures from rising crude oil.
    *   **Dhanuka Agritech** maintained gross margins via product mix but noted generic products face ongoing pricing pressure.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Dhanuka Agritech:** Dahej plant is operating at ~70% utilization, with plans to add 2 new fungicide products (targeting 4 total by FY28).
*   **Dharmaj Crop Guard:** Capital work-in-progress stood at Rs 11.7 crore, with the new herbicide formulation facility near Kerala GIDC Ahmedabad on track for commissioning.
*   **Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals (GNFC):** H1 capex reached Rs 500 crore. The captive coal-based plant began generating steam and power, though grid power remains cheaper due to high coal prices.
*   **Indogulf Cropsciences:** Reallocated Rs 8.34 crore of unutilized IPO proceeds from a planned in-house DF plant to a dedicated climate stress management and plant growth regulators facility at Barwasni, Sonipat (expected operational by Q4 FY27). Average capacity utilization was 60% in H1.

## What changed since last quarter
*(Based on disclosures by Indogulf Cropsciences)*
*   **Guidance & Tone:** Management maintained its stance of declining forward-looking guidance numbers due to industry uncertainty. However, the tone shifted from cautious to slightly more resilient following a sequential recovery driven by deferred demand.
*   **Strategic Shifts:** The company reallocated unutilized IPO proceeds away from an in-house DF plant toward a climate stress management facility at Sonipat to counter shrinking generic margins. 
*   **Risk Evolution:** Old concerns regarding China export incentives faded, while new emphases were placed on Middle East supply chain disruptions and generic price volatility.

## Company one-liners
*   **Dhanuka Agritech:** Delivered resilient Q2 growth driven by a strong insecticide portfolio and disciplined inventory management, despite industry-wide monsoon headwinds.
*   **Dharmaj Crop Guard:** Delivered flat H1 revenues and 3% PAT growth despite the weakest monsoon in a decade, though technical segment margin pressures forced a downward top-line guidance revision to single digits.
*   **Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals (GNFC):** Reported margin compression in Q2 due to lower chemical realizations, higher coal costs, and wage provisions, while management declined short-term guidance amid geopolitical volatility.
*   **Indogulf Cropsciences:** Reported a resilient Q2 with 3% revenue growth and a 27% jump in EBITDA, driven by a higher-margin product mix and cost controls despite severe monsoon headwinds.
*   **Rallis India:** Faced flat revenue and lower profitability due to erratic monsoons, moisture stress in key states, and export logistics challenges.

## What to watch next quarter
*   Rabi season demand recovery and soil moisture levels, particularly in rain-dependent and Southern regions.
*   Progress on capital projects and new facility commercializations (e.g., Indogulf’s Barwasni facility, Dharmaj’s herbicide plant).
*   Price realization trends in key industrial chemicals and the impact of Chinese imports on GNFC's chemical spreads.
*   Inventory normalization and channel restocking rates heading into Q3 and Q4.

*Based on: Dhanuka Agritech Ltd, Dharmaj Crop Guard Ltd, Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd, Indogulf Cropsciences Ltd, Rallis India Ltd*
