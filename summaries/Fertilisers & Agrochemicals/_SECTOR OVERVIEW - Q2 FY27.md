# Fertilisers & Agrochemicals: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Fertilisers & Agrochemicals sector faced a multi-front storm in Q2 FY27. Managements across the sector reported severe headwinds stemming from one of the weakest Kharif seasons in a decade, characterized by a 12–13% national monsoon deficit, delayed planting, and erratic regional rainfall. Crucial agricultural pockets in Southern and Western India (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra) suffered significant moisture stress. 

To compound weather challenges, surging input costs (sulfur, natural gas, phosphoric acid) combined with delayed government NBS subsidy revisions compressed margins for complex fertilizer players. Concurrently, agrochemical companies navigated global pricing pressure in generic molecules and rising logistics/freight costs driven by West Asian geopolitical conflicts. 

However, companies demonstrated resilience by pivoting toward high-margin patented/specialty products, expanding retail/export footprints, and driving structural backward integration.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian fertilizer and agrochemical companies functioned on high-volume asset-heavy manufacturing and traded generic volume distribution through traditional multi-tiered dealer networks. Growth relied heavily on seasonal monsoon patterns and subsidized pricing regimes. Over the past five years, players have increasingly shifted toward proprietary B2C branding, direct-to-farmer retail expansion, specialized contract development and manufacturing (CSM/CRDMO), and backward integration to protect gross margins [Background].

**Shifts underway**
* **Patented & Specialty Pivot over Generics**: Companies are intentionally shedding low-margin generic volumes to protect profitability via proprietary, patented, or specialized formulations. 
  * *Stage:* Scaling.
  * *Evidence:* Best Agrolife deliberately reduced generic sales (-30% YoY) while growing its patented portfolio (+20% YoY) to reach 60% of branded sales, expanding EBITDA margin to 25% [Best Agrolife Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. PI Industries launched its first NCE (Pioxazon) in India [PI Industries Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Sumitomo Chemical launched proprietary products *Topgrain* and *Helibex*, keeping domestic branded sales at 79% [Sumitomo Chemical India Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Dhanuka Agritech's growth was driven by its specialty insecticide portfolio (LaNevo, Miyako) [Dhanuka Agritech Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
  * *Impact:* Protects gross margins from generic price swings and improves cash flow.
* **Industrial Chemical & TAN Diversification**: Fertilizer manufacturers are diversifying into industrial intermediates like Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) and Nitric Acid to de-risk earnings from agricultural seasonality and price-controlled subsidies.
  * *Stage:* Scaling.
  * *Evidence:* Chambal Fertilisers commissioned its TAN project capitalization (Rs 1,500 crore), generating Rs 67 crore in initial revenue at an 18% margin [Chambal Fertilisers & Chemicals Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Deepak Fertilisers reached 98% project completion for its Gopalpur TAN and Dahej Nitric Acid plants [Deepak Fertilisers & Petrochemicals Corporation Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
  * *Impact:* Stabilizes EBITDA margins and provides non-subsidized cash flows.
* **Direct-to-Farmer & Retail Network Expansion**: Fertilizer and agri-input leaders are expanding company-owned retail footprints and biological offerings to bypass intermediary channel drag.
  * *Stage:* Scaling.
  * *Evidence:* Coromandel International expanded its retail network by 80 stores in Q2 to 1,300 stores, targeting 2,000 stores in two years, with retail white-label brands on track to cross Rs 100 crore [Coromandel International Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. PI Industries expanded its domestic biologicals portfolio by 45% YoY [PI Industries Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
  * *Impact:* Drives stickier B2C revenue and direct pricing power.

**Government push**
* **Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) & Special Subsidy Packages**: Government notified special package/Rabi NBS rates to cushion input price spikes.
  * *Impact:* Protects nutrient manufacturers against high sulfur and phos-acid prices. GSFC, Coromandel, and Chambal noted that special DAP packages and Sulphur subsidies restored backward-integrated acid plant operations [Govt: Approved ₹62,370-crore rabi fertilizer subsidy, Nov 2026; Govt: Hike in subsidy for DAP and complex fertilizers, Nov 2026].
* **National Investment Policy for Urea (NIP-2026)**: Provides a normative framework (12%-16% ROE band) encouraging brownfield and greenfield urea expansion.
  * *Impact:* Managements such as Chambal Fertilisers are initiating preparatory activities for a 4th urea plant under NIP-2026 [Govt: Cabinet approves six brownfield urea projects worth Rs 54,000 crore, Nov 2026].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus**: Managements broadly agree that Q2/H1 FY27 Kharif season was severely impacted by uneven monsoon distributions, prolonged dry spells in Central/Western/Southern states, and elevated channel inventory. A subdued Q3 is anticipated across rain-dependent regions.
* **Divergence**: 
  * *H2 Recovery Optimism:* Coromandel International, Chambal Fertilisers, Sharda Cropchem, and Insecticides (India) expect a strong bounce-back in H2 FY27 driven by recent Rabi NBS rate announcements, normalized inventory, and strong pricing in NAFTA/LATAM export markets.
  * *Continued Cautiousness:* Best Agrolife, Dhanuka Agritech, Sumitomo Chemical India, and GSP Crop Science remain extremely cautious, citing persistent El Niño weather patterns into winter, low reservoir levels (down 25% YoY), and weak soil moisture in Southern India.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Surging Raw Material Costs:** Phosphoric acid, raw sulfur (+327% YoY noted by GSFC), and natural gas ($20.2 to $24.5+/NCV noted by Chambal) escalated sharply due to West Asian geopolitical frictions. 
* **Generic Price Degradation vs. Specialty Stability:** Generic agrochemical molecules suffered price drops post-June after a brief spike, compressing gross margins for companies relying on basic technicals (Dharmaj, India Pesticides). Conversely, backward-integrated companies (Deepak Fertilisers via long-term Equinor LNG contracts) or branded/patented product sellers (Best Agrolife, Sharda Cropchem) expanded margins.
* **Freight & Supply Logistics:** Higher ocean freight, container shortages from China, and longer shipping routes added supply chain expenses (Mahamaya Lifesciences, Sharda Cropchem, GSP Crop Science).

## Capex & capacity
* **Chambal Fertilisers:** Commissioned its Rs 1,500 crore TAN project (HDAN, WNA, ANS); evaluating a 4th Urea plant under NIP-2026.
* **Deepak Fertilisers:** Gopalpur TAN (380k MT) and Dahej Nitric Acid (300k MT) projects are ~98% complete, with commercial startup scheduled for Q4 FY27.
* **Coromandel International:** Kakinada granulation plant on track for February commissioning; Ankleshwar fluorination plant and crop protection molecule capacity expansion underway.
* **DCM Shriram:** Commissioned 100 TPD Aluminium Chloride and partial Calcium Chloride plants at Bharuch; 68 MW hybrid power at Kota.
* **Shree Pushkar Chemicals:** Unit 5 dye expansion (6,000 MTPA) commissioned; trial production for Unit 6 expected by Dec 2026; approved new Rs 70 crore chemical plant.
* **Prasol Chemicals:** Executing Phase 1 capex (Rs 250-300 crore) to add 60,000 tonnes capacity.
* **Mahamaya Lifesciences:** Expanding Dahej formulation capacity by March 2027 and building a Rs 30 crore technical plant for 2028.
* **Capex Deferrals/Pauses:** Best Agrolife explicitly deferred all non-essential capex to preserve cash.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Guidance Cuts & Revisions:** 
  * *India Pesticides:* Lowered FY27 revenue expectation from low single-digit growth to matching last year's top-line.
  * *Dharmaj Crop Guard:* Revised FY27 top-line guidance downward from 18-20% growth to single-digit growth.
  * *Best Agrolife:* Admitted its 10-15% revenue growth target is "hard to say" if achievable, though profitability targets remain intact.
  * *PI Industries:* Lowered gross margin expectations to 47%-49% range for the next 2-3 quarters.
* **Project Delays:** Deepak Fertilisers shifted the commercial commissioning timeline for its Gopalpur TAN and Dahej Nitric Acid facilities from Q2 FY27 to Q4 FY27.
* **Tone Shifts:** Coromandel moved from extreme Q1 distress (uncompensated raw material spikes) to cautious optimism for H2 post the Rabi NBS rate announcement. Sumitomo Chemical shifted to a defensive stance for H2 due to strengthening El Niño.

## What we were watching
* ✅ **showed up** · Chambal Fertilisers: TAN plant commenced commercial production during Q2 FY27, producing 16,000 tonnes WNA and 9,500 tonnes ANS/HDAN.
* ✅ **showed up** · Coromandel International: Commissioned and stabilized Kakinada sulphuric/phosphoric acid plants following the August subsidy notification; Kakinada granulation plant on track for February.
* ✅ **showed up** · PI Industries: Commercialized its first NCE (Pioxazon) in India and expanded flow chemistry/pharma R&D facilities.
* ◐ **partly** · Deepak Fertilisers: Acquisition of Chardham Chemicals progressing (revamp underway), but Gopalpur TAN and Dahej Nitric Acid commissioning timelines missed Q2 and shifted to Q4 FY27 (~98% complete).
* ◐ **partly** · India Pesticides: Received UK technical equivalence and Argentina herbicide registration, though Hamirpur facility additions capitalized Rs 65 crore in September (targeting H2 revenue).
* ⏳ **not yet** · GSP Crop Science: IPO-linked debt reduction (Rs 220 crore) and Saykha plant execution details were not updated during the call.
* ✖ **went the other way** · Best Agrolife: Strategy pivot succeeded on margins (EBITDA +30%), but top-line revenue declined 21% YoY due to deliberate exit from generics and uneven Kharif monsoon.

## What to watch next quarter
1. **Rabi Subsidy Liquidation & Pos Cash Flows:** Tracking timely subsidy disbursements following the Government’s Rs 62,370 crore Rabi subsidy notification and its impact on working capital (GSFC, Coromandel, Chambal).
2. **Commercial Commissioning of Mega Projects:** Operational startup of Deepak Fertilisers' Gopalpur TAN and Dahej Nitric Acid plants in Q4 FY27, and Coromandel’s Kakinada granulation facility in February.
3. **Winter El Niño Dynamics:** Impact of low reservoir levels (71% capacity) and sub-normal soil moisture on Rabi sowing liquidation in Southern and Central India.
4. **Export Market Normalization:** Restocking activity and regulatory approval rollouts in NAFTA, LATAM, and Europe for Sharda Cropchem, India Pesticides, and Indogulf Cropsciences.
