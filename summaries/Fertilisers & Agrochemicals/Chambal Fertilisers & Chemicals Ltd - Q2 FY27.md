# Chambal Fertilisers & Chemicals Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong financial performance and revenue growth amid geopolitical tensions, elevated input costs, and uneven monsoon conditions.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (management expects to maintain Q3 margins, but deferred Q4 outlook due to geopolitical situations).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from delayed monsoon onset concerns to actual spatial deficits (12% national rainfall deficit) impacting Kharif offtake and lengthening working capital cycles, alongside delayed Rabi NBS announcements.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: E-token facility impact on retail shelf space and fertilizer usage, and the commercial production and margin performance (18%) of the newly launched Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) plant.
- Last quarter's promises: TAN plant commenced production within the timeline (High Density Ammonium Nitrate plant started commercial production alongside WNA and ANS).

### Highlights
- Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) project commenced commercial production during the quarter; Weak Nitric Acid and ANS operating at optimum levels, while HDAN is being ramped up. Produced 16,000 tonnes of WNA and 9,500 tonnes of ANS and HDAN combined in 40 days.
- JV IMACID resumed production during the quarter at lower capacity, influenced by sulfur availability and pricing.
- Launched 13 products out of 14 planned for FY 2027 in the Crop Protection, Speciality Nutrients, and seeds business; introduced Uttam Fertilis in Q2.
- Partnered with TERI to establish CFCL-TERI Centre of Excellence for Advanced and Sustainable Agriculture Solutions, planning 10 jointly-owned IP products.
- Conducted over 2,800 farmer meetings, 3,600 demonstrations, and analyzed over 38,000 soil samples.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to maintain EBITDA margins in Q3, while Q4 visibility remains subject to geopolitical situations and raw material movements.
- Management expects TAN pricing power to strengthen and margins to improve in Q3 and Q4.
- Management is undertaking preparatory activities for a potential fourth urea plant under NIP-2026, subject to board and government approvals.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Indian fertilizer industry faced challenges from geopolitical uncertainties in West Asia (elevated input and freight costs) and uneven weather (12% rainfall deficit during June-September).
- Kharif sowing was delayed in specific states (Karnataka, Maharashtra, Rajasthan) due to El Niño conditions, lengthening working capital cycles and slowing channel inventory removal.
- Government approval of National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 provides a framework for future domestic capacity.
- Rabi subsidy announcement by the government is awaited.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Traded fertilizer segment absorbed input price pressures; urea margins maintained via efficient capacity operations.
- Power and fuel costs (part of other expenses) increased due to rising gas costs (Q2 gas cost at $20.2/NCV, moving to $24.5+/NCV).
- Complex fertilizer volumes fell 30% as management soft-pedaled/dialed-down low-margin DAP trading by ~1.5 lakh tonnes.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- TAN project capitalization closed at around Rs 1,500 crore; estimated annual depreciation is about Rs 70 crore (~Rs 15 crore per quarter). HDAN capacity utilization already at 83%-84%.
- Fourth urea plant preparations underway under NIP-2026; management notes Brownfield projects will receive highest priority from the government, followed by Greenfield.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total receivables at Rs 4,818 crore as of September 30, 2026 (Subsidy: Rs 4,157 crore, comprising urea escalation of Rs 1,150 crore, pending POS urea of Rs 1,000 crore, pending POS P&K of Rs 500 crore, DAP advantage/disadvantage formula of Rs 350-360 crore, and other items; Market debtors: Rs 661 crore).
- Received Rs 1,300 crore in October against receivables.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions in West Asia putting pressure on global supply chains, freight, and energy-linked raw materials.
- Below-normal, uneven monsoon and El Niño impacts creating regional crop deficits and lengthening working capital cycles.
- Expiration of Gadepan-III policy terms by end of November 2026, with potential EBITDA impact under the new policy starting December 1.
- Long-overdue Rabi NBS subsidy announcements creating uncertainty.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sarthak Jain (Individual Investor)** asked about segment margin pressures. Management explained that traded fertilizers absorbed price pressures, while urea maintained margins via operational efficiencies.
2. **Himani Badetia (AMC Ltd)** asked about the sustainability of TAN margins after an 18% margin start. Management and Vivek Misra stated that entry was during a low season and margins are expected to improve with supply chain stabilization and higher demand.
3. **Arindam Mukherjee (Individual Investor)** asked about subsidy debtor breakdown and complex fertilizer volume drop. Management detailed the Rs 4,157 crore subsidy breakdown and noted that DAP trading was intentionally dialed down by 1.5 lakh tonnes due to low fixed margins.
4. **Saania Jain (Analyst)** asked about the Gadepan-III benefit expiry and its EBITDA impact. Management stated discussions with the government are ongoing via a white paper, policy expires end of November, and quantum of impact will be known soon.
5. **Pavan Mahad (Analyst)** asked about NIP-2026 ROE bands and capex linking. Management clarified that the 12%-16% ROE is a normative policy band, and companies can earn higher returns by outperforming efficiency assumptions without ex-post facto capping by the government.
6. **Nithin Renjith (Analyst)** asked about TAN depreciation and break-even. Management stated project capitalization is around Rs 1,500 crore, quarterly depreciation will be ~Rs 15 crore, and capacity utilization has already reached 83%-84% with low fixed costs.

### One-line takeaway
Chambal Fertilisers reported a 9% revenue growth and steady H1 performance in Q2 FY27 despite adverse weather and input cost pressures, while successfully commissioning its Technical Ammonium Nitrate plant.
