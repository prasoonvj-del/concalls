# Coromandel International Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-18 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue grew 6% YoY supported by subsidy rates and non-subsidy businesses, but EBITDA declined 18% YoY due to compressed NPK fertilizer margins from elevated raw material costs and deficit rainfall.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: No specific numeric guidance raised or cut; management remains "cautiously optimistic" for a better H2 following the Rabi NBS rate announcement and moderating input costs.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from severe distress in Q1 (due to monsoon deficits and uncompensated raw material spikes) to cautious optimism for H2 following the government's timely Rabi NBS rate announcement and special Sulphur subsidy.
- New topics or risks: Introduction of the government's pilot Framework for Fertilizer Sale system causing operational challenges; state-level restrictions on non-subsidized fertilizer sales in UP, Bihar, and Maharashtra. Old risks like extreme Sulphur spikes somewhat mitigated by the new Sulphur subsidy.
- Last quarter's promises: Kakinada phosphoric/sulphuric acid plant back on track after Sulphur subsidy; granulation plant on track for February commissioning; retail network expanded by 80 stores to 1,300.

### Key numbers
*   **Revenue:** Rs 10,348 crore (up 6% YoY)
*   **EBITDA:** Rs 944 crore (down 18% YoY)
*   **PAT:** Rs 523 crore (compared to Rs 793 crore YoY)
*   **H1 Revenue:** Rs 18,563 crore (up 10% YoY)
*   **H1 EBITDA:** Rs 1,705 crore (down 12% YoY)
*   **H1 PAT:** Rs 905 crore (compared to Rs 1,295 crore YoY)
*   **Fertilizer Primary Sales:** 10 lakh tonnes (down 30% YoY)
*   **Fertilizer Production:** 7.7 lakh tonnes (down 15% YoY)
*   **Fertilizer Market Share (H1):** 19% (up from 17% YoY)
*   **SSP Volumes:** 1.9 lakh tonnes (down 13% YoY)
*   **Crop Protection (CPC standalone) PBIT margin:** 16% for Q2, 17% for H1
*   **NACL Industries Revenue:** Rs 374 crore (down 18% YoY)
*   **NACL Industries EBITDA:** Rs 12 crore
*   **Subsidy Receipts (Q2):** Rs 3,086 crore
*   **Subsidy Outstanding (Sept end):** Rs 3,535 crore (vs Rs 3,199 crore YoY)

### Highlights
*   **Retail Expansion:** Added 80 stores in Q2, taking the total network to 1,300 stores across 5 states; retail white-label brands on track to cross Rs 100 crore in annual sales.
*   **New Products:** Launched 10 new products across nutrients and crop protection in Q2 (including 6 in agrochemicals/biologicals). New products contributed 36% to domestic crop protection during the year.
*   **Plant Operations:** Kakinada phosphoric and sulphuric acid plant resumed full capacity operations following the August Sulphur subsidy notification. Kakinada granulation plant remains on track for February commissioning.
*   **NACL Aberration:** Management noted NACL's weak performance was a one-off due to high-cost inventory and inability to pass on prices amid market compression; integration and synergistic optimization are underway.
*   **Record Continuity:** The third major capacity addition (Kakinada granulation plant) continues on schedule for February commissioning, consistent with prior integration goals.

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management expects a demand bounce back in production, sales, and consumption for H2 FY27 driven by revised Rabi NBS rates, improved farm economics, moderating input costs, and a seasonal Rabi pickup.
*   Management expects El Niño to move to a neutral position by year-end, providing high optimism for the forthcoming Kharif season.
*   Management aims to scale the retail network to 2,000 stores over the next two years.
*   Management targets full capacity utilization of the new granulation facility next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   Rainfall deficit of 20%-30% in key markets (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra) impacted agricultural activity and input consumption.
*   Industry-wide production and consumption of NPKs fell 40% in Q2 due to a widening price differential between DAP (pegged at Rs 1,350/bag) and complex fertilizers (NPKs reaching Rs 2,100/bag), shifting the NPK-to-DAP consumption ratio from 60:40 to 30:70.
*   Industry primary sales for DAP and NPK combined reached 104 lakh tonnes in H1 (vs 121 lakh tonnes YoY); NPK sales were 60 lakh tonnes (vs 77 lakh tonnes YoY), and DAP was flat at 44 lakh tonnes.
*   Channel inventory is estimated to be marginally higher by 5%-7% compared to last year.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Global raw material moderation noted: Urea softened earlier, Sulphur trending below USD 900 (with potential below USD 500), DAP stable, and Phos acid rolled over at USD 1,700 due to trailing high Sulphur prices.
*   Subsidy share of the business stood at 81% for Q2 (83% last year) and 79% for H1 (83% last year). Profitability share of the subsidy business dropped to 58% in Q2 (vs 72% last year).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   Kakinada Phos/Sulphuric acid plant operating at full capacity post-August subsidy notification.
*   Expanding capacity for a key crop protection molecule to come on stream by month-end; planning a Fluorination launch plant at Ankleshwar.
*   Battery-grade phosphoric acid initiative has taken a backseat due to management preoccupation with core nutrients but will be re-evaluated in Q4.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   Working capital managed well despite stress. Subsidy outstanding at Rs 3,535 crore as of September. Received an additional Rs 1,200 crore in October. H1 total subsidy received was Rs 4,478 crore.
*   Forex: Rupee traded in the Rs 94.5 - Rs 96.6 range; hedges maintained conservatively.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Deficit rainfall (20%-30%) in key southern and western markets.
*   Disproportionate price differential between DAP and NPKs compressing NPK offtake.
*   State-level restrictions on the sale of non-subsidy fertilizers by fertilizer marketers in UP, Bihar, and Maharashtra impacting non-subsidy product sales (like nano-fertilizers).
*   Regulatory tightening in AgChem (restrictions on Carbosulfan and Paraquat, though impact on Coromandel is not significant).
*   Practical operational challenges in the pilot Framework for Fertilizer Sale system (longer purchase process, digital access, brand preference).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1.  **Prashant Biyani (Elara Securities):** Asked about expected MRP reduction post-subsidy. Management stated NP grades like 20:20 might see a minimum 10%-15% moderation, though rising ammonia prices require monitoring. *(Did not dodge)*
2.  **Riju:** Asked about non-subsidy business EBITDA strength. Management explained that while CPC margins faced headwinds from carryover inventory and soft global prices, retail, bioproducts, specialty nutrients, and SSP performed well. *(Did not dodge)*
3.  **Darshita Shah:** Asked about backward integration spreads post-Sulphur subsidy. Management confirmed that USD 300-USD 350 is a safe assumption for spreads from October onwards. *(Did not dodge)*
4.  **Somaiah V. (Avendus Spark):** Asked about NACL's high-cost inventory impact. Management clarified it was a one-off aberration, and operational/sourcing synergies between Coromandel and NACL are being optimized to improve future performance. *(Did not dodge)*
5.  **Abhijit Akella:** Asked whether the Framework for Fertilizer Sale applies to NPKs and its volume impact. Management confirmed it applies to all and highlighted that industry has given feedback to the government regarding operational restrictions on cash crops. *(Did not dodge)*

### One-line takeaway
Coromandel navigated an unprecedentedly challenging quarter marked by monsoon deficits and severe NPK margin compression, but anticipates a strong recovery in H2 supported by revised Rabi NBS rates, a new Sulphur subsidy, and resilient non-subsidy retail growth.
