# Godrej Agrovet Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-12 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consolidated revenue grew 10% YoY despite challenging weather and inflationary pressures, supported by strong performance in Animal Nutrition and Oil Palm.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated sales: Rs 2,852 crore (up 10% YoY)
- Animal Nutrition revenue growth: 12.6% YoY; cattle feed volume growth: 15% YoY; reported segment results up 29% YoY; underlying segment results up 36% YoY
- Oil Palm segment revenue growth: 28.9% YoY; segment result growth: 14.4% YoY
- Crop Care segment revenue decline: 16.2% YoY
- Dairy revenue growth: 11.4% YoY; value-added products salience increased from 42% to 49%
- Godrej Foods branded volumes growth: ~6%; Yummiez volumes growth: 22% (or 28% volume growth per later comments)
- Astec LifeSciences EBITDA: Breakeven (compared to an EBITDA loss of Rs 11 crore in Q1 FY26)
- Oil Extraction Ratio (OER): 18.8% in Q1 FY27 vs 18.4% in Q1 FY26

### Highlights
- Animal Nutrition benefited from strategic sourcing, operating leverage, and cost discipline; cattle feed and fish feed grew strongly while poultry was selectively pruned.
- Oil Palm growth driven by higher CPO and PKO realizations, increased sales volumes, and improved oil extraction efficiency (OER at 18.8%).
- Crop Care impacted by a delayed monsoon and slower kharif sowing (driest June in a century), particularly hitting the in-house cotton herbicide segment. New launches (Ashitaka, Takai, Ghassnash) scaled up.
- Astec LifeSciences sustained operational recovery, reaching EBITDA breakeven due to margin expansion across enterprise and CDMO categories.
- Dairy value-added product salience rose to 49%, though margins were pressured by high milk procurement prices and packaging inflation.
- Foods business focused on scaling B2C branded portfolio (Yummiez, frozen momos, frozen chicken) while systematically scaling down live bird volume.
- Bangladesh joint venture (ACI Godrej Agrovet) returned to a strong growth trajectory with double-digit volume, revenue, and PBT growth.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects full-year consolidated revenue growth to target double digits.
- Management expects Astec LifeSciences full-year revenue growth to be greater than 20%.
- Management expects FFB volume growth of high single-digit to early double-digit over the long-range plan (LRP) horizon of 4 to 5 years.
- Downstream palm oil integration (specialty fats) is expected to add roughly ~200 bps to the overall Oil Palm EBITDA profile when fully scaled.
- Management deferred giving a firm full-year guidance on Crop Care and overall mid-teens PBT growth until the end of Q2 once the monsoon and kharif season visibility improves.

### Demand & industry commentary
- June experienced a 41% (or 40%) rainfall deficit, impacting early kharif sowing and herbicide demand.
- High milk procurement prices across the industry constrained margins for dairy players while driving farmer adoption of high-productivity branded cattle feed.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Packaging inflation spiked due to Middle East conflicts affecting crude and LPG prices; industry was unable to pass on the full impact immediately.
- Milk procurement prices remained elevated across the industry.
- Animal Nutrition EBIT per tonne guidance maintained at Rs 2,050 to Rs 2,150.
- Crop Care EBIT margin is expected to hold around 26% to 27%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Overall annual capex guided at approximately Rs 300 crore to Rs 350 crore.
- Rolled out India's first integrated palm oil complex in Kannan (first phase: nursery and CPO processing mill); first specialty fats refinery slated for rollout in September.
- Expanding area under oil palm plantation (added 17,000 hectares last year and on track for similar additions this year, focusing on Telangana and Northeast).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Average net working capital improved significantly YoY, enhancing cash generation and ROCE.
- Labour Code wage hike impacts have already been fully factored in with no additional provisioning expected.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Erratic and delayed monsoon patterns impacting crop care and seasonal agricultural demand.
- Geopolitical tensions causing volatility in raw material prices and packaging costs.
- Industry-wide constraints in milk availability keeping procurement prices high.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Abhijit Akella (KIE) asked about Oil Palm 3-5 year volume growth and downstream integration.** Management stated FFB volume growth will be high single-digit to early double-digit, driven by area expansion, geographic diversification (Telangana/Northeast), and juvenile trees entering peak yield. Downstream specialty fats are expected to add ~200 bps to EBITDA profile. Management did not dodge.
- **Abhijit Akella asked about consolidated PBT guidance for FY27.** Management stated they are confident of double-digit growth, but exact range visibility depends on Crop Care performance assessed by end of Q2. Management did not dodge.
- **Probal Sen (Investor) asked about the strategy and capex for the foods/palm oil business.** Management clarified that downstream palm investments fit within the existing Rs 300-350 crore capex guidance and target a 16%-18% IRR. Management did not dodge.
- **Vanshika Jain (Aequitas Investments) asked why Oil Palm margins declined YoY despite higher CPO prices and better OER.** Management explained it was due to a seasonal phasing of FFB volumes and a small monthly government pricing formula adjustment, not a structural issue. Management did not dodge.
- **Hardik Solanki (Analyst) asked why Crop Care lagged peers.** Management responded that direct comparison is difficult due to differing portfolio mixes and heavy exposure of their base to the cotton herbicide window, which suffered from a drought-hit June. Management did not dodge.

### One-line takeaway
Godrej Agrovet delivered a 10% YoY consolidated revenue growth in Q1 FY27, driven by robust performances in Animal Nutrition and Oil Palm, offsetting weather-induced headwinds in Crop Care and cost pressures in Dairy and Foods.
