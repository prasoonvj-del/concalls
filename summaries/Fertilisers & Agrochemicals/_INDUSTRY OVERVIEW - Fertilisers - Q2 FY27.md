# Fertilisers & Agrochemicals - Fertilisers: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the fertiliser and agrochemicals sector reported robust top-line growth driven by realizations and strong industrial segments, but profitability faced severe headwinds from a 12% national rainfall deficit, uneven spatial distribution, and soaring input costs (particularly sulfur and natural gas). While urea volumes remained stable, complex fertiliser (NPK/DAP) offtake dropped sharply as a widening price differential altered consumption ratios. Companies with integrated value chains or robust non-subsidy industrial portfolios (such as Deepak Fertilisers, Chambal Fertilisers, and DCM Shriram) cushioned the blow, whereas pure-play fertiliser margins compressed significantly ahead of Rabi subsidy announcements.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian fertiliser players relied on regulated mass-volume urea manufacturing backed by government subsidy reimbursements and traded P&K fertilisers. Over the past five years, the model shifted toward backward integration into acids (phosphoric and sulfuric), expansion into high-margin industrial chemicals (Technical Ammonium Nitrate, Nitric Acid), and broadening direct retail footprints to bypass traditional dealer friction [Background].

**Shifts underway** - 
*   **Backward integration into raw materials:** Scaling up to insulate against global sulfur and phosphoric acid price volatility. Coromandel International resumed full operations at its Kakinada phosphoric/sulfuric acid plant following the August Sulphur subsidy notification [Coromandel concall Q2 FY27]; GSFC awarded an EPC contract for a ~INR 3,000 crore phosphoric/sulfuric acid project at Sikka to meet 50% of its DAP/NPK requirements [GSFC concall Q2 FY27].
*   **Diversification into industrial chemicals:** Scaling up non-fertiliser industrial products like Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) and Nitric Acid to improve margin stability. Chambal Fertilisers commenced commercial production at its TAN plant (reaching 83%-84% utilization on HDAN) [Chambal concall Q2 FY27]; Deepak Fertilisers reached ~98% completion on its Dahej nitric acid and Gopalpur TAN projects for Q4 FY27 commissioning [Deepak Fertilisers concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push** - 
*   Cabinet-approved nutrient fertiliser subsidy of Rs 62,370 crore for Rabi 2026-27 [Govt: Cabinet approved Rs 62,370 crore nutrient fertiliser subsidy for rabi, Nov 2026], which rewards companies with high complex fertiliser exposure like Coromandel International, GSFC, and Chambal Fertilisers.
*   National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 (NIP-2026) [Govt: National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 framework, Nov 2026], which provides a framework for future domestic capacity and prioritizes brownfield expansions for players like Chambal Fertilisers.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across Chambal Fertilisers, Coromandel International, DCM Shriram, Deepak Fertilisers, GSFC, and Shree Pushkar agreed that the Kharif season suffered from uneven rainfall (a 12% national deficit), El Niño conditions, and delayed sowings that lengthened working capital cycles and built up channel inventories. 
*   **Divergence:** Coromandel and GSFC highlighted a severe price distortion between DAP and NPKs that severely depressed complex fertiliser volumes, whereas Deepak Fertilisers and Shree Pushkar experienced localized headwinds but maintained confidence in their specific industrial and specialty product segments.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Consensus:** All companies cited severe margin compression in the fertilizer segment due to surging raw material costs—notably sulfur (up 327% per GSFC) and natural gas (pushing past USD 24.5/NCV for Chambal)—alongside delayed Rabi NBS rate announcements.
*   **Divergence:** Coromandel and GSFC saw fertilizer EBITDA margins collapse due to uncompensated raw material spikes, whereas Deepak Fertilisers expanded its EBITDA margin by 700 bps YoY to 22.5% through structural long-term LNG sourcing (Equinor) and in-house ammonia integration. DCM Shriram noted chemical production costs rose 6%–8% while selling prices fell 4%–6%, compounded by negative chlorine pricing (-Rs 5,000).

## Capex & capacity
*   **Chambal Fertilisers:** Capitalized its TAN project at ~Rs 1,500 crore (generating Rs 67 crore revenue in Q2) and initiated preparatory work for a fourth urea plant under NIP-2026.
*   **Coromandel International:** Kakinada granulation plant remains on track for February commissioning; added 80 retail stores to reach 1,300.
*   **DCM Shriram:** Commissioned a 100 TPD Aluminium Chloride plant and partial Calcium Chloride plant at Bharuch; completed a 68 MW hybrid renewable power project at Kota.
*   **Deepak Fertilisers:** Dahej nitric acid and Gopalpur TAN projects reached ~98% completion, targeting Q4 FY27 commercial production.
*   **GSFC:** Awarded an EPC contract for a ~INR 3,000 crore phosphoric and sulfuric acid project at Sikka (30-33 month timeline); commissioned a 1,200 MTPS APS C-Train modification.
*   **Shree Pushkar Chemicals:** Commenced commercial production at Unit 5 (~6,000 MTPA dye capacity); added 10 MW DC solar power (total 20.6 MW); board approved Rs 70 crore for a new Unit 5 chemical plant.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Chambal Fertilisers:** Shifted tone from delayed monsoon onset concerns to actual spatial rainfall deficits impacting Kharif offtake; maintained Q3 margin guidance while deferring Q4 outlook.
*   **Coromandel International:** Shifted from severe Q1 distress over uncompensated RM spikes to cautious optimism for H2 following the timely Rabi NBS rate announcement and special Sulphur subsidy.
*   **Deepak Fertilisers:** Commissioning timelines for Gopalpur TAN and Dahej nitric acid shifted from Q2 FY27 to Q4 FY27; the government lifted the TAN export ban in October 2026.
*   **GSFC:** Transitioned from severe margin pinch to expecting H2 recovery supported by the Sikka unit's BCG cost-cutting measures and upcoming NPK subsidy adjustments.

## What to watch next quarter
*   Actual realization of margins following the Cabinet's approval of the Rs 62,370 crore Rabi nutrient subsidy.
*   Commissioning progress and initial capacity ramp-up timelines for Deepak Fertilisers' Dahej nitric acid and Gopalpur TAN plants (targeted for Q4 FY27).
*   The impact of Coromandel's new Kakinada granulation plant (scheduled for February commissioning) on manufacturing volumes.
*   Resolution of policy terms for Chambal's Gadepan-III plant following expiration at the end of November 2026.

*   **DCM Shriram Ltd:** Achieved 22% revenue growth to Rs 3,977 crores, but PBDIT softened due to higher energy costs in chemicals and El Niño headwinds in agri-inputs.
*   **Deepak Fertilisers & Petrochemicals Corporation Ltd:** Posted historic Q2 profitability driven by structural gas/ammonia integration, with major capacity expansions 98% complete for Q4 startup.
*   **Gujarat State Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd:** Delivered record top-line growth offset by severe raw material margin compression in fertilisers, leaving earnings reliant on upcoming subsidy notifications.
