# DCM Shriram Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · Fertilisers & Agrochemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong revenue growth and higher volumes in key segments offset by margin pressure from higher input costs and weak performance in Agri-inputs/Bioseed.

### Key numbers
- **Net revenues:** Rs 3,977 crores vs Rs 3,272 crores in Q2 FY26 (up 22% YoY)
- **PBDIT:** Rs 319 crores vs Rs 408 crores in Q2 FY26
- **Net debt:** Rs 917 crores as on September 30, 2026 vs Rs 773 crores as on September 30, 2025
- **ROCE:** 12% for September 2026 vs 15% last year
- **Interim dividend:** 200% amounting to Rs 62.38 crores
- **Chemicals revenue growth:** 47% YoY; PBDIT Rs 158 crores vs Rs 254 crores YoY
- **Caustic soda volumes:** Up 2% YoY; capacity utilisation at 89% (vs 79%-80% last year)
- **ECU prices:** Higher by 6% YoY
- **Vinyl revenues:** Up 20% YoY; PBDIT Rs 51 crores vs Rs 12 crores YoY
- **PVC volumes & prices:** Volumes up 29%, prices up 25% YoY
- **Carbide volumes & prices:** Volumes up 15%, prices up 16% YoY
- **Sugar & Ethanol revenues:** Up 10% YoY; domestic sugar prices up 14%, volumes lower by 6%; Ethanol volumes up 24%; PBDIT Rs 62 crores vs Rs 33 crores YoY
- **Fenesta revenues:** Up 22% YoY; PBDIT up 15% at Rs 49 crores
- **Shriram Farm Solutions revenues:** Down 10% YoY; PBDIT Rs 78 crores vs Rs 106 crores YoY
- **Fertilizer revenues:** Up 40% YoY; realizations up 46%; PBDIT up 25% to Rs 25 crores
- **Outstanding fertilizer subsidy:** Rs 446 crores on September 30, 2026 vs Rs 187 crores last year
- **Bioseed PBDIT:** Negative Rs 41 crores vs negative Rs 7 crores YoY
- **Bioseed Philippine write-off:** Rs 12 crores for the half year
- **India Bioseed doubtful debt provision:** Rs 7 crores to Rs 8 crores for the quarter
- **Incremental depreciation:** Rs 10 crore for the quarter from the chemical business

### Highlights
- Commissioned 100 TPD Aluminium Chloride plant at Bharuch (taking total to 250 TPD) and partial Calcium Chloride plant (133 TPD out of 266 TPD).
- Commissioned 68 MW hybrid renewable power project at Kota; 58 MW project at Bharuch progressing on schedule.
- Fenesta commissioned Phase 1 of Aluminium Extrusion plant at Kota; wooden doors facility on schedule.
- Board announced interim dividend of 200% (Rs 62.38 crores).
- Previous year Q2 included one-time positive impacts: Rs 76 crore Gujarat government incentive in chemicals and Rs 15.5 crore power tariff revision in sugar.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management avoids giving specific forward-looking turnover/guidance numbers, but notes continued investment in growth.
- India's FY27 real GDP projected at 7.1% by RBI.
- Global sugar market expected to move into a deficit of ~2.6 million metric tonnes in 2026-27 season. Domestic sugar prices expected to remain range-bound around Rs 4,800 per quintal.
- PVC prices likely to remain influenced by energy costs, currency movements, global supply conditions, and trade policies.
- Demerger process is at an advanced stage; management targets bringing it to the board in 2 to 3 quarters (targeting Q1 FY28).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global Q2 FY27 operated amid volatile energy prices, shifting tariffs, tighter global liquidity, and geopolitical friction.
- Indian agricultural and rural sectors faced pressure from El-Nino and below-average reservoir storage.
- Chemicals domestic demand supported by textiles, alumina, and infrastructure; future capacity additions may cause near-term supply-demand imbalances.
- PVC demand weighed down by monsoon and cautious trader bookings; domestic demand expected to improve post-monsoon.
- Ethanol tender for 2026-27 includes only C-heavy molasses and shifts to grain-based feedstocks; maize-based ethanol prices reduced while C-heavy prices unrevised despite higher sugarcane prices.
- SFS saw selective demand due to tight farmer liquidity; Crop Protection vertical registered healthy growth.
- Bioseed affected by severe El Niño conditions in South and Central India, keeping acreage under corn, cotton, and paddy below last year.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Production costs in chemicals increased by 6% to 8%, while selling prices fell 4% to 6%, impacting margins (except hydrogen peroxide).
- Coal prices for power have gone up significantly and continue to rise, impacting caustic energy costs.
- Chlorine prices were negative (-Rs 5,000 in Q2) and dropped further, offsetting caustic trends.
- Natural gas prices rose due to West Asia conflict, increasing urea production costs, fertilizer working capital requirements, and supporting hydrogen peroxide and PVC/energy prices.
- Fenesta margins impacted by product mix, upfront investments in new platforms, and higher input costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Setting up a 40 MW (peak) solar energy plant in Kota for Rs 145 crores.
- Installing filter presses in cement plant at Kota for Rs 60 crores.
- Debottlenecking operations at Bharuch and Kota to improve energy efficiency and increase caustic capacity for Rs 120 crores.
- JV Shriram Teknor Limited setting up new lines for PVC specialty compounds, medical grades, and HFFR grades at a total cost of Rs 133 crores (company equity investment up to Rs 50 crores).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt stood at Rs 917 crores as of September 30, 2026 (vs Rs 773 crores on September 30, 2025).
- Outstanding fertilizer subsidy increased to Rs 446 crores as of September 30, 2026 (vs Rs 187 crores last year).
- Working capital requirements for urea increased due to high gas prices.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- El-Nino conditions, weak monsoons, and below-average reservoir storage impacting Rabi cropping and rural consumption.
- Future capacity additions in chlor-alkali and chemicals may put pressure on prices and margins.
- Feedstock challenges and unfavorable policy pricing for ethanol (reduced maize ethanol price, unrevised C-heavy price against higher sugarcane costs).
- High coal and natural gas prices impacting production and power costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Riya Mehta (Svan Investments) on Chemical Margins & Costs:** Asked about the breakup of margin decline in chemicals. Management explained production costs rose 6%-8% while realizations fell 4%-6%, compounded by negative chlorine pricing (-Rs 5,000 and lower).
2. **Jainam Ghelani (Svan Investments) on Demerger Timeline:** Inquired about the demerger status. Management stated it is at an advanced stage and targeted for the board in 2 to 3 quarters (targeting Q1 FY28).
3. **Rajakumar Vaidyanathan on Vinyl Margins:** Asked if vinyl margins would improve or hold. Management stated margins depend on price movement; if prices remain at current levels, margins might moderate, though MIP provides support.
4. **Saket Kapoor (Kapoor and Co.) on Chlorine Integration:** Asked about captive chlorine consumption. Management stated Bharuch will have ~45% captive and 27% pipeline customers (total ~70% integrated), while Kota will be ~62% captive.
5. **Abhinav on Bioseed Inventory Write-offs:** Asked about inventory write-offs. Management clarified a write-off of ~Rs 12 crores was taken in the Philippines for the half year, and Rs 7-8 crores in provisions for doubtful debts in India.

### One-line takeaway
DCM Shriram delivered a 22% YoY revenue increase to Rs 3,977 crores in Q2 FY27, but PBDIT declined to Rs 319 crores due to higher input/energy costs in chemicals and headwinds in agri-inputs, despite strong operational expansions and volume growth in vinyl and sugar.
