# Construction & Infrastructure: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Management commentary across the Indian Construction & Infrastructure sector in Q2 FY27 reveals a stark divergence between execution momentum and top-line visibility. While standalone quarterly execution was seasonally impacted by monsoons, state election lulls, and localized environmental curbs (e.g., GRAP in NCR), full-year targets are split. Pure-play highway developers face intense competition, land acquisition delays, and execution slowdowns (Ashoka Buildcon, KNR Constructions, Patel Engineering cut revenue guidance). Conversely, companies focused on diversified infrastructure—such as power T&D, water management, industrial PEB, rail signaling, and energy storage—are outperforming and expanding guidance. Monetization of BOT/HAM assets has reached a critical inflection point (Dilip Buildcon, KNR Constructions, Ashoka Buildcon, G R Infraprojects), releasing tied-up equity to deleverage balance sheets or fund growth into non-road verticals.

## Where the industry is heading

**Business model so far**  
For the past two decades, Indian EPC and infrastructure companies primarily operated as asset-heavy highway/civil developers dependent on government award cycles, holding low-yield concession assets on balance sheets. Over the last 5 years, the industry has aggressively transitioned to an asset-light, capital-recycling model: monetizing completed road/solar projects via InvITs or private equity sales, enforcing dynamic price-variation clauses, and pivoting to niche, specialized infrastructure segments [Background].

**Shifts underway**

- **Monetization of BOT/HAM Assets to De-lever and Reallocate Capital**  
  *Stage:* Mainstream  
  Companies are systematically transferring equity in completed HAM/BOT road portfolios to InvITs or financial sponsors to pay down corporate debt or fund high-return capex. KNR Constructions completed the 100% stake transfer of 4 HAM assets to Indus Infra Trust, generating an exceptional pre-tax profit of Rs 292 crore and reducing consolidated debt to Rs 1,180 crore [KNR Constructions Q2 FY27 concall]. Dilip Buildcon executed definitive agreements with Alpha Alternatives to sell its Mekhali transmission and 1,363 MW solar portfolio (returning ~Rs 1,900 crore over 2–3 years) [Dilip Buildcon Q2 FY27 concall]. Ashoka Buildcon signed final transaction documents to monetize 4 HAM assets for Rs 600 crore initial proceeds, with 2 more to follow totaling ~Rs 1,000 crore [Ashoka Buildcon Q2 FY27 concall]. G R Infraprojects plans to monetize 5–6 HAM assets (Rs 1,250 crore equity invested) in FY27 [G R Infraprojects Q2 FY27 concall].  
  *Why it matters:* Reduces balance sheet leverage, unlocks tied-up equity, and insulates earnings from long-term traffic/annuity execution risks.

- **Strategic Diversification Out of Highways into Power T&D, Renewables, and BESS**  
  *Stage:* Scaling  
  With NHAI road project awards seeing aggressive bidding and lower margins, primary road builders are rapidly shifting order books toward power transmission, solar EPC, and battery storage. Ceigall India secured a ~Rs 5,700 crore T&D LOI and expanded non-road share to 45% of its order book [Ceigall India Q2 FY27 concall]. Ashoka Buildcon entered TBCB power transmission via the Sakoli project [Ashoka Buildcon Q2 FY27 concall]. SPML Infra secured a Rs 186 crore order from Power Grid for 765 kV substations and is scaling its Pune BESS assembly facility to 5 GWh by FY28 [SPML Infra Q2 FY27 concall; Investor deck Nov 2026]. Enviro Infra acquired Suyog Urja for Rs 311 crore to add Wind EPC capabilities [Enviro Infra Q2 FY27 concall].  
  *Why it matters:* Diversifies revenue streams, opens higher-margin addressable markets, and reduces exposure to highway tender delays.

- **Self-Sustainable State & Central Redevelopment Models**  
  *Stage:* Scaling  
  State governments and public entities are moving away from budget-funded construction to self-sustaining real estate and infrastructure redevelopment models where land monetization funds EPC works. NBCC is leading this shift, securing marketing fee margins (1% to 1.25%) on self-sustaining projects like 7 GPRA, Amrapali, and state MoUs in West Bengal (Rs 10,000 crore) and Bihar (Rs 10,000 crore), driving standalone EBITDA growth of 154% YoY [NBCC Q2 FY27 concall].  
  *Why it matters:* Eliminates government payment delay risk while generating high-margin fee streams alongside traditional EPC income.

**Government push**  
- **Green Energy Corridor Phase-III:** Approved with an outlay of Rs 1.86 lakh crore, boosting power T&D project awards [Govt: Decided, Nov 2026]. Benefits: Ceigall India, Ashoka Buildcon, SPML Infra, Vikran Engineering.  
- **Infrastructure Equipment Incentive Scheme:** Rs 11,000 crore ($1.16 billion) manufacturing incentive scheme for construction equipment [Govt: Decided, Nov 2026]. Benefits: Structural steel fabricators, PEB suppliers, and fleet operators.

- **Smart Cities & Water Infrastructure (JJM 2.0 & AMRUT 2.0):** Central clearance of contractor dues across states to pave the way for JJM 2.0 [Govt: Decided, Nov 2026]. Benefits: NCC Ltd, LCC Projects, SPML Infra, Denta Water, Om Infra.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence

### Consensus
- **Execution Acceleration in H2:** Virtually all managements (Afcons, Ahluwalia, Annu Projects, Capacit'e, Dilip Buildcon, GPT Infra, SPML Infra, Telge Projects) agree that H2 FY27 will see a massive execution ramp-up compared to H1, driven by post-monsoon site readiness and clearance of mobilization hurdles.
- **Power T&D, Smart Grid, and Renewables Expansion:** Managements consistently view Power Transmission & Distribution (RDSS, 765 kV EHV lines, GIS substations) and Grid-Scale Solar/BESS as the fastest-growing infrastructure verticals with strong funding backing (SPML Infra, Vikran Engineering, Sugs Lloyd, Ceigall India).
- **Water & Urban Infrastructure Resilience:** Water distribution, sewage treatment, and urban mass rapid transit remain highly resilient verticals, benefiting from persistent central/state funding priorities (LCC Projects, Capacit'e, Denta Water, J. Kumar Infra).

### Divergence
- **Highway Bidding Intensity & Growth:** Managements are divided on traditional highway EPC/HAM prospects. Dilip Buildcon, Ashoka Buildcon, and KNR Constructions report intense price competition and tender delays in smaller highway packages, prompting them to intentionally lower their road order share. Conversely, Ceigall India and PNC Infratech see strong road pipelines (>Rs 1.8 lakh crore across NHAI/MoRTH) and maintain active bidding momentum.
- **Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) Cash Collections:** NCC Ltd reported significant payment acceleration in JJM (collecting Rs 1,720 crore in H1) and PNC Infratech realized Rs 267 crore in pending water dues. In contrast, Afcons Infrastructure and Om Infra continue to report stretched working capital and delayed certifications, with Om Infra stating it has "lost faith in JJM revenue" to focus entirely on non-JJM water projects.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Steel & Aluminium Inflation:** EPACK Prefab and Interarch reported gross margin pressure due to a 17%–18% spike in raw steel prices and aluminum formwork costs (rising to Rs 14,500/sqm). Both companies are shifting order books toward 100% variable pricing and pass-through mechanisms.
- **Pass-through Insulation:** Infrastructure contractors (G R Infra, LCC Projects, NCC, SPML Infra, GPT Infra) note that 80%+ of their order books contain formal Price Variation (PV) clauses linked to WPI/RBI indices, insulating operating margins from crude, diesel, and bitumen volatility, albeit with a 1-to-2 quarter realization lag.
- **EBITDA Margins:** Core EPC margins have stabilized in the 9%–12% range for heavy civil (Ashoka 9.5%, G R Infra 10%–11%, Dilip Buildcon 10%–10.5%, ITD Cementation 10%+). Higher specialized margins are reported in Cleanrooms (Fabtech), PEB/BESS (EPACK 10.5%, SPML BESS O&M), and Pure PMC/Marketing Fee models (NBCC 8%–9% overall, driven by 100% margin fee income).

## Capex & capacity
- **Asset Monetization Funding Growth:** Capital expenditure across major contractors is increasingly self-funded via asset monetization proceeds rather than fresh corporate debt. KNR Constructions is deploying asset sale proceeds toward ~Rs 1,000 crore long-term mining capex across the Kusmunda and Banhardih coal mines.
- **Manufacturing & Equipment Expansion:** 
  - **M&B Engineering:** Commissioned Phase 1 Sanand PEB expansion (20,000 MTPA), raising total capacity to 124,000 MTPA, while receiving TÜV Nord CE certification for European structural steel export entry.
  - **Interarch Building Solutions:** Spent >Rs 250 crore capex over 2 years, operationalizing its Andhra Pradesh heavy structure facility.
  - **EPACK Prefab:** Operationalized Ghiloth Unit 5 (13,000 tonnes capacity) and expects its Gujarat greenfield plant (50,000 tonnes) to go live in Q4 FY27.
  - **SPML Infra:** Expanding its Pune BESS assembly facility from 2.5 GWh to 5 GWh by FY28 alongside a 600-container manufacturing plant.
  - **B.R. Goyal Infrastructure:** Approved a new 1.8 lakh cubic meter Ready-Mix Concrete (RMC) plant in Indore.

## What changed since last quarter

- **Guidance Revisions (Cuts vs. Upgrades):**
  - *Cuts:* Ashoka Buildcon cut FY27 standalone revenue growth to 5%–10% (from 10%–15%); Patel Engineering trimmed FY27 revenue growth to 7%–8% (from ~10%) due to delayed forest clearances; RVNL adjusted revenue expectations down to ~10% (from 15%). KNR Constructions trimmed its H2 order inflow target to Rs 4,000–5,000 crore.
  - *Upgrades / Maintained:* G R Infraprojects raised FY27 revenue growth guidance to ~20% (from 15%); NBCC maintained robust consolidated revenue guidance of Rs 15,000–17,000 crore; JSW Infrastructure upgraded FY27 Logistics EBITDA margin guidance to 28% (from 24%).
- **Tone Shifts & Delivery:**
  - *Delivered on Promises:* Dilip Buildcon executed definitive agreements with Alpha Alternatives; Ahluwalia Contracts recovered execution sequentially post-Q1 black-swan events; M&B Engineering commissioned its Sanand facility expansion on October 26, 2026.
  - *Headwinds/Delays:* J. Kumar Infraprojects faced a temporary delay after DMRC canceled an L1 underground metro order to re-issue a limited tender; PNC Infratech saw its NHPC BESS order canceled due to non-signing of the PSA.
