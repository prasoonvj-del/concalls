# Construction & Infrastructure: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Indian Construction & Infrastructure sector demonstrated robust top-line momentum in Q2 FY27, largely supported by record revenues and expanding order books across urban development, power transmission & distribution (T&D), clean energy, and specialty building segments. However, performance remains bifurcated: while building materials, specialized engineering, and private-sector construction players delivered strong top-line expansions, infrastructure developers heavily exposed to central/state civil EPC, water projects (such as Jal Jeevan Mission in U.P.), and domestic road awards faced execution lags, delayed payment certifications, and working capital stretch. Monsoon disruptions, geopolitical uncertainties, and commodity inflation (particularly steel and bitumen) pressed operating margins for several players, prompting a broad strategic pivot toward private sector capex, international expansion, and high-margin non-road verticals.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus on high-growth adjacent sectors:** Managements universally agree that Power T&D, Renewable Energy (Solar EPC, BESS, Wind), Data Centers, Microelectronics/Semiconductors, and Industrial/Logistics infrastructure present massive, unhindered demand tailwinds. 
  * **Ceigall India**, **Desco Infratech**, **Enviro Infra Engineers**, **M&B Engineering**, and **Fabtech Cleanrooms** highlighted surging inquiry pipelines in renewable integration, power transmission grid extensions, and specialized cleanrooms/industrial buildings.
  * **Interarch Building Solutions** and **M&B Engineering** pointed to explosive demand from data center hyperscalers and multi-storey heavy steel structural applications.
* **Divergence on Traditional Road & Water EPC:**
  * **Roads & Highways:** **Ceigall India**, **G R Infraprojects**, and **PNC Infratech** note a steady post-monsoon pickup in NHAI tender pipelines (e.g., MoRTH/NHAI bid pipeline across HAM/BOT/EPC). Conversely, **Afcons Infrastructure**, **B.R. Goyal Infrastructure**, and **IRCON International** cite delayed award pacing, heightened competition, and shifting contractual frameworks as reasons for a temporary operational drag in pure civil road churning.
  * **Water Projects:** **NCC Ltd** noted improving collection momentum from its Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) receivables (receiving Rs 1,720 crore in H1). On the other hand, **Afcons Infrastructure** reported continued stretched liquidity and certification delays in its U.P. JJM project, and **Jain Irrigation** deliberately reduced its exposure to government project-linked water contracts.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Steel & Commodity Pressures:** Elevated domestic steel and aluminum prices created margin headwinds. **EPACK Prefab** experienced a 2.3% H1 gross margin contraction due to steel prices rising 17%-18% during the quarter before pass-through kicked in. **Interarch Building Solutions**, **Fabtech Cleanrooms**, and **Capacite Infraprojects** reported commodity volatility, though they mitigated operational risk through escalation clauses or by migrating toward 100% variable pricing terms on new contracts.
* **Fuel & Bitumen Spikes:** Geopolitical tensions in West Asia led to inflation in petrochemical derivatives, diesel, and bitumen. **G R Infraprojects** noted a 1.5%–2% margin drag from higher fuel and material costs. **B.R. Goyal Infrastructure** noted similar cost pressures, though central government escalation indexation clauses offset pure EPC margin erosion.
* **Accounting & Segment Mix Adjustments:** **Enviro Infra Engineers** attributed an 800-900 bps YoY drop in consolidated EBITDA margins primarily to non-operational accounting adjustments (acquisition interest and profit reversals on EPC-to-IPP transfers). **B.R. Goyal Infrastructure** noted lower blended margins due to a higher revenue mix of Toll Collection Contracts (TCC), which yields higher ROCE despite lower gross margin percentages.

## Capex & capacity
* **Capacity Additions:** Capital expenditure remains active across prefabricated structures, specialized engineering, and manufacturing. 
  * **EPACK Prefab** commissioned 13,000 tonnes of steel capacity at Ghiloth Unit 5 and added a new sandwich panel line, with a greenfield Gujarat plant on track for Q4 FY27 to scale revenue capacity to Rs 2,800-3,000 crore.
  * **M&B Engineering** commissioned its 20,000 MTPA Sanand PEB expansion, raising total PEB capacity to 124,000 MTPA while earning CE certification for European exports.
  * **Interarch Building Solutions** spent over Rs 250 crore on capex over two years, targeting 400,000 tonnes of salable capacity by 2029.
  * **Fabtech Cleanrooms** added a 50,000 sq meter facility in Hyderabad.
* **Port & Logistics Infra:** **JSW Infrastructure** executed Rs 2,100 crore of capex in H1 FY27 toward its slurry pipeline, Jatadhar port works, and rail rake fleet expansion (targeting 110 rakes by March 2027), supporting its long-term plan to scale total port capacity to 400 MTPA by FY30.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Guidance Revisions & Tone Shifts:**
  * **Patel Engineering:** Cut FY27 revenue growth guidance from ~10% to ~7-8% due to delayed project starts from pending forest clearances, shifting to a cautious tone.
  * **G R Infraprojects:** Raised FY27 revenue growth guidance to ~20% (up from 15%) following a 64% YoY top-line surge in Q2.
  * **Afcons Infrastructure:** Withheld full-year FY27 turnover guidance due to geopolitical headwinds, energy availability issues in Bangladesh, and logistics constraints, while reinstating a 20%-25% growth guidance for FY28.
  * **M&B Engineering:** Upgraded demand outlook from "Cautious" in Q1 to "Confident" in Q2, backed by Sanand plant capitalization and hyperscaler data center order wins.
  * **Sterling and Wilson Renewable Energy:** Clarified FY27 revenue growth guidance to "up to 10%" (from a previous 10-15% range) due to domestic NTP/PPA delays and transition phases in international projects.
  * **JSW Infrastructure:** Revised FY27 Logistics EBITDA margin guidance upward from 24% to 28% due to net-off accounting on LSFTO rakes.

## What to watch next quarter
* **H2 Execution Acceleration & Revenue Realization:** Managements across **Afcons**, **Brahmaputra**, **Capacite**, **Interarch**, **NBCC**, **Sterling & Wilson**, and **M&B Engineering** expect heavily backend-loaded H2 execution. Watch whether unbilled revenues convert into reported top-line run rates.
* **Working Capital & Cash Flow Normalization:** Monitor cash conversion metrics across **EPACK Prefab**, **Enviro Infra**, **Afcons**, **Modis Navnirman**, and **Sugs Lloyd**, all of which reported elevated working capital days or negative operating cash flows in H1 due to inventory build-up or billing milestone delays.
* **Asset Monetization:** **G R Infraprojects** (5-6 HAM projects with Rs 1,250 crore equity invested), **Ceigall India** (targeting Rs 250 crore cash inflow and Rs 540 crore debt reduction), and **Patel Engineering** (Rs 100-150 crore targeted annually) have asset sales slated for H2 execution.
* **International Project Starts:** Track international order conversion and commencement for **Afcons** (Rs 4,200 crore L1 European projects), **PNC Infratech** (Georgia road bid and Kazakhstan subsidiary), and **Sterling and Wilson** (Egypt West Minya project).
* **Winter Construction Restrictions:** Watch for seasonal execution loss in Delhi-NCR due to NGT/GRAP restrictions, as flagged by **Capacite Infraprojects** (projecting a Rs 45-50 crore revenue loss) and **NBCC**.
