# Afcons Infrastructure Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While order intake is strong and H2 execution is expected to be better, Q2 performance was impacted by lower turnover, stretched liquidity, delayed collections, and provisioning.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 2,906 crore (down 6.3% YoY from Rs 3,101 crore)
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 5,633 crore (down 13.6% YoY from Rs 6,520 crore)
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 316 crore; Margin: 10.9% (vs Rs 401 crore, 12.9% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 578 crore; Margin: 10.3%
- **Q2 FY27 PBT:** Rs 82 crore (2.8% margin, vs Rs 149 crore, 4.8% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 PBT:** Rs 133 crore (2.4% margin, vs Rs 333 crore, 5.1% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 PAT:** Rs 41 crore (vs Rs 105 crore YoY)
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 71 crore (vs Rs 242 crore YoY)
- **H1 FY27 Net Working Capital:** 147 days
- **Gross Debt (H1 FY27):** Rs 4,765 crore
- **Net Debt (H1 FY27):** Rs 4,050 crore (Net debt-to-equity of 0.74x)
- **Q2 FY27 Depreciation:** Rs 181.51 crore (3.22% of turnover)
- **Order Inflow (H1):** Close to Rs 16,000 crore (excluding L1)
- **Order Inflow (YTD):** Close to Rs 19,500 crore / Rs 19,437 crore (excluding L1)
- **Total Pending Order Book:** Rs 43,836 crore (or in excess of Rs 47,000 crore as stated later by CFO)
- **U.P. Jal Jeevan Mission Pending Order Book:** Rs 474 crore; Outstanding: Rs 372 crore
- **L1 Orders (Europe):** Rs 4,200 crore (2 projects)
- **FY27 CapEx Guidance:** Rs 700 crore
- **FY28 CapEx Guidance:** Rs 650 crore

### Highlights
- Completed the final segment of the Thilamale Bridge (Greater Male Connectivity Project).
- Phase-II of Mithapur–Sipara Elevated Corridor inaugurated by the Chief Minister of Bihar.
- Named Construction Contractor of the Year at the 16th Construction Week Awards.
- Both tunnel boring machines at the Mumbai-Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail project are progressing as scheduled (initial drive completed for one, second completing in November).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management held back full-year revenue/growth guidance due to geopolitical uncertainties, energy availability challenges in Bangladesh, and logistics issues.
- Management reinstated guidance for FY28, expecting a 20%-25% revenue growth over FY26.
- Management is confident of achieving the full-year order inflow guidance of Rs 30,000 crore.
- H2 execution is expected to be stronger than H1.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Infrastructure opportunities in India remain healthy, but award pacing is uneven and competition is intense in certain segments.
- Geopolitical tensions in West Asia continue to impact logistics and input costs.
- Order pipeline: Remaining H2 pipeline is ~Rs 80,000 crores (35% hydro, 35% marine, 10% surface, 20% urban infrastructure). Total TAM for the next 6 quarters is Rs 3.6 lakh crores (65% domestic, 35% international).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Fixed overhead costs were under-recovered due to lower overall turnover.
- SG&A / other costs increased significantly due to new Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning, discount provisioning for arbitration settlements, and provisions for an overseas subsidiary in the Middle East based on negative local/banker feedback.
- Finance costs increased due to higher average borrowings and interest-bearing client advances (41% of overall advances are interest-bearing, 59% are interest-free).
- Effective tax rate was high at 46% of PBT due to consolidation impacts, non-refundable turnover taxes in Bangladesh where no profit is made, and higher tax brackets in certain JVs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- CapEx guidance maintained at Rs 700 crore for FY27 and Rs 650 crore for FY28.
- Depreciation run rate expected to increase to Rs 120 crore–Rs 130 crore range once TBM tunneling activities ramp up post-Q3.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net working capital elevated at 147 days due to funding projects and delays in certification/payments.
- Cash flow from operations in H1 was negative (historical seasonal trend), but expected to improve in H2 via mobilization advances for new orders and realization of stuck receivables.
- Gross debt increased to Rs 4,765 crore due to funding operations and sizable CapEx.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Stretched liquidity and delayed payments/certifications across the market, particularly in the U.P. Jal Jeevan Mission project.
- Geopolitical tensions affecting overseas logistics and energy availability (e.g., Bangladesh).
- Execution constraints at Vadhavan Port due to approach road delays and reclamation handover issues.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Aditya Bhartia (Investec) on ECL Provisioning Change:** Management explained they shifted from past-trend provisioning to an automatic bucket-based ECL matrix in March 2026, alongside conservative provisioning for Middle East receivables and arbitration discount headroom.
2. **Shravan Shah (Dolat Capital) on Withholding FY27 Guidance:** Management cited geopolitical uncertainties, energy issues in Bangladesh, and logistics challenges as reasons for holding back guidance, despite guiding for 20%-25% growth in FY28.
3. **Shirom Kapur (Jefferies) on L1 Pipeline:** Management stated they are L1 in two European projects worth Rs 4,200 crore, expecting conversion in FY28 rather than FY27 due to longer processing times in Europe.
4. **Bhavik Shah (Invexa Capital) on JJM U.P. Execution:** Management clarified that the U.P. pending order book is Rs 474 crore, and payments will release progressively once 75% thresholds are reached.
5. **Yogesh Doshi (Individual Investor) on Payment Terms & Valuations:** Management stated that domestic project payment terms follow Government of India procurement guidelines without interest clauses, but defended Afcons' premium valuation via corporate governance, high EBITDA margins (11%-12%), and global engineering rankings (ENR).

### One-line takeaway
Afcons reported a weak H1/Q2 due to delayed execution fronts and stretched collections, but management remains confident in achieving Rs 30,000 crore order inflows and stronger execution and cash flows in H2.
