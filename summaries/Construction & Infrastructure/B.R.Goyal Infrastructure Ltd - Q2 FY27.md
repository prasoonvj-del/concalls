# B.R.Goyal Infrastructure Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Driven by strong 61% YoY revenue growth, order inflows of INR 1,087 crores, and expansion in toll collection and EPC segments.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** INR 550 crore in H1 FY27 (vs INR 342 crore in H1 FY26, up ~61% YoY).
- **EBITDA (excl. other income):** INR 35 crore in H1 FY27 (vs INR 27 crore in H1 FY26, up ~28% YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 6.38% in H1 FY26 (Note: stated as 6.38% in transcript).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** INR 22 crore in H1 FY27 (vs INR 16 crore in H1 FY26, up ~38% YoY).
- **PAT Margin:** 4.02% in H1 FY27.
- **Order Book:** INR 1,755 crores as of September 30, 2026 (comprising roads INR 589 crores, buildings INR 72 crores, wastewater INR 132 crores, toll collection INR 962 crores).
- **Fresh Order Inflows (H1 FY27):** INR 1,087 crores gross (including Bundelkhand Expressway UPEIDA TCC of INR 377.54 crores, Cyber City civil work of INR 81.75 crores, Ratlam project of INR 13 crores + GST, and six NHAI toll plazas worth INR 589 crores total).
- **Operating Cash Flow:** INR 22.3 crores in H1 FY27 (vs INR 1.9 crores in FY26).
- **Borrowings / Limits:** Cash credit limit of INR 50 crores, bank guarantees of INR 315 crores (total INR 365 crores), plus INR 15 million in equipment loans.
- **Working Capital Limits:** Enhanced to INR 50 crores (from INR 35 crores) following documentation in March '26.
- **RMC Capacity Utilization:** ~70% to 80%-85% operating capacity.

### Highlights
- Secured 24 operating toll plazas across India following the addition of six fresh NHAI plazas (Katpur, Husnapur, Jajau, Surapattu, Chillakallu, and Khambara) and the Bundelkhand Expressway contract.
- Board approved setting up a new RMC plant in Indore with a 1.8 lakh cubic meter capacity, expected to be operational in H1 FY27-FY28 (Note: transcript says H1 FY27-FY28).
- Sales at the BRG Hillview residential plotting project have been paused with 1.6 lakh sq. ft. inventory held back due to expected rate increases and no immediate capital requirements.
- *Vs Record:* Continues the strong financial growth pattern noted in the Nov 2026 investor deck, driven by surging orders in EPC and toll collection.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Revenue Growth:** Management targets a consistent annual revenue growth of 20% to 25% (or 25% to 30% for the current year).
- **Order Inflow Target:** Expects total order book to increase by INR 1,000 crores to INR 1,500 crores more by the end of the current financial year, maintaining a 2.5x order book ratio.
- **H2 Execution:** H2 revenue is expected to match or exceed H1 levels with disciplined execution.
- **Wastewater Execution:** Expects to execute INR 80 crores to INR 90 crores in H2 FY27 from the INR 132 crore unexecuted order book, and target INR 300 crores to INR 400 crores in new wastewater orders by year-end via JVs.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Government policy changes in EPC are altering contractual language and increasing credential requirements, leading to a temporary shift/slowdown in EPC contract churning over the next 1.5 to 2 years.
- Private sector building construction in regions like Indore presents growing opportunities.
- Toll collection demand remains robust due to government infrastructure focus and capex allocations.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Global conflicts have pressured petrochemical products, increasing transportation (diesel) and bitumen costs, alongside cement prices.
- Price variation/escalation clauses provide full compensation from the central government, shielding EPC profitability from raw material price volatility.
- Toll collection contracts have zero depreciation and require only ~5% working capital, yielding higher ROCE (30% to 50%, scaling to 60%-70%) compared to traditional EPC (15% to 25%).
- EBITDA margin growth lagged revenue growth due to a higher revenue mix from toll collection contracts (lower blended EBITDA margins) and raw material cost pressures.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- RMC plant in Indore (established 2008) is nearing capacity; a new identical 1.8 lakh cubic meter plant is approved for Indore, targeting operation in H1 FY27-FY28.
- Exploring HAM and PPP models (including TOT and BOT road projects), though no awards secured yet.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital cycle operates at 45 to 60 days.
- Management does not foresee needing additional working capital or fund-based limits beyond the existing INR 50 crores due to mobilization advances and cash generation.
- Non-fund-based BG requirements are eased by the acceptance of surety bonds by central and state agencies. Short-term loans and advances increased due to working capital limit enhancement (from INR 35 cr to INR 50 cr) and equipment loan enhancements (~INR 10 cr).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Shorter bidding windows for NHAI toll collection tenders (often 10 days from notice) require rapid survey and bidding execution.
- Geopolitical tensions impacting petroleum, bitumen, and diesel prices create input cost volatility (offset by escalation clauses).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Mahesh Kumar (MU Investments) on TCC revenue and margins:** Management stated 40% to 50% of TCC orders are recognized as revenue in year one; explained that TCC ROCE (30%-50%) exceeds EPC ROCE despite lower EBITDA percentages.
2. **Chandrika on revenue mix and guidance:** Management guided that the revenue mix will stabilize at ~50% EPC and ~50% TCC as EPC contract policies evolve.
3. **Anirudh Sharma on cash flow and bank guarantees:** Management highlighted that IPO deployment paybacks and 60% revenue growth drove operating cash flow to INR 22.3 crores, and surety bonds have reduced traditional bank guarantee limits pressure.
4. **Madhav Das on wastewater expansion:** Management clarified that due to lack of prior eligibility, wastewater scaling will occur via strategic partnerships/JVs for name-lending and experience-building.
5. **Yash Parker on EBITDA divergence:** Management explained that lower blended TCC EBITDA margins and higher commodity/sub-contracting costs caused EBITDA to grow slower (28%) than revenue (61%).

### One-line takeaway
B.R. Goyal Infrastructure posted strong H1 FY27 revenue growth of 61% YoY to INR 550 crores, anchored by a balanced mix of high-ROCE toll collection contracts and traditional EPC execution.
