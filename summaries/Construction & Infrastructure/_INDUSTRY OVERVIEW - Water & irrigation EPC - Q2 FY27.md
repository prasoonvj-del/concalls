# Construction & Infrastructure - Water & irrigation EPC: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the water and irrigation EPC sector report a resilient operating environment in Q2 FY27, though performance diverged based on regional weather patterns and order-book composition. While companies dependent on agricultural micro-irrigation faced severe headwinds from drought conditions in southern and western India (notably Jain Irrigation), urban- and infrastructure-focused players maintained robust growth trajectories, healthy order books, and active bidding pipelines. Profitability came under temporary pressure for several players due to initial project execution phases, raw material inflation in HDPE/PVC pipes, and accounting adjustments. However, managements largely maintained their annual guidance, banking on a stronger second half driven by post-monsoon site activity, asset monetization, and accelerating government-backed tenders.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian water and irrigation EPC players operated on low-margin, tender-driven models reliant on state and central government budgets (such as Jal Jeevan Mission), carrying heavy working capital cycles and high exposure to delayed receivables. Over the past five years, the industry has seen a structural shift toward asset-light execution, de-risking through selective bidding, pivoting toward high-margin O&M and renewable energy/BESS integration, and moving away from legacy lumpy project businesses to improve balance sheet quality [Background].

**Shifts underway** - 
*   **Pivoting to Renewable Energy and BESS Integration:** Companies are scaling up renewable EPC and battery energy storage system (BESS) manufacturing to capture high-growth grid-scale storage demand. Enviro Infra acquired Suyog Urja for Rs 311 crore, generating 29% of H1 consolidated revenue from renewables [Enviro Infra concall Q2 FY27]. SPML Infra operationalized Phase 1 of its Pune BESS facility and secured design approvals for the NTPC Barauni BESS project, targeting 5 GWh capacity by FY28 [SPML Infra concall Q2 FY27]. This diversification opens up high-margin revenue streams outside traditional water EPC.
*   **De-risking via Project Business Reduction:** Irrigation players are actively running down legacy project books to curb working capital bloat. Jain Irrigation deliberately let go of Rs 200–250 crore of business in H1 and reduced active projects to 7–8, expecting project operations to hit near-zero by next September [Jain Irrigation concall Q2 FY27]. This protects cash flows against tight state liquidity.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements agree that long-term water infrastructure, transmission, and renewable integration remain critical national priorities. Most expect H2 FY27 to outpace H1 due to the subsiding monsoon and normalization of administrative processes following government transitions.
*   **Divergence:** Agricultural water demand diverged sharply from urban infrastructure. Jain Irrigation cited the worst monsoon in 35-50 years in West and South India, severely hurting micro-irrigation and rural piping demand. Conversely, urban-centric players like SPML Infra and Welspun Enterprises highlighted strong pipelines in water transmission, rehabilitation, tunneling, and extra high-voltage (EHV) transmission.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Agreements:** Managements across the board faced cost pressures. Denta Water cited HDPE/PVC pipe inflation driven by petrol costs; Enviro Infra noted that price escalation clauses do not fully cover 100% of inflation. 
*   **Disagreements/Strategies:** While Denta Water aims for a minimum 20% profit margin and SPML Infra enforces strict Price Variation (PV) clauses and a hurdle rate of $\ge$10% operating margins on all bids, Jain Irrigation noted that polymer price volatility does not hurt its margins because pricing is pegged to average inventory costs rather than spot prices. Enviro Infra's margin compression was primarily attributed to accounting entries (interest on the Suyog Urja acquisition and profit reversals on internal EPC-to-IPP projects) rather than operational failure.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Denta Water:** Capable of executing single projects up to Rs 300 crore standalone, actively bidding in Odisha, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu.
*   **Enviro Infra:** Total Suyog Urja acquisition cost of Rs 311 crore (Rs 111 crore paid, balance Rs 200 crore over two years).
*   **Jain Irrigation:** Planning Rs 30–40 crore capex for a tomato JV in March and ~Rs 100 crore for Phase 2 of its beverage business [Jain Irrigation concall Q2 FY27].
*   **SPML Infra:** Working toward 5 GWh BESS capacity with a 600-container manufacturing facility in Pune targeted for H1 FY28 [SPML Infra concall Q2 FY27].
*   **Welspun Enterprises:** Benefiting from a DGH order to use existing ONGC facilities for the Mumbai gas block, reducing capital investment by $300–400 million [Welspun Enterprises concall Q2 FY27].

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Enviro Infra:** Tone shifted to explaining accounting-led margin moderation; previous expectation of positive operating cash flow (OCF) by September was missed, and OCF is now targeted positive by Q4 FY27 [Enviro Infra concall Q2 FY27].
*   **SPML Infra:** Delivered ahead of schedule on NTPC BESS design approvals previously promised for December 2026; guidance of at least 25% top-line and bottom-line growth maintained [SPML Infra concall Q2 FY27].
*   **Welspun Enterprises:** Maintained 10-20% revenue growth guidance and 18%+ EBITDA margin, bolstered by the successful 51% stake sale in the Mukarba Chowk-Panipat project [Welspun Enterprises concall Q2 FY27].
