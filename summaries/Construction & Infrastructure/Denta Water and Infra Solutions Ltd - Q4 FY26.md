# Denta Water and Infra Solutions Ltd - Q4 FY26 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-06-29 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** Q4 and FY26
**Tone:** Cautious - Revenue growth is expected, but margins face pressure from project mix and raw material costs, alongside past payment delays due to political transitions.

### Key numbers
- **FY26 Revenue from operations:** Rs. 2,503.8 million (growth of over 23% YoY)
- **FY26 EBITDA:** Rs. 834.9 million
- **FY26 Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 609 million
- **Outstanding Order Book (as of March 31, 2026):** Rs. 7,277.76 million across 38 ongoing projects
- **Q4 FY26 Revenue from operations:** Rs. 553.12 million (an increase of Rs. 12 crores YoY)
- **Collection Period:** 45 to 60 days
- **Bidding Pipeline:** 5 to 6 projects valued at around Rs. 600 crores

### Highlights
- Company completed 40 projects to date and focuses on water management (groundwater recharging, drinking water supply, irrigation, wastewater management, and sewage treatment).
- Adopted new patented/advanced technologies like NEBR and CAMUS for sewage treatment plants (STPs) to achieve up to 95%-98% purity, often utilizing subcontract models.
- Maintained a zero-debt profile except for minor funds taken from NBFCs for day-to-day operations.
- Order book mix includes Amrut-2 (e.g., Rs. 232 crore project in Koppal district) and Jal Jivan Mission projects.
- Recent political transition in Karnataka slowed tender finalization over the last 6 months, but the new government is now pushing infrastructure projects forward.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Revenue Growth:** Management expects a minimum 20% to 25% growth in revenue for FY27.
- **EBITDA Margin:** Management guided for EBITDA margins of around 25% to 30% for FY27.
- **Order Inflows:** Management expects to announce new order books in upcoming quarters as they bid aggressively for 5 to 6 tenders.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Jal Jivan Mission is being pushed aggressively by the government in its latest budget speech; Amrut 2 scheme and Swachh Bharat are also key drivers.
- Current monsoon deficiency has not affected project execution as water availability at project sites and reservoir storage (20% to 25% in Karnataka) remain adequate.
- Government financial stability looks better this year, and payments are expected to be fast-tracked from late July or August.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material costs (20% to 30% dependent on UPVC and HDPE pipes tied to petroleum products) increased in Q4 and April/May due to the Iran-US war. Management is negotiating hard with suppliers to mitigate this.
- Margin compression in certain quarters was attributed to executing a mix of smaller-value projects (e.g., Rs. 10 crore to Rs. 50 crore) alongside larger ones.
- Projects with a value of more than Rs. 100 crores have price escalation clauses; those below Rs. 100 crores do not.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capital is currently tied up in inventory and unbilled revenue on ongoing projects; management expects to liquidate roughly 60% of these working capital investments by December/January as monsoon recedes and execution accelerates.
- Exploring expansion into other states (Madhya Pradesh, Orissa) and evaluating opportunities in biogas/renewable sectors with partners.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Zero long-term debt profile; only minor funds borrowed from NBFCs for day-to-day operations.
- Working capital tied up in other asset items, inventory (Rs. 190 crores), and share/trade receivables; cash collections expected to improve by December/January.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in petroleum-derived raw material prices (UPVC/HDPE pipes) driven by geopolitical tensions (Iran-US war).
- Past slowdown in tender floats and payment rationalization due to political transitions and guarantee scheme rationalization in Karnataka.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Himanshu (MB Investments) asked about El Nino water shortages and government payment status:** Management stated Karnataka has no acute shortage (reservoirs at 20-25%), and government payments are expected to accelerate from late July/August.
- **Tejas Khandelwal asked about FY27 execution guidance and price escalation:** Management guided for 20% revenue growth and noted that projects over Rs. 100 crores include price escalation clauses.
- **Maitree Shah asked about margin dip and smaller-value projects:** Management explained that blending smaller-value quick-turnaround projects with larger ones impacts margins, but increases absolute profit volume.
- **Shafat Hussain asked about working capital liquidation and geographic expansion:** Management stated they expect to liquidate ~60% of working capital assets by December/January and are exploring projects in Madhya Pradesh and Orissa.
- **Hitesh challenged the math on bottom-line growth given declining margins:** Management acknowledged the calculation and reiterated that they will push for higher revenue and absolute profit growth over the remaining nine months of FY27.

### One-line takeaway
Denta Water targets 20-25% revenue growth and 25-30% EBITDA margins for FY27, balancing raw material cost pressures and working capital investments with an aggressive push into water infrastructure tenders under a newly stabilized state government.
