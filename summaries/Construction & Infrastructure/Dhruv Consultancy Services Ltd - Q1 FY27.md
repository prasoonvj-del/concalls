# Dhruv Consultancy Services Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-25 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Management cited paper losses and low revenue absorption in Q1 due to estimate corrections and project mobilization phases, but expressed optimism for margin and revenue recovery in H2 as new orders scale up.

### Key numbers
- **Net sales (Q1 FY27):** Rs 15.55 crore
- **Total income (Q1 FY27):** Rs 15.95 crore
- **Operating loss (Q1 FY27):** Rs 3 crore (operating margin minus 23%)
- **Total expenditure (Q1 FY27):** Rs 20.41 crore
- **Order inflows (Q1 FY27):** Rs 90 crore to Rs 100 crore
- **Unexecuted order book:** Rs 300 crore (excluding Q2 additions)
- **Q2 order additions:** Additional Rs 60 crore to Rs 65 crore

### Highlights
- Secured multiple orders in Q1 FY27, including a Rs 19.34 crore assignment for authority engineer and project management services for the 140-kilometer Rowghat-Jagdalpur railway line, a rail-over bridge construction supervision order in Uttar Pradesh, an Rs 8.34 crore order from MPRDC for the Ujjain-Jaora Greenfield Highway, and a record Rs 40.92 crore assignment from OBCC (Odisha Bridge Corporation).
- Received strategic empanelments from BMC (Municipal infrastructure PMC for 5 years) and India Exim Bank (DPR technical advisory and lenders' independent engineering).
- Entered the Wayside Amenities segment via a 15-year lease-based O&M project, forming an SPV named Verul Drivehub Private Limited (55% stake held by Dhruv Consultancy).
- Ranked number 9 in India among 100 infrastructure consultants by NHAI, which provided technical scoring advantages in recent bidding.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects 15% to 20% of the order book to convert into revenue.
- Management stated that Q1 and Q2 typically account for 30% to 40% of annual revenue due to monsoons and award seasons, while Q3 and Q4 see higher revenue recognition and mobilization.
- Management expects billing from newly secured orders to start around Q3 FY27, leading to improved margins.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Government focus on the railway and highway sectors continues to remain strong.
- International expansion targets markets in Africa (Mozambique empaneled, Ghana as preferred bidder), Southeast Asia, the Gulf, and South/Central Asia, aiming for 10% to 15% of the order book from international markets.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Q1 operating loss was attributed by management to "paper losses" from estimate corrections (such as the removal of the network survey vehicle scope by NHAI across India) and insufficient revenue base to absorb high fixed employee and project management costs (employee expenses alone run around Rs 15 crore).
- Operating leverage is sensitive to execution scale, but newly won bids are being submitted with better profit margins.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Upgrading internal systems by developing AI dashboards to manage 70+ assignments and increase operational capacity up to 120 assignments.
- Conducting Building Information Modeling (BIM) training (moving towards 4D/5D/6D BIM and digital twins).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Receivable collection cycle typically ranges between 60 to 90 days, extending up to 120 days in difficult terrains like the Northeast.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Policy changes by clients (e.g., NHAI removing network survey vehicle scope) can lead to contract reductions and estimate corrections.
- Turnaround time in international markets is very slow (4 to 6 months after declaration).
- Geopolitical issues (war situation) impacted some bids in Saudi Arabia.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Mehul Shah (VS Ventures) asked about FY27 revenue guidance and the Q1 operating loss.** Management stated that SEBI LODR rules prevent giving exact numbers, explained that Q1/Q2 are traditionally lower due to monsoons and mobilization, and noted that the Q1 loss was largely a paper loss from estimate corrections that will be absorbed as new orders scale up in Q3/Q4.
2. **Neha Patil (Visible Ultra Source Limited) asked about the Exim Bank empanelment and international opportunities.** Management (Pandurang Dandawate) explained that Exim Bank funds Indian contractors abroad (Middle East, Central Asia, Southeast Asia, etc.) and appoints consultants for DPR vetting and lenders' independent engineering; they noted wins/bids in Mozambique and Ghana.
3. **Sakshi Shinde (Shah Consultancy) asked about quality control across multiple geographies and projects.** Management responded that they deploy 14 to 15 key experienced professionals per site with real-time head office reporting, and are currently developing AI dashboards for tracking project schedules.
4. **Vidhi Purohit (Phoenix Capital) asked about team structuring and hiring for the Rowghat-Jagdalpur railway project.** Management stated that 108 specialized personnel are being hired externally as mandated by the RFP/contract document, which has temporarily elevated employee costs.
5. **Saket Kapoor (Kapoor & Company) asked about the PBT recovery path and profitability trend given the stock price correction.** Management reiterated that fixed costs are currently high relative to the Q1 revenue base, but higher-margin orders secured in Q1 and Q2 will begin billing in Q3, driving margin improvement and better cost absorption.

### One-line takeaway
Dhruv Consultancy reported a Q1 FY27 operating loss due to estimate corrections and low revenue absorption, but anticipates margin and revenue recovery in H2 driven by a record Rs 90+ crore order inflow in Q1, rising ticket sizes, and new verticals like railways and wayside amenities.
