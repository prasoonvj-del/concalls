# Dilip Buildcon Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-18 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management maintains all annual guidance, citing steady execution and a constructive medium-term outlook despite a soft Q1 awarding environment.

### Key numbers
*   **Standalone Revenue:** Rs 1,930 crore.
*   **Standalone EBITDA:** Rs 199 crore (Margin: 10.32%).
*   **Standalone PAT:** Rs 39 crore (vs Rs 123 crore in Q1 FY26, which included Rs 98 crore exceptional gain).
*   **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 2,378 crore.
*   **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 429 crore (Margin: 18.05%).
*   **Consolidated PAT:** Rs 128 crore.
*   **Order Book:** Rs 27,691 crore.
*   **Order Inflow (Q1):** Rs 268 crore.
*   **Standalone Net Debt:** Rs 2,106 crore (vs Rs 1,880 crore as of March 31, 2026).
*   **Consolidated Net Debt:** Rs 7,801 crore.

### Highlights
*   **Order Book Diversification:** Presence across 12 verticals, including mining, roads, irrigation, and renewables.
*   **Mining MDO:** Produced 4.79 million tonnes of coal in Q1. Management remains on track for 57 million tonnes production by FY29.
*   **Project Wins:** Completed 3 HAM projects (Rs 1,700 crore) ahead of schedule. Declared L1 bidder for the Sikasar to Kodar Reservoir Link Canal (Rs 2,524 crore).
*   **Asset Monetization:** Board approved a stake sale in power transmission and solar projects (combined cost ~Rs 8,400 crore) to Alpha Alternatives, with Alpha co-investing 49% during construction.
*   **InvIT:** Held InvIT units worth Rs 1,521 crore (face value). Plans to transfer 11 remaining HAM assets by March 2027.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Revenue Growth:** 30%-40% (maintained).
*   **EBITDA Margin:** 10%-12% (maintained).
*   **New Order Inflow:** Rs 10,000-12,000 crore (maintained).
*   **Debt Reduction:** Rs 600-800 crore (maintained).
*   **Standalone Net Debt:** Management expects to be net debt-free by FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   Sector awarding was "softer" in Q1 due to Bharat Mala pipeline recalibration.
*   NHAI pipeline for FY27 includes 54 projects (2,442 km) worth ~Rs 1.80 lakh crore.
*   Management views current administrative and working capital delays as cyclical, not structural.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Working capital cycle: 133 days (up from 131 days). Management expects normalization to 120 days by year-end.
*   Commodity price volatility (fuel, bitumen) impacted margins, though government reimbursement/relaxation provided some relief.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   Standalone Capex guidance: < Rs 100 crore.
*   Coal Handling Plant (CHP) at Siarmal expected to be operational in ~1.5 years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   Standalone Net Debt/Equity: 0.31x.
*   Working capital cycle expected to normalize in H2.
*   Management stated that equity requirements for the Siarmal project will be funded through internal accruals.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Global uncertainties regarding crude prices.
*   Elongated working capital cycles across the industry.
*   Administrative delays regarding project approvals and payments.
*   Wayanad tunnel incident: Management clarified it was a natural calamity, not a construction defect, and does not impact technical scores.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Debt Reduction:** Management clarified that the Q1 increase in debt is a seasonal working capital build-up; they remain committed to the Rs 600-800 crore reduction target for the year.
*   **Alpha Alternatives Deal:** Management explained that Alpha’s 49% co-investment reduces DBL's equity commitment, while structured equity is used for the remainder, allowing DBL to preserve cash for debt reduction.
*   **MDO Revenue Math:** Management guided for Siarmal revenue at ~Rs 550/tonne and Pachhwara at ~Rs 1,200/tonne.
*   **InvIT Transfers:** Management confirmed 4 projects will be flipped this month, with all 18 projects expected to be transferred by FY27 end or Q1 FY28.
*   **Wayanad Tunnel:** Management reiterated that the incident was caused by extreme rainfall (300mm in one night) and not construction failure.

### One-line takeaway
Dilip Buildcon maintains its growth and debt-reduction guidance, relying on a diversified order book and a strategic asset-monetization model to navigate a soft sector environment.
