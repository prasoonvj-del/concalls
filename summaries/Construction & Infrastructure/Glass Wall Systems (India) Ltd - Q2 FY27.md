# Glass Wall Systems (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Construction & Infrastructure*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported 16% YoY revenue growth in H1, expanding EBITDA margins, a robust Rs. 957 crore order book, and maintained full-year guidance despite temporary international headwinds.

### Key numbers
- H1 FY27 Revenue from Operations: Rs. 239 crore (up 16% YoY)
- H1 FY27 Domestic Revenue: Rs. 140 crore (up 33% YoY)
- H1 FY27 International Revenue: Rs. 76 crore (down 14% YoY)
- H1 FY27 Fenestration Revenue: Rs. 22 crore (up 82% YoY)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs. 60 crore (up 22% YoY)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin: 25.3% (expanding 138 bps YoY; 26.9% adjusted for freight)
- H1 FY27 PAT: Rs. 46 crore (up 13% YoY)
- H1 FY27 PAT Margin: 19.2% (or 19.6%)
- Total Order Book as of September 30, 2026: Rs. 957 crore
- Order inflows booked in H1 FY27: Rs. 291 crore
- Capacity Utilization: 55% - 60%

### Highlights
- H1 FY27 revenue grew 16% YoY to Rs. 239 crore, driven by strong domestic project execution and luxury fenestration demand.
- Freight costs for export clients (Rs. 14 crore in H1) are now recognized as both income and expenses as a pass-through.
- Glass processing unit (funded via IPO) is progressing through advanced civil construction; machinery orders are placed, with commissioning targeted for July/August next year.
- Working capital days stood at negative 19 days as of September end.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guided for FY27 revenue growth between 20% to 22% while sustaining historical EBITDA margins (23% to 25%) and PAT margins (18% to 20%).
- Management expects to book Rs. 500 crore or more in new orders over the next two quarters to maintain an 18-month execution visibility.
- Management expects Q3 and Q4 to be significantly stronger.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic Tier 1 cities exhibit tremendous demand across commercial, hospitality, and residential segments.
- International demand remains structurally strong, but US developers are temporarily holding back new job launches due to uncertainties around the US midterm elections, war, and shipping/commodity prices. Existing US orders on the floor are fully funded and unaffected.
- Management explored the Israeli market and expects order bookings in Q3 and Q4 as an entry point.
- Luxury fenestration segment shows high growth potential driven by premium residential launches in Delhi and Mumbai.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Contracts feature price protection clauses for aluminum and glass base prices, insulating the company from commodity volatility.
- Escalation claims amounting to Rs. 6 crore were certified and booked in Q2 for past cost incurrences.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Glass processing unit project cost is approximately Rs. 70 crore, funded via IPO proceeds; civil construction is in an advanced stage, and commissioning is targeted for July/August next year.
- Glass processing unit is expected to improve EBITDA margins by 1% to 1.5% starting towards Q3/Q4 of next year.
- Current facility can support peak revenues of over Rs. 1,000 crore without material capex.
- Other upcoming capex is expected to remain under Rs. 20 crore over the next year and a half to two years, funded through internal accruals.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital days are at negative 19 days (management normal-case assumption is around 45 days).
- The company maintains cash in the bank.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Short-term headwinds and prudence in the international (US) market regarding new job launches due to geopolitical and shipping uncertainties.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Harsh Kothari (MK Securities)** asked if US customers are cancelling existing orders. Management clarified that existing orders on the floor are 100% funded and unaffected; hesitation is strictly restricted to new job launches.
- **Pritesh Chheda** asked about raw material price protection. Management reiterated that contracts are re-measurable item rate contracts with explicit protection mechanisms for aluminum and glass base prices.
- **Priyanka Agarwal** asked about contract types and escalation revenue. Management clarified no jobs are cost-plus, and Rs. 6 crore of escalation claims were certified and booked in Q2.
- **Ankit Babel** asked about capacity utilization and peak revenue. Management stated utilization is 55%–60% with peak revenue potential exceeding Rs. 1,000 crore from current capacities.
- **Rahul Maheshwari** asked about working capital impact from freight pass-through. Management stated they receive advances against freight costs, resulting in no new working capital blockage.

### One-line takeaway
Glass Wall Systems reported a steady H1 FY27 with 16% revenue growth and margin expansion, supported by a healthy Rs. 957 crore order book and an optimistic H2 outlook despite temporary US new-order headwinds.
