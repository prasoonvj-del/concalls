# Shree TNB Polymers Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-23 · Plastics & Pipes*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management cited a strong upcoming second half, robust order book, and new capacity expansions.

### Key numbers
- H1 FY27 Revenue: Rs 86.08 crore
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs 7.57 crore
- H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin: 8.80%
- H1 FY27 PAT: Rs 2.58 crore (Note: Our record lists net profit as Rs 3 cr for Q2 FY27; transcript states H1 PAT is Rs 2.58 crore)
- H1 FY27 PAT Margin: 3%
- H1 FY27 EPS: Rs 1.68
- Order book as of date: Rs 64.23 crore
- Turnover as of November 13: Rs 32 crore
- Fresh HDPE pipe orders received: Rs 51 crore
- Installed capacity: 26,500 MT, increasing to 36,500 MT post-expansion
- Additional capacity addition: ~10,000 MT
- Potential revenue from new capacity: Rs 135–150 crore
- Target capacity utilisation: ~70-75%

### Highlights
- Management highlighted that H1 is typically slow due to the 3-4 month monsoon season, while 60-65% of business occurs in H2.
- Phase 1 construction for new capacities (drums, bubble-guard/honeycomb sheets) completed via internal accruals; IPO proceeds (received October) will fund upcoming machinery/capex.
- Term-loan repayment made in October using IPO funds.
- *Note vs record:* While earlier records and decks pointed to commercial production starting March 2027, the earnings call clarified that machine delivery and installation will start around March 2027, with trials/commercial production commencing around April-May 2027 and major revenue contributions shifting to FY 27-28 or FY 28-29.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 FY27 to be significantly better than H1, following historical trends where 60% of business takes place in H2.
- New capacity additions are projected to generate Rs 135–150 crore in revenue at full commercial operation, expected in FY 28-29.
- Combined total turnover potential estimated at Rs 250–300 crore once new projects ramp up.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Indian plastic fittings industry growing at 10.4%; global plastic pipes at 6.46%, protective packaging at 5.19%, and polymer production at 13.37%.
- Growth driven by Jal Jeevan Mission, AMRUT scheme, smart-city water initiatives, and piped/natural gas expansion.
- Strong industrial shift from metal and wood to polymers (e.g., in chemicals, automotive packaging, and cable protection).
- Torrent Power is the largest customer for cable protection pipes; other key clients include Tata, Adani, and Amul Dairy.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Second-half margins normally operate at 4.5%–5% due to higher capacity utilization and pricing leverage.
- Government mandates under EPR system require 30% reprocessed material in drums currently, 40% next year, and 60% thereafter.
- New 3-layer drum machinery uses 100% virgin material for outer and inner layers and reprocessed material in the middle, lowering costs by ~30% and expanding margins.
- Foreign technology discussion ongoing to reduce drum weight (e.g., from 8.5 kg to 7.5 kg) to save raw material costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total installed capacity expanding from 26,500 MT to 36,500 MT (+10,000 MT).
- Breakdown of 10,000 MT addition: Honeycomb sheets (~4,000 MT), PP corrugated sheets (~2,000 MT), 210-litre drums (~1,500 MT), 60-litre drums (~500 MT), and plastic pallets (~1,500 MT).
- New land acquired: 10,111 square meters with a ~26,000 sq. ft. shed built for Phase 1. Phase 2 shed (~20,000 sq. ft.) to be funded via IPO proceeds.
- Machinery delivery and installation scheduled around March 2027; commercial production expected in April-May 2027.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Phase 1 construction funded entirely through internal accruals.
- Term-loan repayment executed in October using IPO funds.
- Working capital requirements for upcoming expansion may be funded through bank borrowings.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Monsoons disrupt outdoor pipe laying and drip-irrigation work for 3-4 months, restricting H1 activity.
- Government/direct infrastructure project payments can sometimes face delays, especially around election periods (though mitigated via LCs and bank guarantees).
- Polymer capacity utilisation is naturally capped at ~70-75% due to operational changeover times between different mold sizes.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Nimesh Shah (Investor) on capacity underutilisation risk:** Management stated that existing customer relationships in the chemical and packaging sectors, along with direct B2B marketing, will absorb the new capacity easily given local demand.
2. **Nimesh Shah on H1 PAT margin of 3% and profitability improvement:** Management explained that H2 volumes and pricing leverage typically lift margins to 4.5%-5%, aided by interest savings from IPO debt repayments.
3. **Naman Desai (Investor) on repeat business from major clients:** Management confirmed strong repeat demand as underground cabling replaces overhead lines across infrastructure projects.
4. **Amit Vyas (Investor) on margins of new products vs existing ones:** Management highlighted that the 3-layer drum technology allows the use of cheaper recycled material in the core while maintaining virgin quality layers, yielding higher margins.
5. **Suraj Jain (Investor) on aggregate turnover target for FY 27-28:** Management estimated that total revenues could reach Rs 250–300 crore once new capacities ramp up fully.

### One-line takeaway
Shree TNB Polymers reported a modest H1 FY27 (PAT of Rs 2.58 crore) but anticipates a robust H2 backed by a Rs 64.23 crore order book and ongoing IPO-funded expansions adding 10,000 MTPA capacity by early FY28.
