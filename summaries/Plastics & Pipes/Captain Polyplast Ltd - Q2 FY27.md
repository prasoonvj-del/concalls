# Captain Polyplast Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Plastics & Pipes*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong solar EPC order wins and revenue growth were offset by margin compression due to rising raw material costs and higher interest expenses.

### Key numbers
*   **Total Income:** Rs 106.43 crore (up 32.89% YoY).
*   **EBITDA:** Rs 10.28 crore (up 24.75% YoY).
*   **EBITDA Margin:** Declined YoY (9.66% in Q2 FY27 vs 10.29% in Q2 FY26).
*   **PAT:** Rs 4.48 crore (up 5.55% YoY).
*   **Solar Pump Orders (FY27 YTD):** Rs 82.4 crore for 3,500 pumps.
*   **Non-subsidy/Commercial Sales Mix:** ~10% of micro-irrigation business.
*   **Micro-irrigation Segment Margin:** 7%–8%.
*   **Solar EPC Segment Margin:** 10%–11%.

### Highlights
*   **Solar EPC Growth:** Secured two repeat orders from MSEDCL under PM-KUSUM: Rs 23.6 crore (1,000 pumps) in July and Rs 47 crore (2,000 pumps) in September.
*   **Operational Update:** Ahmedabad manufacturing facility is ramping up; management is progressively shifting component manufacturing in-house to aid margins.
*   **Product Innovation:** Launched automation solutions for existing drip irrigation fields, offering time-based or volume-based irrigation control.
*   **Debt Profile:** Total debt is primarily working capital-related (revolving facility); actual annual repayment obligation is less than Rs 10 crore.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **H2 FY27 Margins:** Management expects EBITDA margin improvement in H2 due to anticipated revisions in benchmark costs for micro-irrigation by central/state governments and increased in-house manufacturing at the Ahmedabad facility.
*   **Working Capital:** Management expects the consolidated working capital cycle to shorten to 5–6 months by the end of FY27.
*   **Revenue Recognition:** Majority of the 3,500 solar pumps order book is expected to be recognized by Q3 FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Micro-irrigation:** Demand is supported by government initiatives like "Per Drop More Crop" and uneven monsoons (El Nino effect) driving adoption in water-deficient pockets.
*   **Solar EPC:** PM-KUSUM scheme continues to drive demand; management is exploring opportunities in states beyond Maharashtra, pending the announcement of the second phase of the scheme.
*   **Subsidy Dependence:** The industry remains ~90% dependent on government subsidies.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Margin Pressure:** EBITDA margin decline in Q2 was attributed to a sharp YoY increase in raw material costs (HDPE and LLDPE).
*   **Interest Costs:** Higher working capital requirements led to increased interest costs in H1 compared to the previous year.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Ahmedabad Facility:** Ramp-up is ongoing; management expects a 0.5% EBITDA margin benefit from this facility by March end, with a total 1%–1.5% benefit expected over three years as production phases in.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Receivables:** Management stated that receivables are primarily from government agencies with minimal risk of non-recovery; collection cycles are typically longer, with H1 receivables higher than H2.
*   **Contingent Liabilities:** Reported at Rs 15.20 crore in FY26, primarily consisting of performance bank guarantees for government contracts.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Why the divergence between revenue growth (25.14% in H1) and PAT growth (7%)?** A: Management cited rising raw material costs and higher interest expenses due to increased working capital needs.
*   **Q: How do you plan to reduce debt?** A: Management clarified that most debt is short-term working capital revolving facilities, not term debt; actual annual repayment is <Rs 10 crore, manageable via accruals.
*   **Q: What is the strategy for non-subsidy sales?** A: Focusing on repeat orders from farmers who have already availed subsidies and expanding export sales.
*   **Q: Is the 1%–1.5% margin improvement guidance for Ahmedabad still achievable?** A: Yes, but it will accrue over a 3-year period as the facility ramps up in phases.

### One-line takeaway
Captain Polyplast is banking on government benchmark price revisions and in-house manufacturing to recover margins in H2, while leveraging strong solar EPC order wins to drive top-line growth.
