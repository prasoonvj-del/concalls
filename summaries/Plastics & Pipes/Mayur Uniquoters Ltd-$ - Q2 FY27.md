# Mayur Uniquoters Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Plastics & Pipes*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consolidated revenue increased by 18% YoY, and PBT and PAT increased by 43% and 42% YoY, respectively, driven by strong export growth and warehouse stock clearances.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** INR282.95 crore (up 18% YoY)
- **Consolidated PBT:** INR79.31 crore (up 43% YoY)
- **Consolidated PAT:** INR57.99 crore (up 42% YoY)
- **Standalone Revenue:** INR252.99 crore (up 6% YoY)
- **Standalone PBT:** INR70.46 crore (up 9% YoY)
- **Standalone PAT:** INR51.27 crore (up 7% YoY)
- **Total Quantity Sold:** 77.51 lakh meters
- **PU Revenue:** INR6.28 crore
- **Segment Breakdown (Standalone):**
  - Export General: INR33.00 crore
  - Export OEM: INR57.46 crore
  - Total Export: INR90.46 crore
  - Total Domestic OEM: INR62.80 crore
  - Replacement: INR45.02 crore
  - Footwear: INR45.34 crore
  - Furnishing: INR7.19 crore
  - Others: INR2.18 crore
  - Domestic Total: INR162.53 crore

### Highlights
- Consolidated revenue growth outperformed standalone growth due to higher dispatches/sales from overseas warehouses rather than direct dispatches from India, reducing inventory at warehouses.
- Employee costs reduced sequentially due to the reversal of extra provisions made in Q1 on account of changes in Labor Codes; normal run-rate is around INR12.5 crore per quarter (12.5% yield).
- Management reported robust relationships with global OEMs like Mercedes-Benz and BMW in South Africa, and Ford and Chrysler in the U.S.
- CSR initiatives included planting around 45,000 rubber trees, adopting Happy Schools, and focusing on health, sanitation, and family welfare.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects sales growth momentum to continue over the next 2-3 years.
- Export OEM sales are projected to reach around INR250 crore to INR300 crore this year (compared to ~INR280 crore consolidated last year).
- Management expects a sustained gross margin of around 55% to 56%.
- Top-line growth of 10% to 15% is expected to continue on both standalone and consolidated bases.

### Demand & industry commentary
- U.S. automotive market has been slow for the past 2 quarters as consumers are buying fewer new cars, leading automotive companies to revise projections downwards.
- South Africa is performing well with BMW and Mercedes-Benz touching full projections.
- Footwear business is witnessing improvement in both value and volume, though focus remains selectively on high-margin segments.
- Furnishing retail business faced some domestic price competition over the last 5-6 months, but has a large network of 750-850 sub-dealers/dealers and 8-10 distributors.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margins fluctuated due to raw material and crude oil price movements, with management guiding to a sustainable 55%-56%.
- Freight costs spiked due to the West Asia crisis and crude oil prices (reaching COVID-era peaks), but management noted they have already come down by 20%-25% from peak levels. Management stated price hikes will only be discussed with customers if increases become permanent and stable.
- PU business is currently operating at 25% capacity utilization and requires 40% utilization to reach breakeven; it is not yet generating profit but is managed through tax benefits.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Machinery and equipment for the additional line are expected by November-end/December, with installation planned for the March quarter and commercial operations expected in Q1.
- Management is considering setting up a new plant in India (specifically the South region) and evaluating a plant in Mexico, though plans are not yet finalized.
- Solar project (Bhadla Mini Grid Solar) payback period is stated to be a maximum of 2 years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- The company maintains a strong cash flow position with almost no debt.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in U.S. consumer demand for new vehicles and downward revisions in OEM projections.
- Geopolitical tensions (West Asia crisis) impacting freight costs and raw material prices.
- Low capacity utilization (25%) in the PU business.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Rachna Kukreja (SiMPL) asked about volume growth and reasons for lower export dispatches.** Management explained that U.S. demand was soft, so they pushed overseas warehouses to clear existing stock rather than making fresh dispatches from India.
2. **Anubhav Mukherjee (Prescient Capital) asked about sustainable gross margins given quarterly fluctuations.** Management guided to a sustainable range of 55% to 56%.
3. **Bhanu Batra (Ants and Bees Investments) asked about passing on surging freight/PVC costs due to the West Asia crisis.** Management stated that freight rates have already retreated 20-25% from peaks, and they will only engage customers for price hikes if cost increases become permanent.
4. **Nirali Gopani (Unique PMS) asked about succession planning and the failed AGM resolution.** Management clarified that the resolution regarding remuneration and perquisites was not passed by shareholders, but it will be revised and resubmitted; the third generation joined 6 months ago to learn the business.
5. **Pulkit Singhal (Dalmus Capital Management) asked about export OEM revenue projections.** Management stated last year's consolidated export OEM was ~INR280 crore, and they expect INR250 crore to INR300 crore this year, growing 10%-15% thereafter.

### One-line takeaway
Mayur Uniquoters reported a strong Q2 FY27 with consolidated revenue up 18% YoY and PAT up 42% YoY, aided by warehouse inventory clearance and solid international OEM demand despite a soft U.S. automotive market.
