# Plastics & Pipes - Plastic products & packaging: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Q2 FY27 presented a contrasting environment for the plastics, packaging, and recycling sector. While structural domestic demand, healthcare (specifically GLP-1 pens), and wire & cable segments drove stellar performances for several players (e.g., Shaily Engineering, Xpro India, Ddev Plastiks, Mold-Tek), the broader industry faced severe external headwinds. Escalating geopolitical conflicts in West Asia triggered steep raw material inflation (polymer price spikes of 20% to 45%) and logistics disruptions, causing container shortages, port congestion, and soaring export freight rates. 

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus Agreement:** Managements across the board agreed that domestic demand remains healthy and resilient. Strong tailwinds were reported in infrastructure, power transmission/data centers (highlighted by Ddev Plastiks), pharma/FMCG (Mold-Tek), premium office furniture (Atharva Poly-Plast), and chemical packaging (Pyramid Technoplast). 
* **Divergence & Weakness:** Export markets experienced friction due to logistics bottlenecks and freight inflation (noted by All Time Plastics, Mold-Tek, Pyramid, and Ddev). Sectoral divergence was also visible in specific niches: Mold-Tek highlighted a 12.3% YoY drag in its lubricant packaging segment due to client-side input issues, whereas Shaily Engineering saw a massive 78% YoY surge in its Healthcare segment, contrasting with flat consumer segment demand.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Raw Material Inflation:** Companies faced heavy cost pressures. All Time Plastics reported 40% to 45% RM inflation; Mold-Tek noted raw material prices jumped 60% over 6 months; Atharva Poly-Plast cited a 22% polymer price rise driven by crude. 
* **Logistics & Freight:** West Asia conflicts severely impacted shipping. Ddev Plastiks noted export freight rates spiked to USD 300–350 per ton, while All Time Plastics faced port congestion and container backlogs. Jain Resource Recycling and Pyramid Technoplast mitigated import risks by shifting toward local sourcing (e.g., Pyramid shifting to 80% local procurement).
* **Margin Pass-Through:** Approaches to margin protection varied. All Time Plastics and Atharva Poly-Plast absorbed initial cost shocks or faced a 1-month lag in price adjustments, compressing margins. Conversely, Mold-Tek and Pyramid Technoplast successfully passed on hikes, with Mold-Tek expanding EBITDA per kg to INR46.5.

## Capex & capacity
Managements actively pursued capacity expansions and vertical integration despite geopolitical uncertainties:
* **All Time Plastics:** Preparing for its Bamboo facility commercial production in November 2026 and adding 14 injection molding machines.
* **Atharva Poly-Plast:** Planning FY28-FY29 capacity growth as utilization nears 88%.
* **Ddev Plastiks:** Advancing HFFR capacity and reviewing BESS project rollout for Q1 FY28.
* **Jain Resource Recycling:** Commissioned copper anode facilities and its Ahmedabad subsidiary, while targeting Q3 for wire rod, busbar, and plastic recycling plants.
* **Mitsu Chem Plast:** Finalizing its fully automated Khalapur facility for 1,000-liter IBC containers to launch in Q3 FY27.
* **Mold-Tek Packaging:** Expanding capacity toward 70,000 tonnes by early next fiscal year.
* **Pyramid Technoplast:** Investing Rs 20–25 crore for Kutch expansion (10,000 IBCs/month) by March 2027.
* **Shaily Engineering Plastics:** Expanding its pen manufacturing lines and eyeing a baseline capex of ~INR100 crores for semiconductor packaging.
* **Xpro India:** Successfully doubled its dielectric film capacity with the new Barjora line operational and advanced utility commissioning for its UAE subsidiary.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Guidance Upgrades:** Ddev Plastiks raised its annual revenue estimate from INR 3,340 crores to INR 3,800 crores due to higher average realizations. 
* **Guidance Maintained:** Most companies maintained their volume and revenue targets (e.g., All Time Plastics at 15–20% revenue growth; Jain Resource at ~30%; Mitsu Chem at Rs 1,000 cr by FY28; Pyramid at ~15%; Shaily exceeding its 36 million pen target).
* **Tone Shifts:** All Time Plastics notably shifted its tone from "Cautious" to "Negative" due to severe West Asia shocks, while Jain Resource turned more positive as its copper anode and subsidiary operations commenced.

## What to watch next quarter
* **Normalization of Logistics:** Monitoring West Asia shipping lane stability, container availability, and the moderation of export freight rates.
* **Capacity Ramp-ups:** Execution and commercial traction on newly launched or upcoming projects—such as Shaily’s second pen qualification line, Mitsu Chem’s IBC facility, Jain Resource’s copper/plastic recycling lines, and Xpro’s UAE plant.
* **Margin Recovery:** Whether companies lagging in price pass-throughs (like All Time Plastics and Atharva) successfully realize price revisions in H2.

## Company one-liners
* **All Time Plastics Ltd:** Suffered margin compression from severe RM inflation and logistics disruptions, but maintained its FY27 growth guidance.
* **Atharva Poly-Plast Ltd:** Delivered robust H1 growth post-IPO, supported by high-margin engineering capabilities and marquee global furniture clients.
* **Ddev Plastiks Industries Ltd:** Raised its annual revenue target to INR 3,800 crores on strong wire & cable demand despite freight cost pressures.
* **Jain Resource Recycling Ltd:** Posted a 58% revenue surge with lower working capital days and successful multi-metal capacity expansions.
* **Mitsu Chem Plast Ltd:** Prioritized bottom-line stability and high-margin product mixes while preparing for its Q3 IBC container launch.
* **Mold-Tek Packaging Ltd:** Achieved a 36% profit increase driven by pharma and FMCG volume growth, offsetting headwinds in lubricants.
* **Pyramid Technoplast Ltd:** Reported 48% revenue growth and strong volume recovery by pivoting to local sourcing during Middle East transit hurdles.
* **Shaily Engineering Plastics Ltd:** Crossed INR100 crores in quarterly EBITDA, propelled by a 78% jump in healthcare (GLP-1 device) revenues.
* **Xpro India Ltd:** Benefited from doubled dielectric film capacity and strong refrigerator sheet sales, driving 47% YoY revenue growth.
