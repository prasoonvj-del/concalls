# Apollo Pipes Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-03 · Plastics & Pipes*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Soft start to the year due to geopolitical supply chain disruptions, extreme PVC price volatility, and inventory write-downs.

### Key numbers
- **Sales volume:** Flat YoY
- **Consolidated EBITDA margin (normalized business):** 7%
- **Apollo standalone EBITDA margin (normalized):** 8%
- **Kisan standalone EBITDA margin (normalized):** 6%
- **Inventory days:** 80 days
- **Debtor days:** 30 days
- **Net working capital days:** 45 days (target to reduce by 10-15 days)
- **Capex guidance:** Rs. 200 crores total for the year (FY27 and FY28 each, approx. Rs. 100 crores per year)
- **Window profile revenue contribution target:** 7% to 8% in FY27, scaling to 10% on existing capacity
- **Long-term revenue goal (by FY31):** Rs. 5,000 crores

### Highlights
- Q1 FY27 demand suffered due to a steep 30% drop in PVC prices in the first 20 days of April.
- Margins were impacted by inventory write-downs, aggressive pricing, and fixed expenses for new business verticals (Varanasi plant and Window profile business).
- CPVC portfolio grew YoY in Q1 despite flattish overall company growth; co-branding with Lubrizol is showing results.
- Scheme for merging Kisan with Apollo Pipes has been filed.
- Varanasi plant is ramping up; targeting 30% capacity utilization in FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 FY27 to be much better than H1 once the monsoon impact wanes and construction activity picks up.
- Confident of high double-digit volume growth for FY27 and coming years.
- Q2 FY27 volume growth expected to be double-digit YoY and higher QoQ.
- Company expects 7% to 8% EBITDA margin over the next 12 to 15 months, with potential to inch up by 50-100 bps as plants stabilize and operating leverage kicks in.
- Long-term target by FY31: four large plants across India, each contributing Rs. 800 crores to Rs. 1,000 crores revenue with 10%+ EBITDA margin from PVC pipes, plus Rs. 1,000 crores from other new products, totaling Rs. 5,000 crores.

### Demand & industry commentary
- April was severely impacted by a ~30% decline in PVC prices, hurting both real estate and agricultural demand.
- May and June witnessed recovery and good sales momentum at primary and secondary levels; July is doing decently well.
- Government infrastructure business (O-PVC or HDPE) remains a major drag with almost zero activity so far in the new financial year, though management hopes for a pickup from the 'Nal Se Jal' program in the next 3-4 months.
- Smaller industry players suffered significant disruptions and inventory losses due to PVC price volatility, enabling Apollo Pipes to maintain flat volumes and gain market share.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- PVC resin prices fell by Rs. 32 per Kg in April, dropped further by Rs. 5 per Kg in June, but stabilized recently following the imposition of Minimum Import Price (MIP) at $766 per MT (~Rs. 82 per Kg).
- Additional upfront costs from the Varanasi plant and direct D2C salary costs for the Window profile business impacted Q1 margins by ~0.5% at the Apollo standalone level.
- Inventory write-downs from P&L further dented reported EBITDA.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- FY27 and FY28 capex pegged at ~Rs. 200 crores total (~Rs. 100 crores annually), funded entirely through internal cash flows.
- Varanasi plant total capacity potential is ~Rs. 300 crores revenue; targeting 30% utilization in FY27, 50-60-70% in FY28, and balance in FY29.
- Land parcels identified for a future South India plant; land acquisition may be completed in FY27, with plant setup starting from Q2 of next year.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- The company is almost net cash and intends to fund all ongoing capex through internal accruals without raising fresh debt or equity.
- Working capital management: Inventory stands at 80 days, debtors at 30 days, and net working capital days at 45 days. Management aims to lower net working capital days by 10-15 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical disruptions in global supply chains.
- Extreme volatility in PVC resin prices causing inventory write-downs and cautious restocking by channel partners.
- Heavy rains at ports causing temporary de-stuffing and delivery delays for imported resin.
- Sluggish government infrastructure disbursements impacting O-PVC and HDPE demand.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sneha (Nuvama) on MIP impact and ground prices:** Management explained that MIP is set at $766/MT (~Rs. 82/Kg), stabilizing market prices near Reliance levels. Prices cannot fall below the MIP floor.
2. **Ameya (Value Equity) on market share gains:** Management confirmed that smaller players suffered due to inventory volatility, allowing Apollo to maintain flattish volumes in a contracting industry and gain market share.
3. **Sagar Pamnani (Bajaj Alternate) on funding for future capex:** Management stated the company is net cash, and the upcoming Rs. 600-700 crores capex over 5 years will be 70-80% funded via internal cash flows, with no major debt/equity dilution expected.
4. **Aasim Bharde (DAM Capital) on inventory losses and margins:** Management clarified that the majority of consolidated EBITDA in Q1 was impacted by inventory losses, but normalized business margins were 7%.
5. **Karan (AM Tech) on Kisan operating losses:** Management noted that at the business EBITDA level, Kisan is making 5% to 6%, but reported P&L is impacted by optical inventory losses and weak macro support. Volume recovery is expected from Q2/Q3.

### One-line takeaway
Apollo Pipes reported a soft, flat-volume Q1 FY27 marred by severe PVC price volatility and inventory write-downs, but management remains confident of high double-digit volume growth and margin recovery in H2 FY27 supported by stabilizing resin prices, new plant ramps, and new product scaling.
