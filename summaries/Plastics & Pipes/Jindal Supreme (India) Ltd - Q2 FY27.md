# Jindal Supreme (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Plastics & Pipes*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Driven by strong volume growth, higher capacity utilization, improved product mix, and a successful IPO leading to a significantly deleveraged balance sheet.

- **Q2 FY27 Revenue from operations:** Rs 216.22 crore (up 36% YoY from Rs 158.84 crore in Q2 FY26)
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 16.94 crore (up 122% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** 7.8% (up from 4.8% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 PAT:** Rs 9.91 crore (up 132% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 PAT Margin:** 4.6% (up from 2.7% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 Revenue from operations:** Rs 407.16 crore (up 23% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 30.70 crore (up 64% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** 7.5%
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 18.19 crore (up 72% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 PAT Margin:** 4.5%
- **H1 FY27 Capacity Utilization:** 65.5% (vs 61.6% in FY26)
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** 0.48x in H1 FY27 (vs 1.2x in FY26 and 2.1x in FY24)
- **Cash Position:** Rs 38.47 crore as of 30 September 2026
- **Net Working Capital Days:** 64 days (vs 57 days in FY26)
- **Full Year FY27 Revenue Guidance:** Rs 850 crore to Rs 900 crore
- **Full Year FY27 EBITDA Guidance:** Rs 60-odd crore

### Highlights
- First post-IPO earnings call following a fresh issue raising Rs 99.89 crore.
- Utilized Rs 71 crore of IPO proceeds towards debt repayment.
- Share of value-added products increased from 49% to 57% YoY.
- EBITDA per ton improved significantly from Rs 2,100 to Rs 5,800 in H1 FY27.
- Commercial operations achieved for a new metal beam crash barrier line (expanding capacity from 24,000 to 48,000 tonnes) and a new galvanizing line adding 18,000 tonnes (total galvanizing capacity 63,000 tonnes).
- *Record Check:* While the transcript mentions Q2 revenue at Rs 216.22 crore, OUR RECORD listed Rs 2,163 cr (which appears to be a typo in the historical record).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects capacity utilization to reach 70% in Q3 FY27, 75% in Q4 FY27, and ~80% in Q1 FY28.
- Full year FY27 revenue guided at Rs 850 crore to Rs 900 crore; EBITDA guided at Rs 60-odd crore.
- Target to increase value-added product mix to 70%-75%.
- Target to bring net working capital days back down to 45-50 days in H2.
- Long-term sustainable EBITDA margin targeted at double digits once capacity utilization reaches 80%.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Management remains very optimistic, backed by government schemes such as Jal Jeevan Mission, Amrut scheme, Smart Cities project, dedicated freight corridors, and bullet train projects.
- Indian pipes and tube industry expected to grow at 7% to 8% CAGR to about $24 billion by 2036.
- Demand split: ~20% through 53 dealers, ~30% via EPC companies, ~30% via crash barrier direct contractors, and the balance through direct institutional/one-time buyers.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Company operates as a re-roller; steel price increases/decreases are passed on with about a 2-week lag.
- EBITDA margin expansion driven by higher value-added product share (57.5%), better capacity utilization (65.46%), and fixed cost absorption on higher volumes.
- Crash barriers sold on 100% advance payment; poles to EPC companies backed by Letters of Credit.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total installed capacity currently stands at 171,000 tonnes per annum across Hisar, Haryana.
- Upcoming brownfield expansions in Hisar: 5th galvanizing line of 24,000 tonnes per annum (operational by Q2 FY28) and nut-and-bolt manufacturing (operational by Q4 FY27). Total capacity expected to reach ~200,000 tonnes by end of FY27.
- Greenfield expansion planned in Raipur, Chhattisgarh: acquiring ~75-80 acres of land (due diligence underway, expected to close in Q4). Phase 1 capex of ~Rs 150 crore for 150,000 tonnes capacity, targeting production by March 2028. Full site potential estimated at ~Rs 600 crore in a phased manner.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Deleveraged balance sheet with debt-to-equity dropping to 0.48x.
- Short-term borrowings increased slightly due to higher inventory stocking up (inventories grew from Rs 115 crore to Rs 100 crore [as stated]).
- Operating cash flow was negative (Rs 6 to Rs 7 crore) in H1 due to built-up inventory and increased receivables (Rs 42 crore vs Rs 33 crore last year).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- None explicitly flagged as material risks by management, other than normal industry-wide monsoon and festival seasonality affecting Q2.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Arvind Jain (Motilal Oswal) on GI tubular poles opportunity:** Management stated that poles are targeted for hilly areas (for streetlights and telecom towers) and expect 80%-85% utilization in this category over the next 3 years.
2. **Abhijeet Porwal on negative operating cash flow despite higher PAT:** Management explained it was due to inventory accumulation and higher receivables, which will normalize in H2. *(Management did not dodge).*
3. **Raj Shah on reconciliation of debt reduction vs IPO proceeds:** Management clarified that long-term debt was repaid using Rs 71 crore of IPO proceeds, while short-term borrowings rose temporarily to fund higher inventory. *(Management did not dodge).*
4. **Anubhav Podar on greenfield expansion funding and timeline:** Management stated Phase 1 capex of ~Rs 150 crore in Raipur will be funded via internal accruals and some debt, targeting production by March 2028. *(Management did not dodge).*
5. **Nirmal Jain on long-term sustainable EBITDA margin:** Management stated current 7%-8% is the bottom-case scenario, and double-digit margins are targeted once utilization hits 80%. *(Management did not dodge).*

### One-line takeaway
Powered by a successful IPO that drastically reduced debt, Jindal Supreme delivered its strongest H1 performance yet and is aggressively expanding via brownfield and greenfield projects to capture robust infrastructure demand.
