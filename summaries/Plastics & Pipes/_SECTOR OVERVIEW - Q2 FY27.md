# Plastics & Pipes: Sector Overview (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The sector has largely moved past the volatility of Q1 FY27. Managements across the board are reporting a recovery in volumes and a stabilization of polymer prices, aided by government measures like the Minimum Import Price (MIP) on PVC resin. While the overall tone is positive, there is a clear distinction between companies benefiting from value-added product mixes (Shaily, Mold-Tek) and those heavily reliant on commodity piping, where margin management remains a delicate balance of passing on raw material costs versus maintaining market share. Geopolitical tensions in West Asia remain the primary macro overhang, impacting freight costs and supply chain predictability.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** There is broad agreement that underlying demand in housing and infrastructure remains healthy. Several companies—**Prince Pipes**, **Finolex Industries**, and **Supreme Industries**—reported that October volumes tracked strongly, suggesting a positive start to H2.
*   **Divergence on Government Projects:** A notable disagreement exists regarding the "Nal Se Jal" scheme. **Supreme Industries** is explicitly not optimistic about contributions this year, citing that state governments are failing to match central funds, leading to payment delays and contractor inactivity. Conversely, **Finolex Industries** views the fund releases as a potential indirect boost to demand.
*   **Segment-Specific Weakness:**
    *   **Lubricants:** Both **Mold-Tek Packaging** and **Supreme Industries** flagged the lubricant segment as a drag, with Mold-Tek noting a 12.3% decline due to input issues at major private lube clients.
    *   **Consumer/Industrial:** **Shaily Engineering** noted subdued consumer demand in Europe and the U.S., while **Supreme Industries** reported a 14% volume decline in its consumer products segment.
    *   **Infrastructure:** **Apollo Pipes** noted that the government infrastructure segment (HDPE/O-PVC) remains muted due to a lack of activity.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Price Pass-Through:** Most companies are successfully passing on raw material price hikes, though with a lag. **Supreme Industries** and **Finolex Industries** noted a 10–15 day lag in price adjustments to assist distributor balance sheets. **Finolex** specifically attributed Q2 margin pressure to a time-lag effect where higher-cost inventory procured in Q1 was consumed in Q2.
*   **Margin Drivers:** Companies with high-value product mixes are outperforming. **Shaily Engineering** achieved its highest-ever quarterly EBITDA (INR 102 crores) with a 31.9% margin. **Mold-Tek** saw EBITDA per kg rise to INR 46.5.
*   **Geopolitical Impact:** Almost all players (including **Xpro India**, **Shaily**, and **Finolex**) cited West Asian geopolitical tensions as a source of volatility for freight and polymer costs.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Expansion:** The sector remains in a growth phase.
    *   **Apollo Pipes:** Targeting 2,88,000 tonnes capacity in 2 years (from 2,40,000); planning a South India greenfield plant.
    *   **Supreme Industries:** Progressing on new units in Bihar, Jammu, Erode, Puducherry, and Malanpur.
    *   **Xpro India:** New Barjora line is operational; UAE subsidiary commercial operations expected later in FY27.
    *   **Shaily Engineering:** Abu Dhabi facility on track for FY28; second pen-injector line starting qualification.
    *   **Prince Pipes:** Expanding DECILO polypropylene manufacturing to Silvassa by Q4.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance Shifts:** **Apollo Pipes** lowered its long-term revenue goal to Rs. 4,000 crores by 2030 (from Rs. 5,000 crores by FY31).
*   **Tone Shifts:** **Apollo Pipes** and **Finolex Industries** shifted from "cautious" to "positive" regarding H2 prospects.
*   **Strategic Moves:** **Apollo Pipes** entered the ceramic tiles business via a 76% stake in Mazzini Tiles. **Mold-Tek** consolidated Hyderabad units to improve efficiency.

## Company one-liners
*   **Apollo Pipes:** Reported 13% volume growth and entered the ceramic tiles market to diversify.
*   **Atharva Poly-Plast:** Delivered 77.5% YoY revenue growth post-IPO, driven by high-value engineering solutions.
*   **Finolex Industries:** Achieved 28% volume recovery despite margin pressure from raw material cost lags.
*   **Mitsu Chem Plast:** Focused on high-margin product mix, resulting in a 102% YoY EBITDA jump.
*   **Mold-Tek Packaging:** Profits jumped 36% YoY, driven by strong pharma and food/FMCG demand.
*   **Prince Pipes:** EBITDA grew 82% YoY, supported by value-added product expansion and distribution gains.
*   **Shaily Engineering:** Crossed the INR 100 crore EBITDA milestone for the first time, led by healthcare.
*   **Supreme Industries:** Piping volumes grew 24% in Q2, with a strong 23% start to October.
*   **Xpro India:** Revenue grew 47% YoY as new dielectric film capacity ramped up.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **Geopolitical Fallout:** Whether West Asian tensions further disrupt freight and polymer supply chains.
*   **Government Spending:** Any signs of improvement in "Nal Se Jal" funding and execution, which would impact piping demand.
*   **Turnaround Progress:** The breakeven timelines for **Apollo Pipes'** Kisan unit and **Prince Pipes'** Bathware (Aquel) business.
*   **Capacity Ramp-ups:** The utilization levels of **Xpro India's** new Barjora line and **Shaily Engineering's** new pen-injector line.

*Based on: Apollo Pipes Ltd, Atharva Poly-Plast Ltd, Finolex Industries Ltd, Mitsu Chem Plast Ltd, Mold-Tek Packaging Ltd, Prince Pipes and Fittings Ltd, Shaily Engineering Plastics Ltd, Supreme Industries Ltd, Xpro India Ltd*
