# Plastics & Pipes: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The sector delivered a mixed quarter: volume growth was broad-based and double-digit for most organized players, but margins diverged sharply. Companies with strong value-added mixes (Prince, Supreme, Shaily, Mold-Tek) expanded EBITDA margins, while those exposed to commodity PVC, export freight spikes or raw-material lags (All Time, Ddev, Finolex, Supreme’s packaging) saw compression. Managements uniformly cited West Asia geopolitics as a driver of polymer inflation and shipping disruption, but the organized segment continues to gain share from unorganized peers as raw-material volatility squeezes smaller players.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Indian plastics & pipes has historically been a volume-led, distribution-heavy commodity business (PVC piping, basic packaging). Over the last ~5 years, the model has shifted toward value-added products (CPVC, PPR, silent pipes, fittings), backward integration into resin/compounding, organized players consolidating share, and diversification into adjacent building materials (bathware, ceramics) and high-margin niches (healthcare devices, dielectric films, metal recycling). **Government push**
- **MIP on suspension-grade PVC resin ($767/t CIF) & removal of customs duty exemptions** – supports domestic resin prices, benefits integrated pipe makers. [Govt: DGTR/Finance Ministry, Nov 2026] → Supreme, Finolex, Prince, Apollo.
- **Nal Se Jal / Jal Jeevan Mission / AMRUT / Smart City water initiatives** – structural demand for piping, but state funding delays persist. [Govt: Central/State, ongoing] → Supreme, Finolex, Prince, Shree TNB, Apollo.
- **EPR mandates (30% recycled content in drums, rising to 60%)** – drives recycling capex and cost savings. [Govt: MoEFCC, ongoing] → Shree TNB, Pyramid, Jain Resource Recycling.
- **Karnataka AAR: Paper bags (HSN 48194000) attract 5% GST** – positive for paper packaging segment. [Govt: Karnataka AAR, Nov 2026] → Mold-Tek, Xpro (paper packaging exposure).
- **Gujarat AAR: Biodegradable bags attract 5% GST subject to biodegradability claim** – positive for biodegradable packaging. [Govt: Gujarat AAR, Nov 2026] → Mold-Tek, Xpro.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
**Consensus**: Domestic demand healthy across housing, infrastructure, power T&D, data centers, and consumer durables. Organized players gaining share as unorganized peers struggle with RM volatility. Volume growth targets 10-25% YoY for FY27 across the board.
**Divergence**: 
- **All Time Plastics** sees export headwinds on specific articles due to high price points and China’s $100/t freight advantage.
- **Apollo Pipes** notes government infrastructure (HDPE/O-PVC) remains muted.
- **Finolex** flags potential El Niño impact on agri demand in some states, though tractor/2-wheeler bookings positive.
- **Supreme Industries** reports Nal Se Jal business subdued due to state funding gaps and unpaid contractor bills.
- **Mold-Tek** highlights lubricant segment drag from private lube majors’ input issues.
- **Shaily Engineering** notes consumer demand in Europe/US subdued due to geopolitics.
- **Ddev Plastiks** cites Middle East freight spike (USD 300-350/t) hurting export margins.
- **Prince Pipes** sees competitive intensity as “healthy and non-predatory” due to demand tailwinds.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Raw material inflation**: All Time 40-45% polymer price rise; Ddev export freight USD 10-20 → 300-350/t; Mold-Tek RM prices up 60% in 6 months (passed through); Supreme crude-linked polythene costs high; Finolex EDC $450-500, VCM $900-950, Ethylene $1,150-1,200; Shaily polymer inflation lag 2-4 months; Xpro PP resin/shipping volatility; Atharva 22% polymer price rise from 20% crude increase.
- **Margin dynamics**: Prince EBITDA margin +300bps YoY to 12%; Supreme targeting 14-14.5% operating margin; Shaily EBITDA margin 31.9% (+10bps); Mold-Tek EBITDA/kg INR 46.5 (from ~39); Pyramid EBITDA/ton Rs 13,718 (+33%); Apollo standalone EBITDA/tonne targeting Rs 8,000-10,000 in H2; Finolex guiding ~Rs 15/kg EBITDA; All Time expects H2 recovery as price revisions take effect; Ddev squeezed to 9% EBITDA margin, guiding sustainable Rs 14,000-17,000/ton; BLS stable at 13-13.5% EBITDA margin, targeting +100-150bps; Mitsu double-digit EBITDA margins sustainable; Atharva engineering services 50-65% EBITDA margin; Jain EBITDA margin moderating to 4.1% H1 due to mix; Xpro cost pass-through incomplete.

## Company one-liners
- **All Time Plastics Ltd**: Despite a challenging quarter marked by 40-45% raw material inflation and logistics-driven margin compression, the company maintained its FY27 growth guidance, citing strong order books and expected margin recovery in H2.
- **Apollo Pipes Ltd**: Apollo Pipes reported a recovery in Q2 FY27 with 13% YoY volume growth and returning profitability, while strategically expanding into the ceramic tiles segment and outlining a Rs. 4,000 crore revenue vision for 2030.
- **Atharva Poly-Plast Ltd**: Atharva Polyplast delivered robust H1 FY27 revenue growth of 77.5% YoY and strengthened its balance sheet post-IPO, driven by high-value engineering capabilities and marquee global clientele in premium furniture and appliances.
- **BLS Polymers Ltd**: BLS Polymers delivered strong H1 FY27 growth (revenue +36%, PAT +51%), backed by rising manufacturing scale, stabilizing margins around 13-13.5%, and ongoing capacity expansion toward 87,301 MTPA.
- **Ddev Plastiks Industries Ltd**: Ddev Plastiks delivered robust Q2 FY27 revenue growth driven by strong wire & cable demand and export traction, leading to an upward revision in annual revenue guidance to Rs 3,800 crore despite logistics headwinds and freight inflation.
- **Finolex Industries Ltd**: Finolex Industries reported a strong 28% YoY volume recovery in Q2 FY27 driven by channel restocking and stabilized PVC pricing, though margins faced a time-lagged raw material cost squeeze.
- **Jain Resource Recycling Ltd**: Jain Resource Recycling delivered a strong Q2 FY27 with a 58% YoY revenue jump and improved working capital efficiency, supported by commercial traction in copper anode and multi-metal capacity expansions.
- **Mitsu Chem Plast Ltd**: Mitsu Chem Plast delivered strong profitability growth and stable margins in Q2 FY27 by focusing on high-margin product mixes, while preparing for commercialization of its 1,000-liter IBC project in Q3 to support its long-term growth targets.
- **Mold-Tek Packaging Ltd**: Mold-Tek delivered robust Q2 FY27 performance with a 36% jump in profits and higher EBITDA per kg (INR46.5), driven by strong growth in pharma and food/FMCG segments offsetting declines in lubricants.
- **Prince Pipes and Fittings Ltd**: Prince Pipes delivered robust Q2 FY27 volume growth of 22% YoY and EBITDA growth of 82% YoY, underpinned by value-added product mix expansion, strict working capital discipline, and favorable industry consolidation.
- **Pyramid Technoplast Ltd**: Pyramid Technoplast reported strong Q2 FY27 performance with 48% YoY revenue growth and 21% volume growth, driven by solid volume recovery, rising realizations, and expanding green energy and recycling benefits.
- **Shaily Engineering Plastics Ltd**: Shaily Engineering Plastics delivered its highest-ever quarterly revenue of INR320 crores and crossed INR100 crores in EBITDA, driven by a 78% YoY surge in its Healthcare segment, which now accounts for 55% of total revenue.
- **Shree TNB Polymers Ltd**: Shree TNB Polymers reported a modest H1 FY27 (PAT of Rs 2.58 crore) but anticipates a robust H2 backed by a Rs 64.23 crore order book and ongoing IPO-funded expansions adding 10,000 MTPA capacity by early FY28.
- **Supreme Industries Ltd**: Supreme Industries recovered strongly in Q2 with 24% piping volume growth and maintained a robust full-year margin target of 14%–14.5% despite macro headwinds and elevated raw material costs.
- **Xpro India Ltd**: Xpro India delivered strong YoY revenue (47.4%) and PAT (62%) growth in Q2 FY27, driven by market share gains in refrigerator sheets and strong momentum in newly doubled dielectric film capacities despite geopolitical and shipping headwinds.
