# Takyon Networks Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-25 · IT & Software*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Negative - Exceptional losses driven by surging IT hardware component costs against fixed-price government contracts, resulting in severe margin erosion.

### Key numbers
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs. 30.61 crore
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Negative Rs. 7.95 crore
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** Negative 25.95% (~26%)
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Negative Rs. 8.45 crore
- **H1 FY27 PAT Margin:** Negative 27.35%
- **H1 FY27 EPS:** Negative Rs. 5.90
- **Q1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs. 9.96 crore (Loss: Rs. 7.20 crore)
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue:** Rs. 20.65 crore (Loss: Rs. 1.25 crore)
- **Undisputed Order Book (as of Nov 15, 2026):** Rs. 46.65 crore (Expected ~Rs. 36 crore to be executed in H2)
- **New Orders (April–September):** ~Rs. 22 crore
- **Fresh Orders (Last 45 days post-September):** Rs. 35+ crore (including Rs. 10 crore from railways, Rs. 20 crore from energy exchange platform)
- **Receivable Days (as of Sept 30, 2026):** 178 days (improved from 228 days as of March 2026)

### Highlights
- H1 performance severely impacted by slow execution pace and soaring IT hardware component prices (memory, storage, network equipment) with longer OEM lead times.
- Q1 suffered heavy operational losses (gross margins hit by service/cost increases); Q2 losses narrowed significantly to Rs. 1.25 crore as revenue doubled to Rs. 20.65 crore.
- Management admitted to uploading incorrect initial financial figures for H1FY27 and H1FY26, which was subsequently corrected by CFO Vivek Singh.
- Company is actively expanding into enterprise technology (Delhi/NCR), cybersecurity, and power sector infrastructure to reduce reliance on government/PSU clients.
- Targeted near-term goal in H2 is to grow enterprise customer sales contribution to close to double digits.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects business momentum to be meaningfully stronger in H2, consistent with seasonal patterns, and anticipates an "exceptionally better H2 than H1."
- Guidance to achieve a targeted operating margin of 15–20% on new sales over the next 6 months.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains strong in government, railways, power, and cybersecurity digital infrastructure.
- Component price inflation and lead times escalated rapidly (with certain costs rising up to 300% during H1), posing industry-wide challenges for system integrators. OEM price validity has now shortened to 15–30 days.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cost of goods sold (COGS) reached Rs. 31.50 crore, exceeding operating revenue in H1 due to fixed-price government contracts lacking escalation clauses and surging component costs.
- Fixed costs remained disciplined and flat compared to the previous half.
- 80% of margin erosion attributed to project-level cost overruns, and 20% to delays in execution.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Expanding geographic presence into the Northeast and targeting BFSI sector bids in the West.
- Partnering with strategic OEMs (HPE Aruba, Juniper, Ruckus, HP, Dell, Palo Alto, Fortinet, Ivanti).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Receivable days reduced to 178 days as of September 30, 2026, down from 228 days in March 2026, driven by structured age-wise reviews and dedicated follow-ups.
- Other income in H2 FY26 included a one-time gain from the sale of a commercial vacant property, which explains the lower other income base in H1 FY27.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High client concentration in state government and PSU fixed-price contracts lacking price-escalation clauses.
- Extreme vulnerability to rapid IT hardware component price volatility and longer OEM lead times without the ability to pass costs onto government clients without risking blacklisting.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Amit Jain (Retail Investor) questioned why management failed to forecast price hikes and stock planning, resulting in a 70% loss relative to Q1 revenue.** 
   * *Management Answer:* Explained that government contracts operate on long sales cycles with fixed prices and no escalation clauses. Because competitive bidding requires confirmed orders before procurement, they could not stockpile beforehand and had to absorb component cost spikes of up to 3x to avoid blacklisting. OEM price validities shrunk significantly.
2. **Amit Jain asked why other income dropped to one-fifth of the previous half.** 
   * *Management Answer:* Clarified that other income in March 2026 included a one-time gain from selling a commercial vacant property.
3. **Manish Pasad (Investor) questioned how a trading and service business could incur an unimaginable loss of Rs. 8.5 crore without markup safeguards.** 
   * *Management Answer:* Reiterated that COGS (Rs. 31.50 crore) exceeded revenue due to unexpected component price surges on old fixed-price government contracts; however, Q1 was the outlier, and Q2 performance stabilized significantly.
4. **Manish Pasad pointed out that H1FY27 and H1FY26 figures appeared identical upon initial publishing.** 
   * *Management Answer:* CFO Vivek Singh took complete personal responsibility for the data entry mistake and confirmed the correct results were subsequently uploaded.
5. **Investor asked about specific steps to improve gross margins and control operating costs.** 
   * *Management Answer:* Stated they are focusing on selective bidding, avoiding price gaps by partnering closely with OEMs on shorter price validities, and targeting enterprise sales with a 15-20% margin.

### One-line takeaway
Takyon Networks reported a disastrous H1 FY27 with negative EBITDA and PAT margins due to unhedged component price inflation on fixed-price government contracts, but management expects a sharp operational recovery in H2 backed by a Rs. 46.65 crore order book and enterprise client diversification.
