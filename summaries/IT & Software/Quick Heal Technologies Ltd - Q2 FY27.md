# Quick Heal Technologies Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · IT & Software*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong sequential recovery in revenue and profitability, but continued year-on-year revenue decline and structural headwinds in the consumer segment.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 65 crore (up 44% QoQ, down 22% YoY)
- **Enterprise Revenue:** Rs 35 crore (up 18% QoQ, down 4% YoY)
- **Consumer Revenue:** Rs 36 crore (up 86% QoQ, down 40% YoY)
- **Total Expenditure (excl. depreciation):** Flat at Rs 63 crore
- **EBITDA:** Positive Rs 2.3 crore (compared to loss of Rs 17.6 crore in Q1)
- **PAT:** Rs 5.8 crore (compared to loss of Rs 5.3 crore in Q1)
- **PAT Margin:** 9%
- **Total Receivables:** Rs 98 crore (down from Rs 107 crore in Q1, down from Rs 176 crore in Q2 FY26)
- **Deferred Revenue:** Rs 48 crore (compared to Rs 52 crore in Q1)
- **Enterprise Order Book:** Maintained above Rs 57 crore
- **Employee Cost:** Dipped by Rs 17 crore YoY

### Highlights
- Secured strategic wins in H1 FY27 across defence, BFSI, and a large IT services company for new-generation products; government business momentum continued.
- Transitioning portfolio toward data privacy, exposure management, and ransomware resilience in the enterprise segment.
- Tightened credit and commercial controls in the consumer business to improve receivables quality and channel discipline, impacting near-term volumes.
- Launched data privacy offerings (aligned with DPDP law) last year, seeing good traction and expected by management to match endpoint security business soon.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management does not disclose specific revenue or margin guidance.
- Management anticipates enterprise revenue contribution to grow beyond 80% over the next three years.
- DPDP compliance solutions viewed as a significant medium to long-term growth driver, expected to match the endpoint security business soon.
- International expansion to remain selective across existing 70+ countries (with focus on markets like Italy, Philippines, and South America).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Attack surfaces are expanding, driven by digitization and AI adoption, creating greater awareness and demand among mid-market and small enterprises.
- Consumer antivirus demand globally and domestically continues to decline due to operating system default security policies (e.g., Microsoft).
- Small, medium, and micro businesses in India have historically spent less on cybersecurity, but spending and awareness are increasing due to sophisticated threats and data protection regulations (DPDP law).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Total expenditure (excl. depreciation) remained flat at Rs 63 crore.
- Continuous cost optimization yielded a YoY reduction in employee costs (dipped by Rs 17 crore YoY).
- Cost run rate expected to remain in a similar range while selective headcount and R&D/sales investments continue.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Debt-free balance sheet provides flexibility to continue investing in R&D and market expansion.
- International presence spans over 70+ countries; focusing on key established geographies like Italy, Philippines, and South America.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Balance sheet remains strong and debt-free.
- Total receivables reduced significantly to Rs 98 crore (down from Rs 176 crore a year ago) due to tightened credit controls.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Dominance and default security policies of operating system providers (specifically Microsoft) pose a persistent challenge to the B2C consumer antivirus business globally.
- Lower historical cybersecurity spending habits and budget allocation among small and medium businesses in India.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sheetal Shah (Individual Investor) asked about 10-year financial stagnation, and long-term revenue/margin aspirations.** 
   - *Management Answer:* Explained the structural shift from B2C consumer (impacted by OS providers) to B2B enterprise over the past decade. Enterprise contribution is now ~50% and expected to exceed 80% in 3 years. Declined to give specific revenue/margin guidance. (Did not dodge, provided strategic context).
2. **Sheetal Shah asked about the biggest challenges in consumer and enterprise segments.** 
   - *Management Answer:* Consumer challenge is Microsoft's default free/expired antivirus handling. Enterprise challenge is market fragmentation and lower spending/awareness among small/micro businesses in India. (Direct answer).
3. **Sheetal Shah asked about the revenue potential of the DPDP (Data Protection) product vertical.** 
   - *Management Answer (Harish Kumar):* Declined to give projected revenue numbers for individual products, but stated it is a significant medium-to-long-term growth driver expected to match the endpoint security business soon. (Did not dodge, gave directional comparison).
4. **Shubham Sahani (RSPN Ventures) asked if AI tailwinds are driving demand on the consumer side.** 
   - *Management Answer:* Stated they do not see demand changes on the consumer side from AI, but AI-based attacks are driving awareness and security investments in mid-market and large enterprises. (Direct answer).
5. **Shubham Sahani asked for the revenue split between domestic and international clients.** 
   - *Management Answer:* Stated the company does not currently report domestic and international splits separately. (Directly answered by withholding current reporting metric).

### One-line takeaway
Quick Heal is successfully driving operational turnaround and margin recovery through strict cost and credit controls, while aggressively pivoting its product portfolio toward enterprise cybersecurity and data privacy (DPDP) to offset structural declines in the consumer segment.
