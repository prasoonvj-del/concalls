# eClerx Services Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-03 · IT & Software*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue and EBITDA grew YoY and sequentially, but deal wins (ACV) softened due to client delays and AI spending shifts, and H1 margins remained below the prior year.

### Key numbers
- Operating Revenue (USD): USD 127.7 million (up 10.6% YoY, 12.9% in constant currency, 1.5% QoQ)
- Operating Revenue (INR): INR 11,887 million (up 18% YoY)
- Operating EBITDA: INR 2,839 million
- Operating EBITDA Margin: 23.9% (up 90 bps QoQ)
- PAT: INR 1,804 million
- PAT Margin: 14.9%
- Total Revenue (including other income of INR 240 million): INR 12,126 million (up 3.6% QoQ)
- H1 USD Operating Revenue: USD 253.6 million (up 12.8% YoY)
- H1 EBITDA Margin: 23.5%
- H1 Total EBITDA (including other income) Margin: 24.8%
- New Deal Wins (ACV) for Q2: USD 31 million (vs. USD 41 million last quarter)
- New Deal Wins (ACV) for H1: USD 72 million (vs. USD 78 million in H1 last year)
- Analytics and Automation Annual Revenue Run Rate: Approx USD 109 million (~20% of revenue, up 27% YoY and 5% QoQ)
- Headcount / Seat additions: Offshore delivery headcount up 3.5% QoQ (+724 additions); India seat count up ~1,400 to 16,824 seats
- Utilization: 74.9% (vs. 75.5% in Q1)
- Attrition: 23.4% (marginally up due to Q1 wage increments)
- Net Operating Cash Flow (Q2): INR 2,694 million
- OCF-to-EBITDA Ratio: 87.5%
- Cash and Cash Equivalents: INR 14,318 million
- EPS: INR 19.6
- Return on Equity (ROE): 24.7%
- CapEx: INR 585 million in Q2; INR 919 million total for H1
- DSO: 79 days (unchanged)
- Top 10 concentration: 60% (unchanged)
- Clients above USD 3 million: 25 (up from 20 a year ago)

### Highlights
- Marked the 13th consecutive quarter of sequential revenue expansion.
- Analytics and automation segment grew 2.7x faster than the company average. Over 280 employees earned Claude AI certifications.
- Won two awards at the NASSCOM BPM Excellence Awards 2026 (for talent and customer experience) and achieved Great Place to Work recognition in India for the second consecutive year. Also received CSO 100 and CIO 100 recognitions for agentic AI use cases.
- Emerging business segment expanded to 12.6% of revenue (up from 11.7%).
- Statutory PF wage ceiling revised by the government from INR 15,000/month to INR 25,000/month effective September 17, 2026, creating an expected 20 bps margin impact.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains confident of keeping full-year operating EBITDA margins in the **24% to 28% range**.
- CapEx spend in H2 is expected to roughly match H1 spend (~INR 90 crores each).
- Management is cautiously optimistic on a recovery in financial services (BFSI) by Q4.
- Management is still working toward matching or exceeding FY26 ACV numbers, though acknowledging it will be difficult due to delayed decision timelines.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand environment is mixed; clients remain selective with tighter budgets and longer decision cycles as spending shifts toward AI-related investments.
- **Emerging Business:** FinOps full finance cycle shows ongoing growth opportunities.
- **CMT:** Revenue flat sequentially; Cairo and Manila grew; deployed QA 360 at a large client; healthy pipeline.
- **BFSI:** Revenue up ~3% QoQ from short-term engagements. U.S. financial crime compliance (FCC) regulation easing has reduced refresh volumes at existing clients. Traction seen with new clients (e.g., AML/KYC for a payments company) and document digitization.
- **Hi-Tech & M&D:** Traction in Hi-Tech driven by agentic AI and transformation services. Fashion, luxury, and retail flat YoY, impacted by seasonal August holidays in Italy and weak demand in China and Europe.
- **Analytics & Technology:** Expanding AI teams in the EU and U.S., placing engineers closer to clients.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Delivery costs improved by 110 bps sequentially, primarily due to INR depreciation.
- G&A increased by 14 bps (due to legal and professional fees); S&D increased by 10 bps.
- D&A increased to INR 558 million (4.7% of operating revenue) due to new facilities in Mohali and Chandigarh; expected to rise by another ~20 bps in H2 as Mumbai and Pune facilities become operational.
- Subcontracting costs increased due to rapid technology business growth and evaluating contractor performance prior to permanent onboarding.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Q2 CapEx was INR 585 million (H1 total: INR 919 million).
- Operationalized new seat capacity in Mohali and Chandigarh (India seat count increased by ~1,400 to 16,824).
- Additional seats expected to go live in Mumbai and Pune in H2 (Q3 and Q4).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash and cash equivalents stand at INR 14,318 million.
- DSO steady at 79 days.
- Effective Tax Rate (ETR) modeled at ~25%.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Clients are taking longer to make decisions and initiating smaller deal values to test AI efficacy before committing to larger programs.
- Easing U.S. financial crime compliance (FCC) regulations led to lower refresh volumes in existing BFSI accounts.
- Luxury market headwinds in China and Europe impacting fashion/luxury verticals.
- Nearshore delivery centers carry lower margins than India BPO centers, creating a revenue-mix drag on margins.
- Government statutory PF wage ceiling revision impacts margins by 20 bps.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Saurabh Dhole (Motilal Oswal) on contract parameters vs. AI tool costs:** Management stated contract parameters are materially unchanged, but clients are starting with smaller deal sizes to test AI. AI tool costs are monitored closely, not unlimited, and currently form an insignificant portion of delivery costs.
2. **Dipesh Kumar Mehta (Emkay Global) on deal intake vs. headcount:** Management explained that right-shifting of deal decisions occurred, and headcount was added slightly in advance for expected work, leading to lower utilization and softer ACV in H1.
3. **Pulkit Chawla (B&K Securities) on AI deal margins and subcontracting:** Management noted AI deal margins don't immediately look better due to upfront investments ahead of the curve. Subcontracting increases are driven primarily by fast-growing technology needs and evaluation of contractors.
4. **Sandeep Shah (Equirus) on BFSI recovery timing:** Management shifted expectations for a BFSI recovery from Q3 to Q4 due to ongoing high volatility in financial services.
5. **Girish Pai (BOB Capital) on market share loss in BFSI:** Management denied losing market share, attributing softness in the FCC book to easing U.S. regulations and client AI adoption timelines rather than competitive losses.
6. **Shradha Agrawal (AMSEC) on top 10 client growth and AI pass-through:** Management noted top 10 client growth has moderated due to base effects and budget constraints, but relationships remain strong with client count above USD 3 million expanding from 20 to 25.

### One-line takeaway
eClerx posted its 13th consecutive quarter of sequential revenue growth (up 12.9% CC YoY) and improved operating EBITDA margins to 23.9%, even as client AI experimentation lengthened decision cycles and compressed new deal intake.
