# Jindal Saw Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-22 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Margins and profitability faced severe pressure due to geopolitical disruptions in MENA, softer domestic water infrastructure execution, and rising raw material/logistics costs, though management expects H2 to be better.

### Key numbers
- **Standalone Gross Revenue:** INR 3,881 crores (up 3% QoQ, up 14% YoY)
- **Standalone EBITDA:** INR 265 crores (down from INR 341 crores in Q1 FY27 and INR 335 crores in Q2 FY26)
- **Standalone PAT:** INR 71 crores (compared to INR 110 crores in Q1 FY27 and INR 80 crores in Q2 FY26; includes a INR 27 crores tax credit)
- **Standalone H1 FY27 Gross Revenue:** INR 7,637 crores (up 13% YoY from INR 6,736 crores)
- **Standalone H1 FY27 EBITDA:** INR 606 crores (down from INR 894 crores YoY)
- **Standalone H1 FY27 PAT:** INR 181 crores (down from INR 443 crores YoY)
- **Consolidated Gross Income (Q2 FY27):** INR 4,523 crores (up 0.8% QoQ, up 6% YoY)
- **Consolidated EBITDA (Q2 FY27):** INR 312 crores (down from INR 420 crores in Q1 FY27 and INR 482 crores in Q2 FY26)
- **Consolidated PAT (Q2 FY27):** INR 35 crores (compared to INR 91 crores in Q1 FY27 and INR 139 crores in Q2 FY26)
- **Consolidated H1 FY27 Gross Income:** INR 8,999 crores (up 8% YoY)
- **Consolidated H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Down 37%, with EBITDA margin contracting by 5.9 percentage points to 8%
- **Consolidated H1 FY27 PAT:** Down 77% YoY
- **Standalone Net Debt (as of Sep 30, 2026):** INR 2,245 crores (vs. INR 2,346 crores as of June 30, 2026)
- **Consolidated Net Institutional Debt (as of Sep 30, 2026):** INR 2,174 crores (vs. INR 2,473 crores as of June 30, 2026)
- **Order Book:** Confirmed order book of USD 1.135 billion (~INR 10,700 crores); plus Letter of Intents (LOIs) of USD 575 million + additional USD 50 million received post board meeting (total potential order book around INR 16,200 to INR 17,000 crores).
- **Abu Dhabi DI Plant Q2 Volume:** 29,000 metric tonnes (vs. 34,000 metric tonnes in Q1 FY27); subsidiary order book at ~USD 195 million (177,000 tonnes) ensuring 9-12 months visibility.
- **Jindal Hunting Q2 Revenue:** INR 9.70 crores; loss of INR 1.5 crores.

### Highlights
- Gross revenues improved YoY and QoQ, but operating profitability compressed significantly due to geopolitical conflicts in West Asia, delivery constraints, and rising costs.
- Normalized finance cost stood at INR 77 crores (standalone reported finance cost at INR 92 crores including INR 15 crores negative forex impact).
- Abu Dhabi seamless pipe plant (300,000 tonnes per annum capacity; ~USD 330 million investment) is progressing through initial development and construction; commercial operations targeted for FY28-29.
- Saudi Arabia SAW pipe JV (with Buhur, 51:49 structure) has secured land and established LCs for long-lead items.
- API license for seamless pipes was reinstated in June 2026.
- In the Jindal ITF vs. NTPC litigation, Delhi High Court division bench delivered a split verdict; matter referred to a third judge with next hearing in January 2027.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 to be better than H1, with improving volumes and margins from Q3/Q4 onwards as new non-MENA oil and gas export orders start executing.
- Production and dispatches for new LOIs are expected to start around February/March.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic water infrastructure segment continues to witness softness, though fund releases under Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) are happening gradually via state governments.
- MENA export demand is hampered by shipping and logistics delays due to regional conflicts, though underlying economic fundamentals remain unchanged.
- Anti-dumping duty imposed by Saudi Arabia on Indian DI pipe exporters (ranging from 16% to 30%) does not impact Jindal Saw directly as they supply Saudi Arabia via their Abu Dhabi facility, potentially creating a competitive advantage.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating margins compressed due to surging raw material costs (particularly coking coal for DI pipes), increased domestic and international logistics costs, and sub-optimal capacity utilization.
- Pellet business profitability impacted by high stripping ratio, low Fe content in iron ore, and rising diesel prices.
- Company is focusing on overhead reduction, cost control, and lowering inventory and receivables.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Abu Dhabi seamless plant construction is accelerated via existing leasehold land and infrastructure; financial closure expected in coming months.
- Saudi Arabia SAW pipe JV is progressing with land secured and long-lead item LCs established.
- Saudi DI pipe finishing project has been put on hold due to the regional scenario; market will continue to be served from Abu Dhabi.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Standalone net debt reduced to INR 2,245 crores (includes long-term NCDs of INR 522 crores subscribed by LIC, repayable in FY28, FY29, and FY30).
- Consolidated net institutional debt reduced to INR 2,174 crores.
- CARE Ratings reaffirmed CARE A1+ for short-term debt and CARE AA (Stable) for long-term debt in June 2026.
- Reduction in current assets observed in Q2; management expects further reduction in inventory and receivables to improve liquidity and lower bank borrowings.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Ongoing geopolitical tensions and escalation in Saudi Arabia/West Asia causing shipping delays, revenue recognition lag, and underutilization of dedicated export facilities.
- Delivered-basis contracts exposing the company to surging international and domestic logistics costs.
- Softness in domestic water infrastructure offtake and execution delays by EPC contractors on the ground.
- High stripping ratio and lower iron ore grade pressuring the pellet business.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shaurya Shah (Equirus Securities) asked about the firmness and execution timeline of the new LOIs and whether they replace MENA volumes.** Management stated that total LOIs stand at USD 625 million (including USD 480 million exports in CIS/South-East Asia), production is expected to start in Feb/March, and while MENA job-work capacity is currently underutilized, alternative transport solutions are being explored.
2. **Sailesh Raja asked about the trajectory for gross profit per kg and capacity utilization.** Management acknowledged margins fell due to product mix and geopolitics, targeting a return to double-digit/previous margin levels by Q3/Q4 as oil and gas export shares increase.
3. **Vipulkumar Shah questioned the lower realization per ton in export volumes.** Management explained that export volumes include a large 600,000-tonne job-work order for water pipes where raw material is supplied free of cost by the buyer, making the reported value-wise share lower (28% value vs 41% volume).
4. **Saket Kapoor inquired about headwinds in the pellet segment.** Management clarified that high stripping ratios and lower-grade ROM (run-of-mine) lower the yield from iron ore, driving up operating costs; environmental clearance (EC) applications are pending with MoEF.
5. **Parth Bhavsar asked about the ground reality of Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) fund releases.** Management responded that fund releases are happening gradually and slower than total payables, with orders now primarily being issued through state governments rather than centrally.

### One-line takeaway
Despite 14% YoY growth in standalone Q2 revenue, Jindal Saw's profitability contracted sharply due to MENA geopolitical disruptions, logistics inflation, and higher raw material costs, though management anticipates a recovery in H2 driven by new non-MENA oil and gas export orders.
