# Rathi Steel & Power Ltd-$ - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-27 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Supported by strong volume growth, improving product mix, and higher profitability despite softer steel realizations and volatile energy prices.

### Key numbers
- **Total income:** Rs 193.67 crore (up 24.6% YoY)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 7.77 crore (up 24.83% YoY)
- **PAT:** Rs 3.48 crore (up 84.5% YoY)
- **PAT Margin:** 1.8% (expansion of 58 bps YoY)
- **Total sales volumes:** 28,372 metric tonnes (up ~30% YoY from 21,864 metric tonnes)
- **TMT bar volumes:** 18,677 metric tonnes (more than doubled from ~8,200 metric tonnes in Q1 FY26)
- **Stainless steel volumes:** Soft by approximately 10% to 12% YoY (approx. 9,000 metric tonnes)
- **Rolling mill capacity utilization:** ~51%–52% achieved in FY26; management guides for in excess of 60% for FY27.
- **Capex incurred in FY27:** Rs 4 crore to Rs 5 crore (full-year expected spend of Rs 15 crore plus, excluding new projects/value-added initiatives).
- **Renewable power consumption:** In excess of 20% to 25% of total consumption.
- **Credit period offered to customers:** 30 days to 40 days maximum on average.

### Highlights
- TMT bar volumes more than doubled, strengthening the overall product mix and supporting revenue expansion.
- Successfully completed trial runs for integrating the steel melting capacity with the TMT plant (previously operational only for stainless steel).
- Maintained a brand usage fee/financial burden for the "Rathi" brand license (held by a family trust) at a negligible Rs 400 to Rs 500 per annum.
- Operating setup relies on domestic raw material procurement (>95% to 100% domestic this quarter) to protect against currency fluctuations.
- Product focus remains on margin-accretive products rather than top-line sales value or higher per-kg selling prices (e.g., 200 series stainless steel constitutes 80% to 90% of the stainless steel range).

### Guidance & outlook
- **Capacity Utilization:** Management maintains guidance to achieve rolling mill utilization in excess of 60% for FY27. Melt shop expansion/refining equipment addition will be explored once utilization exceeds 70% to 75%.
- **CAGR Growth:** Management aspires to grow at a 20% CAGR over the next 2-3 years, taking FY25 as the base year.
- **EBITDA Margins:** Management aspires to reach a 2% to 3% improvement in EBITDA margins overall over the next two years.
- **Guidance status:** Maintained (CAGR and utilization targets).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Q1 performance was supported by strong volume growth, improving product mix, and expanding market reach.
- Operating environment remains characterized by softer steel realizations, volatile energy prices, geopolitical uncertainties, and fluctuating demand.
- Stainless steel volumes were impacted by geopolitical disruptions and high ocean freight affecting export-oriented end-users, but are expected to stabilize at a "new normal."
- TMT demand in the NCR region remains robust due to active infrastructure and real estate projects; secondary steel production functions largely as a regional play due to high freight elements.
- Revenue mix for Q1: TMT bars contributed 47% to 48%, and stainless steel contributed the remaining (~52% to 53%). End-user breakdown: TMT primarily goes to real estate (~47%-48%), while wire rods (~50%-52%) cater to B2B, engineering, and household infrastructure applications.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material procurement is >95% to 100% domestic, reducing currency and transit volatility. Major raw materials like ferro alloys are hedged based on order books.
- Stainless steel yields better integration margins compared to TMT bars, where basic semis are currently bought from the market, reheated, and rolled (though integration trial runs are underway).
- Power costs are optimized via renewable open access power (exchanges/contracts), accounting for over 20%–25% of total consumption.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Ghaziabad facility has an installed steel melting capacity of ~85,000 tonnes per annum and a rolling capacity of ~200,000 tonnes per annum.
- Normal annual maintenance/upgradation capex is Rs 10 crore to Rs 12 crore; total expected capex for FY27 is pegged at Rs 15 crore plus, funded largely through internal accruals.
- Direct charging system trial runs for TMT are ongoing; full-scale stabilization is expected by Q4 (post-monsoon and construction curbs).
- Rooftop solar initiative is still being evaluated for technical feasibility/viability by the technical team and has not been finalized.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Steel is a working capital-intensive industry; current working capital requirements are managed via extended credit periods from raw material suppliers.
- Management is in talks with lenders to refinance existing debt at lower borrowing costs and raise need-based working capital limits to replace supplier credit.
- Debt levels are described as moderate.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Q2 is impacted by the rainy season, which hampers steel application usage. Q3 in the NCR region typically faces construction halts due to rising pollution and regulatory restrictions.
- Geopolitical uncertainties and high ocean freight affect export-oriented stainless steel end-users.
- Increased domestic stainless steel supply following IBC acquisitions of peer units created a demand-supply mismatch.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Keval Gala (Gala Ventures) asked about TMT/stainless steel volume momentum in Q2 and rolling mill utilization.** Management stated they hope to maintain Q1 momentum despite monsoon disruptions in Q2, noted a ~10%-12% soft patch in stainless steel due to exports, and maintained guidance for rolling mill utilization in excess of 60% for FY27.
2. **Rachi Jain asked about full-year capex plans and renewable power savings.** Management replied that normal capex is Rs 10-12 crore, with FY27 expected at Rs 15 crore plus funded via internal accruals, and renewable power consumption remains above 20%-25%.
3. **Priya Jain asked about the rationale for not expanding beyond NCR and achieving the 20% CAGR target.** Management explained that secondary scrap-based/recycling steel producers operate on a regional model due to high freight costs, and growth will be driven by ramping up rolling mill utilization (currently ~50%) and debottlenecking existing assets (Rathi 2.0).
4. **Devang Mehta asked about stainless steel product margins and top customer concentration.** Management noted stainless steel provides higher integration margins than TMT, and offered to connect the analyst with the IR team for precise customer concentration figures (did not dodge, but deferred exact data).
5. **Deepak Poddar asked about EBITDA margin levers and historical range-bound performance (4%-4.5%).** Management outlined three levers for margin expansion: 20% CAGR volume growth, refinancing working capital at lower costs, and integrating TMT 550D operations post-monsoons. They aspire to improve EBITDA margins by 2%-3% over a two-year horizon.
6. **Keval Gala asked about the timeline for the TMT direct charging system and rooftop solar plans.** Management stated that TMT direct charging trial runs are underway across sizes, with full ramp-up expected by Q4 after teething troubles and seasonal curbs pass; rooftop solar remains under technical evaluation and is not yet finalized.

### One-line takeaway
Rathi Steel & Power delivered strong Q1 FY27 results with a ~30% volume jump and an 84.5% surge in PAT, driven by surging TMT demand and ongoing operational integration, while management targets >60% capacity utilization and long-term margin expansion.
