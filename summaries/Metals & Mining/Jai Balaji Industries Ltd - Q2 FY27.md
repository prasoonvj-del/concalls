# Jai Balaji Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong YoY growth in revenue, EBITDA, and PAT driven by higher volumes, improved realizations, and operational efficiencies, despite near-term softness in DI pipes.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (capex completion targeted by end of November to January of 2026-2027; total capex of Rs 1,112 crore nearly completed).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly to note that monsoon months contributed to a temporary quarter-on-quarter dip in volumes/production, while reaffirming medium-term optimism for DI pipes as Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 is extended till December 2028.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Management added discussions on securing packing credit facilities from HDFC Bank at lower rates (7%) and exploring potential group-level investments in West Bengal.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines): Capex execution remains on track for commissioning by the end of Q3 FY27.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue growth YoY:** 22%
- **Adjusted EBITDA growth YoY:** 93%
- **Adjusted EBITDA margin:** ~9% (up from 5% in Q2 FY6 / last year)
- **PAT growth YoY:** 150% (reported as nearly tripling / 192%)
- **PAT margin:** ~5% (up from 2% in Q2 FY6 / last year)
- **H1 FY27 Revenue growth YoY:** 23%
- **H1 FY27 Adjusted EBITDA growth YoY:** ~64% (margins at 9% vs 6%)
- **H1 FY27 PAT growth YoY:** ~67% (margins at ~5%)
- **Net term debt:** Rs 145 crore at the end of H1 FY27 (vs Rs 169 crore at the end of FY26; gross debt of Rs 188 crore)
- **Capex deployed in Q2 FY27:** Rs 36 crore (total program of Rs 1,112 crore nearly completed)
- **Working capital and additional limits:** Rs 395 crore working capital limits plus Rs 137 crore facility sanctioned by HDFC Bank
- **DI pipe capacity:** >5.5 lakh tonnes per annum
- **Specialized ferroalloys capacity:** Enhanced from 1.66 lakh tonnes to 1.9 lakh tonnes per annum
- **Blast furnace capacity:** Enhanced from 6.3 lakh tonnes to 7.5 lakh tonnes per annum
- **Sinter capacity:** Enhanced from 9 lakh tonnes to around 12 lakh tonnes per annum
- **DI order book:** 80,000 tonnes to 90,000 tonnes (~50% index-based with PV clause)

### Highlights
- Profitability improved due to higher volumes across key products, improved realizations, and enhanced operational efficiencies.
- Integrated operations supported by three dedicated railway sidings, optimizing logistics and turnaround times.
- Working capital banking relationships expanded to private sector banks like HDFC Bank and Kotak Bank, lowering packing credit costs to ~7%.
- Group-level investment plans for West Bengal are on the drawing board and will be announced after a one-quarter hiatus following current capex completion.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects gross debt to decline from Rs 188 crore to Rs 140 crore by March, potentially reaching Rs 100 crore to Rs 110 crore depending on cash surplus.
- Management expects the new blast furnace module to improve existing blast furnace EBITDA by around 25%-30%.
- Management targets sustainable EBITDA margins of around 10%-10.5% with new capacities coming online.
- Management expects ferroalloy margins to be sustained at 15%-16%.
- Management anticipates DI pipe demand to pick up next financial year or just prior as government fund releases improve under Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 (extended till December 2028).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Ductile iron (DI) pipes industry experiencing near-term demand softness due to slower government spending, delayed fund releases, and project execution backlogs.
- Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 and AMRUT 2.0 provide strong structural medium-to-long-term support.
- Ferroalloy segment showing encouraging momentum supported by healthy steel demand and specialized/high-performance steel needs.
- Export presence spans over 40-45 countries, though global freight increases have temporarily impacted DI pipe exports.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Thermal coal and coking coal prices have increased, with negative impacts visible in Q3 and Q4.
- Commodity price increases are passed on rapidly (15-20 days on spot), while value-added products like ferroalloys and DI pipes take 2 to 3 months to pass on price hikes.
- Packing credit cost reduced from 12% to 7% via new HDFC Bank facilities.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total overall investment program of Rs 1,112 crore is nearly completed, funded predominantly through internal accruals.
- Rs 36 crore deployed in Q2 FY27; completion targeted by end of November to January of 2026-2027.
- Enhanced blast furnace, sinter, and specialized ferroalloy capacities progressing towards commissioning by end of Q3 FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net term debt stood at Rs 145 crore at the end of H1 FY27 (vs Rs 169 crore at the end of FY26).
- Gross debt stood at Rs 188 crore in Q2 FY27.
- Secured working capital limits of Rs 395 crore and an additional Rs 137 crore facility from HDFC Bank.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Subdued government fund releases and slow spending delaying DI pipe execution and contractor willingness to deploy fresh funds.
- Geopolitical uncertainties and high freight rates constraining DI pipe exports.
- Potential cost pressures from rising coking coal and thermal coal prices in H2.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q: What is the expected timeline for optimum capacity utilization and incremental revenue/EBITDA from the Rs 1,200 crore capex?** A: Management stated blast furnace enhancement should improve EBITDA by 25%-30%, and ferroalloy expansion will further integrate operations and add upside, but refrained from giving exact forward-looking numbers.
- **Q: Can you elaborate on the proposed West Bengal investments?** A: Management noted plans are at the drawing board stage involving group engineering companies and will be announced after a one-quarter hiatus following current capex completion.
- **Q: Why did trade payables/liabilities result in a cash outflow of ~Rs 290 crore in H1 FY27?** A: Management cited debt payments of Rs 60-70 crore and an increase in inventory levels by approximately Rs 200 crore.
- **Q: How will the new HDFC Bank facilities impact borrowing costs?** A: Management explained that availing packing credit from HDFC reduces borrowing costs from 12% to 7%, and utilization levels will optimize.
- **Q: What capacity utilization makes DI pipe expansion economically attractive, and what is current utilization?** A: Management stated current utilization is around 30%-40% (diverting unsold pipes to pig iron), while 60%-70% utilization makes it economically very attractive.

### One-line takeaway
Jai Balaji delivered a strong Q2 FY27 with a 93% surge in EBITDA and a 150% jump in PAT, driven by operational efficiencies and specialized ferroalloys, while awaiting a government-led demand revival in DI pipes to utilize its newly expanded capacities.
