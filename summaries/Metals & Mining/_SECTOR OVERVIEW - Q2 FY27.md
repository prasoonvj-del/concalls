# Metals & Mining: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the sector report a **divergent quarter**: domestic volume growth remains robust (8–10% YoY steel consumption, double-digit stainless/aluminium demand), but margins are split. Companies with strong value-added mixes and backward integration (APL Apollo, Sambhv Steel, Hindalco, Tata Steel India, Jindal Steel) expanded EBITDA/tonne, while those exposed to export logistics, commodity pipes, or European regulatory overhang (Jindal Saw, Ratnamani, Scoda Tubes, Godawari Power, Maan Aluminium) saw compression. Capex intensity is at a multi-year high — **>Rs 1.5 lakh crore** of announced projects across the 46 companies — funded largely by internal accruals. Geopolitical freight spikes (Middle East) and EU anti-dumping duties on Indian cold-rolled steel are the most cited external headwinds.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** – Indian metals & mining has historically made money through integrated asset ownership, volume-led scale in commodity grades, and captive raw material (iron ore, coal). Over the last ~5 years the model has shifted toward **value-added product mixes, downstream fabrication, recycling/circularity, and green energy integration** to de-risk from cyclical spreads and meet ESG/customer demands [Background].

**Shifts underway**
- **Value-added product mix upgrade** – *mainstream* – APL Apollo (branded/roofing 92–93% of volume, EBITDA ₹6,000/t), Sambhv Steel (VAP 27% YoY growth, EBITDA ₹8,000–8,500/t guidance), Venus Pipes (spooling/fittings 5.2% of revenue, targeting ₹70 cr H2 from data centres), Jindal Stainless (EBITDA/t guidance raised to ₹20–22k), Goodluck India (VAP 78% of EBITDA), Hi-Tech Pipes (VAP share 40%→55% target), Ratnamani (RFSS 54.7% EBITDA margin). **Why it matters**: structural margin lift, lower cyclicality.
- **Backward integration & captive raw material** – *scaling* – Hindalco (captive coal/bauxite, alumina refinery expansion), Tata Steel (iron ore sales 8–10 Mt FY27, 14–15 Mt FY28; Kalinganagar slurry pipeline saving ₹700/t), Jindal Steel (slurry pipeline commissioned, captive coal 50–55%→65–70%), Lloyds Metals (slurry pipeline lowering logistics), Godawari Power (5.4 Mt beneficiation plant Q3 FY27), Sarda Energy (Avagudem manganese block, Shahpur West coal mine), Nava (Zambia solar, Maamba Phase II). **Why it matters**: cost security, margin resilience during input spikes.
- **Green energy & decarbonisation** – *early/scaling* – Hindalco (643 MW RE, targeting 884 MW RE + 125 MW RTC by FY27), Tata Steel (cost transformation ₹7,000 cr, UK EAF 3.2 Mt), Nava (100 MW Zambia solar commissioned, 300 MW Phase II by Jul 2027), Lloyds Metals (EV dumpers, 1.2 Mt steel plant), Godawari Power (20 GWh BESS, 3 GWh C&I BESS, 100 MW solar), HEG Advanced Materials (RePlus 1→6 GWh, BESS IPP), Sarda Energy (50 MW captive solar, 600→1,200 MW thermal). **Why it matters**: lowers power cost (₹5–6/unit target), creates new revenue streams, aligns with carbon border taxes.
- **Recycling & critical minerals** – *early* – Gravita India (lead/aluminium/plastic/copper recycling, US scrap sourcing subsidiary), Pondy Oxides (copper cathode 36 kt Phase I by Dec 2026, 60% internal scrap feed), Hindustan Zinc (critical minerals: tungsten, REE, yttrium, potash; Gundlupet mining lease), Deccan Gold (Kyrgyzstan gold, Finland/Spain/Mozambique critical minerals), Nava (Zambia lithium exploration, Cote d’Ivoire manganese), Midwest (Sri Lanka heavy mineral sands, Indonesia rare earths). **Why it matters**: policy tailwinds (battery waste export ban, RCM on e-waste), import substitution, higher margins.
- **Capacity expansion at scale** – *mainstream* – APL Apollo (8 Mt by FY28), Hi-Tech Pipes (2 Mt by FY29), Jindal Steel (11 Mt FY27), SAIL (20→35 Mt over 4 years), Hindalco (Rs 50k+ cr projects), Tata Steel (Rs 20k cr annual), Lloyds Metals (Rs 13k cr FY27), Hindustan Zinc (Rs 80k cr HZL 2.0 to 2 Mt), Jindal Stainless (Rs 2,400–2,600 cr FY27), Shyam Metalics (Vision 2031 multi-metal). **Why it matters**: volume growth visibility, but execution risk and leverage watch.
- **Digital/AI integration in operations** – *early* – Rajputana Stainless (Fulcrum OS integrating dispatches, production, QC). **Why it matters**: yield improvement, working capital efficiency.

**Government push**
- **PLI Scheme (Specialty Steel)** – rewards domestic value-added capacity; Sambhv Steel secured PLI 1.2 approval (~13% revenue incentive on seamless pipes) [Sambhv Steel concall Q2 FY27].
- **Quality Control Orders (QCO) on stainless** – suspension from Apr 2026 led to Chinese dumping (200/300 series); Jindal Stainless flags “surge in substandard imports”; DGTR ADD decision expected Dec 2026 [Jindal Stainless concall Q2 FY27].
- **Battery waste export ban (Nov 2026)** – secures domestic feedstock for recyclers; Gravita India notes “57th GST Council recommended bringing electrical and electronic waste scrap under RCM” [Gravita India concall Q2 FY27]; Pondy Oxides not yet in Li-ion but monitors [Pondy Oxides concall Q2 FY27].
- **Vehicle scrapping rules tightened (Nov 2026)** – formalises metal recycling; positive for organised recyclers (Gravita, Pondy) but not yet discussed on calls.
- **Decentralised environment clearance for non-coal mining (Nov 2026)** – speeds mine approvals; not explicitly cited but aligns with Ashapura Minechem (GSM port jetty Mar 2027), Hindustan Zinc (Gundlupet lease executed), Sarda Energy (Avagudem preferred bidder) [Govt: Env Ministry, Nov 2026].
- **EU anti-dumping duty 9.5% on Indian cold-rolled steel (Nov 2026)** – Jindal Stainless cites “reduced EU export quotas”; Tata Steel flags “external regulatory uncertainties in Netherlands”; Venus Pipes says “EU quotas have not significantly hurt run-rate”; Ratnamani notes “50% import tariff on US exports” (different market) [Govt: EU, Nov 2026].
- **Revised mining/mine closure guidelines (Nov 2026)** – not directly referenced; Ashapura Minechem awaits Guinea quota clarity.
- **MCL IPO / Coal India fundraising (Nov 2026)** – not discussed in these calls.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
**Consensus**: Domestic demand strong across infrastructure, housing, auto, solar, data centres, defence.  
- **Steel**: SAIL, Tata Steel, Jindal Steel, Jai Balaji cite 8–10% YoY consumption growth; WSA projects 8% for CY26/27.  
- **Stainless**: Jindal Stainless, Rajputana Stainless, Sambhv Steel see robust auto/rail/metro/white goods/defence demand; India per capita 3.5 kg vs China peak 8 kg (IMFA).  
- **Aluminium**: Hindalco (India demand +6% YoY Q2), NALCO (alumina realization ₹35k/t H2), Maan Aluminium (domestic diversification).  
- **Copper**: Hindalco (refined demand -3% YoY on weak durables), Gravita (demand-supply gap high), Pondy Oxides (copper volumes 3x YoY).  
- **Iron ore/Pellets**: Lloyds Metals (10 Mt H1 production), NMDC not in set but SAIL targets 48–50 Mt FY27.  
- **Solar/Structural tubes**: APL Apollo, Hi-Tech Pipes, Venus Pipes, Sambhv Steel, Jindal Supreme cite 40–50 GW annual solar additions → ~1 Mt steel tubes addressable.  
- **Data centres**: Venus Pipes (spooling LOI ₹185 cr), Goodluck India (aerospace/defence), APL Apollo (DRDO/ISRO tubes), Hi-Tech Pipes (emerging demand).  
- **Defence/Aerospace**: APL Apollo (250×250 mm tubes for DRDO/ISRO), Goodluck India (155 mm shells sole qualified bidder), MIDHANI (AMCA titanium trials, S-400 certified), JTL Industries (defence 140→750 t/month by Q4).

**Divergence**:  
- **Exports**: Venus Pipes (exports 41% of revenue, US strong), Hi-Tech Pipes (exports growing), APL Apollo (UAE disrupted, container freight high), Ratnamani (US DDP orders inflate costs), Jindal Saw (MENA logistics disrupted), Tata Steel UK (import glut, inventory 865 kt), Jindal Stainless (EU quotas reduced).  
- **Water infrastructure**: Jindal Saw, Maharashtra Seamless, Jai Balaji flag soft Jal Jeevan Mission execution; SAIL expects pickup.  
- **China dumping**: Jindal Stainless, Sambhv Steel, Ratnamani flag Chinese 200/300 series coils undercutting; QCO suspension blamed.  
- **Auto/2W demand**: Vardhman Special Steels sees SUV/2W strong, tractors slowing; Usha Martin wires auto/springs strong.

**Coking coal**: Tata Steel (consumption cost +$15/t Q3), SAIL (imported ₹22,930/t Q2 → ₹23,800–23,900 Q3), Jindal Steel (+$20–25/t Q3), Godawari Power (+₹1,000–1,500/t), Sarda Energy (Australian coal high, freight $36–38/t vs $18–22).  
**Freight/logistics**: APL Apollo (freight -₹150/t QoQ but ocean containers high), Hi-Tech Pipes (ocean freight/logistics up), Ratnamani (geopolitical freight spikes, US DDP basis), Scoda Tubes (freight 1.4%→2.9% of revenue), Venus Pipes (export freight + fuel ~1% of sales), Jindal Saw (surging international/domestic logistics).  
**Power/fuel**: APL Apollo (₹6.2–6.3/unit, target ₹5), Godawari Power (+₹0.50–0.75/unit), Sarda Energy (fuel oil usage due to coal rake shortage), NALCO (power/fuel +₹317 cr H1).  
**Raw material shortages**: Bansal Wire (acute wire rod shortage Oct, suppliers diverted to TMT), Ashapura Minechem (Guinea monsoon, freight), Gallantt Ispat (metallics sharp rise H2 Q2).  
**Margin trends**:  
- **Expanding**: APL Apollo (₹6,000/t sustainable, Q3 >₹6,000), Sambhv Steel (FY27 ₹8,000–8,500/t), Venus Pipes (H2 margin improvement guided), Jindal Stainless (EBITDA/t guidance raised ₹20–22k), Hindalco (upstream ₹7,009 cr EBITDA, 52% margin), Tata Steel India (₹17,257/t), Jindal Steel (₹10,529/t), Goodluck India (precision tubes 11–13%), Usha Martin (₹40,680/t), Gravita (Cu ₹84,671/t, Al ₹22,664/t), MIDHANI (H2 EBITDA margin >22.8%).  
- **Compressed**: Jindal Saw (consol EBITDA margin 8% H1), Ratnamani (standalone 12.6%), Scoda Tubes (10.6% Q2, -479 bps YoY), Godawari Power (12% Q2), Maan Aluminium (2.2% H1), Gallantt Ispat (9.6% Q2), Bhagyanagar (5.08%, guidance 4.5–5%), Pondy Oxides (lead EBITDA/t pressured), Ashapura Minechem (Guinea $5.3/t vs $6.3 Q1).  
- **Hedging**: Hindalco (H2 Al 29% at $3,000, INR 21% at 93), Tata Steel (not detailed), Gravita (Cu/Pb fully hedged), Usha Martin (pass-through in specialized), Venus Pipes (new orders pass freight).
