# Usha Martin Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-03 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a strong start to the year with double-digit revenue and PAT growth, robust cash generation, and margin expansion despite geopolitical challenges in the Middle East and higher input costs.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated Revenue: Rs 1,033 crore (up 16% YoY)
- Operating EBITDA: Rs 208 crore (up 44% YoY)
- EBITDA Margin: 20.1% (up 380 bps YoY)
- Profit After Tax (PAT): Rs 142 crore (up 41% YoY)
- Operating Cash Flow Before Tax: Rs 242 crore (116% cash conversion of operating EBITDA)
- Net Cash Position: Rs 465 crore
- Capex Incurred: Rs 73 crore in Q1 FY27
- Return on Capital Employed (ROCE): 21.4% (vs 20.6% as of March 2026)
- Wire Rope Segment Value Growth: 18% YoY (volumes marginally lower)
- Domestic Wire Rope Volume Growth: ~12% YoY (value grew ~21%)
- Middle East Wire Rope Volumes: Down ~28% YoY; realizations improved ~36%
- Wires Volume Growth: ~19% YoY; revenue growth ~32% YoY
- LRPC Segment Revenue Growth: 3.9% YoY
- Value-Added Wire Rope Component: 73% (vs 70% in FY26)
- EBITDA per Ton (Wire Rope): Rs 40,581
- Realizations: Wires ~Rs 85,000/ton; LRPC blended ~Rs 79,000/ton; Domestic rope ~Rs 1,90,000/ton; International rope ~Rs 3,70,000 to Rs 3,80,000/ton

### Highlights
- First time the company achieved a turnover of Rs 1,000 crore in a single quarter.
- Value grew faster than volume across businesses due to improved realizations and a shift toward specialized products.
- Secured first international order for plasticated LRPC strand for a stay cable application.
- Oceanfibre (synthetic slings business under BSUK) continued steady upward revenue trend, scaling to a 2 million to 3 million GBP level.
- Long-term credit rating upgraded by India Ratings and Research to IND AA- from IND A+ with a stable outlook.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains volume growth guidance of 10% to 12% and value growth around 15% for FY27, assuming Middle East operations remain at similar levels or stabilize.
- Capex guidance for FY27 maintained at Rs 250 crore to Rs 300 crore annually (including routine/maintenance capex).
- Management expects a sustainable baseline EBITDA margin of a minimum of 20%, with potential for upward movement as product mix improves.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains healthy across crane, elevator, and fishing segments in the domestic market, and mining/elevator in the U.S., and oil/offshore renewables in Europe.
- Middle East demand impacted (~28% volume drop) due to geopolitical conflicts and delayed/stalled projects in Saudi Arabia; port, marine, and construction sectors affected.
- Elevator market in India growing at ~20%.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Wire rod prices were ~7% higher YoY; zinc prices increased by ~28%; freight costs remained elevated.
- Successfully passed on raw material (steel, gas, zinc) cost increases to customers through pricing actions.
- Commodity products (wires, LRPC) feature 100% pass-through of steel price increases.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex focused on expanding capacity in specialized wire ropes and improving manufacturing efficiency.
- Elevator rope capacity expansion underway by ~6,000 metric tons per annum; commissioning in phases beginning October, completion expected by Q1 FY28.
- Plasticated LRPC annual capacity is 6,000 tons (targeting 3,500 to 4,000 tons in FY27).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Closed the quarter with a net cash position of ~Rs 465 crore.
- Free cash flow stood at Rs 135 crore after funding Rs 73 crore capex.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Ongoing geopolitical conflicts and market disruptions in the Middle East causing project delays and lower stocking by distributors.
- Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) exposes the wires export segment (7217) in the definitive period, with wire rope (7312) expected to be impacted post FY28.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Rajesh Majumdar (360 ONE Capital)** asked about the timeline for wire rope volume upticks given flattish recent volumes. **Management** explained that a 30% volume dip in the Middle East offset growth in India, the US, and Europe, but maintained 10% to 12% volume growth guidance for the year based on strong inquiry pipelines.
2. **Varun Jain (Dolat Capital)** asked about the Total Addressable Market (TAM) and margins for Oceanfibre. **Management** stated the TAM is 1.5 billion to 2 billion, with gross margins of 65% to 70%, driven by specialized heavy-lifting applications.
3. **Vinit Thakur (Plus91 AMC)** questioned how 10% to 12% volume growth is achievable given flattish current YoY volumes. **Management** reiterated confidence, stating it assumes Middle East operations do not deteriorate further while other geographies grow.
4. **Shivkumar Prajapati (Mirae Asset)** asked about turnaround strategies for non-core U M Cables and PAT-negative Thailand operations. **Management** stated they are evaluating utilizing the West India cable facility for value-added wire/ropes and are formulating a 6-month strategic integration plan for Thailand.
5. **Shraddha Kapadia (SMIFS Limited)** questioned why EBITDA per ton only rose 3% QoQ despite a 13% rise in steel prices. **Management** clarified that steel price percentage increases cannot be applied uniformly to finished wire rope selling prices; absolute steel price increases are passed on fully in commodities.

### One-line takeaway
Usha Martin delivered a strong Q1 FY27 with 16% revenue growth, 44% EBITDA growth (20.1% margin), and a robust net cash balance of Rs 465 crore, driven by a rich product mix and value-led growth despite Middle East headwinds.
