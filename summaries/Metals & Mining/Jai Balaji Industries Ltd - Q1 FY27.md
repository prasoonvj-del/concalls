# Jai Balaji Industries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-17 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong operational efficiency and ferroalloy performance offset by subdued DI pipe demand due to government project delays.

### Key numbers
*   **Revenue:** Rs 1,683 crore (+24% YoY).
*   **Adjusted EBITDA:** Rs 154 crore (+46% YoY).
*   **PAT:** Rs 85 crore (+21% YoY).
*   **Operational EBITDA margin:** 9%.
*   **PAT margin:** 5%.
*   **Net term debt:** Rs 188 crore.
*   **Value-added products contribution:** 42% of total sales.

### Highlights
*   **Segmental Revenue Mix:** Ferroalloys (27.33%), Pig Iron (19.13%), DI Pipes (14.95%), TMT bars (14.83%), Sponge Iron (7.4%), Coke (7%), Billets (3.54%).
*   **Price Realizations:** Ferroalloy prices increased 46% YoY; Pig iron prices up 16% YoY.
*   **Deleveraging:** Net term debt reduced from Rs 3,408 crore in FY21 to Rs 188 crore in Q1 FY27.
*   **Logistics:** Three dedicated railway sidings are operational, reducing transportation costs and turnaround time.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Revenue:** Management expects revenue of Rs 7,000 crore to Rs 7,500 crore by FY28 in a normal market.
*   **DI Pipes:** Management expects a post-monsoon recovery in dispatches as government fund releases improve.
*   **Ferroalloys:** Management expects capacity utilization to remain at 80-90% post-commissioning of new modules.
*   **Margins:** Management targets long-term margins of 15-20% for specialized ferroalloys.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **DI Pipes:** Market remains subdued due to slow government ordering and project execution backlogs.
*   **Structural Drivers:** Management highlighted Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 (outlay Rs 8.69 lakh crore) and AMRUT 2.0 as key long-term demand drivers.
*   **Ferroalloys:** Segment continues to witness strong momentum with healthy steel demand.
*   **Plastic Pipe Project:** Management stated the project is in abeyance as they failed to sell any units, confirming it will not replace steel/DI pipes.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Margins:** Conventional steel products (5-7%); Specialized ferroalloys (15-18%).
*   **Pricing:** Management noted DI pipe prices are at "rock bottom" and cannot get worse.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Capacity Expansion:**
    *   DI pipe: Increased to 5.5 lakh TPA (from 5 lakh TPA).
    *   Specialized ferroalloy: Increasing to 1.9 lakh TPA (from 1.66 lakh TPA).
    *   Blast furnace: Increasing to 7.5 lakh TPA (from 6.3 lakh TPA).
    *   Sinter: Increasing to 12.08 lakh TPA (from 9.08 lakh TPA).
*   **Capex Outlay:** Revised to Rs 1,112 crore (from Rs 1,000 crore) due to technical upgrades, inflation, and currency devaluation; Rs 1,076 crore already invested.
*   **Timeline:** Enhanced capacities expected to be commissioned by Q3 FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Debt:** Net term debt is Rs 188 crore; total sanctioned debt (fund + non-fund) is Rs 525-550 crore.
*   **Debt-Equity:** Net term debt to equity ratio is 0.07 (as of FY26 end).

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Government Backlog:** Delays in fund releases for water projects are constraining order flows.
*   **Competition:** High competitive intensity in the DI pipe market until industry capacity utilization crosses 65-70%.
*   **Execution:** Potential for further time overruns on capex projects.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Why did capex increase from Rs 1,000 cr to Rs 1,112 cr?** A: Management cited technical upgrades, inflationary trends, and currency devaluation.
*   **Q: When will DI pipe orders pick up?** A: Management expects a post-monsoon recovery as government fund releases improve.
*   **Q: Why is top-line growth not reflecting the heavy capex?** A: Management explained that capex was largely for backward integration (cost optimization) and DI pipe capacity, which awaits market revival.
*   **Q: What is the status of the plastic pipe replacement project?** A: Management stated it is in abeyance as they could not sell a single piece.

### One-line takeaway
Jai Balaji is banking on a post-monsoon recovery in government water infrastructure spending to utilize its expanded DI pipe capacity, while relying on strong ferroalloy margins to sustain current performance.
