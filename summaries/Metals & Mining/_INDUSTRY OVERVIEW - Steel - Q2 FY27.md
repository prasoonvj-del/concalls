# Metals & Mining - Steel: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Across the Indian steel sector, Q2 FY27 was characterized by strong volume growth and robust underlying domestic demand, countered by input cost inflation—driven by rising coking/thermal coal prices and freight rate hikes due to West Asian conflicts—and temporary monsoon-related mining disruptions. While major integrated players like **Tata Steel**, **SAIL**, and **Shyam Metalics** reported healthy top-line and EBITDA expansions, several secondary and merchant players like **Gallantt Ispat** and **Godawari Power** faced margin compression due to plant shutdowns, regulatory constraints, and high merchant raw material sourcing costs. 

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across the board (including **Gallantt Ispat**, **Godawari Power**, **Jindal Steel**, and **SAIL**) agree that domestic steel demand remains resilient, supported by government infrastructure spending, housing, and urbanization. 
*   **Divergence:** 
    *   *Product Segments:* **Sarda Energy** noted that flat steel demand is more buoyant than long sections. **Vardhman Special Steels** highlighted robust demand from passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, and non-automotive sectors, though tractors showed signs of slowing.
    *   *Specialized Segments:* **Jai Balaji** reported near-term demand softness in Ductile Iron (DI) pipes due to delayed government fund releases under the Jal Jeevan Mission, whereas **Shyam Metalics** and **Tata Steel** observed strong uptake in specialty alloys and stainless steel.
    *   *Exports & Imports:* **Jindal Stainless** and **Tata Steel** flagged severe pressure from cheap, substandard imports (particularly from China) due to suspended quality control orders and inventory gluts in export markets like the UK.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Input Cost Pressures:** Nearly all companies cited rising coking coal, thermal coal, and ocean freight costs. **Gallantt Ispat** noted that coal and freight stayed elevated, impacting raw material costs by ~5%. **Godawari Power** suffered from high market iron ore sourcing costs due to internal overburden dumping constraints. **SAIL** reported imported coking coal consumption prices rising to INR 22,930 per tonne in Q2.
*   **Mitigation & Divergence in Margins:** 
    *   **Jindal Stainless** raised its EBITDA per tonne guidance to INR 20,000–22,000 despite import pressures.
    *   **Shyam Metalics** leveraged backward integration and captive coal blocks to maintain stable operating EBITDA margins of 13.8%.
    *   **Gallantt Ispat** and **Godawari Power** experienced temporary margin dilution due to operational shutdowns and merchant raw material dependence.
    *   **Jindal Steel** commissioned a 192-km slurry pipeline expected to save INR 700/tonne in freight costs.

## Capex & capacity
Aggressive brownfield and greenfield capex programs are underway, funded largely through internal accruals:
*   **SAIL:** Guiding for INR 15,000 crore capex in FY27, scaling up total capacity from 20 MT to 35 MT over 4 years.
*   **Tata Steel:** Executing the 4.8 MTPA NINL expansion and UK EAF project, alongside a Rs 7,000 crore cost transformation target.
*   **Jindal Steel:** Continuing execution on its growth capex program (INR 7,500–10,000 crore annually) targeting a 11 million tonne production goal for FY27.
*   **Shyam Metalics:** Incurred INR 1,291 crore in H1 capex, advancing its Vision 2031 expansion across flat, stainless, and aluminum foil segments.
*   **Godawari Power:** Approved INR 1,000 crore capex for a container manufacturing line, INR 135 crore for a BESS line, and progressed its CRM relocation to Maharashtra.
*   **Gallantt Ispat:** Spent INR 278 crore in H1 on its Gorakhpur capacity expansion (to 1.3 MTPA) and solar power plants.
*   **Jai Balaji:** Nearly completed its Rs 1,112 crore capex program, enhancing blast furnace, sinter, and ferroalloy capacities.
*   **Vardhman Special Steels:** Applied for brownfield expansion (to 360,000 tonnes) and laid the foundation stone for a new forging plant.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Godawari Power:** Cut FY27 production guidance for iron ore mining (from 3.4M to 2.5M tonnes) and iron ore pellets (4.4M to 3.5M tonnes) due to space constraints for overburden dumping.
*   **Jindal Stainless:** Revised full-year volume growth guidance down to 4%–6% (from 7%–9%), but raised its EBITDA per tonne guidance to INR 20,000–22,000.
*   **SAIL:** Revised full-year sales volume guidance slightly to around 21 million tonnes (from 22 million tonnes) following monsoon disruptions.

## What to watch next quarter
*   Regulatory approvals and tree-cutting clearances for **Godawari Power**’s beneficiation plant.
*   Ramp-up and capacity utilization of **Gallantt Ispat**'s expanded Gorakhpur plant post-shutdowns.
*   Anti-dumping duty decisions on Chinese stainless steel imports expected by December 31 for **Jindal Stainless**.
*   Progress on **Tata Steel**'s UK operations approaching breakeven as excess import inventory clears out.
*   Commissioning of **Sarda Energy**'s Shahpur West coal mine and 50 MW solar project.

## Company one-liners
*   **Gallantt Ispat:** Margins faced temporary compression from plant shutdowns and coal inflation, but management expects a swift H1 recovery backed by new capacity.
*   **Godawari Power and Ispat:** Strong revenue growth was overshadowed by mining constraints and high raw material costs, leading to downward production guidance revisions.
*   **Jai Balaji Industries:** Delivered stellar YoY profitability growth through operational efficiencies while awaiting a government-led demand revival in DI pipes.
*   **Jindal Stainless:** Achieved a resilient Q2 operational recovery with margin expansion, prompting higher EBITDA/tonne guidance despite cheap import headwinds.
*   **Jinal Steel:** Posted record sales and revenue while executing strategic cost-saving initiatives like the slurry pipeline and coal gasification.
*   **Sarda Energy & Minerals:** Secured record H1 EBITDA and PAT anchored by strong power realizations and a net-debt-free balance sheet.
*   **Shyam Metalics and Energy:** Reported robust revenue and PAT growth driven by volume expansion and downstream scaling under its Vision 2031 strategy.
*   **Steel Authority of India:** Delivered strong H1 volume growth and lower debt, offsetting input cost pressures with aggressive capacity and raw material expansions.
*   **Tata Steel:** Leveraging high-margin India operations and cost transformation programs to offset European trade distortions and regulatory headwinds.
*   **Vardhman Special Steels:** Capitalized on strong automotive and non-automotive demand to deliver a 39% revenue surge and record sales volumes.
