# Lloyds Metals and Energy Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-09 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Best ever H1 revenue, EBITDA, PAT, and H1 margins in the company's history on both a stand-alone and consolidated basis.

### Key numbers
- Stand-alone Revenue (Q2 FY27): Rs 4,132 crore (up 62% YoY)
- Stand-alone Revenue (H1 FY27): Rs 9,545 crore (up 94% YoY)
- Stand-alone EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs 1,538 crore (up 83% YoY)
- Stand-alone EBITDA (H1 FY27): Rs 3,658 crore (up 126% YoY)
- Stand-alone PAT (Q2 FY27): Rs 1,112 crore (up 84% YoY)
- Stand-alone PAT (H1 FY27): Rs 2,639 crore (up 113% YoY)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin: 38.33% (up 542 bps YoY)
- Q2 FY27 EBITDA Margin: 37.22% (up 419 bps YoY)
- Iron Ore Production (Q2 FY27): 3.95 million tonnes (up 15.5% YoY)
- Iron Ore Production (H1 FY27): 10 million tonnes (up 35% YoY)
- Pellet Production (Q2 FY27): 2.11 million tonnes; H1 FY27: 3.81 million tonnes
- Stand-alone Net Debt (as of Sep 30, 2026): Rs 9,316 crore
- Stand-alone Capex (H1 FY27): Rs 6,052 crore
- Thriveni H1 FY27 Revenue: Rs 4,608 crore (up 43% YoY)
- Thriveni H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs 884 crore (up 85% YoY); Margin: 19.18% (up 436 bps YoY)
- Thriveni H1 FY27 Cash PAT: Rs 558 crore (up 116% YoY); Margin: 12.11% (up 410 bps YoY)

### Highlights
- Best-ever H1 performance driven by higher iron ore EC limits, faster-than-planned pellet plant ramp-up, and improved sponge iron volumes.
- Value-added products contributed 61% of stand-alone revenue and 67% of EBIT in Q2 (vs. 43% and 49% YoY).
- Commissioning of the first slurry pipeline lowered logistics and freight costs on iron ore.
- Thriveni completed equipment mobilization for FY27; operationalized 88 electric equipment units at Western Hill, along with crushers and heavy earthmoving machinery.
- Gold mining plan for Thriveni Lloyds Swarnagiri (formerly GeoMysore) approved for 1.5 million tonnes per annum capacity.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects FY27 volume to touch about 8 million tonnes and rise to around 9.5 million tonnes next year.
- Full-year FY27 capex expected to be around Rs 13,000 crore, with a similar figure (~Rs 10,000 crore or more) planned for FY28.
- Copper production target for Chemaf revised to 5,850 tonnes for FY27.
- Chemaf debt restructuring expected to close within the next 3 to 4 months (before the end of FY27), targeting long-term net debt around $500 million.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global copper market described as quite bullish; sulphuric acid faces shortages globally, creating high costs for operations lacking captive supply.
- Iron ore pricing is managed via an open market approach based on end-product demand, stock, and order books.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Slurry pipeline operation structurally reduced freight costs.
- Thriveni faced margin headwinds in Q2 due to historical monsoon slowdowns and spikes in HSD and explosive prices; pass-through mechanisms with clients (like NTPC) are underway.
- DRC diesel costs remain high (~$3.2 per liter) due to West Asia crises and landlocked logistics.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- 1.2-million-tonne integrated steel plant (wire rod) progressing toward pre-commissioning in Q4 (blast furnace/coke oven) and Q1 2027 (steel plant/wire rod mill).
- Beneficiation project volumes expected to start in FY29.
- Chemaf projects (Etoile and Mutoshi) targeting completion by the end of FY27 and production in FY28.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Stand-alone net debt stands at Rs 9,316 crore; consolidated net debt remains below Rs 20,000 crore (largely driven by acquired Chemaf debt).
- Took enabling resolution for raising up to $300 million in NCDs for potential capex opportunities.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical headwinds and high fuel/explosive costs impacting mining contractor operations.
- DRC landlocked status and reliance on third-party market ore/sulphuric acid shortages creating near-term cost challenges for Surya Mines.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Jashandeep Chadha (Nomura) asked about iron ore realization premiums over benchmarks and material balance discrepancies.** Management stated their pricing reflects high quality and is determined via internal market assessments, noting that lower Q2 production was due to monsoon seasonality and opening balances accounted for material reconciliation.
2. **Siddharth Gadekar (Equirus Securities) asked about the Chemaf debt restructuring timeline and post-restructuring net debt.** Management replied it will close in 3 to 4 months (before FY27 end), bringing net debt down to around $500 million including plant completion capex.
3. **Manav Gogia (Aditya Birla) asked about the Tata Steel MDO/equipment rental scope and volumes.** S.K. Naredi clarified that equipment rental to Brahmani River Pellets (BRPL) and Tata mines is expected to yield 12-14 million tonnes of iron ore and 1.5-1.8 million tonnes of manganese next year.
4. **Vikash Singh (ICICI Securities) asked about capital allocation rankings across businesses.** Management declined to rank them, stating each vertical (Thriveni services, copper, iron ore, steel) is independently EBITDA-accretive and acts as a strategic hedge.
5. **Ritesh Bhagwati (Alpha Plus Capital) questioned the reduction in FY27 copper volume guidance.** Hemankur Upadhyaya explained the target was revised to 5,850 tonnes because third-party ore sourcing at Surya Mines is less lucrative amid high third-party costs, shifting focus to Chemaf.

### One-line takeaway
Lloyds Metals delivered a record H1 FY27 driven by structural integration, lower logistics costs via slurry pipelines, and strong value-added product contributions, even as downstream steel and overseas copper projects progress toward multi-year scale-up.
