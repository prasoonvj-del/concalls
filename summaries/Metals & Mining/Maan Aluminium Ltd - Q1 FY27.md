# Maan Aluminium Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-19 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue moderated sequentially due to sluggish export markets and duty impacts, but profitability and EBITDA improved due to cost discipline and a better operational mix.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 232 crore (up 10% YoY from Rs 211 crore in Q1 FY26; down from Rs 255 crore in Q4 FY26).
- **Manufacturing Turnover:** Rs 70+ crore in Q1 FY27 (with exports constituting roughly 40%).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 7 crore in Q1 FY27 (up ~40% QoQ from Rs 5 crore in Q4 FY26).
- **EBITDA Margin:** ~3% in Q1 FY27 (compared to ~2% in Q4 FY26).
- **PAT:** Rs 3 crore in Q1 FY27 (compared to Rs 2 crore in Q4 FY26).
- **Basic EPS:** Rs 0.52 in Q1 FY27 (compared to Rs 0.29 in Q4 FY26).
- **Production Volumes:** 1,558 metric tonnes in Q1 FY27 (extrusion).
- **CapEx Done in Q1:** Less than Rs 5 crore.
- **FY26 Full Year Revenue:** Rs 809 crore (broadly in line with FY25 revenue of Rs 810 crore).
- **FY26 Full Year EBITDA:** Rs 31 crore (up ~21% YoY from Rs 30 crore in FY25).
- **FY26 Full Year PAT:** Rs 13 crore (compared to Rs 16 crore in FY25).
- **CapEx Plan:** Cumulative planned capex of ~Rs 166 crore over the next three years (including ~Rs 90 crore allocated toward new plants under development, and a ~Rs 45 crore capex for the Dewas facility).

### Highlights
- Company is transforming from a conventional aluminium extrusion player into a high value-added aluminium converter.
- Integrated manufacturing platform capacities: Foundry at 12,000 tonnes/annum, extrusion at 24,000 tonnes/annum, anodizing at 3,600 tonnes/annum, and machining at 1,400 tonnes/annum.
- Current capacities utilization: Anodizing at 45%–50%, machining at 55%.
- Italian press at Pithampur is online; management expects a 3-year ramp-up (currently achieved ~25% ramp-up).
- Strong balance sheet with low leverage; management stated no debt will be taken for ongoing capex programs.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guided for a "flattish growth" in terms of volumes for the year due to sluggish export market conditions.
- Management expects the Dewas precision tubing plant to be online before the mid of next year (FY28).
- Employee expenses are expected to remain stable despite upcoming capacity additions because the technical teams and R&D staff are already onboarded.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Export business previously constituted 60%–70% but has dropped to ~45% due to duties impacting a major part of the export business.
- International shipping issues, Strait of Hormuz conflict, and high freight rates (multiplied 5x to 10x) are causing shipment delays and increasing costs.
- Export customers served across the US, UAE, Australia, UK, Qatar, and Israel.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Most manufacturing volume is hedged based on orders (less than 5% left unhedged); commodity price movements do not significantly impact them as pure converters.
- Plain extrusion margins range between 6% to 10%, while value-added products yield 15%+ margins.
- Gas and energy prices have increased due to the Middle East crisis; management has successfully transferred ~50% of cost increases to customers and is working on the balance 50% via contract renewals.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Cumulative 3-year capex plan is ~Rs 166 crore (~Rs 90 crore for new plants).
- Dewas facility (~Rs 45 crore capex) is a strategic project for aluminium precision tubing targeted at aerospace, defense, and automotive sectors; currently fed by Pithampur raw materials while its own plant capacity comes online in 6 to 8 months. Expected peak asset turnover of 2x to 3x.
- Major capex outflows are scheduled for H2 FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital cycle elongated slightly due to higher raw material procurement for upcoming investments and customer credit periods.
- Finance costs remain contained (~Rs 1 crore in Q1). Company is deleveraging and holds sufficient cash.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Import duties negatively impacting a major portion of the export business.
- Geopolitical tensions, Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions, and high freight rates (5x-10x increase) impacting margins and causing shipment delays.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Samay Shah (Nuvama Wealth) asked about capacity volumes, EBITDA per ton, and shipment delays.** Management clarified they track blended EBITDA, reported extrusion production of 1,558 metric tonnes for Q1, and confirmed that Red Sea/Strait of Hormuz shipping delays and freight cost spikes are ongoing.
2. **Jigar Jani asked about the business model, hedging mechanism, and margin profile of value-added products.** Management explained that manufacturing is >95% hedged against orders, extrusion margins are 6%–10%, and value-added processing margins exceed 15%.
3. **Jigar Jani asked about the status, asset turns, and timelines of the Dewas capex.** Management stated the Dewas precision tubing plant requires technical compliances and is expected to go online before mid-next year, targeting a 2x to 3x asset turnover.
4. **Samay Shah asked if debt would be raised given the elongation of working capital days.** Management clarified that no debt will be raised; the company has enough cash and is deleveraging.
5. **Samay Shah asked about the future trajectory of employee expenses upon capacity scaling.** Management answered that employee expenses will not increase significantly because the specialized technical teams and R&D staff are already on the payroll.

### One-line takeaway
Maan Aluminium reported a 10% YoY revenue increase to Rs 232 crore with a 40% sequential EBITDA recovery to Rs 7 crore, as management focuses on shifting from export-heavy trading to high-margin downstream domestic manufacturing despite freight and duty headwinds.
