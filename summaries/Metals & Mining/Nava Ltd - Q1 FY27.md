# Nava Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-19 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consolidated total income reached an all-time quarterly high supported by disciplined cost management and a diversified portfolio.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated total income: INR1,269 crores (all-time quarterly high)
- Standalone total income: INR689 crores
- MEL (300 MW plant) PLF: 89.3%
- MEL Phase 2 project cost: INR400 million (INR300 million debt, INR100 million equity)
- MEL Phase 2 expected revenue: INR200 million
- Zambia sugar plant expected revenue: INR55 to INR60 million per annum
- Manganese concession area: 360 square kilometers (detailed exploration done on 2 square kilometers)
- Deferred tax expense (consolidated): INR40 crores (Q1 FY27)
- Metals production committed under long-term/quarterly contracts: ~70%

### Highlights
- MEL's 300-megawatt plant operated strongly at 89.3 PLF.
- International operations provided value through dividend flows and financial strength.
- 100-megawatt solar power project in Zambia set to be commissioned by the end of September.
- Orissa unit resumed operations on August 1, 2026, following a shutdown for long-term maintenance activities.
- Management engaged a third-party study to evaluate options for land assets (including Hyderabad and Samalkot).
- Manganese exploration in Ivory Coast showed promising results; management is working to convert the exploration license to an exploitation license.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects MEL Phase 2 commissioning to move to Q2 of FY27-28 (June-July timeframe) due to logistical delays.
- Zambia sugar plant commissioning targeted for Q4 of FY28.
- MEL Phase 2 Return on Equity (RoE) envisaged at about 15%.
- Zambian energy EBITDA margin sustainable around 45% to 50%.
- Consolidated EBITDA margin expected between 35% to 40%.
- Metals business margins expected to remain stable or slightly increase through the end of the financial year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Geopolitical and supply chain disruptions create significant challenges, prompting remedial actions.
- Metals business is volume-driven; spot market prices saw a slight improvement of 5% to 10% QoQ.
- Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) in nuclear power are gaining traction, though the space is new and evolving.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Lower coal and manufacturing costs supported standalone improvement.
- Zambian energy EBITDA decline was attributed to less reversal of ECL credit.
- Finance costs incurred on MEL Phase 2 are being capitalized as required by IFRS and Ind AS.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Thermal energy, renewable energy, avocado, and sugar projects in Zambia are in active implementation.
- MEL Phase 2 project cost is INR400 million (INR300 million debt, INR100 million equity).
- 100-megawatt solar power project in Zambia launching by end of September.
- Ivory Coast manganese: Exploitation license application in progress; factory setup pending JV partnership approval for a government-controlled mine.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Land assets are recorded at historical cost (e.g., Nacharam land historical cost is around INR40 lakhs).
- Kwacha-USD exchange rate stood at around INR18 as of June 30.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical and supply chain disruptions causing logistical delays for critical material and equipment for MEL Phase 2.
- Currency fluctuations impact deferred tax expenses via Kwacha-USD exchange rates.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Vansh (Individual Investor) asked about the delay and revenue potential of the MEL Phase 2 thermal plant:** Management explained that logistical delays pushed complete commissioning to Q2 of FY27-28, with expected revenue of INR200 million and a debt-equity mix of 300 million to 100 million.
- **Vijay (Individual Investor) questioned why land assets are held at historical cost and not revalued:** Management stated accounting standards allow historical cost, and a third-party study is currently evaluating current asset options.
- **Sai Shreyas (Analyst) asked about the decline in Zambian energy EBITDA margins and sustainable levels:** CFO B. Srinivasa Rao attributed the decline to lower ECL credit reversals and guided a sustainable EBITDA margin of 45% to 50%.
- **Sai Shreyas asked about segment vs. consolidated EBITDA and intersegment eliminations:** CFO explained transfer pricing eliminations and stated consolidated EBITDA levels range between 35% to 40%.
- **Vijay asked about Indian expansion plans in renewables and nuclear:** Management stated they are location-agnostic, focusing on best returns and risk across geographies including India, while monitoring evolving SMR nuclear technology.

### One-line takeaway
Nava reported a record consolidated total income of INR1,269 crores for Q1 FY27, driven by strong core operations and disciplined cost management, despite minor logistical delays pushing MEL Phase 2 commissioning to Q2 FY28.
