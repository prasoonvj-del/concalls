# Indian Metals & Ferro Alloys Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong operational performance, successful capacity additions, and confidence in long-term demand despite minor margin pressure from input costs.

### Key numbers
*   **Q2 FY27 EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 28,000.
*   **FY27 production guidance:** 380,000 tonnes.
*   **FY28 production guidance:** 475,000 tonnes.
*   **Capex approved:** Rs 375 crore (for mining expansion and low-carbon ferrochrome production).
*   **Ethanol project:** Expected revenue of Rs 320 crore; 10% EBITDA margin; 15% IRR.
*   **MTM forex loss:** Rs 8 crore.

### Highlights
*   **KNR-2 (Acquired unit):** Reached rated capacity of 84,000 tonnes per annum in October; expects full rated capacity of 100,000 tonnes per annum by December.
*   **KNR-1 (Greenfield):** Furnace 1 achieved hot metal tapping on August 25; Furnace 2 expected to tap around November 15.
*   **Mining:** Board approved capex to increase Mahagiri underground mine output from 0.6 million tonnes to 0.75 million tonnes per annum.
*   **Aviation:** Aviation assets transferred to a 100% subsidiary to remove compliance burdens from the main operating entity.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Production:** Management expects to cross 100,000 tonnes of output in Q3 FY27 for the first time.
*   **Margins:** Management expects a slight tightening of margins in the near term due to rising input costs (met coke) but believes volume growth will offset this in absolute terms.
*   **Efficiency:** Management expects EBITDA to be Rs 1,500–2,000 per tonne higher in the future due to economies of scale and efficiencies at the Kalinganagar site.
*   **Ethanol:** Project commissioning expected in January 2027.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Market:** Management remains confident in the industry; expects South African production ramp-up to be balanced by a reduction in Chinese capacity.
*   **India:** Sees significant opportunity in domestic stainless steel growth, noting India's per capita consumption is 3.5kg compared to China's peak of 8kg.
*   **Strategy:** Targeting 40% of output for the domestic market by 2028.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Input costs:** Met coke prices are rising (currently ~Rs 375-380); management noted higher royalty outgoes due to elevated chrome ore prices.
*   **Startup costs:** Incurred one-off costs related to the startup and stabilization of new furnaces and power utilization inefficiencies.
*   **Energy:** Expects 70MW of renewable energy to flow from late November, which will help reduce power costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Mining:** Capex approved to accelerate reaching 1.2 million tonnes of ore output and eventually exceed it.
*   **New Product:** Diversifying into low-carbon ferrochrome (expected early 2028) to bolster margins and diversify the product basket.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Working capital:** Ethanol project working capital requirement estimated at 45-60 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Seasonality:** Ore raising impacted by extended monsoon season.
*   **Geopolitics:** Shipping costs have increased due to the global geopolitical situation.
*   **Operational:** Initial inefficiencies in power utilization and startup costs at new sites.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Impact of Chinese stainless steel curtailment?** Management clarified that China is phasing out older, inefficient furnaces for newer ones, not reducing overall demand.
*   **Q: Status of critical minerals?** Management stated there is no update; they have participated in auctions but have not had a breakthrough yet.
*   **Q: Cost per tonne guidance?** Management declined to provide specific forecasts but reiterated that rising met coke and royalty costs are the primary drivers of current cost increases.
*   **Q: Why transfer aviation assets?** Management explained it is to move non-core compliance requirements away from the main operating company.

### One-line takeaway
IMFA is scaling production capacity significantly through KNR-1 and KNR-2, and while near-term margins face pressure from rising input costs, management expects volume growth and operational efficiencies to drive superior performance.
