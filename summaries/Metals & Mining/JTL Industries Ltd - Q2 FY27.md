# JTL Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue, EBITDA, and PAT grew significantly YoY alongside higher sales volumes and improving EBITDA per ton.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations:** INR 613.8 crores (up 43.0% YoY)
- **EBITDA:** INR 50.8 crores (up 46.5% YoY)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 8.3% (vs 8.1% in Q2 FY26)
- **PAT:** INR 29.0 crores (up 30.7% YoY)
- **Normalized PAT (excl. non-cash depreciation at JTL Defence):** INR 31.8 crores
- **Sales Volume:** 1,00,484 metric tonnes (up 23.2% YoY)
- **EBITDA per tonne:** INR 5,053 (up 19% YoY)
- **H1 Revenue:** INR 1,335.4 crores (up 37.2% YoY)
- **H1 EBITDA:** INR 109.5 crores (up 88.7% YoY)
- **H1 PAT:** INR 64.3 crores (up 66.2% YoY)
- **H1 Sales Volume:** 2,18,997 metric tonnes (up 20.2% YoY)
- **H1 Operating Cash Flow:** Positive INR 50.0 crores (vs outflow of INR 80.3 crores in H1 FY26)
- **H1 Capex:** INR 76.5 crores (INR 67.0 crores standalone, mostly at Mangaon facility)

### Highlights
- Reported highest-ever half-yearly sales volume, EBITDA, and PAT.
- Cold trials completed for the pickling and GP lines in Maharashtra; awaiting enhanced electricity connection for commercial production.
- *Continuity note:* The Maharashtra facility expansion trials align with our record, with management noting that commercial production is expected by the end of Q3/Q4 pending power connectivity.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 volume to be ~20% higher than H1, targeting ~2,60,000 tonnes in H2.
- Maintains annual volume target of 30% growth over last year's 4 lakh tonnes (totalling ~5,15,000 tonnes).
- FY28 capacity utilization targeted at ~55% on a 1.7 million tonnes capacity.
- Export volume target for the year: 50,000 to 55,000 tonnes.
- EBITDA per tonne expected to hover around INR 5,000 to INR 5,250 for the next financial year.
- Capex guidance: INR 125 crores to INR 150 crores in H2 FY27; FY28 maintenance capex of INR 40 crores to INR 50 crores plus ~INR 75 crores for the API pipe line.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand experienced temporary latency due to the monsoon, Ganpati festival, and approaching Diwali, but October performance was good.
- Demand expected to pick up robustly after November 15 for the rest of the quarter.
- Primary HR coil prices remain high due to supply contractions from maintenance shutdowns at Tata, SAIL, and AM/NS; secondary coil priced around INR 64,000.
- Price differential between primary and secondary pipes ranges between INR 12 to INR 20 per kg.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Additional non-cash depreciation charge of INR 2.78 crores included in Q2/H1 PAT due to asset revaluation at JTL Defence in March 2026.
- Value-added products (VAP) contribution stood at ~28,000 tonnes in Q2 (~30% to 35% of H1 mix), expected to reach ~50% for next financial year.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Mangaon facility awaiting electricity enhancement to start commercial production of the GP line (adding ~10,000 tonnes monthly).
- Color-coated CRM complex expected to add ~15,000 tonnes monthly, taking total company run-rate from 40,000 tonnes to ~65,000 tonnes per month by year-end.
- API line expected to commission by H1 FY28, catering to both domestic and export markets.
- JTL Defence current production run-rate is ~140 tonnes/month (targeting 250 tonnes/month by December and 750 tonnes/month by Q4), expanding into coin blanks and bullet cups.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital days increased from 75 days in Q1 to 83 days in Q2, partly due to stranded inventory from container unavailability and festival holidays.
- Government sales portion reduced to 3% (from 20% historically).
- Management aims to reduce working capital cycle to ~60 days by next year and 35 days by FY29 by increasing dealer-oriented business and implementing vendor financing schemes.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Container non-availability and dynamic freight costs creating latency in export shipments.
- Geopolitical issues in the Gulf (Hormuz closure) causing delivery delays.
- Temporary demand softness/latency linked to festive seasons and heavy monsoons.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Akshay Shetty (Mirae Asset) on H2 volume bridge & step-up:** Management explained that run rates will jump from 40,000 to 50,000-65,000 tonnes/month once the Maharashtra GP and color-coated lines commence commercial production upon receiving electricity clearance.
2. **Pratik (SMIFS) on export environment:** Management acknowledged dynamic container availability and port delays, but noted new trial orders in Canada and the US are gaining traction, targeting 50,000-55,000 export tonnes for the year.
3. **Pratik (SMIFS) on working capital deterioration:** Management attributed the rise to 83 days to stuck inventory caused by container shortages and festivals, reiterating a long-term goal of 35 days by FY29 via dealer-led shifts.
4. **Pallav Agarwal (Antique Stock Broking) on EBITDA per tonne trajectory:** Management guided for a stable INR 5,000-5,250 range for FY28 despite new capacity additions dampening near-term utilization.
5. **Pallav Agarwal on JTL Defence progress:** Management stated volume is scaling from 40 tonnes/month at start-up to 140 tonnes currently, targeting 250 tonnes by December and entering high-margin coin blanks and bullet cups.

### One-line takeaway
JTL Industries delivered robust 43% revenue and 47% EBITDA growth in Q2 FY27, backed by higher VAP sales, and remains poised for volume acceleration as its Maharashtra downstream capacities come on stream.
