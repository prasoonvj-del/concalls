# APL Apollo Tubes Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-29 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Best quarter in terms of sales volume, EBITDA, and net profit driven by brand strength, value-added products, and cost efficiency, despite headwinds in UAE and domestic steel supply.

### Key numbers
- **Sales Volume:** 960,000 tonnes (Q2); APAPOLLO and roofing products sale volume increased by 20% YoY in H1.
- **Roofing capacity / Q1 volume:** 700,000 tonnes capacity; 135,000 tonnes done in Q1 (with 175,000 tonnes quarterly capacity scope); targeting 175,000 tonnes in Q3.
- **EBITDA per tonne:** Rs. 6,000 (Q2); management expects Q3 to be above Rs. 6,000.
- **Cash on books:** Rs. 16 billion as of September 2026 (up 15% vs Rs. 14 billion in June quarter).
- **Net cash balance:** Rs. 1,600 crores.
- **Working capital days:** Zero.
- **Capex:** Rs. 400 crores spent in H1 FY27.
- **Electricity cost:** Reached around Rs. 6.2 to Rs. 6.3 per unit (targeting Rs. 5 next year).
- **Freight cost:** Reduced by Rs. 150/tonne in Q2.
- **Dubai capacity:** Reaching 0.5 million tonnes by March 2027 (brownfield expansion underway); expected EBITDA potential of Rs. 8,000 to Rs. 10,000 per tonne.

### Highlights
- Delivered best-ever quarter for sales volume, EBITDA, and net profit despite disruptions in UAE operations due to Middle East issues, domestic steel shortages, and container trade disruptions for exports.
- Focused on core strengths: brand APL Apollo and increasing share of value-added products beyond construction and renewable sectors (entering Defence and Aerospace with a specialized tube mill producing 250x250 mm sizes with 16 mm thickness; sample approvals expected in 2-3 months).
- Stopped non-profitable/low-margin business segments due to steel shortages and Dubai issues, which significantly improved profit per tonne without sacrificing absolute profit.
- Light structures contributed 25% to the product mix in Q2.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is on track to achieve 20% growth on an absolute EBITDA basis by March 2027 fiscal, targeting Rs. 2,200 crores absolute EBITDA for FY27 (and crossing Rs. 2,500 crores in FY28).
- Maintained 15% volume growth guidance for profitable businesses (APL Apollo brand and roofing products); declined to commit on overall volume growth due to Dubai and domestic steel supply uncertainties.
- Expects double-digit volume growth momentum to continue in H2.
- Targeting 7 million tonnes of greenfield plus brownfield capacity, plus 1 million tonnes of debottlenecking capacity in the next two years.
- Dubai capacity expansion to 0.5 million tonnes by March 2027; management states that once geopolitical and container freight issues stabilize, a potential Rs. 3,000 crores absolute EBITDA is achievable for FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic housing demand remains robust, offset by a shortage of steel due to shutdowns at steel plants.
- Enquiries and order book demand remain very high, though some customers delay quotations due to higher steel prices.
- Monsoon deficit (13%) is not expected to have any material negative impact on housing demand (maybe a 2-4% minor swing at worst).
- Black pipe prices in the channel are around Rs. 70,000 against HR coil at around Rs. 60,000.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross profit per tonne contracted slightly (by around Rs. 500 per tonne QoQ) due to an increased share of light structures (25% mix) which have lower NSR.
- EBITDA per tonne of Rs. 6,000 is deemed sustainable by management due to structural shifts away from low-margin business, roofing expansion, and cost management.
- Electricity cost reduced to Rs. 6.2-6.3/unit; freight cost cut by Rs. 150/tonne in Q2.
- Inventory is low (1.5 to 2 lakh tonnes total, with OEM/dealer order books covering 75,000 to 1 lakh tonnes, leaving 50,000 to 60,000 tonnes in spare).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Spent Rs. 400 crores in H1 FY27 on capex (compared to Rs. 600 crores in the full year FY26); H2 capex outflow expected to be low.
- Target to achieve 7 million tonnes greenfield/brownfield capacity + 1 million tonne debottlenecking in the next 2 years.
- Dubai brownfield expansion adding two new mills to reach 0.5 million tonnes by March 2027.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Zero working capital days.
- Total cash and financial assets (including FDs) stand around Rs. 2,500 crores; net cash is Rs. 1,600 crores.
- Free cash flow generated in H1 was Rs. 700 crores (minus Rs. 400 crores capex = Rs. 200 crores net FCF after dividend outflows); expected to surpass Rs. 1,300 crores for full-year FY27.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- UAE operations disrupted by ongoing Middle East geopolitical situation (imports/exports halted).
- High container freight prices impacting Indian export sales.
- Shortage of steel in domestic markets due to steel plant shutdowns.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Darshan (Dolat Capital) on volume growth guidance revision:** Management clarified that while the overall consolidated volume guidance is kept open due to Dubai/steel issues, the profitable APL Apollo brand and roofing segments maintain a 15% growth guidance.
2. **Sailesh Raja on roofing sheet drivers and capacity:** Management stated capacity is 700,000 tonnes (175,000 tonnes quarterly capability), with 50% going to housing, 25% commercial/industrial, and 25% other industrial applications (including solar and PUFF panels).
3. **Akshay Chheda on gross profit per ton contraction QoQ:** Management explained that realization and GP per ton are lower on light structures, which formed 25% of the mix in Q2.
4. **Sneha on EBITDA per ton sustainability:** Management reiterated that Rs. 6,000/tonne is sustainable and Q3 will be even better, backed by structural shifts away from non-profitable segments and cost efficiency rather than transient inventory gains.
5. **Shaleen Kumar on FY28 EBITDA potential:** Management concurred that if Dubai normalizes alongside container freight stabilization and steel availability, an absolute EBITDA of Rs. 3,000 crores is attainable.

### One-line takeaway
APL Apollo delivered its highest-ever quarterly sales volume, EBITDA, and net profit in Q2 FY27 by pivoting away from low-margin segments toward branded value-added products, offsetting persistent disruptions in its UAE operations and domestic steel supply.
