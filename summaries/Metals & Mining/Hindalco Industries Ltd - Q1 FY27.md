# Hindalco Industries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-13 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Exceptional performance with record EBITDA and margins across upstream/downstream aluminium and copper, alongside strong Novelis recovery.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated revenue/financials not stated as single consolidated revenue figure in transcript, but consolidated segment EBITDA was INR13,481 crore (up 58% YoY).
- Consolidated PAT: INR7,013 crore (up 75% YoY).
- India Business Segment EBITDA: INR8,606 crore (up 73% YoY); PAT: INR5,301 crore (up 86% YoY).
- Upstream Aluminium shipments: up 3% YoY; revenues up 44% YoY; EBITDA: INR7,390 crore (up 81% YoY); EBITDA per tonne: USD2,331; EBITDA margin: 55%.
- Indian Downstream Aluminium shipments: 104 kt (up 3% YoY); EBITDA: INR298 crore (up 30% YoY); EBITDA per tonne: over USD303.
- Copper metal shipments: 105 kt (down 16% YoY); CCR volumes: 96 kt (down 8% YoY); Copper EBITDA: INR918 crore (up 36% YoY).
- Novelis shipments: 916 kt (down 5% YoY); Adjusted EBITDA: $516 million (up 24% YoY) or $563 per tonne (up 30% YoY); run-rate cost savings: $225 million.
- Consolidated net debt-to-EBITDA: 1.95x.
- Renewable energy capacity: 470 MW (plus 35 MW pump storage tie-ups); targeted to reach 884 MW solar/wind/hydel and 125 MW RE RTC by end of FY27.
- Aluminium specific GHG footprint: 19 tonnes of CO2 per tonne of aluminium.
- Hedging: FY27 aluminium hedged at 29% at USD3,004/tonne; currency at 18% at INR91.63/USD. FY28 hedged at 21% at USD3,160/tonne.

### Highlights
- Zero fatalities across Indian operations with LTIFR improving to 0.21.
- 80% of total waste generated was recycled (142% bauxite residue recycling excluding Utkal, 95% ash recycling, 127% copper slag recycling).
- Cumulative tree plantation crossed 6.3 million trees.
- Novelis restarted the Oswego mill in June, ramping up steadily towards normal operating levels.
- Commissioned Aditya battery foil, Taloja AC Fins facilities, and advanced specialty alumina facility.
- Shifted to the new Indian tax regime, permanently lowering the effective tax rate to 26%.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains long-term Novelis EBITDA per tonne guidance of $600.
- Novelis 3-year cost reduction goal: permanently reduce cost structure by $350 million to $400 million by FY28 exit.
- Net leverage at Novelis expected to come down below 4x by year-end; consolidated net debt-to-EBITDA expected to remain around 2x.
- India cash costs expected to increase by another 5% to 6% Q2 to Q1 due to higher seasonal northern coal prices during monsoon.
- Downstream aluminium sustainable long-term EBITDA per tonne guided around over $300 (though near-term guided around $250).
- Bay Minette 600 kt greenfield rolling and recycling facility on track for completion this year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global GDP growth projected by IMF at 3% in 2026; Indian RBI projects FY27 GDP growth at 6.7%.
- Global aluminium market expected to face a deficit of 1 million tonnes in CY26.
- India aluminium demand in Q1 FY27 estimated at 1.5 million tonnes (up 3% YoY), driven by auto (GST 2.0 benefits), packaging, industrial machinery, and consumer durables; electrical segment saw temporary contractor-led deferrals.
- Indian refined copper demand declined 9% YoY in Q1 FY27 to 359 kt due to inventory optimization and cautious purchasing.
- Global copper concentrate supply tight with TC/RCs staying at historic/negative lows (-26 cents to -30 cents per pound).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- India cash costs rose 5% Q1 vs Q4.
- Novelis faced $70 million in Section 232 tariffs this quarter due to higher import reliance resulting from Oswego fire reconfigurations.
- Global alumina prices around $330-$340/tonne.
- Sulfuric acid prices remain elevated.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- High capex year for the India business.
- Upstream expansion projects (Aditya Alumina refinery and smelter expansions) and captive coal mines (Chakla, Bandha, Meenakshi) progressing on track (Chakla expected to yield ~1 million tonnes and Bandha ~0.5 million tonnes in FY28).
- Bay Minette 600 kt project on track for completion this year.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated net debt-to-EBITDA at 1.95x (below the 2x threshold).
- Novelis expects $300 million to $400 million in working capital release over the next couple of quarters.
- Cumulative cash flow impact from Oswego fire stands at $1.4 billion net of $300 million recoveries; final net cash loss expected to be approximately $600 million.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions, commodity price volatility, tighter financial conditions, trade fragmentation, and weather-related disruptions.
- Tight copper concentrate availability and depressed TC/RCs.
- Ongoing Section 232 tariff impacts at Novelis expected to persist for the next couple of quarters.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Satyadeep Jain (Ambit Capital)** asked about the new 0.25% brand royalty to Birla Group Holdings. Management stated it transitions family stewardship to structured governance, applies to Hindalco and Novelis (capped at INR225 crore per year for 12 months), and is below materiality thresholds.
- **Pinakin (HSBC)** asked about sequential volume recovery. Management confirmed volumes in Q2 for both copper and downstream aluminium will be higher.
- **Sumangal Nevatia (Kotak Securities)** questioned the sustainability of USD300 downstream margins. Management noted USD250 is more sustainable near-term, while long-term remains over $300.
- **Ritesh Shah (Investec)** asked about Novelis working capital release. Management guided to $300-$400 million over the next couple of quarters.
- **Rahul Gupta (Institutional Equities)** asked about the path to 4x downstream EBITDA by FY30. Management clarified the target encompasses aluminium downstream, specialty alumina, and copper downstream combined.

### One-line takeaway
Hindalco delivered an exceptional quarter with record upstream aluminium and copper EBITDA, supported by strong Novelis operational recovery and structural cost-efficiency programs, despite near-term headwinds from tariffs and tight raw material markets.
