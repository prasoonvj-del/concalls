# Bhagyanagar India Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-07-30 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported a strong quarter with turnover over Rs 700 crores, record EBITDA and PAT margins, and normalization of sourcing from June after early disruptions.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: > Rs 700 crore
- EBITDA margin: 5.43% (up 63% YoY, 10.38% QoQ)
- PAT: Rs 20 crore (up 167% YoY, 9.5% QoQ)
- PAT margin: 2.87% (up 84% YoY, 14% QoQ)
- Sales volumes: 5,278 tonnes (down 6.5% YoY, 9.5% QoQ)
- Value-added product share: Highest ever at 63%
- EBITDA per kg: Rs 72
- ROE: 29%, ROCE: 18.5%
- Export contribution: 18% of sales
- Capex plan: Rs 40 crore over FY27 and FY28
- Projected debt in FY30: Rs 300 to 350 crore (specifically Rs 325 crore per CFO Surendra Bhutoria)
- Long-term target: Rs 5,000 crores of revenue by FY30

### Highlights
- Sourcing was disrupted in April and May due to trade route and material shortages, but management leveraged this to increase margins. June normalized with 2,200 tonnes sold in that month alone.
- Newly launched products (transformer products, tin-coated bus bars, data center products) saw large dispatches. Tin-coated products volume reached ~250 tonnes; data center bus bars reached ~600 tonnes.
- Completed capacity addition of 35,000 metric tonnes, which is now online.
- Corporate restructuring: Formed a new wholly-owned subsidiary, Tieramet Limited, to house the copper business, after which Bhagyanagar Copper will merge into Bhagyanagar India and Tieramet will be demerged outside. NCLT hearing scheduled for August 7, 2026. Bhagyanagar India will retain three land parcels and the windmill project.
- Exported over 100 tonnes of bus bars to North America in Q1.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects 12% to 15% volume growth for the full year FY27.
- Full-year EBITDA margin guidance maintained between 5% and 5.5%.
- Value-added product mix is projected to reach 63%-64% for FY27 and gradually creep up to 68-69% over 3-4 years.
- Export contribution targeted at 12-15% for the year.
- Confident of achieving Rs 5,000 crores in revenue by FY30 regardless of copper prices.
- Raising Rs 52 crores in the first round (funds in bank by August), with a second tranche around March post-demerger.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India projected to consume 2.4 million metric tonnes of copper by 2030; secondary copper demand expected to double in 5 years from 0.7 million to 1.4 million metric tonnes.
- Primary copper refining charges have gone negative globally as ore goes to China due to high sulphuric acid byproduct prices; Indian primary players (Vedanta, Hindalco, Adani) are also buying recycled copper.
- Recycled copper market in India expected to grow at a CAGR of 12-13%.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- 75% of scrap is imported.
- Purchases and sales are hedged daily; ~85% of inventory is hedged on the exchange.
- Value-added margins: Auto and switchgear have double-digit margins; transformer winding and bus bars are in single digits.
- Customs duty was removed on January 1, 2025, aiding margins.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex of Rs 40 crores planned over FY27 and FY28.
- Next phase of 10,000 tonnes capacity expansion (from 35,000 to 45,000 tonnes) projected to become operational between April and June next year (more likely June).
- CTC wire capacity currently ~80 metric tonnes/month, expanding to 150 metric tonnes/month.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- GST input credit reversal case of Rs 17-18 crores (specifically Rs 17 crores deposited) related to FY2022-23 due to retrospective cancellation of a supplier's registration. Management is very confident of winning in the tribunal.
- Projected debt for FY30 is Rs 300 to 350 crore (CFO noted ~Rs 325 crore).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical trade route disruptions and material shortages caused volume drops in April and May.
- GST retrospective supplier registration cancellation led to a Rs 17 crore cash deposit under protest.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Disha C (on volume growth reduction and margins):** Management explained volume guidance was trimmed to 12-15% due to April-May disruptions, but June normalized (2,200 tonnes). Higher Q1 margins resulted from tight market conditions during the shortage.
2. **Naman Parmar (on the Rs 17-18 crore GST impact):** CFO Surendra Bhutoria clarified it relates to a 2022-23 supplier whose registration was retrospectively cancelled. Management has full proof of receipt and payment and is very confident of winning at the tribunal after depositing Rs 17 crores.
3. **Ankit Gupta (on EBITDA per kg vs. percentage margins):** Management stated they look at margins in percentage terms (guided at 5-5.5%) because purchases and sales are LME-linked. If copper prices rise 20%, percentage margins stay at 5%, but EBITDA per kg rises 20%.
4. **Prateek Shrivastava (on green copper certification):** Asked if green copper tags command export premiums. Management replied they qualify as green copper due to solar power use, but see no real marketing traction or premium for the tag yet.
5. **Rishabh Modi (on real estate inquiries):** Asked about inquiries. Management clarified the inquiry was regarding Bhagyanagar Properties Limited (a separate company with no shareholding overlap), which was closed long ago without repercussions; Bhagyanagar India's three industrial land parcels have no issues.
6. **Analyst question via Q&A tab (on MCX margin funding from fundraise):** Management answered that MCX hedging margin requirements depend entirely on copper price movement; if prices double, funds are needed, otherwise no funds are required.

### One-line takeaway
Bhagyanagar India delivered a record Q1 FY27 with robust 5.43% EBITDA margins and Rs 700+ crore revenue driven by market disruptions and a rising share of value-added products, while advancing its Tieramet copper business demerger.
