# Maan Aluminium Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-24 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27 (with specific reference to Q2 FY27)
**Tone:** Mixed - Healthy revenue growth driven by trading, but profitability came under pressure due to input costs, product mix, and establishment costs at uncommissioned facilities.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained the cumulative capex plan of ~Rs 166 crore over the next 3 years; no specific numbers or quantitative guidance provided for future quarters/revenues.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from a cautious, duty-impacted export focus to actively stabilizing and balancing the domestic market alongside exports.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Onboarding costs, trial runs, rejections from customer trials, and pre-commencement expenses at the Dewas facility emerged as key margin detractors. Geopolitical factors causing import/export friction and delaying machinery arrivals were highlighted.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: The Dewas facility is still in its development/readiness phase with commercial operations yet to commence; the extrusion press is delayed due to geopolitical factors and is now expected in 3 to 4 months.

### Key numbers
- **H1 FY27 Revenue from Operations:** Rs 511 crore (up ~27% YoY from Rs 402 crore in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 Other Income:** ~Rs 4 crore.
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 11.5 crore (down from Rs 18.2 crore in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** ~2.2% (down from 4.5% in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 PBT:** Rs 5.6 crore (compared to Rs 11.4 crore in H1 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 4 crore (down from Rs 8.5 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 279 crore (up sequentially from Rs 232 crore in Q1 FY27).
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 4.4 crore (down sequentially from Rs 7.1 crore in Q1 FY27).
- **Q2 FY27 PAT:** Rs 0.9 crore (down sequentially from Rs 3.1 crore in Q1 FY27).
- **H1 Stock-in-Trade (Trading Purchases):** ~Rs 311 crore.
- **Cumulative 3-Year CapEx Plan:** ~Rs 166 crore (indicative).
- **Tied-up CapEx:** ~Rs 40 crore for Dewas extrusion machine + ~Rs 10 crore for fabrication machines (total ~Rs 50 crore to Rs 60 crore already tied up/ordered).
- **Foundry Capacity:** 12,000 tonnes per annum.
- **Extrusion Capacity:** 24,000 tonnes per annum.
- **Active Customers:** More than 300.

### Highlights
- Revenue expanded 27% YoY in H1 FY27, largely supported by the trading and distribution vertical, but margins compressed because turnover growth did not translate proportionately into operating contribution.
- Dewas facility is yet to commence commercial operations and is currently in its development and readiness phase, incurring preparatory expenses without corresponding revenue.
- Strategic transition underway to diversify markets and strengthen the domestic business alongside exports.
- Management stated that out of pending capex, roughly Rs 50 crore to Rs 60 crore is already committed/tied up (including Rs 40 crore for the Dewas extrusion machine, expected to arrive in 3 to 4 months).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects cash generation and visible results from Pithampur and Dewas investments in another 1 to 1.5 or 2 years.
- Management declined to provide specific quantitative guidance on top-line, capacity utilization timelines, or exact future revenue splits due to ongoing machine procurement and stabilization phases.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Aluminium industry remains closely linked to international commodity prices, affecting procurement costs and pricing realizations.
- Export markets include the US, UAE, UK, Qatar, Israel, and Australia.
- Strategic focus includes expanding into domestic markets and onboarding customers in automotive, construction, and solar sectors.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- H1 FY27 EBITDA margin moderated to 2.2% from 4.5% YoY; Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin compressed further as revenue rose to Rs 279 crore while EBITDA dropped to Rs 4.4 crore.
- Margin pressures driven by commodity price volatility, timing of passing on costs, sales mix, fixed costs, and initial customer onboarding costs (trials, sample development, rejections, lower yields).
- Dewas establishment and manpower costs weighed on profitability ahead of commercial operations.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Cumulative 3-year capex plan stands at ~Rs 166 crore, covering the Pithampur facility and new plants under development (dies, general capex, and new facilities).
- Dewas extrusion machine is in production with vendors and expected in 3 to 4 months; other fabrication machines worth ~Rs 10 crore are also on order.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Operating cash flows were impacted by working capital absorption, specifically movements in receivables and current assets.
- Management is prioritizing collection efficiency, closer monitoring of receivables, and disciplined fund deployment.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Profitability decline despite healthy top-line growth.
- Geopolitical factors delaying machinery imports and lengthening gestation periods.
- Initial trial costs, process stabilization losses, and fixed cost overheads at new facilities weighing on margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Jatin Damania asked about the revenue breakdown between trading and manufacturing, and whether suboptimal manufacturing utilization led to losses.** Management explained that margins were pressured by commodity price linkages, product mix, fixed manufacturing overheads, customer onboarding/trial costs, and pre-commencement expenses at Dewas.
2. **Jatin Damania asked why capex spent over the last 30 months (~Rs 88 crore) hasn't converted into revenue, given extrusion capacity expanded to 24,000 tonnes while volumes are around 8,000 tonnes.** Management responded that the global environment and assumptions shifted due to geopolitical factors, and customer onboarding/domestication is a gradual process taking 1 to 2 years to show results.
3. **Jatin Damania questioned the shifting strategy from exports to domestic and the timeline for full capacity operation at Pithampur.** Management declined to give a specific calendar year for full operation, stating that machines are with vendors and visibility will improve post-stabilization, though cash generation from Pithampur/Dewas is expected in about 2 years.
4. **Jatin Damania asked how the remaining Rs 80 crore capex will be funded and what peak debt will be.** Management reiterated that capex is conditional on market demand and project readiness, and disclosed that ~Rs 40 crore for the Dewas extrusion machine and ~Rs 10 crore for fabrication machines are already ordered/tied up.
5. **Prathamesh Shah asked for timelines and revenue/margin expectations for the new facility.** Management stated that results will be visible in 1.5 to 2 years, but refrained from giving numerical guidance.
6. **Prathamesh Shah asked which industries are targeted for value-added products.** Management listed automotive, construction, and solar.

### One-line takeaway
Maan Aluminium delivered a 27% YoY jump in H1 FY27 revenue to Rs 511 crore led by trading, but profitability halved as fixed overheads, customer onboarding costs, and pre-operating expenses at Dewas pressured margins.
