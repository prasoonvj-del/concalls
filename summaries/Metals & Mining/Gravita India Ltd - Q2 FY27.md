# Gravita India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-31 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Resilient performance supported by diversified recycling, improved product mix, operational efficiencies, and higher value-added contributions despite supply chain challenges.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 1,427 crore (Up 38% YoY, consol)
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** Rs 143.37 crore (Up 28% YoY, consol)
- **EBITDA Margin:** >10% (consol)
- **PAT:** Rs 102 crore (Up 6% YoY, consol)
- **PAT Margin:** >7% (consol)
- **H1 FY27 Growth (YoY):** Revenue up 40%, EBITDA up 29%, PAT up 10%
- **Volume:** Total volumes 52,199 tonnes in Q2 FY27 (Up 3% YoY)
- **Lead EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 22,620 (Q2 FY27)
- **Aluminum EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 22,664 (Q2 FY27)
- **Plastic EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 10,334 (Q2 FY27)
- **Copper EBITDA per tonne:** Rs 84,671 (Q2 FY27)
- **Copper Segment Capacity Utilisation:** 50% during H1 FY27
- **RMIL Contribution:** Rs 420 crore revenue with 8.5% EBITDA margin in Q2 FY27
- **Value-Added Products:** Contributed 64% of consolidated revenue in H1 FY27
- **Capex Plan:** Total capex of Rs 1,680 crore through FY29 (Rs 815 crore for existing businesses, balance for new verticals like lithium-ion and copper); approx Rs 600 crore spent till date.
- **Copper Expansion (Mundra):** 59,200 tonnes per annum capacity, estimated investment of ~Rs 64 crore, phased commissioning by March 2029.
- **Copper Expansion (Mandvi):** 29,400 tonnes per annum capacity, estimated investment of ~Rs 160 crore, on track for commissioning within next 12 months.
- **RMIL Rights Issue Investment:** Rs 94.42 crore approved.
- **US Subsidiary Initial Capital:** US$ 50,000.

### Highlights
- Delivered resilient performance in Q2 and H1 FY27 amid geopolitical developments and logistics disruptions impacting scrap movement.
- Board approved incorporating a wholly-owned subsidiary in the US ("Gravita Recyclers USA, Inc.") to build a scrap procurement network.
- RMIL received ICRA stable AA rating for long-term facilities and A1+ for short-term facilities.
- 57th GST Council recommended bringing electrical and electronic waste scrap under the reverse charge mechanism (RCM), expected to enhance domestic procurement.
- Plastics segment continued strong growth momentum.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains confident in delivering on Vision 2030 and sustaining long-term growth.
- Lead volume growth expected to be around 18% to 33% (transcript says "18% to 20% CAGR") over the next three years.
- Copper sustainable EBITDA margin expected around Rs 65 to Rs 70 per kg in the short term, scaling to Rs 80 to Rs 90 per kg over the next 2-3 years after backward integration.
- Capacity expansion target: taking total capacity from ~480,000 tonnes to 800,000 tonnes using the Rs 1,680 crore capex plan through FY29.
- Lithium-ion battery recycling revenue not factored into Vision 2030 targets (upside if it comes).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains strong; the primary constraint in recent quarters was raw material availability due to sudden disruptions.
- Worst is over for scrap sourcing issues; new geographies are being developed, and domestic RCM is improving organized vs unorganized parity.
- EU WSR guidelines: India has written to the EU to remain part of the scrap chain; negotiations are ongoing.
- Import duty on copper scrap was removed by the government to promote domestic processing.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Working capital cycle in lead is around 80 to 90 days.
- Sourcing mix for lead in Q2: 26% Indian battery scrap, 74% imported scrap. Going forward, management expects 40%-50% domestic and 50%-60% overseas sourcing.
- Hedging: Copper and lead are fully hedged; aluminum is not hedged as it is not traded.
- Other income in Q2 was Rs 18 crore (Rs 9.39 crore operational income, Rs 8.61 crore non-operational).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex of Rs 1,680 crore through FY29 remains on track.
- US subsidiary will operate primarily on a lease model for yards; working capital will be deployed as operations scale.
- RMIL capacity utilization expected to improve to 65%-70% in the next 2 to 3 quarters via debottlenecking.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Expansion projects funded through internal accruals.
- Copper imports have lengthened the working capital cycle in copper.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical developments and logistic disruptions impact the availability and movement of recyclable materials.
- Unorganized sector price competition in domestic sourcing (mitigated by RCM).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Amit Dixit (GS) on West Asia crisis impact and lead volumes:** Management stated the worst is over, new procurement geographies are being developed, and domestic RCM is boosting domestic supply.
2. **Shreya Masalia (Ashika) on sustainable copper EBITDA/tonne:** Management guided for Rs 65-70/kg sustainable in the short term, rising to Rs 80-90/kg in 2-3 years post-backward integration.
3. **Vikash Singh (ICICI Securities) on US subsidiary and RMIL ramp-up:** Management clarified the US entity is a sourcing arm to bypass aggregators and control raw material costs. RMIL utilization will take 2-3 quarters to reach 65-70%.
4. **Prolin Nandu (Edelweiss) on sourcing moats and collection depth:** Management explained their yard model eliminates aggregators, yielding 2-3% additional margins.
5. **Bhavya Gandhi (Bajaj Alternate) on BWMR/EPR rules and 7-year window:** Management noted the RCM will fast-track the formalization of the battery recycling sector, and an EPR credit demand surge is expected when the 7-year window closes.

### One-And-Only Takeaway
Gravita delivered resilient Q2 FY27 results (revenue +38% YoY, PAT +6% YoY) despite raw material headwinds, and is mitigating sourcing risks through international yard expansion in the US, domestic RCM tailwinds, and copper integration.
