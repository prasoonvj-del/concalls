# Tata Steel Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Metals & Mining*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong margin resilience and volume growth in India alongside Netherlands operational recovery offset by UK import glut pressures and European regulatory uncertainties.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue (H1 FY27):** Rs 1,26,231 crore
- **Consolidated EBITDA (H1 FY27):** Rs 20,035 crore (16% margin, EBITDA per ton +21% YoY / +Rs 2,160/t)
- **Standalone Q2 EBITDA:** Rs 10,315 crore (+10% QoQ); EBITDA per ton: Rs 17,257
- **Operating Cashflow before Capex (Q2 FY27):** Rs 5,160 crore
- **Capex:** Rs 3,280 crore in Q2 FY27 (H1 FY27 total spend: Rs 6,860 crore)
- **Net Debt:** Rs 91,423 crore; Net Debt/EBITDA: ~2.4x; Total Liquidity: Rs 40,076 crore
- **Netherlands Q2 EBITDA:** €72 million (€56/ton) vs €4 million in Q1 FY27

### Highlights
- Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded by ~90 bps QoQ to 16% despite input cost pressures from West Asia conflicts.
- India deliveries rose 16% QoQ (outpacing production growth of 7% QoQ); Tata Tiscon retail franchise reached ~$1.8 billion in annual revenues.
- Direct Sheet Plant in Netherlands restarted in early August 2026, running at rated capacity with Chromium-6 emissions below prescribed limits.
- UK business impacted by import surges under transitional safeguard arrangements; UK flat steel inventories reached ~865 kt (~60% above normal levels).
- Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) reached 27% for India operations and 14% on a consolidated basis.
- Continuation of previous trend: Amalgamation of NINL with Tata Steel remains on track for completion in FY27, subject to NCLT hearing in late November 2026.

### Guidance & outlook
- **H2 FY27 Performance:** Management expects H2 FY27 performance to be stronger than H1 FY27 across both India and Netherlands, driven by higher volumes and pricing realization.
- **UK Operations:** Management expects UK operations to approach breakeven in Q4 FY27 as excess market inventory gets flushed out.
- **Q3 FY27 Realisations (vs Q2):** India realisations expected to increase by Rs 3,500–3,600/ton; UK higher by £28–30/ton; Netherlands lower by €11/ton due to product mix (despite product-level prices being higher by €30/ton).
- **Cost/Consumption:** Coking coal consumption costs expected to increase by ~$15/ton in Q3 FY27 in India and Netherlands; Netherlands iron ore consumption price to drop by $5/ton.
- **Cost Transformation Target:** Target of ~Rs 7,000 crore cost savings maintained for FY27 (Rs 1,800 crore achieved in H1 FY27); launched a multi-year structural cost transformation program leading up to 2030.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Persistent Chinese steel exports exceeding 10 MT/month (>100 MT expected in CY2026) continue impacting global trade flows.
- Ongoing conflict in West Asia elevated oil and freight rates by ~40%, pushed coking coal from $235/t to $280/t, and increased war-risk insurance premia and transit times (by 10–14 days).
- UK domestic demand in auto and construction sectors expected to decline by 2–4% in CY2026.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Standalone Q2 material costs increased by Rs 4,475 crore (Rs 4,128/ton) QoQ due to higher raw material costs (Rs 2,408 crore) and inventory drawdown (Rs 2,066 crore).
- Imported coking coal consumption cost rose 3% (Rs 250 crore) QoQ due to coal blending strategies.
- Standalone conversion costs improved by Rs 3,317/ton QoQ, driven by operating leverage on higher volumes.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- **NINL Expansion:** 4.8 MTPA expansion entered execution phase, on track for FY2030 commissioning; blast furnace package being ordered. One rebar mill to come onstream ahead of the upstream complex.
- **Downstream Projects:** 0.7 MTPA HRPGL project entered construction phase; doubling of tinplate capacity progressing with civil/structural works.
- **UK EAF:** 3.2 MTPA EAF project execution on track with major enabling works completed and key contracts awarded.
- **Iron Ore Sales:** Target 8–10 MT iron ore sales in FY27 (vs ~2 MT in FY26), ramping up to 14–15 MT in FY28.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt reached Rs 91,423 crore; ~Rs 2,200 crore of the increase was due to lease recognition following TMILL consolidation.
- Paid dividend of ~Rs 5,000 crore during Q2 FY27. 
- Interest coverage ratio remains above 5x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High inventory glut in the UK market due to pre-quota import surges compressing UK delivery volumes (~0.38 MT in Q2).
- External regulatory uncertainties in Netherlands concerning closure timing of Coke & Gas Plants 1 and 2, steel slag off-take disruptions, and Joint Letter of Intent framework (extended by 5 months to March 1, 2027).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Ashish Kejriwal (Nuvama):** Asked for margin and breakeven guidance for Netherlands and UK operations.
   - *Management:* Expects Netherlands EBITDA to improve further in H2 FY27. Expects UK to approach breakeven in Q4 FY27 as import inventories flush out and contract renegotiations take effect.
2. **Rahul Gupta (Morgan Stanley):** Inquired if UK operations will permanently drag group profitability post-FY27.
   - *Management:* Explained UK operates as a downstream extension of Tata Steel India's system; processing substrate made in India yields ~Rs 8,000/t EBITDA in India and ~Rs 5,000–6,000/t in UK, delivering positive system economics (~Rs 13,000–14,000/t overall).
3. **Sumangal Nevatia (Kotak Securities):** Asked for Q3 FY27 realization guidance and iron ore sales trajectory.
   - *Management:* Realisations in Q3 expected +Rs 3,500–3,600/t in India, +£28–30/t in UK, and -€11/t in Netherlands due to mix. Iron ore sales targeted at 8–10 MT in FY27 and 14–15 MT in FY28.
4. **Ritesh Shah (Investec):** Asked about trade tariffs on UK-to-EU exports and UK government support.
   - *Management:* Stated slabs exported from India to UK face no tariffs. Engaged with UK government regarding public procurement, EAF grant, grid connectivity timelines, and trade quotas.
5. **Parthiv Jhonsa (Anand Rathi):** Asked whether net debt can return to sub-1.5x levels and inquired about the BRPL arrangement.
   - *Management:* Sub-1.5x net leverage is not a target as the company prioritises India growth; comfortable maintaining ~2.3x–2.4x while deploying ~Rs 20,000 crore annual capex. BRPL will act as Mine Developer and Operator (MDO) for Joda mines.

### One-line takeaway
Tata Steel is offsetting European trade distortions and regulatory delays by leveraging expanding, high-margin India operations and executing a Rs 7,000 crore cost transformation program.
