# Metals & Mining - Copper, zinc & other non-ferrous: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for non-ferrous metals and recycling companies presented a bifurcated picture. Pure-play primary producers like Hindustan Zinc delivered record profitability driven by surging zinc/silver prices and sharply lower costs. Conversely, secondary recyclers and processors such as Bhagyanagar India (Tieramet), Gravita India, and Pondy Oxides faced acute margin pressures and temporary volume headwinds due to a steep correction in global copper prices, West Asian geopolitical shipping disruptions, and tight scrap availability. Despite near-term logistical and commodity price volatility, managements across the board remain focused on aggressive capacity expansions and downstream value-addition.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Historically, the Indian non-ferrous and recycling sector relied on transactional scrap imports, unorganized domestic aggregation, and basic smelting with thin margins. Over the past 5 years, the model has shifted toward direct international yard sourcing, formalization via reverse charge mechanisms (RCM), backward integration into value-added products (like copper cathodes and fine powders), and green energy integration. [Background]

**Shifts underway**
- **Backward integration into higher-margin copper refining:** Companies are scaling up local cathode and wire production to capture higher margins rather than merely trading scrap. Pondy Oxides is executing a Rs 200 crore investment for a 36,000 MTPA copper cathode facility (Phase 1 commissioning by December 2026) [Pondy Oxides concall Q2 FY27], while Gravita is expanding its Mundra (59,200 MTPA) and Mandvi (29,400 MTPA) copper capacities [Gravita India concall Q2 FY27]. This shift is scaling and aims to lift sustainable copper EBITDA to Rs 80–90/kg [Gravita India concall Q2 FY27].
- **Direct global procurement networks (bypassing aggregators):** To mitigate geopolitical logistics shocks and secure raw materials, recyclers are setting up international footprints. Gravita India approved incorporating a wholly-owned US subsidiary ("Gravita Recyclers USA, Inc.") utilizing a lease model for yards to bypass middlemen and capture an extra 2–3% in margins [Gravita India concall Q2 FY27].
- **Diversification into critical minerals:** Primary producers are aggressively exploring future-facing minerals. Hindustan Zinc is executing exploration programs across three critical mineral blocks (targeting completion by Q3 FY29) and has executed a mining lease for the Gundlupet REE & Yttrium block [Hindustan Zinc concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push**
- The 57th GST Council recommendation to bring electrical and electronic waste scrap under the reverse charge mechanism (RCM) rewards organized players like Gravita India by leveling the playing field against the unorganized sector [Govt: 57th GST Council, Oct 2026].
- The Ministry of Environment's decentralisation of environment clearance for non-coal mining accelerates project execution for primary miners like Hindustan Zinc [Govt: Environment Ministry, Nov 2026].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements agree that long-term structural demand for non-ferrous metals (copper, zinc, silver) remains robust, underpinned by global infrastructure development, urbanization, and energy transition trends. Hindustan Zinc highlights a structural global zinc market deficit of ~180 KT for 2026, alongside a 6th consecutive year of silver deficit. Bhagyanagar notes AI data center bus bar volumes are surging at 20%-25% annually.
- **Divergence:** While Hindustan Zinc enjoys unmitigated primary demand strength and tight global balances, secondary recyclers experienced demand friction. Bhagyanagar lowered its volume guidance due to a temporary "market freeze" caused by a 15%-16% steep copper price correction and US inventory overhang. Pondy Oxides faced lead volume constraints due to West Asian shipping route disruptions, forcing a downward revision to its annual lead volume target.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Hindustan Zinc** reported a massive reduction in its zinc Cost of Production (COP), falling 29% YoY to $703 per ton (ex-royalty), aided by higher operational efficiencies and strong sulfuric acid byproduct realizations, prompting a downward revision in full-year COP guidance to $825–$850/ton.
- **Recyclers (Gravita, Pondy Oxides, Bhagyanagar)** faced cost inflation. Pondy Oxides cited compressed lead segment margins due to higher power, fuel, fluxes, and logistics costs, though variable contract renegotiations starting Jan/Feb aim to pass these on. Gravita maintained stable lead EBITDA per tonne (~Rs 22,620) and strong copper performance, leveraging MCX hedging (fully hedged for copper and lead). Bhagyanagar noted 85% of its copper exposure is hedged on MCX, insulating it somewhat from the sudden 15%-16% price crash.

## Capex & capacity
- **Hindustan Zinc:** Reiterated full-year capex guidance of Rs 5,000 crore, progressing on its 250 KTPA integrated zinc expansion and tailings reprocessing plant.
- **Gravita India:** Advancing its total capex plan of Rs 1,680 crore through FY29 to scale total capacity from ~480,000 tonnes to 800,000 tonnes.
- **Pondy Oxides:** Incurred ~Rs 60 crore out of ~Rs 140 crore for Phase I of its copper cathode facility, targeting trial runs in Q4 FY27.
- **Bhagyanagar India:** Spent Rs 9.45 crore in H1 out of a total Rs 40 crore capex plan focused on scrap processing and byproduct value addition, aiming to expand copper capacity to 45,000 tonnes by Q4 FY28.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Bhagyanagar India:** Lowered volume growth guidance from 12%-15% to 10% YoY and trimmed EBITDA margin guidance to 4.5%-5.0% (from 5%-5.5%). Tone shifted from unmitigated optimism to addressing copper price corrections, US inventory overhang (~1 million tonnes), and Q3 headwinds. Successfully executed its copper business demerger into Tieramet Limited (shares allotted 1:1 on Oct 8, 2026).
- **Gravita India:** Maintained a positive tone. Shifted focus toward international raw material security by approving a US subsidiary ("Gravita Recyclers USA, Inc.").
- **Pondy Oxides:** Lowered its FY27 lead volume expectations (earlier target of ~1.25–1.3 lakh tonnes no longer holds; aiming closer to 75,000 tonnes). Tone shifted from blaming pure supply chain delays to actively mitigating prolonged West Asian shipping route disruptions and high input costs through customer contract renegotiations.
- **Hindustan Zinc:** Upgraded cost efficiency metrics, revising full-year zinc COP guidance significantly lower ($825–$850/ton vs. previous $975–$1,000/ton).

## What we were watching

## What to watch next quarter
- Pace of recovery in secondary lead volumes and success of customer contract renegotiations at Pondy Oxides.
- Q3 earnings impact and inventory normalization at Bhagyanagar (Tieramet) following the steep copper price correction.
- Execution milestones on Gravita’s US subsidiary scrap yard model and Mandvi copper expansion.
- Sustainability of lower zinc production costs and silver byproduct realizations at Hindustan Zinc.

- **Hindustan Zinc Ltd:** Hindustan Zinc posted record financial performance driven by robust volume growth, strong silver contributions, and a 29% drop in zinc cost of production.
