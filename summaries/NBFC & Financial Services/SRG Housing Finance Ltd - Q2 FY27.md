# SRG Housing Finance Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-09-19 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
### Tone: Cautious - Management organized the call to address a negative newspaper article regarding an NHB audit and RFA tagging, while assuring stakeholders of strong fundamentals and liquidity.

### Key numbers
- Loan book / AUM: Rs. 130 crore (as stated by management)
- Active loan accounts: 15,000 (out of 25,000 total accounts)
- Average ticket size: Rs. 12 lakhs
- Monthly collections: Rs. 8 to 10 crore
- Capital Adequacy Ratio: 39.21%
- Profit After Tax (PAT) growth: 25% QoQ (as stated by management) / 33% YoY for FY25-26
- Promoter stake increase: Promoters increased stake by 1%

### Highlights
- Management strongly denied Economic Times newspaper allegations (dated 10th September 2026) claiming Rs. 400 crore was siphoned off or diverted to promoter-related entities.
- Clarified that National Housing Bank (NHB) tagging as a Red Flag Account (RFA) is part of an ongoing routine supervisory audit process that started in July.
- Board passed a resolution to convert the company from a Housing Finance Company (HFC) to a Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) to enable multi-product operations and multi-fold growth.
- Fresh sanctions and disbursements have been temporarily slowed down as a precautionary measure, expecting impact in Q2 numbers.
- Confirmed credit rating agency Acuite has kept the rating under watch with no credit rating downgrade.
- Stated that the management and senior leadership team are stable with zero resignations in the last 3-4 months.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects the NHB audit and red flag closure to be completed soon (within 1-2 weeks / soon).
- Normal business operations and speed are expected to resume in Q3 and Q4.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains intact, and the slowdown in fresh sanctions/disbursements is purely a voluntary, precautionary step by management.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- No specific discussion on costs or margins apart from noting operating costs remain stable during the temporary slowdown.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Not discussed.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Maintained a liquidity buffer of 2 to 3 months of repayments.
- Submitted liquidity flow statements to rating agencies until March 2027.
- Zero history of delayed or defaulted installments to any lender, bank, financial institution, or NHB.
- Zero promoter-related or related-party loan accounts.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Negative media reports alleging fictitious accounts, NPA manipulation, and siphoning of Rs. 400 crore.
- Temporary slowdown in disbursements impacting business momentum.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Jinal Sheth (Awriga Capital) asked why the account was red-flagged.** Management replied it is an internal regulatory audit process and reasons/classifications will be known once the audit is officially completed.
2. **Harsh Chaurasia (Individual Investor) asked if a third-party re-inspection could be done post-audit to restore reputation.** Management stated they are open to getting it done for personal satisfaction after the NHB audit concludes.
3. **Rahul Jain (Individual Investor) inquired about cash buffers and liquidity.** Management stated they maintain a liquidity buffer of 2 to 3 months of debt obligations and collections remain strong at Rs. 8-10 crore monthly.
4. **Prashant Talpade asked about the rationale for converting from an HFC to an NBFC.** Management explained that an NBFC license allows multi-product offerings and multiple avenues for growth.
5. **Ravi Purohit suggested sharing regular monthly collection data to build trust, similar to MFI practices.** Management acknowledged the suggestion and noted they are already sharing details with rating agencies.
6. **Nihar Jena asked about contingency plans if the audit resolution extends beyond 3 months.** Management reiterated 100% confidence that the audit will close in 1-2 months and things will normalize.

### One-line takeaway
Management strongly refuted media allegations of fund siphoning and NPA manipulation, attributing the Red Flag Account tag to a routine ongoing NHB audit while assuring adequate liquidity and normal debt repayments.
