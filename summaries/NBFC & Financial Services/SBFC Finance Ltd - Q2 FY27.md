# SBFC Finance Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-30 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered consistent sequential AUM and profit growth, achieving 15% ROE alongside stable asset quality and improving operating efficiency.

### Key numbers
- **AUM:** INR 12,592 crore, up 27% YoY and 6% QoQ
- **MSME AUM:** INR 9,669 crore, up 18% YoY and 4.3% QoQ (MSME disbursements grew 6% QoQ to INR 855 crore)
- **Gold Loan AUM:** INR 2,906 crore
- **PAT:** INR 139 crore, up 27% YoY and 7% QoQ
- **ROA:** 4.58% (or 4.6%)
- **ROE:** 15.04%
- **Net Interest Yields:** 18% (up 10 bps)
- **Cost of Funds:** 8.41% (down 55 bps YoY, 1 bp QoQ)
- **Spreads:** 9.63% (up from 9.4% last quarter)
- **Opex ratio:** 4.07% (down 22 bps QoQ)
- **Credit Cost:** 1.37% (down from 1.44% last quarter)
- **GNPA:** 2.64% (down 13 bps YoY, 2 bps QoQ)
- **PCR:** 42.24%
- **Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR):** 31% with debt-to-equity at 2.31x
- **Liquidity:** INR 1,782 crore closing liquidity with LCR at 194%

### Highlights
- Reached 15% ROE on a low leverage of 2.3x while maintaining conservative provisioning (balance sheet provisioning at 2.0% of loan assets).
- Co-origination disbursements rebounded to INR 112 crore from INR 78 crores last quarter.
- Branch count stood at 257 as of September 2026 (added 1 branch during the quarter, 6 in the half-year).
- Digital adoption: 100% digital KYC in September, 87% e-signed disbursements, 54% account aggregator usage, and 73% AI-assisted personal discussions. AI voice deployed for pre-due collections handling over 4 lakh calls.
- Ratings upgraded from stable to positive by CARE and India Ratings.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guides for a 5% to 7% growth on a quarterly basis for AUM and disbursements.
- Opex reduction guided at 25 bps for FY27.
- Management expects credit costs to have peaked at last quarter's levels and stabilize around current rates unless impacted by El Nino.
- Capital is sufficient to scale AUM up to INR 20,000 crore before needing further capital raises.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Private and public sector banks are moving towards higher-ticket prime borrowers (single-digit pricing), while the micro MSME category (sub-INR 7 lakh) has seen delinquencies touching over 4% for the industry, creating headroom in the INR 10 lakh to INR 25 lakh and INR 25+ lakh segments.
- Gold loans have held up well with each branch adding approximately INR 40 lakh a month in net growth (INR 1.2 crore per branch per quarter), though competitive intensity is rising with large players opening hundreds of branches.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Yields held at 18% (above the guided range of 17.5%).
- Spreads at 9.63% are currently above the guided range of 9% to 9.25%, though management warned not to pencil 9.6% as the new norm due to rising gold competition.
- Following a 25 bps repo rate increase, management increased PLR by 25 basis points in October to pass on borrowing cost increases and protect spreads.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Branch count reached 257 (236 branches conduct gold loans). Upfront branch expansion was done in H2 FY26.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Carried excess liquidity with average daily borrowings moving toward INR 2,000 crore in Q2; closing liquidity at INR 1,782 crore.
- Borrowing mix: ~50% linked to MCLR, ~20% linked to repo rate, remainder in T-Bills, external benchmarks, and fixed rate.
- Sold a lower-ticket vintage LAP portfolio via ARC during the quarter (no gold loans sold).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Stage 2 bucket increases in gold loans are driven by customer behavioral transition from bullet payments to monthly interest payments post-RBI circulars.
- Potential risks from El Nino and state-level loan waivers being announced due to drought, though no immediate portfolio impact is currently visible.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Ankit Kanodia (on Gold Loan Granularity & Industry Cautiousness):** Management stated they are guarded against high-volume, high-value gold lending and maintain a strict no-tolerance LTV monitoring and auction process.
2. **Prithviraj Patil (on Asset Quality / Slippages & ARC sales):** Management clarified that the uptick in Stage 2 slippages is entirely driven by gold loans due to the shift to monthly interest payments, while the ARC sale comprised a vintage LAP portfolio.
3. **Mayank Mistry (on Gold LTV Sensitivity & Auctions):** Management explained they maintain a strict LTV monitoring process with recall triggered at 85%-90%, and are auction-ready within the regulatory 7-to-30 day window upon default/delinquency.
4. **Nischint Chawathe (on MSME Disbursement Pickup & Lending Rates):** Management expects double-digit disbursement growth YoY for the second half of the year, noting that sub-INR 7 lakh loans constitute less than 6% of the AUM as they run down that segment.
5. **Pavan Kumar (on 30-day DPD and Credit Costs):** Management noted that 0+ delinquencies and credit costs have peaked and are stabilizing, noting that higher-yielding segments inherently carry 0+ around 8%-92%.

### One-line takeaway
SBFC Finance delivered a stable Q2 FY27 with 27% YoY AUM growth, 15% ROE, and improved operating efficiency while maintaining conservative provisioning and robust liquidity.
