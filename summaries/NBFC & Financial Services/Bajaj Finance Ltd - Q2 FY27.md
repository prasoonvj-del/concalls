# Bajaj Finance Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-23 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong all-around performance across AUM growth, profitability, asset quality, and customer additions.

### Key numbers
* **AUM:** Rs 5,84,624 crore, up 26.5% YoY (net AUM addition of ~Rs 38,000 crore in Q2).
* **PAT:** Rs 6,367 crore, up 29% YoY (Rs 6,619 crore before macro overlay provision, up 34% YoY).
* **PBT:** Rs 8,537 crore, up 29% YoY (Rs 8,874 crore before macro overlay provision, up 34% YoY).
* **New customer additions:** 4.5 million (4.42 million); total franchise reached ~129 million.
* **New loans booked:** ~13.5 million, up 11% YoY.
* **Return Metrics:** ROA at 4.6%, ROE at 20.6%.
* **Opex-to-total income:** 33%.
* **Asset Quality:** GNPA at 1.06%, NNPA at 0.42% (42 bps), PCR at 60%.
* **Credit Cost:** Loan loss to average AUF at 1.56% (1.32% excluding macro overlay), down from 1.88% YoY.
* **Cost of funds:** 7.31%, down 9 bps QoQ.

### Highlights
* Board approved capital raise up to Rs 11,700 crore via QIP and Rs 5,800 crore via preferential warrants to parent Bajaj Finserv on October 1 to support long-term growth and balance sheet resilience.
* Urban consumer finance grew 43% YoY, rural consumer finance grew 63% YoY, gold loans grew to Rs 24,000+ crore (4.1% of balance sheet), and MSME grew 3% YoY. Captive book wound down to ~Rs 2,400 crore.
* Expanded gold loan distribution by 320 branches in Q2 to 2,022 branches; targeting ~2,800 standalone gold branches by end of FY27.
* Scaled FINAI team to 328 employees; agentic AI bots now handling 75% of DIY customer service requests.
* Bajaj Housing Finance (BHFL) PAT grew 18% YoY with AUM/disbursements growing 25% YoY; monthly Sambhav affordable home loan disbursements crossed Rs 500 crore.

### Guidance & outlook
* Management raised full-year FY27 AUM growth guidance to 26%–27% (from 20%–25% medium-term corridor).
* Management expects to book 60–62 million new loans and add 18–20 million new customers in FY27.
* Opex-to-NTI expected to improve by 25 to 40 bps in FY27.
* Management expects credit costs for FY27 to remain comfortably in a low corridor.
* NIM expected to face marginal headwind/compression beyond the initial 10–15 bps full-year guidance due to ongoing high cost of funds.

### Demand & industry commentary
* Demand across retail lines remains strong; early festival season performance (first 9–10 days) shows solid momentum.
* Rural demand is being calibrated closely due to uneven monsoon/El Nino impact in specific districts (notably parts of Maharashtra).
* Open market two-wheeler financing asset quality shows lower overall asset performance vs consumer loan segments, requiring tighter cross-sell filters.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
* Cost of funds reduced by 9 bps QoQ to 7.31%, aided by interest rate hedging at BFL and BHFL levels.
* NIM compressed by 10–12 bps QoQ in Q2 (down 5 bps YoY in H1). Management expects additional marginal NIM compression if rate hikes continue.
* Fee and non-interest income grew 20% in H1; management comfortable sustaining long-term fee growth of 15%–18%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
* Expanded gold loan branch network to 2,022 branches (+320 branches in Q2), targeting 2,800 branches by FY27 end.
* Facial recognition camera tech deployed in 534 stores and 1,163 service branches.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
* Liquidity buffer remained strong at just below Rs 20,000 crore.
* Additional macroeconomic overlay provision of Rs 337 crore created in Q2 (total macro provision standing at Rs 226 crore and management overlay provision at Rs 512 crore).
* Lowered target leverage corridor to 5.0x–5.5x (from 5.5x–6.0x); current leverage stands at 5.1x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
* Short-tenor gold loan schemes caused a transient Rs 1,047 crore bump in Stage 2 assets (Rs 550 crore already resolved by call date). BFL discontinued offering short-tenor gold loan schemes in August.
* Draft RBI guidelines on revolving credit facilities and insurance distribution commission changes represent potential regulatory headwinds, though management is preparing mitigation models.
* Stress and delinquency risks in multi-unsecured leverage customers (3PL), prompting BFL to cut originations to 3PL customers down toward 3.5%.

### Notable analyst Q&A
* **Why stop short-term gold loans despite easier LTV management?**  
  *Management:* Short-tenor loans (e.g., 3 months) force frequent branch visits for rollovers and accidentally push customers into delinquency and default. BFL prefers 12-month tenure schemes with optional monthly/quarterly interest payments to prevent unwanted auctions.
* **Why raise capital now instead of diluting stake in Bajaj Housing Finance (BHFL)?**  
  *Management:* Raising capital now supports BFL’s doubled balance sheet size over 3 years and lowers leverage corridor (to 5.0x–5.5x). It also gives BHFL time to shift mix toward prime/near-prime prior to mandatory listing dilution within its 3-year deadline.
* **What is the impact if RBI bans revolving loan structures?**  
  *Management:* There would be a transient impact on AUM growth and marginal compression in NIMs/profitability. BFL has already developed new fixed-term loan variants to launch by November 1st.
* **Are you seeing stress in rural consumer/MSME asset quality due to monsoons?**  
  *Management:* MSME GNPA uptick is pure math due to low denominator growth; underlying vintage metrics (3/6/9 MOB) are strong. Pruned origination in drought-affected districts (e.g., Maharashtra) and reduced multi-loan (3PL) customer origination from 14% to 7%, with a target of 3.5%.

### One-line takeaway
Bajaj Finance delivered a standout Q2 FY27 with 26.5% AUM growth and low credit costs, backed by a proactive capital raise to maintain 20%+ ROE resilience.
