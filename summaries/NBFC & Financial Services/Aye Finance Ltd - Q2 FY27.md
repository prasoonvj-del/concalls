# Aye Finance Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-29 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong growth in AUM and PAT, with consistent improvement in asset quality and operational efficiency metrics.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained for all metrics (AUM growth 25-30%, Credit cost 3.5-4%, NIM 14.25-14.75%, Opex 8.25-8.75%).
- **Tone and demand view:** Shifted from "steady recovery" to "constructive demand" and "festive pick-up."
- **New topics or risks:** Drought in specific districts (Maharashtra, Karnataka, MP) is now a concrete operational reality (18% of AUM), whereas it was a forecast in Q1. Gold loan pilot is now a more active evaluation.
- **Last quarter's promises:** Branch expansion (30+ branches) is on track for Q3; management successfully reduced opex ratio and credit costs as planned.

### Key numbers
- **AUM:** Rs 7,597 crore (+26% YoY, +4% QoQ).
- **Disbursements:** Rs 1,403 crore (+10% YoY, +15% QoQ).
- **PAT:** Rs 87 crore (+151% YoY, +16% QoQ).
- **GNPA:** 4.20% (down 30 bps QoQ, 70 bps YoY).
- **Credit Cost:** 3.82% (down 21 bps QoQ, 119 bps YoY).
- **Opex Ratio:** 8.61% (vs 8.93% in Q1).
- **NIM:** 15.6%.
- **Capital Adequacy:** 41%.
- **New Borrowers:** 49,000 added in Q2.

### Highlights
- Seventh consecutive quarter of GNPA decline.
- AUM per employee improved 18% YoY (0.67 crore vs 0.57 crore).
- Cost-to-income ratio reduced to 49%.
- Incremental cost of borrowing declined to 10.04% (vs 10.20% in Q1).
- 95% of the book originated after June 2024.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to meet FY27 guidance: AUM growth (25-30%), Credit cost (3.5-4%), NIM (14.25-14.75%), Opex (8.25-8.75%), ROA (4-4.5%).
- Management expects credit costs to drop further toward 3.5% by year-end.
- Management expects OER to improve to 8% in the next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Grassroot micro-MSME segment showing steady momentum.
- Drought declared in parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, and MP (18% of AUM); management has slowed disbursements in these specific districts.
- Festive season expected to boost H2 disbursements.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Blended cost of borrowing declined 15 bps to 10.63% QoQ.
- Management attributes NIM performance to improved funding mix and rating upgrade to IND A+.
- Management believes they can absorb potential insurance commission impacts via OER reduction, lower borrowing costs, and falling credit costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Branch count: 571 (flat in Q2).
- 30+ new branches to become operational in Q3.
- Management plans to open ~35-40 new branches and split ~40 branches annually.
- Existing infrastructure has capacity for Rs 4,000-4,500 crore additional AUM.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Capital adequacy at 41%; management sees no equity dilution for the next 2 years.
- Borrowing mix: 60% fixed, 40% floating.
- Management is not seeing difficulty in fundraising despite liquidity tightening.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Drought conditions in specific districts (Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka).
- Potential impact of future insurance commission recommendations (management believes they have levers to offset this).
- Slight dip in bucket 1 collection efficiency in UP (attributed to seasonal factors/festivals).

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q: Confidence in 30% AUM growth?** A: Management says it's possible but depends on H2 performance; repeat loans and potential gold loan launch are levers.
- **Q: Why did ECL provisions increase?** A: Management included management overlays to increase transparency; write-offs increased because fully provisioned books were written off.
- **Q: Gold loan strategy?** A: Pilot phase; targeting existing customers in same cities as current branches; not an aggressive push.
- **Q: Impact of interest rate hikes?** A: Management simulated that a 50 bps hike has only a 7 bps impact on FY27 ROA; they have pricing power but don't currently need to hike rates.

### One-line takeaway
Aye Finance delivered a strong quarter with improving efficiency and asset quality, maintaining its full-year guidance while proactively managing localized drought risks.
