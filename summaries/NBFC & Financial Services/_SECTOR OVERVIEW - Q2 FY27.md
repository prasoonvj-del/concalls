# NBFC & Financial Services: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the Indian NBFC and Financial Services sector reveals a landscape shaped by strong underlying domestic retail demand, resilient systematic investment flows, and rapid technology adoption (AI and digital platforms). However, operators are simultaneously navigating margin compression from a tightening interest rate cycle (compounded by recent repo rate hikes), elevated operating costs due to tech/talent investments, and brewing regulatory headwinds—most notably the IRDAI consultation paper proposing changes to insurance commission structures. While asset quality remains broadly stable across most segments, localized stress has emerged in rural/agri pockets due to deficient rainfall and droughts (notably in parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Madhya Pradesh).

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across the board (e.g., **AAVAS**, **Aye Finance**, **Home First**, **PNB Housing**) agree that domestic consumption and primary market activity (IPO buoyancy) remain resilient. Retail credit demand is robust, and the festive season (Dussehra/Diwali) is widely expected to drive H2 volume acceleration.
*   **Divergence:** While asset management companies (**360 ONE**, **HDFC AMC**, **KFin Tech**) are bullish on structural domestic financialization via SIPs, retail brokerages (**5paisa**, **Anand Rathi**, **SMC Global**) reported a "soft quarter" in Q2 with slower trading activity and dropping derivatives volumes following regulatory interventions. Similarly, while microfinance players (**CreditAccess Grameen**, **Satin Creditcare**, **Spandana**) see strong underlying cash flows, they are exercising caution and restricting sourcing in drought-affected districts.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Cost of Funds & Spreads:** The sector is facing upward pressure on borrowing costs following repo rate hikes. Companies like **Bajaj Housing Finance**, **L&T Finance**, and **PNB Housing** are actively managing liability mix (increasing NCDs, ECB, and floating-rate instruments) and passing on rate hikes to protect spreads. **Manba Finance** and **Satin Creditcare** noted relief from recent credit rating upgrades (e.g., Manba upgraded to A-).
*   **Operating Expenses & Tech Investments:** Elevated opex remains a common theme as companies invest heavily in technology, digital apps, and AI platforms (e.g., **5paisa**, **Angel One**, **L&T Finance**, **NSDL**). Several AMCs (**Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC**, **HDFC AMC**, **Nippon India AMC**) noted higher employee and ESOP costs impacting near-term profitability.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance Raises & Cuts:** **CreditAccess Grameen** raised its full-year profitability (ROA/ROE) and NIM guidance. **OnEMI Technology Solutions (Kissht)** raised its AUM growth guidance from "40% plus" to "60% plus." Conversely, **SMC Global** lowered its NBFC AUM growth target due to a faster-than-planned rundown of legacy loans.
*   **Tone Shifts:** Sentiment across several broking and retail platforms (**5paisa**, **Geojit**, **SMC Global**) shifted from optimistic to cautious due to market volatility and regulatory-induced volume moderation. Conversely, affordable housing and microfinance players (**Aye Finance**, **Spandana**) turned more constructive as monsoon clarity and festive demand emerged.
*   **New Regulatory Overhangs:** The proposed IRDAI consultation paper on insurance commission rationalization emerged as a sector-wide talking point, with players like **PB Fintech**, **Home First**, **Bajaj Housing**, and **L&T Finance** evaluating potential impacts on fee income.
