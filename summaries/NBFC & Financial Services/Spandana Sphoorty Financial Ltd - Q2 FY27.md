# Spandana Sphoorty Financial Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported a strong quarter with AUM crossing INR5,000 crores, improved asset quality, and expanding margins, despite minor weather-related watch-outs in Maharashtra.

### Key numbers
- AUM: INR5,364 crores (10% QoQ growth)
- Subscribers added: 1.5 lakh (10% QoQ increase)
- New to Spandana / new to credit customer share: ~65%
- Monthly opex: ~INR50-odd crores
- Yield: 24.9% (up from 24.6% in Q1)
- Marginal cost of borrowing: 10.6% (including CGS; 10.8% ex-CGS)
- Borrowing mix from banks: 57%
- Debt raised during the quarter: INR1,427 crores (Total debt raised over period: INR7,485 crores; closing liquidity: INR1,379 crores as of September 30)
- Funds under CGS scheme garnered and utilized: INR995 crores
- GNPA: 2.54% (or 2.5%); NNPA: 0.5%
- Stand-alone PAT: INR35 crores (Consolidated PAT: INR18 crores, after a one-time INR7.9 crores DTA reversal for the Criss Finance merger)
- X-bucket collection efficiency: 99.6%
- 31-90 delinquent bucket: 0.55%
- Disbursements in Q2 FY27: INR1,481 crores (54% to new customers, 11% to reactivated dropouts, 35% to existing guardrail-meeting customers)
- NIM: 13.6% (compared to 12.5% in Q1)
- PPOP: INR52 crores (grew QoQ from INR16 crores in Q1; doubled to INR79 crores including write-backs)
- Borrowers per loan officer: 211; Average MFI outstanding: INR45,000

### Highlights
- Completed the partly paid rights issue, infusing INR400 crores (97% participation rate in the second tranche of INR194 crores).
- New Loan Origination System (LOS) piloted; full branch migration expected over the next couple of months.
- Criss Finance Limited (CFL) merger initiated; awaiting regulatory nod in 3-4 months.
- Strengthening leadership with a leaner regional structure to reduce layers and improve supervision.
- Identified Maharashtra and Karnataka as states with potential El Nino/drought stress; Maharashtra branches (127 total) bifurcated, with 18 critical branches placed under stringent sourcing norms (staying away from new-to-credit customers, stricter norms for new-to-Spandana, and mandatory timely repayment for repeat loans). Total combined book in these two states is ~INR600 crores (96% current, 3% GNPA).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to reach INR10,000 crores AUM mark by March '28.
- Long-term (BAU from FY27 onwards) ROA target: 3.5% to 4%; ROE target: 14% to 15% (with ~4x gearing).
- FY27 guidance of INR6,000-6,500 crores disbursements, INR6,000+ crores AUM, and INR675 crores opex maintained as of end-October.
- Next leg of capital raising anticipated around the middle of next year if plans play out.

### Demand & industry commentary
- MFI customers are multi-talented and multi-skilled with multiple income sources, not purely dependent on single-line agri-farming or daily wages.
- Natural calamities historically do not cause structural microfinance breakdowns; resilient customers typically bounce back within 3 to 4 months.
- Growth in the MFI sector is calibrated, expected at 10%-15% to 20%-25%.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Marginal cost of borrowing reduced by 260 bps since Q3 FY26 to 10.6%.
- Yield improved to 24.9% driven by new portfolio origination under strengthened underwriting.
- NIM expanded sequentially to 13.6%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- CFL micro-LAP product strategy revamped: shifting focus from INR1-5 lakh segment to INR5-15/20 lakh segment.
- LAP target: grow book from INR305 crores to INR1,000 crores over 2-3 years across 6 existing states.
- LAP branch count being optimized from 86 to around 70-75 exclusive branches.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Strong liquidity of INR1,379 crores maintained.
- Technical write-off policy set at 455 days (90 DPD + 365 days).
- Post-FY26 book GNPA stands low at 0.5%.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Potential stress in agricultural pockets of Maharashtra and Karnataka due to deficient rainfall/drought conditions.
- Stricter monitoring and restricted onboarding implemented in 18 critical branches in Maharashtra.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Meet Sangoi (PL Capital)** asked about October collections and drought/flood impact. Management stated collections and business are steady, with targeted efforts limited to specific Maharashtra pockets.
- **Shreepal Doshi (Equirus)** asked about agri-income dependency and LAP expansion. Management clarified that MFI borrowers have diversified rural incomes, and LAP is scaling up in the INR5-20 lakh ticket size across 6 states with a 60-65% LTV and 19-22% pricing.
- **Divyansh Gupta (Latent PMS)** inquired about credit cost guidance and GNPA movements. Management explained that gross GNPA hovers around 2.5%-3%, and the reduction was partly aided by an ARC sale in the subsidiary; post-FY26 book GNPA is 0.5%.
- **Ashwini Agarwal (Demeter Advisors)** asked about FY29 ROA/ROE and capital raising timeline. Management targeted a 3.5%-4% ROA, 14-15% ROE, INR10,000 crores AUM by March '28, and potential capital raising by mid-next year.
- **Abhijit Tibrewal (Motilal Oswal)** questioned whether drought could trigger credit costs. Management responded that customer intent to repay remains high and historical natural calamities have not structurally distorted the MFI industry.

### One-line takeaway
Spandana reported a steady sequential turnaround with AUM crossing INR5,000 crores, lower borrowing costs, and rising NIMs, while proactively ring-fencing operations against isolated weather stress in Maharashtra.
