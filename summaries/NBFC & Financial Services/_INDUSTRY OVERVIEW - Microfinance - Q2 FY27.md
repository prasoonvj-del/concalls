# NBFC & Financial Services - Microfinance: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the microfinance sector reported a strong Q2 FY27, characterized by record profits, robust AUM growth, and improving cost efficiencies, even as they navigated localized weather-related stress in certain agricultural pockets. Consolidated AUM growth remained healthy across players: Arman Financial crossed the Rs 3,000 crore milestone with AUM at Rs 3,209 crore (+51% YoY); CreditAccess Grameen (CA Grameen) reached Rs 30,946 crore (+19.5% YoY); Muthoot Microfin stood at Rs 15,323 crore (+22% YoY); Satin Creditcare reached Rs 16,589 crore (+31% YoY); and Spandana Sphoorty crossed Rs 5,000 crore, standing at Rs 5,364 crore (+10% QoQ). Profitability surged universally, supported by declining borrowing costs and expanding net interest margins (NIMs), though several players proactively built provisions and management buffers to insulate against seasonal and regional risks.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - The Indian microfinance sector has traditionally relied on Joint Liability Groups (JLG) with frequent cash or group-collection models, channeling institutional bank liquidity to underbanked rural borrowers. Over the past five years, the business model has undergone structural shifts toward digital collections (UPI/e-NACH), granular branch rationalization, and conscious diversification into non-JLG and secured retail lending asset classes [Background].

**Shifts underway** -
*   **Diversification into non-MFI and secured retail portfolios:** Scaling up individual loans, mortgages, and micro-LAP to reduce group lending concentration risk. Muthoot Microfin grew its non-JLG portfolio to 31% of AUM; Satin expanded its non-MFI portfolio to 20% of consolidated AUM; CA Grameen expanded retail finance to 23.3% of total AUM. This matters for earnings stability and lowering credit volatility [Muthoot Microfin concall Q2 FY27; Satin Creditcare concall Q2 FY27; CreditAccess Grameen concall Q2 FY27].
*   **Adoption of digital collections:** Scaling up digital repayment mechanisms (UPI and e-NACH) to lower operational friction and collection costs. Arman reported 49% of microfinance collections via digital channels; Muthoot reached 47%; CA Grameen reached 26.3%; Satin reached 25% organically. This matters for driving operating leverage and reducing cost-to-income ratios [Arman Financial concall Q2 FY27; CreditAccess Grameen concall Q2 FY27; Muthoot Microfin concall Q2 FY27; Satin Creditcare concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push** - 
*   RBI's push for NBFCs to utilize CKYCR for digital customer onboarding rewards streamlined compliance and faster loan processing, benefiting lenders scaling digital origination [Govt: RBI Urged NBFCs to use CKYCR for onboarding, Nov 2026].
*   RBI's mandates on dematerialised securitisation notes impact the securitisation and direct assignment market structure for NBFCs [Govt: RBI Mandates dematerialised securitisation notes and minimum 1 crore investment per investor, Nov 2026].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements across the board note that underlying rural cash flows and customer intent to repay remain resilient. Industry growth is projected at a calibrated 10%–25%. Furthermore, major players like Arman and CA Grameen highlighted that traditional banks have significantly pulled back on direct microfinance, Business Correspondent (BC), and co-lending models, allowing well-capitalized NBFC-MFIs to capture market share.
*   **Divergence (Weather and Regional Stress):** Views diverge slightly on the materiality of isolated weather disruptions. CA Grameen acknowledged temporary stress due to drought in 6 districts of Central Maharashtra and floods in UP/Bihar/MP, prompting them to pause new sourcing in affected Maharashtra districts. Spandana identified weather watch-outs in Maharashtra and Karnataka, placing 18 critical branches under strict sourcing norms. Conversely, Muthoot Microfin (3.6% drought exposure, 81% covered by NATCAT insurance) and Satin Creditcare (negligible 1% exposure to Maharashtra/Karnataka and effective recovery in Assam floods) reported minimal structural impact on asset quality. Arman noted minor fresh delinquency buckets in October across Maharashtra and Karnataka (7.6% of AUM) due to drought conditions.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
Borrowing costs improved universally across the sector. Arman’s incremental borrowing cost eased to 11.3% (from 11.7% in Q1). CA Grameen’s marginal cost of borrowing stood at 9.1% with blended costs expected to stay in the 9.2%–9.3% range. Muthoot Microfin brought its cost of borrowing down to 9.93% (entering single digits for the first time), with incremental costs at 9.69%. Satin’s marginal cost of borrowing dropped 65 bps YoY to 10.28%. Spandana reduced its marginal cost of borrowing by 260 bps since Q3 FY26 to 10.6%. NIMs remained robust, ranging from Muthoot's 12.5% and Satin's ~14.5% to CA Grameen's 15.1% and Arman's 18.2%, providing healthy buffers to absorb potential credit cost spikes.

## Capex & capacity
Expansion strategies focused heavily on network optimization rather than aggressive unbridled branch addition. CA Grameen added 60 branches (total 2,326). Muthoot Microfin rationalized 80 underperforming branches while maintaining a network of 1,675 branches and expanding into new geographies like J&K, Assam, and Andhra Pradesh. Satin added branches via subsidiaries, bringing its total to 2,075 branches. Spandana optimized its micro-LAP branch count from 86 to 70–75 exclusive branches while scaling up across 6 states.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **CreditAccess Grameen:** Significantly upgraded its FY27 guidance, raising ROA expectations to 5.2%–5.8% (from 4%–4.8%), ROE to 21%–24% (from 16%–20%), and NIM guidance to 14.4%–14.7% (from 12.8%–13.2%). Its tone also shifted from reporting "no visible impact" in Q1 to explicitly acknowledging temporary regional stress in Maharashtra and central flood-hit zones.

## What to watch next quarter
*   The progression of collection efficiencies and asset quality in drought-affected pockets of Maharashtra and Karnataka (monitored closely by CA Grameen, Spandana, Arman, and Muthoot).
*   The renewal status of the CGFMU credit guarantee scheme for Arman Financial and its subsequent P&L impact.
*   Progress on CA Grameen promoter discussions regarding a strategic investor at the HoldCo level.
*   Credit cost trajectories, specifically CA Grameen’s expectation of credit costs inching up and Satin’s continued building of management overlay buffers.
