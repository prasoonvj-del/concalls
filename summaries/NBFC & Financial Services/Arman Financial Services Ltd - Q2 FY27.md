# Arman Financial Services Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-13 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported record quarterly profits and strong AUM growth, citing successful internal operational restructuring and improving operating leverage.

### Key numbers
*   **Consolidated AUM:** Rs 3,209 crore (up 51% YoY).
*   **Disbursements:** Rs 818 crore (up 72% YoY, 19% QoQ).
*   **Consolidated PAT:** Rs 51 crore (vs Rs 8 crore in Q2 FY26).
*   **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 97 crore (vs loss of Rs 7 crore in H1 FY26).
*   **Gross Total Income:** Rs 235 crore (up 48% YoY).
*   **Consolidated NIM:** 18.2%.
*   **GNPA:** 2.73% (vs 2.76% in Q1); **NNPA:** 0.92%.
*   **Collection Efficiency:** 96.5%.
*   **Cost-to-Income Ratio:** 42.6% (down from 51.7% in Q4 FY26).

### Highlights
*   Consolidated AUM crossed the Rs 3,000 crore milestone for the first time.
*   Management successfully separated credit and recovery functions from branch operations, which they claim has resulted in 15% lower default rates for loans originated under the new structure.
*   Digital collections (UPI/e-NACH) now account for 49% of microfinance collections.
*   The company continues to see the benefits of operational changes initiated in H2 FY26, marking four consecutive quarters of improvement.
*   *Note:* The call confirms the strong growth trajectory noted in our previous records, with the company surpassing its entire FY26 PAT within the first six months of FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management targets reducing operating expenses to ~7% of AUM by March 2027 (currently ~8%).
*   Management believes an AUM of Rs 5,000 crore is achievable in approximately 18 months.
*   Management remains positive for H2 FY27, noting that Q4 is historically the strongest quarter.
*   Management stated there is no immediate need to raise capital.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   Competitive intensity remains, though management noted that some banks are pulling back on direct microfinance/BC/co-lending models.
*   Management is monitoring drought conditions in Maharashtra and Karnataka (which account for 7.6% of consolidated AUM); while impact is not material yet, they observed minor deterioration in fresh delinquency buckets in October.
*   Management emphasized that they are not chasing growth at the expense of underwriting standards; rejection rates remain high.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Incremental borrowing cost improved to 11.3% (from 11.7% in Q1).
*   Operating leverage is improving as operating expenses are no longer growing at the same pace as AUM.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Capital Adequacy:** 36.26% (Standalone), 37.19% (Namra).
*   **Liquidity:** Rs 334 crore (cash, bank balances, liquid investments, and undrawn CC limits) plus Rs 150 crore in undrawn sanctions.
*   **Total Borrowings:** Rs 2,659 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **CGFMU Renewal:** The scheme renewal is pending; 51% of MFI and 30% of MSME portfolios are currently covered. Management noted that if the scheme is not renewed, they will continue operations as they did for 34 years prior to the scheme.
*   **Provisioning:** Provisions increased this quarter due to the lack of CGFMU coverage; management noted that if the scheme is renewed retrospectively, it could be P&L neutral.
*   **UPI MDR:** Potential impact of UPI MDR costs is being monitored; management is pushing for industry-level exemptions.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   *Ronak Chheda (Awriga Capital) asked about competitive intensity.* Management stated that while competition exists, they are gaining market share, and banks are selectively pulling back from the sector.
*   *Rudraksh Raheja asked about the dip in collection efficiency and PAR 30-90 spike.* Management attributed this to normal seasonal monsoon impacts and specific regional issues (floods/droughts), stating they are not overly concerned.
*   *Bhumin Shah asked about CGFMU impact on provisioning.* Management explained that provisions rose because the insurance was not available for the first six months; if renewed, they expect potential P&L neutrality.
*   *Akhilesh B. asked about the Rs 5,000 crore AUM target.* Management confirmed it is achievable in ~18 months and reiterated no capital raise is needed.
*   *Rishabh Tripathi asked about "antifragile" strategy.* Management explained the theme refers to their ability to learn and improve processes after every credit cycle (e.g., AP crisis, COVID).

### One-line takeaway
Arman Financial delivered record profitability and strong AUM growth, successfully leveraging internal operational restructuring to improve efficiency while maintaining strict underwriting discipline despite minor regional monsoon-related headwinds.
