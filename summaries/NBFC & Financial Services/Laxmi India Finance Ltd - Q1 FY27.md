# Laxmi India Finance Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-26 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted strong AUM growth, improved borrowing costs, and a clear strategy for expansion despite a specific asset quality issue.

### Key numbers
*   **AUM:** ₹1,721 crore (up 27% YoY).
*   **Disbursements:** ₹230 crore (up 38% YoY).
*   **PAT:** ₹16.43 crore (up 70% YoY).
*   **NIM:** 11.36%.
*   **Gross NPA:** 2.08% (0.83% excluding Upmoney exposure).
*   **Return on Equity (ROE):** 13.86%.
*   **ROTA:** 3.45%.
*   **Cost of Borrowing:** ~10.48% (down from 11.73% pre-IPO).
*   **Yields:** Secured MSME (32.48%), Vehicle (19.87%), Wholesale (16%), Personal/Business (14–15%).

### Highlights
*   **Business Model:** Primarily secured MSME lending (80–82% of business). Focus on non-income-documentation (NIP) segment in rural/semi-urban areas.
*   **Network:** 196 branches across 6 states (Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, UP, Maharashtra).
*   **Liability Franchise:** ~50–53 lenders; 80–82% of borrowing from banks. Weighted tenor of borrowings is ~53 months.
*   **Technology:** End-to-end digital onboarding and verification; manual process limited to physical document signing (transitioning to electronic soon).
*   **Operational Model:** Hub-and-spoke cluster model (6 branches per cluster).

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Growth:** Management targets ~30% CAGR in AUM.
*   **Expansion:** Plans to open 30–35 new branches in FY27.
*   **Cost of Borrowing:** Management expects a further reduction of 22–25 bps in the current financial year.
*   **ROTA:** Management aims to reach 4% ROTA (currently 3.45%).

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Competition:** Management believes their deeper penetration in Tier 2/3/tehsil locations and "doorstep service" model provides a competitive advantage over public-sector banks and small finance banks.
*   **Product Strategy:** Moving toward digital-only personal loans for salaried professionals; exploring gold loans (currently on hold due to capex requirements).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Branch Economics:** Typical branch breaks even in 7–9 months at an AUM of ₹1.5–2 crore. Monthly operating expenses per branch are ~₹1.5–2 lakh.
*   **Efficiency:** Aiming to improve ROTA by reducing borrowing costs, operating costs, and salary costs through technology and efficiency.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Direct Assignment (DA):** ~₹320 crore gross amount involved in DA transactions to manage ALM and liquidity.
*   **Leverage:** Net leverage is ~2%.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Asset Quality (Upmoney):** An exposure of ~₹18–19 crore is under resolution. Management has made a 70% provision.
*   **DA Risk:** Following the Upmoney incident, the company has restricted DA activities to existing geographies and strengthened due-diligence checkpoints.
*   **Regulatory:** Supply-chain financing plans are on hold due to recent RBI notices on revolving credit.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Can you maintain 30% growth with underwriting discipline?** A: Yes, management cited the small base (₹1,700 crore), maturing branches, and new state entries as drivers.
*   **Q: What is the bounce rate?** A: Bounce rate is ~30%, with a 93–94% closure rate. PAR 30 is ~7%.
*   **Q: Why the Upmoney issue?** A: Management admitted it was a lapse in due diligence; they have since restricted activities to existing geographies and now retain all security documents in-house.
*   **Q: Any co-lending plans?** A: None currently, but it is a future consideration to generate additional income from buyout cases.
*   **Q: What is the scope for further borrowing cost reduction?** A: Management expects 22–25 bps reduction this year, aided by new low-cost bank relationships (e.g., ICICI Bank).

### One-line takeaway
Laxmi India Finance is leveraging a high-yield, secured MSME-focused model with a deep rural branch network to target 30% AUM growth, while actively working to reduce borrowing costs and resolve a legacy asset quality issue.
