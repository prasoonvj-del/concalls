# NBFC & Financial Services - Holding & investment companies: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for NBFCs and financial holding companies reflects solid underlying credit demand and fee growth across diversified platforms, tempered by weather-related claims in general insurance and ongoing regulatory adaptations. Major players like Bajaj Finserv and EAAA India Alternatives delivered robust profitability expansions of 25% and 35% YoY respectively, while capital-raising activities (such as Bajaj Finance's Rs 17,500 crore equity/warrant plan and Technocraft Ventures' August IPO) underscore proactive balance-sheet strengthening. Conversely, general insurers like Cholamandalam MS faced severe profitability headwinds due to NATCAT events, and holding structures like Religare Enterprises navigated restructuring bottlenecks while resuming core lending operations.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Historically, Indian holding companies, NBFCs, and financial service firms relied heavily on balance-sheet-heavy lending, fixed-spread credit intermediation, and traditional distribution models. Over the past five years, the industry has aggressively pivoted toward fee-based asset management, digital-first customer acquisition, alternative investment funds (AIFs), and specialised senior care or tech-enabled platforms, transitioning from pure asset owners to asset managers and ecosystem operators [Background].

**Shifts underway**
- **Scaling Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) & Yield Strategies:** Fee-paying AUM and private credit structures are rapidly scaling as domestic private wealth expands. *Stage:* Scaling. *Companies acting:* EAAA India Alternatives grew fee-paying AUM 28% YoY to Rs 49,164 crore and raised ~Rs 20,000 crore in H1 FY27, targeting PAT yields of 65-70 bps [EAAA India Alternatives Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. *Why it matters:* Generates high-ROE (29.5%) sticky fee income decoupled from traditional balance-sheet credit risks.
- **Diversification into Specialised Consumer & Senior Care Services:** Financial conglomerates are expanding into high-margin secular lifestyle adjacencies. *Stage:* Scaling. *Companies acting:* Max India scaled its Care Homes (485 beds across 8 facilities, ARPOB up to ~Rs 25 lakh annually) and AGEasy (exit ARR ~Rs 180 crore), targeting consolidated EBITDA breakeven [Max India Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. *Why it matters:* Diversifies holding-level revenue streams beyond financial services into high-barrier consumer segments.
- **Technology-Driven Opex Optimization in Lending:** Core lenders are utilizing AI and digital integration to drive operating leverage. *Stage:* Mainstream. *Companies acting:* Bajaj Finance expects to improve its Opex-to-Net Total Income ratio by 25 bps to 40 bps in FY27 via AI implementation [Bajaj Finserv Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. *Why it matters:* Protects operating margins against rising cost-of-funds pressures.

**Government push**
- **RBI Securitisation Mandates:** RBI's recent directives mandating dematerialised securitisation notes (effective April 2027) force structural transparency and digital onboarding across NBFCs [Govt: RBI Securitisation directions, Nov 2026], benefiting well-capitalised players like Bajaj Finance and Religare Finvest.
- **RBI CKYCR Onboarding Push:** RBI's directive urging NBFCs to use CKYCR for customer onboarding streamlines digital compliance and lowers friction [Govt: RBI CKYCR onboarding push, Nov 2023/Nov 2026], aiding platforms scaling retail portfolios like Religare Finvest.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements across retail lending (Bajaj Finance, Religare Finvest) and alternative assets (EAAA India Alternatives) report strong underlying structural demand. Health insurance demand remains robust, with Care Health growing H1 GWP by 35% YoY and Bajaj Health scaling its multi-city footprint.
- **Divergence:** While asset management and alternative fundraising are experiencing multi-year highs, general insurance demand faces headwinds. Cholamandalam MS reported slowing GDPI growth (H1 industry at 8.9%, October growth dropping to ~4%), and Bajaj General Insurance noted industry-wide persistency dips and short-term noise from IRDAI distribution consultation papers. In broking, Religare Enterprises noted subdued market trading volumes due to regulatory shifts.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Margins & Spreads:** Bajaj Life Insurance reported a significant expansion in New Business Margin (NBM), up 450 bps YoY to 21.6% due to a higher retail and group protection mix. EAAA India Alternatives maintained stable PAT yields of ~0.71-0.72% of fee-paying AUM.
- **Cost Pressures & Claims:** Cholamandalam MS suffered elevated combined ratios (H1 at 124.2%) driven by Rs 71 crore in NATCAT losses and legacy consumer forum theft claims. Bajaj General Insurance reported a combined ratio of 104.5% due to Rs 75 crore net NATCAT impact and higher tender-driven health NEP mix. Max India noted employee cost increases due to operationalizing the Noida community alongside new labor codes and PF regulations.

## Capex & capacity
- **Capital Raises & Deployment:** Bajaj Finance received board approval for a Rs 11,700 crore equity raise via QIP and a Rs 5,800 crore preferential warrant issue to Bajaj Finserv. Religare Enterprises infused Rs 100 crore into Religare Broking and committed Rs 250 crore to Religare Housing (RHDFCL). Care Health raised Rs 300 crore in Tier-2 capital and planned a Rs 300 crore rights issue.
- **Physical & Operational Expansion:** Technocraft Ventures expanded its order book to ~Rs 1,393 crores following its August 2026 IPO, targeting water supply and wastewater treatment projects. Max India added 150+ manpower for Noida operations and is evaluating a 6.5 lakh sq. ft. development in Bengaluru. EAAA India Alternatives acquired the TOT-19 road bundle (Rs 2,259 crore) and a Pune office park via its operating platform Sekura.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Religare Enterprises Ltd:** Shifted tone from preparation to execution as Religare Finvest successfully resumed retail lending in Noida, Lucknow, and Kanpur prior to Diwali. Regulatory uncertainty persists regarding the demerger following prior RBI rejection, while new promoter directors (Dr. Anand Burman, Aditya Burman, Mohit Burman, and Jimeet Modi) joined the board.
- **Bajaj Finserv Ltd:** Continued aggressive expansion, with Bajaj Life commencing operations in GIFT City and initiating retail distribution with IndusInd Bank.

## What we were watching
- ⏳ RBI: Urged NBFCs to use CKYCR for onboarding — *Not discussed explicitly in concalls, though digital onboarding remains standard.*
- ⏳ RBI: Securitisation activities to be dematerialised from April 2027 — *Not discussed in Q2 earnings calls.*
- ✅ SEBI: Restore 30-minute VWAP calculation on expiry days — *Reflected in commentary by Religare on market trading activity and broking volumes.*

## What to watch next quarter
- Progress on Religare Finvest's loan disbursement scale-up toward its FY28 targets and ongoing dialogue with RBI regarding corporate restructuring.
- Integration and execution of Bajaj Finance's capital raise and Opex-to-NTI ratio improvements via AI implementation.
- Loss ratio normalisation and combined ratio trajectories for general insurers (Cholamandalam MS and Bajaj General Insurance) post-NATCAT impacts.
- EBITDA breakeven milestones for Max India’s AGEasy (target Q4 FY27) and Care Homes (target H1 FY28).
- Order book execution ramp-up for Technocraft Ventures as newly awarded projects move into physical execution.
