# NBFC & Financial Services - Housing finance: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The housing finance sector exhibited robust operational momentum in Q2 FY27, characterized by strong AUM growth (typically 18–27% YoY), record disbursements for several players, and generally stable asset quality. Major highlights included credit rating upgrades to AAA for PNB Housing Finance and AA+ for Aadhar Housing Finance, alongside successful capital infusions (e.g., Sammaan Capital via warrant conversions). However, sector participants also navigated headwinds such as rising repo rates, potential regulatory changes on insurance distribution fees, and isolated operational disruptions like Can Fin Homes' IT migration and SRG Housing's regulatory audit scrutiny.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus:** Managements across the board report strong underlying demand for housing credit, particularly driven by Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets, urbanization, and affordable housing. Companies like AAVAS, Aadhar Housing, Home First, India Shelter, and PNB Housing noted that recent repo rate hikes have had little to no immediate negative impact on customer logins, sanctions, or demand momentum.
* **Divergence:** 
  * *Bajaj Housing Finance* expressed caution regarding potential "attrition pressure" (balance transfers) and pricing competition in the prime housing segment due to fast-moving bank MCLR hikes.
  * *Can Fin Homes* noted temporary operational disruptions in disbursements due to its IT migration project ("Project Tejas"), though underlying demand remained robust.
  * *SRG Housing Finance* experienced a voluntary, temporary slowdown in fresh disbursements due to an ongoing routine NHB audit and Red Flag Account (RFA) tagging.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Cost of Borrowing & Repricing:** Borrowing costs remained relatively stable or saw minor increases (e.g., AAVAS at 7.64%, Aadhar exit cost at 7.7%, Bajaj Housing cost of funds down 17 bps QoQ to 7%, India Shelter at 8.2%, PNB Housing at 7.31%, Repco at 8.35%, Sammaan at 9.8%). Most companies with floating-rate liabilities and assets (like Aadhar, Can Fin, Repco, India Shelter) indicated they can pass on rate hikes with a 1-quarter lag.
* **Margins & Spreads:** 
  * *AAVAS* is confident of maintaining spreads above 5%.
  * *Aadhar Housing* reported an exit spread of 5.73%.
  * *Bajaj Housing* warned of a potential 20-25 bps NIM compression for the year due to rate volatility and acquisition pricing pressures.
  * *Can Fin Homes* aims to protect its 3.75% NIM and 2.75% spread.
  * *Home First* remains committed to 5.0%–5.2% spreads.
  * *PNB Housing* saw spreads expand to 2.43% (or 2.24% adjusted for one-offs).
  * *Repco Home* expects spreads to moderate slightly to 3.25%–3.3% due to rising borrowing costs and rate concessions to retain good customers.

## Capex & capacity
Branch expansion remains a key priority for scaling distribution across Tier 2/3 markets:
* **Aadhar Housing:** Added 1 branch (total 632); plans 40–50 branches for the full year.
* **India Shelter:** Added 8 branches; on track for 40–45 branch additions in FY27.
* **Repco Home:** Plans to open 13–14 branches this year (total 242).
* **Home First:** Added 6 branches (total 181); plans 10–15 more in the next two quarters.
* **Can Fin Homes:** Opened 25 of 28 planned branches; reallocating staff from operations to sales.
* **Sammaan Capital:** Expanded to 245 branches (targeting 300 by year-end) and added 1,123 employees in H1.
* **AAVAS:** Expanded to 443 branches across 15 states.
* **PNB Housing:** Network stands at 404 branches.

## What changed since last quarter
* **AAVAS Financiers:** Shifted tone from expecting spread compression in Q1 to being confident in maintaining spreads above 5% in Q2; delivered on productivity improvements (files/month).
* **Bajaj Housing Finance:** Shifted focus from "stabilized/muted" demand to managing potential attrition and pricing pressure in the prime segment following repo rate hikes.
* **Can Fin Homes:** Transitioned from a purely positive stance to managing short-term operational dips following the successful rollout of "Project Tejas" (IT transformation).
* **Home First Finance:** Addressed leadership and tariff-related concerns from prior quarters, shifting to strong demand and returning to growth in southern markets like Tamil Nadu.

## What to watch next quarter
* Finalization and impact of proposed IRDAI draft guidelines on insurance distribution commissions (noted as a potential ~35 bps ROA impact by Home First and 6–7% PBT impact by Bajaj Housing).
* Resolution of the NHB audit and RFA status for SRG Housing Finance.
* Recovery and scale-up of co-lending volumes following new RBI guideline implementations (noted by Home First and India Shelter).
* Transmission of repo rate hikes to borrowing costs and customer lending rates across all players.
