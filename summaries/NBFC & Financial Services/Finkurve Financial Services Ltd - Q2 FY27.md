# Finkurve Financial Services Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · NBFC & Financial Services*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Positive** - Operating profitability grew 62% YoY and AUM increased ~95% YoY.

### Key numbers
- AUM growth YoY: ~95%
- Operational profitability growth YoY: ~62%
- Total income growth YoY: ~88%
- Branch network: Increased from 92 to 122 branches (as of September 30, 2026)
- Capital adequacy: 28.7%
- ROA: 3%
- ROE: Crossed 10%
- Leverage: More than 2.5x
- Yield on gold loans: Rose from 20% to 20.8%
- Share of co-lending (off-book): ~5.8% (up from 1% or 2%)
- Personal loan portfolio: Average of Rs. 45 crores to Rs. 50 crores QoQ
- NCD borrowings: Account for around 57% of total borrowings, supported by ~30,000 retail bondholders
- GNPA: Increased to around 2.6%
- NNPA: Stood at 2.4%
- Collection efficiency: Declined to around 90% (from approximately 98% in the previous quarter)

### Highlights
- Management announced the transition towards adopting a single unified "Augmont" brand across the gold loan business to reduce customer confusion and leverage the brand's credibility.
- Appointed a new Chief Compliance Officer and continued to invest in risk, technology, operational, and governance frameworks.
- Unlike past periods of rapid sequential growth (e.g., ~16% in Q1, over 30% in Q4), AUM moderated to around 3% QoQ in Q2 due to industry transition, regulatory changes, and gold price corrections.
- *Continues pattern from record:* Continued scaling of the tech-enabled gold loan platform leveraging the Augmont ecosystem.

### Guidance & outlook
- AUM growth guidance maintained at 50% to 60%.
- Branch expansion guidance: 50 to 60 branches for the year (aiming for ~150 branches by December end, adding 28 more branches in H2).
- Medium-term target for off-book AUM (co-lending): To reach approximately 30% to 35% (one-third) of the overall portfolio over the next 3 years.
- Personal loan portfolio target: Aspire for a 90-10 portfolio mix (90% secured gold, 10% personal loans).
- Internal NCD borrowing cap: Capped at not more than 40% to 45% of overall borrowings.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Gold loan sector is adapting to the revised regulatory framework (including new repayment structures like paying monthly/bimonthly/quarterly instead of bullet repayments, and slab-wise LTV restrictions).
- Correction in gold prices from earlier highs led customers to temporarily defer taking loans.
- Unorganized gold loan market is shrinking fast as private moneylenders lose access to bank repledging facilities under RBI restrictions, driving customers to the organized segment.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Yield on gold loans improved from 20% to 20.8%.
- NCD borrowings are currently on par with or sometimes cheaper than institutional borrowings due to rising repo-linked bank rates.
- Management expects net interest margin (NIM) to increase as cost of funds comes down and per-branch outstanding loans/AUM increase, reducing the cost-income ratio.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 4 branches in Q2 (total 122 branches); planning to reach ~150 branches by December.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Maintained adequate liquidity in terms of ALM with positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets.
- Co-lending partnership expanded; two active partners, one new partner going live in Q3, and an old partner being revived for Q4.
- Personal loans (short-term unsecured) have average ticket sizes of Rs. 5,000 to Rs. 2 lakhs (average ~Rs. 20,000) and tenures from 15 days up to 3, 6, to 9 months.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Transition to periodic repayment frameworks (monthly/quarterly) under new RBI guidelines made it difficult for some customers to regularize accounts, leading to a spike in GNPA (2.6%) and a drop in collection efficiency (to ~90%).
- Gold price volatility and correction caused a temporary slowdown in loan demand.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Vedant Trivedi asked about differentiation against banks/large NBFCs and the revenue/AUM divergence.** Management stated they target underserved Tier 2/3 customers with faster turnaround times, and revenue grew due to higher yields (20.8%) and increased co-lending.
2. **Zaina Patel asked about NCD concentration and borrowing diversification.** Management stated no fresh NCDs were raised in Q2, internal exposure is capped at 40-45%, and bank borrowings/co-lending will increase.
3. **Nikita Mehta asked about the sharp increase in GNPA to 2.6% and collection efficiency drop.** Management clarified that in gold loans, higher NPAs do not imply credit losses due to high customer equity/margins, and the spike is due to customers transitioning to periodic repayment structures.
4. **Anirudh Sharma asked about the slowdown in sequential AUM growth (3% QoQ).** Management attributed it to a one-quarter industry transition, regulatory framework changes, and sharp gold price corrections.
5. **Vivek Jha asked about LTV increases and RBI gold loan directions for loans above Rs. 2.5 lakhs.** Management explained that LTV includes accrued interest and consumption loans are capped at 85% per RBI, while documentation for >Rs. 2.5 lakh loans now includes business proofs and income assessment for cash flow-based lending.

### One-line takeaway
Finkurve reported robust H1 FY27 operational profitability and AUM growth despite a sequential Q2 moderation, navigating new RBI gold loan norms by leveraging co-lending partnerships, higher yields, and an expanding branch network under the Augmont brand.
