# TCPL Packaging Ltd-$ - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-17 · Paper & Packaging*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered record quarterly performance with broad-based and profitable growth, alongside a strategic entry into an adjacent battery materials business.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated Total Income: Rs 495 crore (up 16% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 88 crore (up 17% YoY)
- EBITDA Margin: 18%
- Cash Profit: Rs 76 crore (up 56% YoY)
- PAT: Rs 40 crore (up 79% YoY)
- Flexible Packaging Capacity Expansion: ~30% increase with an estimated capex of Rs 50 crore to Rs 60 crore; operational by January/February 2027 (Q4 FY27).
- Battery Separator Film Project (Phase 1): Proposed investment of ~Rs 125 crore over the next 18 months; commercial production targeted for Q4 FY28; initial capacity of ~70 million square meters per annum. Long-term vision up to 500 million square meters per annum. Phase 1 topline expected at Rs 150 crore to Rs 200 crore.
- Total FY27 Capex Budget: Between Rs 100 crore to Rs 150 crore (excluding separator project land/initial spend).

### Highlights
- Recorded optimal utilization in the existing Flexible Packaging facility, triggering a new high-speed manufacturing line expansion.
- Announced entry into the Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) battery materials value chain through lithium-ion battery separator films via a 100% subsidiary.
- Domestic packaging demand remained strong with high single-digit volume growth and double-digit value growth.
- Export business recorded steady YoY growth following a low base in the previous year.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains optimistic about the packaging demand environment driven by consumption, premiumization, sustainable packaging, and outsourcing.
- No specific numerical guidance for FY27 given due to external uncertainties, but expects historic trends to continue with margins following the topline.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic packaging demand has improved, with robust growth across both Folding Cartons and Flexible Packaging.
- Near-term export outlook remains cautious due to global uncertainties, though sentiment for sourcing from India (Europe, UK) remains positive.
- Adoption of mono-material sustainable packaging by brand owners has been slower than expected due to post-COVID cost pressures and lack of Indian government mandates, but remains a strong long-term driver.
- No current domestic commercial production of lithium-ion battery separator films; market is currently dominated by China, Korea, and Japan.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material price increases are passed on to customers with a lag of over a quarter.
- Interest costs normalized compared to Q1 FY26, which included a large one-time mark-to-market forex loss.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Flexible packaging: Adding a 4th line (30% capacity increase) for Rs 50-60 crore, operational by Jan/Feb 2027.
- Folding cartons: Overall capacity utilization is at 70-plus percent; enabling capex underway to quickly add capacity in factories like Chennai as orders pick up.
- Battery separator film: Phase 1 investment of ~Rs 125 crore over 18 months, targeting 70 million square meters per annum by Q4 FY28. Long-term potential to scale up to 500 million square meters per annum (estimated potential revenue of Rs 1,100 crore to Rs 1,300 crore).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Not specifically discussed in detail, other than noting a normal cost structure and disciplined capital allocation.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global operating environment uncertainties affecting exports.
- Slower-than-expected adoption of sustainable mono-material packaging by domestic brand owners.
- Battery business is noted as "very unforgiving," requiring stringent quality and customer qualification.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Abhisar Jain (Monarch AIF)** asked about the rationale and technology for the battery separator film. *Management answered that the product leverages decades of core competencies in polymer processing, precision manufacturing, and R&D, developed in-house without external tech transfers.*
2. **Rohan Kalle (InCred Research)** inquired about customer qualification timelines for separator films. *Management stated that qualification will take at least a year (FY28-29) for testing and commercial supply.*
3. **Pavan Kumar (RatnaTraya Capital)** asked if raw material price hikes are fully passed on. *Management confirmed that EBITDA margins could not be maintained without passing on costs, though a lag of over a quarter exists.*
4. **Jayesh Shroff (Cask Capital)** questioned the traction of sustainable mono-material packaging (Innofilms). *Management admitted brand adoption has been slow due to post-COVID cost/margin pressures and lack of local mandates, but noted long-term viability and export traction.*
5. **Pulkit Singhal (Dalmus Capital)** asked about lead times and ownership of the separator subsidiary. *Management confirmed it will be a 100% owned subsidiary and that Phase 1 machinery lead times are comparable to packaging machinery.*

### One-line takeaway
TCPL Packaging reported a record Q1 FY27 with 16% revenue growth and 79% PAT growth, while strategically expanding its flexible packaging capacity and announcing a diversification into lithium-ion battery separator films.
