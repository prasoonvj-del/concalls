# Knack Packaging Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-02 · Paper & Packaging*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Highest-ever quarterly revenue driven by strong export volumes and deep customer wallet share, despite industry-wide margin pressures from raw material inflation.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated Revenue from Operations (Q2 FY27): Rs 294.76 crore, up 52.5% YoY (vs Rs 193.26 crore in Q2 FY26).
- Consolidated Revenue from Operations (H1 FY27): Rs 569.11 crore, up 47.2% YoY (vs Rs 386.73 crore in H1 FY26).
- EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs 49.77 crore, up 29.9% YoY (vs Rs 38.30 crore in Q2 FY26).
- EBITDA Margin (Q2 FY27): 16.35%, down 285 bps YoY (vs 19.25% in Q2 FY26).
- EBITDA (H1 FY27): Rs 108.94 crore, up 41.5% YoY (vs Rs 76.98 crore in H1 FY26).
- EBITDA Margin (H1 FY27): 19.14%, down 77 bps YoY (vs 19.91% in H1 FY26).
- PAT (Q2 FY27): Rs 25.02 crore, up 21.2% YoY (vs Rs 20.64 crore in Q2 FY26), including a Rs 2.62 crore share of loss from the Mexico JV.
- PAT Margin (Q2 FY27): 8.22%, down 212 bps YoY (vs 10.34% in Q2 FY26).
- PAT (H1 FY27): Rs 55.55 crore, up 34.5% YoY (vs Rs 41.30 crore in H1 FY26), including a Rs 6.06 crore loss from the Mexico JV.
- Volume Growth (Q2 FY27): 11,252 metric tonnes, up 3% QoQ and 13% YoY.
- Plant Capacity Utilization: 90% to 91%.
- Debt-to-Equity Ratio: 0.26x (improved from 0.68x YoY).
- Working Capital Cycle: 75 days (vs 76 days in Q2 FY26).
- ROE: 19.42%; ROCE: 28.76%.

### Highlights
- Delivered highest-ever quarterly revenue, supported by higher export volumes, deeper wallet share with global FMCG, pet food, and agri customers, and new international accounts.
- Served over 2,040 customers with 13,974 SKUs as of September 30, 2026.
- Export sales expanded to 75 countries across 6 continents.
- On October 2, 2026, announced a 3-year contract renewal and new orders worth ~Rs 150 crore per year with a major international pet food/animal nutrition customer in the US.
- Added 5,040 metric tonnes capacity in August, currently ramping up.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects margin recovery starting Q3 FY27 as price adjustments and cost pass-throughs flow through to realizations.
- Target to improve EBITDA margin further by increasing value-added product share, controlling costs, leveraging integrated setup, and scaling the Mexico JV toward breakeven.
- Target for expanding production capacity is set for October 2027.
- Management remains confident about achieving yearly targets.

### Demand & industry commentary
- West Asia conflict disrupted supplies and polypropylene prices, causing raw material costs to surge over 50% within weeks; Red Sea disruptions increased freight costs.
- Global paper supply/pricing struggles are driving migration from paper pinch-bottom bags to PLWPP and plastic pinch-bottom bags.
- Domestic dog food/pet nutrition packaging is growing in double digits.
- Rice industry demand is picking up in Q3, supporting pinch-bottom bag consumption.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Polymer prices rose from ~Rs 92 to a peak of ~Rs 143-Rs 160 due to geopolitical conflicts.
- EBITDA per kg dropped to ~Rs 45 in Q2 FY27 from ~Rs 54 in Q1 FY27.
- Gross margin took an immediate ~7% impact because production cycles take 1 to 1.5 months, while polymer prices spiked 25% to 30% instantly.
- 45% to 50% of customers are on conversion cost models with monthly or quarterly price revision mechanisms.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Overall installed capacity stands at 42,000 metric tonnes annually (actual output ~39,000-40,000 metric tonnes), scaling toward 69,000 to 72,000 metric tonnes post-expansion by October 2027.
- Pinch-bottom machine capacity utilization is running at 60% to 70% on new additions; target to reach 24,000-30,000 metric tonnes of pinch-bottom capacity by FY28.
- Sayem Knack JV (Mexico): Greenfield facility has an installed capacity of 400 metric tonnes/month, currently producing 80-90 metric tonnes, with expected breakeven at 120-130 metric tonnes expected in Q3/Q4.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt-to-equity ratio improved to 0.26x from 0.68x YoY.
- Working capital cycle remained stable at 75 days.
- Operating cash flow declined due to higher inventory (up by Rs 26.9 crore) to support scale-up, and higher receivables (up by Rs 55 crore) driven by significant export growth.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High volatility in crude oil and polypropylene prices.
- Lag of 1 to 2 quarters in passing on sudden raw material price hikes to customers under contract models.
- Greenfield project losses from the Mexico JV impacting consolidated PAT.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Bhawana Israni (Ambit Asset Management) asked about volume growth and capacity utilization:** Management stated Q2 volume was 11,252 metric tonnes (up 13% YoY, 3% QoQ) with 90-91% capacity utilization, and expects 50% utilization of the newly added August capacity in Q3.
- **Aman Chawla asked why contracted revenues failed to shield margins during raw material spikes:** Management explained that orders are priced at order date (not delivery date) and go through a 1-1.5 month production lag; furthermore, price spikes of 25-30% occurred instantaneously while customer contracts operate on monthly or quarterly reset lags.
- **Hitesh Shah asked about pinch-bottom machine utilization:** Management clarified that pinch-bottom machines are conversion lines running at 60-70% utilization, expected to scale up as the rice season begins in Q3.
- **Prakash Kapadia asked about operating cash flow contraction:** Management attributed the decline to a Rs 26.9 crore inventory increase for scale-up and a Rs 55 crore increase in receivables due to faster-growing, longer-cycle export sales.
- **Abhijit Mitra asked whether absolute per-tonne EBITDA should be tracked instead of percentage margins:** Management agreed that absolute per-kg numbers (~Rs 45 in Q2 vs ~Rs 54 in Q1) are helpful and confirmed expectations to recover toward prior levels as Q3 seasonality and price adjustments kick in.

### One-line takeaway
Knack Packaging delivered strong 52.5% YoY revenue growth in Q2 FY27 driven by export volumes, but EBITDA margins contracted by 285 bps to 16.35% due to a sharp, unhedged lag in passing on sudden polypropylene price spikes.
