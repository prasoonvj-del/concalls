# TCPL Packaging Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Paper & Packaging*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Company achieved record quarterly revenue (>Rs 500 crore) and EBITDA (>Rs 100 crore) for the first time.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained; management continues to target historical revenue growth trajectories without providing specific numerical guidance.
- **Tone and demand view:** Remained positive; demand remains healthy despite global geopolitical and logistics headwinds.
- **New topics:** Detailed discussion on the Noida subsidiary's underperformance due to smartphone market volume declines; clarification on battery separator technology (wet process/coating).
- **Last quarter's promises:** No specific update provided on the previously announced flexible packaging capacity expansion (scheduled for Jan/Feb 2027).

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** Rs 564.6 crore (+20.2% YoY).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 100.1 crore (+27.5% YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 17.7% (+100 bps YoY).
- **PAT:** Rs 45.3 crore (+57.7% YoY).
- **Cash Profit:** Rs 84.9 crore (+44.2% YoY).
- **H1 FY27 Total Income:** Rs 1,059.5 crore (+18.2% YoY).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 188 crore (+22.5% YoY).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 85.3 crore (+67.2% YoY).

### Highlights
- Crossed Rs 500 crore in quarterly total income and Rs 100 crore in quarterly EBITDA for the first time.
- Incorporated TCPL Energy Materials Limited (wholly owned subsidiary) on September 24, 2026, for lithium-ion battery separator films.
- Received industry awards: 'Packaging Converter (F&B) of the Year' (PrintWeek 2026), 4 India Star Awards, and 6 IFCA Awards.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to sustain historical revenue growth trajectory and enhance cash generation.
- Management stated that margin improvement is driven by scale and operational efficiency; sustainability depends on unpredictable external factors.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic packaging demand sustained double-digit growth.
- Flexible packaging segment outperformed paperboard packaging.
- Noida subsidiary (hard packaging) performance was disappointing due to high memory chip prices depressing smartphone volumes and export-oriented business disruptions.
- Chennai facility utilization is steadily improving; management expects potential for another line next year.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating environment remains impacted by elevated raw material and logistics costs.
- Margin expansion attributed to pricing actions, favorable product mix, and positive operating leverage.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- **Battery Separator Films:**
    - Initial phase (Phase 1) focused on establishing manufacturing infrastructure and capabilities.
    - Technology: Will utilize wet process (coating machines initially, backward integration to extrusion later).
    - Chemistry: Agnostic to battery type (LFP/NMC, cylindrical/prismatic/pouch).
    - Revenue potential: Up to Rs 200 crore on current investment; potential for Rs 1,000–2,000 crore at full scale in a couple of years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital: No material change; management noted the position is "okay" but not optimal.
- Debt: Operating at ~2x debt-to-EBITDA; management views this as reasonable and has no immediate priority to reduce it.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical and logistics disruptions impacting exports.
- Noida subsidiary volume decline due to smartphone market conditions.
- Battery separator business: High technical complexity and long customer qualification cycles.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Battery Separator Moat:** Management (Vidur Kanoria) stated the moat is the "process know-how," which is complex and not widely established in India.
- **Competition:** Management (Saket Kanoria) acknowledged the possibility of competition but believes they can move as fast as any entrant.
- **Noida Subsidiary:** Management (Akshay Kanoria) attributed the weak performance to a decade-low in Indian smartphone production caused by high memory chip prices.
- **Debt:** Management (Saket Kanoria) confirmed that the current ~2x debt-to-EBITDA level is reasonable and not a priority for reduction.
- **Flexible vs. Paperboard:** Management confirmed flexible packaging has performed better and experienced stronger growth.

### One-line takeaway
TCPL Packaging delivered record-breaking financial results while maintaining a disciplined, phased approach to its new battery separator film venture.
