# Insurance: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
* **Regulatory overhaul across distribution and accounting:** The sector is navigating two structural transitions: the adoption of Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS / IFRS) and IRDAI’s proposed consultation paper on distribution economics, Expense of Management (EoM) caps, and commission limits. Companies across life (ICICI Life, LIC, Max Financial, SBI Life), general/health (Go Digit, ICICI Lombard, Niva Bupa, Star Health), and insurtech/intermediaries (Medi Assist, Turtlemint) are assessing these shifts. Managements generally view the distribution proposals as constructive for transparency and policyholder value, with larger players (LIC, SBI Life, Star Health) noting their existing scale and lower expense ratios position them advantageously.
* **NATCAT losses weigh on Q2 general insurance underwriting:** Catastrophic weather events—specifically the Gujarat floods and eastern cyclones—impacted Q2 underwriting results across general insurers. GIC provisioned Rs 450–500 crore for Gujarat floods, Go Digit absorbed Rs 63 crore in net flood losses, and ICICI Lombard recorded Rs 1.18 billion in Q2 CAT losses. 
* **Life insurance product mix shift:** Underwriting focus remains on non-participating (non-par) savings, retail protection, and annuities, helping drive Value of New Business (VNB) margin expansion across major life insurers despite stock market volatility curbing demand for Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs).

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence

### Consensus
* **Strong momentum in protection and annuities:** Life insurers report robust demand for non-par, term, and annuity products. ICICI Life saw retail protection grow 47.2% YoY and annuities grow 50% YoY in H1. LIC’s individual protection APE jumped 58.59% YoY. Max Financial reported 130% growth in its annuity business and 31% in pure retail protection. SBI Life grew non-par guaranteed savings by 27% and protection APE by 26%.
* **Retail health outperformance:** Health insurance demand remains strong in the retail segment, aided by post-GST affordability and expanding consumer awareness. ICICI Lombard recorded 66.4% YoY growth in retail health for H1; Niva Bupa reported 47% retail health GWP growth (37% base growth); and Star Health grew fresh retail GWP by 35% YoY in Q2.
* **Automobile sales supporting motor insurance:** ICICI Lombard highlighted strong motor premium growth (+13.0% in H1), supported by a 21.1% YoY expansion in vehicle sales via VAHAN and rising EV penetration.

### Divergence
* **ULIP demand impacted by equity market volatility:** Volatile stock markets and continuous FII selling late in Q2 muted ULIP sales across the industry. LIC noted ULIP growth was subdued across the market; ICICI Life cited equity market pressure impacting linked business sentiment; and SBI Life saw its ULIP mix decline to 62% in H1 FY27 from 65% in H1 FY26. However, SBI Life expects historical H2 operational seasonality to sustain its full-year IRP growth guidance of 14%–15%, while ICICI Life expects retail protection growth to moderate from 40%–50% levels in H2.
* **Commercial lines pricing vs. property stabilization:** ICICI Lombard saw commercial lines (ex-fire) grow 20% in Q2, while fire de-growth moderated to -21.2% in Q2 (improving to -6.0% in September). Managements agree property pricing discipline is firming up: GIC noted domestic fire insurance rates controlled their slide from September onward, and Go Digit cited fire pricing increases since mid-September.
* **Group/B2B health pricing discipline:** Go Digit actively chose not to renew government health and health reinsurance contracts worth Rs 95 crore due to unattractive pricing. Niva Bupa intentionally kept group business flat/degrowing due to aggressive market pricing. Conversely, Medi Assist noted broader industry group health premiums grew ~15.8% in H1, with its managed group premiums growing over 21% (including acquisitions).

## Costs, raw materials & margins

### Combined ratios & underwriting performance
* **General Insurers:**
  * **ICICI Lombard:** Reported a Q2 combined ratio of 104.5% (102.7% excluding CAT losses of Rs 1.18 billion). Its motor combined ratio in Q1 stood at 108.3%, creating a 23.1 percentage point advantage over the industry average motor combined ratio of 131.4%. Target motor loss ratio remains in the 65%–67% range.
  * **Go Digit:** Recorded a Combined Insurance Service Ratio (CISR) of 99.8% (97.4% ex-flood losses). Attritional loss ratios for private car own damage (OD) improved sequentially from 85.3% in Q1 to 81.1% in Q2.
  * **GIC:** Experienced an increase in its Q2 combined ratio to 110.23% (vs 109.15% in Q2 FY26) and incurred claims ratio to 87.39%, driven by flood provisions and reserve strengthening for its exited American motor and cargo runoff treaty.
* **Standalone Health Insurers (SAHI):**
  * **Star Health:** Achieved its 5th consecutive quarter of underwriting improvement, with Q2 CISR improving to 98.0% (from 100.9% YoY) and underwriting profit turning positive to Rs 99 crore.
  * **Niva Bupa:** Improved its H1 CISR by 365 bps YoY to 99.5%, driven by a 287 bps reduction in claims ratio (retail loss ratio at 67.4%) and a 78 bps lower expense ratio.

### VNB Margins & operational efficiencies
* **LIC:** Expanded H1 Net VNB margin by 470 bps YoY to 22.3%, supported by product mix (non-par savings up 35% on APE) and an 87 bps improvement in overall expense ratio to 10.41%.
* **ICICI Life:** Delivered a VNB margin of 26.0% in H1 (up 130 bps from FY26 full-year levels), driven by protection mix expansion and an 8% CAGR reduction in operating expenses over three years.
* **Max Financial:** Reported H1 VNB margin of 25.4% (27% in Q2), benefiting from a 174 bps improvement in policyholder opex ratio to 13.7%. Management maintains its full-year VNB margin target of 25%–26%.
* **SBI Life:** Registered a Q2 VoNB margin of 29.3% (H1 at 27.9%), supported by dynamic repricing and a low operating expense ratio of 6.9% (5.8% ex-GST). Reaffirmed long-term margin guidance of 26%–28%.
* **Turtlemint:** Lowered corporate overheads as a percentage of revenue from 28% to 21% in H1, expanding H1 Service EBITDA margin to 13% (up from 11%).

## Capex & capacity
* **Distribution & Branch Expansion:** SBI Life added 14 new branches and 72,000 gross agents in H1. Star Health expanded its agent network by 39,000 to reach 8.7 lakh agents. Turtlemint grew its Digital Partner base by 69,000 to 7,28,000. Max Financial expanded its ecosystem with 5 new distribution partnerships.
* **Digital & AI Deployment:** ICICI Lombard targets raising digital sourcing contribution from 6% to 15%, leveraging GenAI bots for over 40% of agency daily health underwriting. Star Health integrated Salesforce CRM and scaled AI call bots to process 7.5 million minutes of volume at <20% of human cost. Medi Assist deploys AI platforms handling over 1 million claims monthly. Turtlemint rolled out "Ninja AI" and AI tutor tools.
* **Geographic & Network Scaling:** Medi Assist expanded its international presence into Southeast Asia via Mayfair and added 4 new insurers to its platform (adding Rs 758 crore in contracted premiums). Niva Bupa expanded its Preferred Provider Network (PPN) setup across 49 cities and 1,132 hospitals.

## What changed since last quarter

* **GIC:** Lowered tone from optimistic targets (previously 103% domestic / 95% foreign combined ratio over 2–3 years) to stating a 105% full-year combined ratio is achievable, citing ongoing NATCAT exposures and international soft market pressures.
* **Go Digit:** Tone shifted from cautious to more stable. Private car own damage attritional loss ratios stabilized and improved (81.1% in Q2 vs 85.3% in Q1), delivering on promises made in July. Gujarat floods (Rs 63 crore net impact) emerged as a major new topic.
* **Niva Bupa:** Emphasized demand resilience post-GST while addressing new channel broker concerns regarding retail claims rejection rates versus corporate policies.
* **Turtlemint:** Shifted from operational optimism to a analytical/defensive stance regarding proposed IRDAI distribution reforms. Moved from a specific breakeven target to focusing on protecting absolute service EBITDA and utilizing a ~Rs 50 crore discretionary fixed-cost buffer if needed.

## What to watch next quarter

* **Final IRDAI Distribution Guidelines:** Implementation timelines and finalized caps for distribution commissions and EoM frameworks across life, general, health, and insurtech intermediaries.
* **Q3 NATCAT Exposures:** Winter weather events and global catastrophe renewals impacting reinsurers (GIC) and domestic general insurers (ICICI Lombard, Go Digit).
* **Ind AS / RBC Transition Details:** Dedicated disclosures and analyst sessions (Go Digit in February, SBI Life near year-end) regarding Indian Accounting Standards and Risk-Based Capital transitions.
* **Max Financial’s Axis Bank Share Recovery:** Recovery of Axis Bank counter share, which dipped to 62%–63% in Q2.
* **Product Launches:** SBI Life's planned launch of a deferred annuity product in Q3 FY27.
* **Medi Assist Account Retention:** Normalization of integrated Paramount portfolio retention back toward historical levels of 93%–94% over the next 3–4 quarters.
