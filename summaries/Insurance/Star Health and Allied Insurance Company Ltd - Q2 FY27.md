# Star Health and Allied Insurance Company Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-31 · Insurance*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY2027
**Tone:** Positive - 5th successive quarter of improvement in loss ratios and underwriting results, driven by structural turnaround, risk selection, and productivity enhancements.

### Key numbers
- **Gross Written Premium (1/n basis):** Rs. 5,275 crore (up 19% YoY) for Q2; Rs. 9,562 crore (up 19% YoY) for H1.
- **Fresh Retail Health GWP (1/n basis):** Up 35% YoY for Q2; up 36% YoY for H1.
- **New-to-Insurance Mix:** 93% for Q2 (vs. 89% in Q2 FY26).
- **Underwriting Result:** Rs. 99 crore for Q2 (vs. loss of Rs. 40 crore in Q2 FY26); Rs. 210 crore for H1 (vs. loss of Rs. 24 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Combined Insurance Service Ratio (CISR):** 98% for Q2 (improved by 2.9%); 97.9% for H1 (improved by 2.4%).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 265 crore for Q2 (up over threefold from Rs. 80 crore); Rs. 815 crore for H1 (up 57% YoY).
- **Normalized PAT:** Rs. 390 crore for Q2 (up 70% YoY); Rs. 775 crore for H1 (up 56% YoY).
- **Normalized Annualized ROE:** 15.1% for H1 FY27 (vs. 11.2% in H1 FY26).
- **Retail Health Market Share:** 30% for Q2; 29% for H1 FY27.
- **Agent Network Additions:** 39,000 added in H1 FY27, reaching 8.7 lakh agents; agency productivity improved by 19% YoY.
- **Retail Claim Settlement Ratio:** 92.4% for H1 FY27 (up over 1%).
- **Renewal Ratio:** 102% (improved by 4%).
- **NPS:** 65 as of September 2026 (up 4 points).
- **Cashless Settlement Share:** 83% of 22.5 lakh claims settled in H1.
- **Equity Exposure:** Slightly less than 14% of the investment portfolio; cap set at 15%.
- **REITs/InVITs Allocation:** Fully invested up to regulatory limit of 3%.
- **AIF Allocation:** Approximately 2.1% to 2.2% of funds deployed.

### Highlights
- Marked the 5th successive quarter of improvement in loss ratios and underwriting performance.
- Discontinued the recognition of insurance finance charges (previous periods restated; no impact on PAT or underwriting results).
- Proprietary channels (agency and digital D2C) contributed over 90% of retail business.
- Digital D2C channel grew 137% YoY in H1; CRM upgraded to Salesforce and AI bots scaled up (processed 7.5 million minutes of call volume at <20% cost of human deployment).
- Partnership with Amplify Health went live for automated FWA capabilities.
- Multi-year policies represent close to 50% on a fresh basis; premium earned over the term lends revenue visibility.
- Renewal book operating at less than 100% combined ratio.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aspires to grow fresh business at 20%+ consistently for a long period and maintain it for the current year.
- Aiming to achieve a normalized ROE of mid-teens to higher teens over a period of time.

### Demand & industry commentary
- GST reforms in the previous year expanded affordability and unlocked demand, creating a higher base for H3/H4.
- Proposed IRDAI distribution reforms and Public Insurance Registry (PIR) welcomed as steps toward transparency, reduced information asymmetry, and enhanced customer outcomes.
- Focus shifting from commissions to product strength, claims experience, service, and brand.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Loss cost held at mid-single digits.
- Commission ratio moderated due to previous year initiatives (such as senior citizen commission reduction from 15% to 10%) flowing through on a full-year/deferment basis.
- Internal estimates indicate a credible path to proposed Expense of Management (EOM) limits via prudent management, operating leverage, and digitization.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 39,000 new agents in H1 FY27; total agency count at 8.7 lakh.
- Fresh business growth in semi-urban and rural geographies is over 3 times that of metro and urban geographies.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Portfolio duration increased to approximately 4 years via long-duration G-Sec calls to capitalize on rising yields.
- Debt portfolio held to maturity (HTM); equity, ETFs, REITs, and InVITs classified as FVTPL under IFRS.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Headline growth rates will optically normalize as last year's GST-led growth enters the base.
- Regulatory focus on dark patterns requires continuous review of digital journeys.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Swarnabha Mukherjee (360 ONE Capital) on fresh growth moderation and investment yield strategy:** Management stated fresh business is targeted to grow at 20%+; CIO noted equity exposure is near 14% (capped around 15%) and duration was increased to ~4 years via G-Secs.
- **Shreya Shivani (Nomura) on product pricing and EOM caps:** Management noted 35+ products are priced based on portfolio performance and tier-specific zoning; EOM compliance is maintained and plans are in place for final regulations.
- **Supratim Datta (Jefferies) on loss ratio drivers and renewal book:** Management clarified that vintage/renewal book (making up ~80% of the portfolio) drove the improvement, with the renewal book now operating at less than 100% CoR.
- **Nidhesh Jain (Investec) on D2C sourcing, dark patterns, and home care:** Management highlighted a dedicated digital SBU, adoption of Young Star neo for under-40 customers, and adaptation to incoming dark pattern regulations; home care/telemedicine handled over 1 lakh consultations/services in the quarter.
- **Sanketh Godha (Avendus Spark) on retail GWP breakup and accounting classification:** Management noted growth is a combination of value, volume, sum insured, and price hikes; CFO clarified debt is held to maturity while equity/REITs/InVITs are FVTPL.
- **Shobhit Sharma (Elara Securities) on commission ratio moderation and multi-year payouts:** CFO explained moderation reflects full-year impact of commission rationalization (e.g., senior citizens from 15% to 10%); upfront commissions are paid to selected agents under IGAAP, while IFRS defers it over the policy period.

### One-line takeaway
Star Health delivered its 5th successive quarter of structural underwriting turnaround in Q2 FY27, backed by proprietary distribution growth, disciplined risk selection, and lower loss ratios, leading to a threefold surge in reported PAT to Rs. 265 crore.
