# General Insurance Corporation of India - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · Insurance*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Marked improvement across key underwriting metrics, reduced underwriting losses, and higher solvency.

### Key numbers
- Gross premium income (Q1 FY27): Rs 13,475.36 crore (vs Rs 12,388.01 crore YoY)
- Investment income: Rs 3,265.51 crore (vs Rs 3,313.74 crore YoY)
- Incurred claim ratio: 85.04% (vs 90.42% YoY)
- Combined ratio: 104.88% (vs 106.94% YoY)
- Profit before tax (PBT): Rs 2,490.25 crore
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 1,922.04 crore
- Solvency ratio: 4.32 as on June 30, 2026 (vs 3.85 as on June 30, 2025)
- Gujarat flood provisioning: Rs 440 crore (or Rs 442 crore)
- Investment book breakdown: 73.4% in fixed income securities, ~17% in equity, ~8.67% in money market instruments
- Investment book value: Rs 120,000+ crore (Market value: Rs 157,800 crore, with equity market value at around Rs 58,000 crore)

### Highlights
- Delivered marked improvement in key underwriting metrics, lower claims experience, and a reduced combined ratio leading to lower underwriting losses.
- Foreign portfolio reported an underwriting profit with a 95 combined ratio (though management noted seasonal/accounting impacts and advised waiting for multiple quarters).
- Subsidiary impacts: AICL profit decreased by ~60%; South Africa subsidiary reported a loss of Rs 287 crore and Moscow subsidiary reported a loss of Rs 29 crore in Q1.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management targets an overall premium growth of roughly ~10% (with foreign expected to grow more pronouncedly than domestic).
- Long-term domestic/international mix goal remains 50/50, but medium-term target is adjusted to 60/40 due to faster domestic outgrowth.
- Combined ratio targets: Domestic book targeted at 103 and foreign book targeted at 95 over the next 2 to 3 years.
- Credit rating upgrade to 'A' expected over a 4 to 5 year horizon, subject to conditions and IFRS/RBC implementations.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global reinsurance industry demonstrates resilience with strong capitalization and sustained demand for risk transfer; abundant market capacity has increased competition, particularly in property catastrophe.
- Primary domestic commercial/fire market experiencing steep price declines due to reinsurance support capacity and aggressive behavior across all player segments (Indian reinsurers, foreign reinsurer branches, GIFT City players, and cross-border reinsurers).
- IRDAI issued directives/advisories on July 22 flagging rates quoted on the direct side.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Domestic health reinsurance strategy focuses on the retail health segment rather than group/corporate segment for better profitability.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- *Not discussed.*

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt is held to maturity and kept at cost; equity is marked to market.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Catastrophe exposure and potential impact of recent flood losses (e.g., Gujarat floods with provisioning of ~Rs 440 crore). GIC's participation in cat event losses historically tends to be around 30% to 40% of market losses.
- Competitive pricing pressures and softer international/domestic rate environments.
- Foreign motor treaty historical performance required corrective measures and hard underwriting decisions.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Avinash Singh (Emkay Global) on competitive intensity & life reinsurance:** Management noted aggressive stance across all market segments (local, foreign branches, GIFT City, cross-border). On life, management stated life is a focus area backed by actuarial analysis, and results should be viewed over a cycle rather than short periods.
2. **Sanketh Godha (Avendus Spark) on foreign combined ratio & domestic softness:** Management explained foreign improvement involves class-by-class underwriting guideline reviews, though Q1 may include accounting entries and seasonality. Domestic motor treaties faced competitive pressures and deterioration.
3. **Karthikeyan K (Individual Investor) on international revenue recovery & rating:** Management stated reaching prior peak revenues will take 3-4 years due to softening trends, prioritizing ROE and risk-return optimization over top-line targets.
4. **Jenish Shah (Individual Investor) on Gujarat flood provisions and solvency:** Management confirmed a provision of Rs 440-442 crore for Gujarat floods in Q1, and explained that high solvency (4.3) reflects improving performance and conservative positioning ahead of IFRS/RBC implementations.
5. **Shubham Kothari (Individual Investor) on subsidiary performance:** Management clarified that consolidated profit was lower due to subsidiary losses/decreases, specifically AICL profit dropping 60%, South Africa showing a Rs 287 crore loss, and Moscow showing a Rs 29 crore loss in Q1.

### One-line takeaway
GIC reported a resilient Q1 FY27 with improved combined ratios (104.88%), higher solvency (4.32), and strong PAT of Rs 1,922.04 crore despite provisioning for Gujarat floods and subsidiary-level losses.
