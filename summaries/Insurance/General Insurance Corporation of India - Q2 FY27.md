# General Insurance Corporation of India - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-13 · Insurance*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Healthy investment income and strong solvency were offset by rising underwriting losses, a higher combined ratio, and lower PAT compared to Q2 FY26.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Management noted a full-year combined ratio of 105% is achievable, but refrained from making firm projections due to upcoming Q3 catastrophe exposures and Ind AS transitions. (Previous quarter: targeted 103 for domestic and 95 for foreign over 2-3 years, and ~10% overall premium growth).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly to a more cautious stance regarding second-half competitive pressures and continued NATCAT exposures, though domestic fire pricing discipline showed some stabilization from September onward.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Detailed discussion on the transition to Ind AS and its expected volatility/discounting impact; specific mention of the American motor and cargo runoff treaty reserve strengthening contributing to foreign loss ratio changes.
- Last quarter's promises: Broker outreach initiatives were expanded for 1st January foreign renewals, as discussed this quarter.

### Key numbers
- Gross premium income (Q2 FY27): Rs 11,228.21 crore (vs Rs 9,601.70 crore in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: Rs 24,703.57 crore (vs Rs 21,989.71 crore YoY).
- Gross premium income breakdown (H1 FY27): Domestic Rs 19,794.41 crore (up 15.9% YoY); International Rs 4,909.16 crore (broadly flat YoY). Split: 80% domestic, 20% international.
- Investment income: Rs 4,238.26 crore in Q2 (vs Rs 3,791.67 crore in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: Rs 7,503.78 crore (vs Rs 7,105.41 crore YoY).
- Incurred claims ratio: 87.39% in Q2 FY27 (vs 81.50% in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: 86.19% (vs 86.45% YoY).
- Combined ratio: 110.23% in Q2 FY27 (vs 109.15% in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: 107.39% (vs 107.41% YoY).
- Adjusted combined ratio (including policyholders' investment income): 85.92% for H1 FY27 (vs 83.74% in H1 FY26).
- Underwriting loss (H1 FY27): Rs 1,709.06 crore (vs Rs 1,234.59 crore in H1 FY26).
- Profit before tax (PBT): Rs 3,101.54 crore in Q2 FY27 (vs Rs 3,472.76 crore in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: Rs 5,591.79 crore (vs Rs 5,716.30 crore YoY).
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 2,455.62 crore in Q2 FY27 (vs Rs 2,866.79 crore in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27: Rs 4,377.66 crore (vs Rs 4,619.02 crore YoY).
- Solvency ratio: 4.18 as of September 30, 2026 (vs 3.85 as of September 30, 2025).
- Net worth excluding fair value change: Rs 53,402.32 crore (vs Rs 46,669.38 crore YoY).
- Net worth including fair value change: Rs 93,138.68 crore (vs Rs 88,709.19 crore YoY).
- Gujarat flood provisioning: Rs 450 crore to Rs 500 crore included in Q2 claims.
- NSE OFS contribution to profit/fair value: Rs 1,082 crore.
- International portfolio combined ratio (H1 FY27): 108.24% (vs 119.87% in H1 FY26).

### Highlights
- Domestic premium grew 15.9% YoY in H1, driven by healthy signings in health reinsurance and proportional treaties, compensating for a shrunk domestic property book.
- International portfolio showed combined ratio improvement to 108.24% in H1 FY27 from 119.87% in H1 FY26, though still un-derwriting unprofitable.
- NSE unlisted investment was revalued to market value following its listing, with unrealized appreciation recognized in the fair value change account.
- In line with previous records, GIC maintained a strong solvency ratio of 4.18.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management believes a 105% full-year combined ratio is achievable, subject to Q3 global catastrophe exposure.
- Management expects competitive conditions to persist in international markets through H2 FY27.
- Management plans to explicitly guide for NATCAT activity starting FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global reinsurance capacity remains healthy, increasing competition across business lines and geographies.
- Domestic primary property insurance rates have held up and controlled their slide from September onward.
- International markets feature abundant traditional and alternative capital, with competitive pressures expected to persist for another year.
- Industry-wide NATCAT loss estimate for recent eastern cyclone/floods cited by management as around 25% to 30%, with GIC's share typically 25% to 30% of market losses.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Health reinsurance growth focused more on retail segment rather than group segment.
- Foreign motor and cargo segments experienced reserve strengthening for the American runoff treaty (exited December 2021).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Catastrophe exposure and potential impact of recent flood losses (Gujarat floods provisioned at Rs 450-500 crore).
- Persistence of competitive pressures and softer pricing environments both domestically and internationally.
- Volatility and reserve movements across old runoff treaties and crop drought conditions.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Avinash Singh (Emkay Global) on NATCAT losses and combined ratio guidance:** Management stated NATCAT guidance will be provided starting FY28, and a 105% full-year combined ratio is viewed as achievable subject to Q3 cat exposure. Management also discussed Ind AS transition impacts.
2. **Sanketh Godha (Avendus Spark) on domestic loss ratio improvement and foreign book:** Management explained that Rs 450-500 crore was provided for Gujarat floods with reporting lags, and international loss ratios rose due to conservative reserve maintenance for the American motor/cargo runoff treaty.
3. **Swati Agarwal (Individual Investor) on NSE valuation:** Management confirmed NSE shares were valued at cost previously and are now marked to market, with the Rs 1,082 crore OFS gain/unrealized appreciation routed to the fair value change account.
4. **Karthikeyan K (Individual Investor) on foreign portfolio growth and IBNR reserves:** Management stated foreign growth depends on upcoming Jan 1 renewals via broker outreach, and IBNR increases reflect business growth, crop drought provisioning, and class-specific loss experiences.
5. **Mahek (Emkay Global) on eastern cyclone impact and new reinsurers:** Management stated concrete figures for recent cyclones are pending data collation, estimating industry losses at 25-30% with GIC absorbing a proportional share, and noted they will monitor the behavior of new reinsurers.

### One-line takeaway
GIC delivered stable H1 performance with robust solvency (4.18) and a strong PAT of Rs 4,377.66 crore, despite higher Q2 incurred claims and underwriting losses driven by flood provisioning and conservative reserve strengthening.
