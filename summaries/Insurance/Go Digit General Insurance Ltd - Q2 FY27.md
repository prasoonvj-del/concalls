# Go Digit General Insurance Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Insurance*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While overall revenue increased slightly and private car own damage loss ratios showed sequential improvement, profitability was impacted by Gujarat flood losses and lower profit commission on reinsurance.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Management provided no explicit formal numerical guidance last quarter or this quarter.
- Tone and demand view: Tone shifted slightly from cautious to more stable; management noted that private car own damage attritional loss ratios improved (down from 85.3% in Q1 to 81.1% in Q2) and negative growth rates moderated compared to Q1.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Gujarat flood losses (contributing Rs. 63 crore net) became a major topic this quarter.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Management had stated in July that they expected private car own damage loss ratios to stabilize in Q2; the call confirmed this materialized as attritional loss ratios improved and frequency fell.

### Key numbers
- **Insurance Revenue:** Rs. 2,720 crore vs Rs. 2,675 crore (last year Q2)
- **PAT (excluding mark-to-market):** Rs. 208 crore vs Rs. 277 crore (last year Q2)
- **PAT (excluding MTM and flood losses):** Rs. 255 crore vs Rs. 285 crore (last year Q2)
- **PAT (Q1 vs Q2 without flood):** Rs. 239 crore (Q1) vs Rs. 255 crore (Q2)
- **Combined Ratio (CISR):** 99.8% vs 94.9% (97.4% excluding flood vs 94.5% in Q1)
- **Solvency Ratio:** 2.40x
- **ROE:** 4.4% on iGAAP net worth for the quarter
- **Assets Under Management (AUM):** Rs. 24,371 crore (up 15.9%)
- **Motor Market Share:** 5.68% in Q2 vs 5.58% in Q1
- **Motor Own Damage Market Share:** 4.99% in Q2 vs 4.76% in Q1
- **GDPI Growth:** -1.2% (industry at +7%)
- **GWP Growth:** -3.6% (flat if excluding health reinsurance inward and government health business)
- **Motor Segment Growth:** -4.1% (Private car down 10.7%, standalone OD down 40%; Two-wheeler up 18.6%; CV down 20%)
- **Fire Gross Written Premium Growth:** -29.9%
- **Premium Retention:** 84.3% vs 79.1%
- **Long-term Advance Premium:** Rs. 3,556 crore as of September 30, 2026, vs Rs. 3,209 crore in March 2026
- **Equity Mix in AUM:** 9.6% (with Rs. 148 crore in unrealized capital gains)
- **Flood Losses (Net):** Rs. 63 crore in Q2 vs Rs. 10 crore in Q2 last year

### Highlights
- First full quarter reporting on Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS / Indian Accounting system) with a transition date of April 1, 2025.
- Gujarat floods and a smaller event in the Northeast resulted in net flood losses of Rs. 63 crores.
- Private car own damage attritional loss ratio improved from 85.3% in Q1 to 81.1% in Q2.
- Management chose to not renew government health and health reinsurance premium worth Rs. 95 crores due to unattractive pricing.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains its philosophy of choosing profitability over growth in a soft market.
- Management is not betting on third-party price hikes, court judgments, or capital markets.
- Management plans to discuss Indian Accounting System details further at the Annual Analyst Day in February.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Market remains soft overall, except for property (fire), where pricing discipline and an increase have been observed since mid-September.
- No third-party price hikes implemented yet.
- Commissions across the industry continue to remain high.
- Discussion paper on Expenses of Management (EoM) acknowledges that previous guidelines increased commissions instead of reducing them, and suggests weighting crop and group health differently.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating expense ratio increases driven primarily by the rising proportion of two-wheeler business, which carries higher commissions.
- Net cost of reinsurance was Rs. 70 crore vs Rs. 382 crore last year.
- Insurance finance expense charged against investment income was Rs. 171 crore in Q2 due to discounting of claims under Indian Accounting Standards.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Equity allocation reached 9.6% of AUM; management indicated intent to move toward 12.5% of AUM to equity including REITs based on market opportunities.
- Fixed income duration adjusted to 4.9 in October (from 4.5 in March '26 and 5.2 in March '25), with investment yields around 8% in October.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Additional net worth of roughly Rs. 3,300+ crore cannot currently be distributed or used to write business under iGAAP solvency rules.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Flood losses impacting quarterly claims cost.
- High commissions continuing in the industry.
- Unattractive pricing in certain segments like school buses, auto-rickshaws, and government health.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Mahek Shah (Emkay Global) on the distribution paper and TP book profitability:** Management noted the discussion paper acknowledges flaws in current EoM rules and suggested analysts model financials based on existing disclosures while avoiding speculation on final guidelines.
2. **Sanketh Godha (Avendus Spark) on discounting factors in loss ratios and illiquidity premiums:** Management and the Appointed Actuary explained that discounting uses risk-risk free yield curves adjusted for 25% of the spread against AAA corporate bond yields, and noted that discounting impacts both claims and investment income symmetrically.
3. **Nidhesh Jain (Investec) on fixed commission regimes and accounting standards:** Management stated fixed commissions do not change underwriting fundamentals or distribution strategies, and reiterated that CISR under Ind AS reflects true economic timing.
4. **Dipanjan Ghosh on group health trends and equity allocation:** Management noted employer-employee group health loss ratios exceed 100% industry-wide due to EoM distortions, and confirmed equity allocation could expand to 12.5% over the medium term.
5. **Ansuman Deb (ICICI Securities) on IDBI Bank's role and green shoots:** Management stated they have no internal information on the Fairfax-IDBI Bank transaction beyond public announcements, and confirmed private car OD attritional loss ratios are showing green shoots of recovery.

### One-line takeaway
Go Digit delivered a stable Q2 under new Ind AS reporting, absorbing Rs. 63 crore in flood losses while demonstrating improving private car own damage loss ratios and disciplined growth.
