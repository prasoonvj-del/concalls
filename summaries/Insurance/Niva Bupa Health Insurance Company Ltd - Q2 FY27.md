# Niva Bupa Health Insurance Company Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-10 · Insurance*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - H1 performance showed strong growth in top-line, retail market share gains, and a doubling of PAT alongside improved loss and expense ratios.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: None explicitly changed; management maintained its focus on portfolio management and targeting mid-to-high teens ROE.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly to emphasize continuing volume growth and resilience despite potential regulatory impacts on distribution/commissions, highlighting the elasticity of demand seen post-GST.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Discussion around detailed broker feedback regarding claims rejection in retail vs corporate, and deeper commentary on upcoming distribution norm regulations and potential commission capping.
- Last quarter's promises: Continued aggressive retail growth (reported retail growth at 47% for H1) and maintaining disciplined group business.

### Key numbers
- H1 GWP (without 1/n): Rs 5,004 crore (up 26% YoY)
- H1 Retail health growth (without 1/n): 47% reported, 37% base growth
- Retail mix: 77% of book (up from 75% in Q1)
- Profit After Tax (PAT - Ind AS): Rs 273 crore for H1 (up from Rs 132 crore YoY)
- Retail market share: 11% (up from 9.9% YoY)
- Expense of Management (EoM): 34.7% (allowable threshold is 36.1%)
- Combined Insurance Service Ratio (CISR): 99.5% for H1 (improved by 365 bps YoY)
- Retail loss ratio: 67.4% in H1 (improved by 77 bps YoY)
- Solvency ratio: 2.03x as of September 30, 2026 (regulatory minimum 1.50)
- Annual investment yield (excluding ETF): 7.4% with AUM near Rs 10,500 crore
- Blended NPS: 63 (up from 57 YoY)
- Claims settlement rate: 95.6% for H1 (up from 94.6% YoY)
- PPN footprint: 49 cities, 1,132 hospitals, 22% of cashless claims flowing through

### Highlights
- Industry common empanelment MOU signed with over 5,000 hospitals; 18 standard treatment workflows going live out of 154 ICMR defined ones.
- Company transitioned to Ind AS.
- Ranking improved in Great Places to Work survey to 41st overall and top 25 in BFSI.
- Group business remained flat or degrowing due to disciplined pricing stance.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects 7.4% to 7.5% investment yield to be sustainable in the foreseeable future.
- Target to maintain mid-to-high teens ROE over the medium term.
- Combined insurance service ratio target between claims and cost to remain around 98% to 99%.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Industry awareness campaigns (~Rs 150 crore spent over 3 years) are positively impacting customer KPIs and claims perception.
- GST tailwinds and standardization initiatives continue to support industry momentum.
- B2B/group market pricing remains aggressive; retail products face higher structural rejections due to waiting periods and 2-year exclusions compared to corporate policies.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- CISR improved by 365 bps YoY to 99.5%, driven by a 78 bps reduction in expense ratio and a 287 bps reduction in claims ratio.
- Retail loss ratio improved by 77 bps YoY to 67.4% due to writing high LTV business and stable seasonal infection trends.
- New business combined ratio is around 102%-103%, while the renewal book runs at a better 97%-98% combined ratio with renewal loss ratios around 75%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Continued scaling of the PPN setup across 49 cities and 1,132 hospitals.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Solvency ratio stands at 2.03x; management stated no equity capital raising requirement is foreseen, though an enabling board approval exists to raise up to Rs 500 crore in debt if needed.
- Investments: ETFS, AIFs, and liquid mutual funds are classified at fair value through P&L (ETF at ~5.5% of AUM); government and corporate bonds are classified at fair value through OCI.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Channel feedback from brokers indicating potential reputational and customer friction issues regarding retail claims settlements and rejections.
- Solvency ratio has experienced a downward trend over the past 7-8 quarters due to rapid premium growth under I-GAAP calculations.
- Potential impact of draft distribution norms on commissions and channel economics.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shreya Shivani (Nomura) asked about drivers of loss ratio improvement (monsoon vs wellness/efficiencies) and updates on Dr. Prakash committees.** Management replied that loss ratios were aided by stable seasonal infections and lifetime value (LTV) focus, while empanelment and standard treatment workflows are scaling.
2. **Riddhesh Gandhi (Discovery Capital) flagged broker feedback regarding low retail claim payments and higher ombudsman/rejection issues.** Management countered that public statistics, settlement rates (95.6%), and a rising NPS prove otherwise, and noted that brokers often conflate retail products (which have waiting periods and exclusions) with corporate books.
3. **Prayesh Jain (Motilal Oswal) asked about the fresh vs renewal retail mix performance, aging impacts on pricing, and large hospital chain adoption.** Management stated fresh loss ratios are improving via higher sum-insured ReAssure 3.0 products, age is factored into pricing, and standardization initiatives (STWs) are hospital-size agnostic.
4. **Karan Shah (CWC) asked about the continuous decline in the solvency ratio and impact of proposed distribution commission cuts.** Management responded that the 2.03x solvency is seasonally lower in H1, comfort remains high with no equity raise needed, and debt options (up to Rs 500 crore) are available if required.
5. **Srihari (Avendus Spark) inquired about group business loss ratio trends, attachment/Policybazaar business impacts from regulations, and debt classification in OCI vs P&L.** Management clarified that group loss ratios improved due to prudent flat/degrowing B2B books, and that bonds are classified under fair value through OCI.

### One-line takeaway
Niva Bupa delivered a strong H1 FY27 with GWP reaching Rs 5,004 crore and PAT doubling to Rs 273 crore, underpinned by 47% retail growth and a lower combined insurance service ratio of 99.5%.
