# Insurance - General & health insurance: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the general and health insurance sector reflects a dichotomy between robust retail health demand and ongoing underwriting pressures. While retail health players like Star Health, Niva Bupa, and ICICI Lombard reported strong top-line growth and margin improvements, the broader industry faced profitability headwinds from Q2 catastrophe events—specifically the Gujarat floods, which impacted GIC Re, Go Digit, and ICICI Lombard. Furthermore, companies are actively navigating the transition to Ind AS accounting standards, while structural cost discipline and proprietary distribution expansion remain key focal points.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally driven by intermediated agency models and corporate group health/motor underwriting, the industry has evolved toward digital-first D2C platforms, AI-driven fraud detection/claims processing, and alternative risk transfer. [Background]

**Shifts underway**
- **Digital D2C and GenAI Integration** (Stage: Scaling) — Companies are aggressively scaling digital platforms and AI tools to drive efficiency. Star Health grew its digital D2C channel by 137% YoY and deployed AI bots handling 7.5 million call minutes; ICICI Lombard integrated a WhatsApp-based GenAI bot for over 40% of agency health underwriting [Star Health concall Q2 FY27; ICICI Lombard concall Q2 FY2027].
- **Ind AS Accounting Standards Transition** (Stage: Mainstream) — Insurers are shifting to fair-value and discounted cash-flow reporting frameworks. Go Digit reported its first full quarter under Ind AS, while GIC Re, Niva Bupa, and Star Health are managing its valuation and reserving impacts [Go Digit concall Q2 FY27; GIC Re concall Q2 FY27; Niva Bupa concall Q2 FY27; Star Health concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push**
- Panel to assess distribution and commission reforms [Govt: Panel to assess distribution and commission reforms, Nov 2026].
- GST Council recommendations on removing restrictions on Input Tax Credit (ITC) on Group-Health and Life Insurance [Background].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements across retail health (Star Health, Niva Bupa, ICICI Lombard) agree that underlying retail health demand remains exceptionally strong, aided by GST tailwinds and rising health awareness. In property lines, fire insurance pricing discipline has shown stabilization and improvement since mid-September (noted by GIC Re and Go Digit).
- **Divergence:** While retail segments surged, commercial and group health lines saw divergence. Go Digit and Niva Bupa deliberately chose to flatline or degrow certain group health and government portfolios due to unattractive pricing. In contrast, ICICI Lombard actively expanded its commercial lines, growing non-fire commercial lines by 20% in Q2 FY27.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Loss Ratios & Combined Ratios:** Underwriting profitability varied significantly. Star Health reported a combined insurance service ratio (CISR) of 98% and an underwriting profit of Rs. 99 crore. Niva Bupa improved its CISR by 365 bps YoY to 99.5%. Conversely, GIC Re's combined ratio ticked up to 110.23% in Q2 due to rising incurred claims (87.39%) and a Rs. 450-500 crore provision for Gujarat floods. Go Digit's CISR stood at 99.8%, impacted by Rs. 63 crore in net flood losses, though its private car own damage attritional loss ratios improved sequentially from 85.3% to 81.1%. ICICI Lombard maintained a stable combined ratio of 104.5% (inclusive of ₹1.18 billion in CAT losses).
- **Operating Efficiencies:** Medi Assist drove its EBITDA margin up to 20.4% in Q2 through Paramount integration, headcount rationalization, and AI deployment.

## What changed since last quarter
- **GIC Re:** Shifted to a more cautious stance on H2 NATCAT and competitive pressures; withdrew firm full-year combined ratio guidance, noting a 105% target is merely "achievable" subject to Q3 exposures.
- **Go Digit:** Tone shifted from cautious to stable as private car own damage attritional loss ratios improved and frequency moderated.
- **Star Health & Niva Bupa:** Maintained aggressive retail growth momentum (Star Health fresh retail up 35%; Niva Bupa retail up 47%) and sustained margin recovery trends.

## What to watch next quarter
- Q3 global and domestic catastrophe exposures and their impact on reinsurance books (GIC Re, ICICI Lombard).
- Regulatory developments surrounding proposed distribution norm revisions and commission caps.
- Further synergy realizations and retention rate normalizations post-Paramount integration (Medi Assist).

## Company one-liners
- **General Insurance Corporation of India (GIC Re):** Delivered stable H1 performance with robust solvency (4.18) and a PAT of Rs. 4,377.66 crore, despite higher Q2 incurred claims and underwriting losses.
- **Go Digit General Insurance Ltd:** Absorbed Rs. 63 crore in flood losses while demonstrating improving private car own damage loss ratios and disciplined growth under new Ind AS reporting.
- **ICICI Lombard General Insurance Company Ltd:** Delivered resilient Q2 FY2027 performance with PAT growing 27.5% YoY to ₹10.31 billion, supported by retail health momentum and superior underwriting discipline.
- **Medi Assist Healthcare Services Ltd:** Completed its Paramount integration, delivering a 20.4% Q2 EBITDA margin and announcing a buyback, while positioning its AI platform for structural growth.
- **Niva Bupa Health Insurance Company Ltd:** Achieved a strong H1 FY27 with GWP reaching Rs. 5,004 crore and PAT doubling to Rs. 273 crore, underpinned by 47% retail growth and a lower CISR of 99.5%.
- **Star Health and Allied Insurance Company Ltd:** Delivered its 5th successive quarter of underwriting turnaround in Q2 FY27, backed by proprietary distribution growth and disciplined risk selection, leading to a threefold surge in PAT to Rs. 265 crore.
