# Mamata Machinery Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-23 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While H1 performance was severely impacted by external headwinds and a plant flooding incident, management expects a strong recovery in H2 backed by a robust order backlog and ready stock.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue from operations:** Rs. 41.1 crores (down 23% YoY from Rs. 53.4 crores in Q2 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 Revenue from operations:** Rs. 77.3 crores (down 16% YoY from Rs. 92 crores in H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 Gross Margins:** Held at 60% (vs 61% in H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** EBITDA loss of Rs. 5.55 crores (vs EBITDA of Rs. 9.87 crores in H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 Converting Division Revenue:** Rs. 40.9 crores (down 14% YoY from Rs. 47.7 crores in H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 Packaging Division Revenue:** Rs. 10.9 crores (down 67% YoY from Rs. 33.3 crores in H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 Co-extrusion Division Revenue:** Rs. 3.5 crores (down 53% YoY from Rs. 7.5 crores in H1 FY26)
- **Order Backlog:** Close to Rs. 160 crores
- **Ready Finished Goods Awaiting Dispatch:** Close to Rs. 35 crores
- **Trade Receivables Aging (>6 months):** Reduced to close to Rs. 12 crores

### Highlights
- Management cited three major headwinds in H1 FY27: West Asia crisis/elevated crude and polymer prices leading to customer deferrals, US market headwinds impacting the packaging division, and torrential rainfall in late July causing flooding at the Ahmedabad factory and a complete 1-week shutdown with 5-6 weeks of disturbed operations.
- The plant disruption effectively reduced the fiscal year to a 10-month working period. Management stated all damages are covered under insurance and claims have been submitted.
- **RecTech™ Milestones:** Received 100% recyclability certification in the EU from Institut cyclos-HTP GmbH (August) and APR Design Recognition for Recyclability in the US (October), completing independent validation across India, Europe, and the US. Two patents were granted in India in August.
- **New Product Launches:** Launched Vega 500 SF for processing monomaterial films; made a breakthrough in liquid packaging with customer demonstrations underway.
- *Record continuation:* Consistent with the 2026-11-07 investor deck, H1 earnings faced severe macroeconomic headwinds and plant disruptions, but management anticipates a strong H2 recovery.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident of achieving FY27 revenues on par with FY26, and is targeting mid to high single-digit growth by accelerating manufacturing execution.
- Expects materially better performance in the packaging and converting divisions in H2.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Order intake in the domestic packaging market remains firm, with the opportunity pipeline growing by more than 3x and expanding in African/ROW markets.
- US packaging demand remains muted due to inflation (~6%) and consumer sentiment slowdown. US import duties were normalized to 10% recently, and shipping costs/times have increased significantly (container costs tripled to USD11,000, sailing time increased to 47 days).
- Government of India recently imposed anti-dumping duty on PET films, removing the 50 bps cost delta and making RecTech™ film marginally cheaper than PET-PE film.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Negative operating leverage from lower top-line and higher operating expenses (including exhibition/trade show costs in Q1) weighed heavily on H1 profitability.
- Crude oil and polymer prices remained elevated through H1, increasing customer working capital requirements and leading to delivery push-backs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Management is increasing investments in the packaging division (feet on the street, backend design, channel development).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Confirmed order backlog stands at close to Rs. 160 crores, with advances secured against all backlog orders.
- Aging receivables >6 months reduced to approximately Rs. 12 crores.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unresolved West Asia crisis and elevated crude/polymer prices.
- Persistent US market headwinds and consumer inflation.
- Factory disruption fallout resulting in a 10-month effective operating year.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Dhiren Anurag (Individual Investor) questioned the guidance revision from 15% growth to on-par/mid-single digits and the feasibility of H2 execution.** Management replied that strong order books (Rs. 160 crores backlog) and Rs. 35 crores of ready finished goods give confidence to deliver H2 numbers despite a 10-month operating year.
2. **Aditya Baid asked about flood-related revenue impact and costs.** Management stated Rs. 25 crores of dispatches moved out of Q2, insurance claims have been submitted, and no extraordinary costs have been booked yet pending insurance clearance.
3. **Mosam Shah asked about RecTech™ revenue potential and the impact of anti-dumping duty.** Apurva Kane noted that while exact revenue impact will take time, the elimination of the 5% cost delta due to anti-dumping duties on PET films will accelerate early adopter decision-making by March.
4. **Yug Baid asked about US headwinds and tariff impact.** Management explained that US packaging demand is muted due to consumer inflation (~6%), and shipping costs/times from India to the US have tripled/doubled.
5. **Dharmin Parekh questioned why Mamata focuses on niche products rather than mass markets or pharma packaging.** Management responded that their philosophy is to target niche applications with better margins (60%-120% more premium than domestic competitors) rather than chasing low-margin top lines, and that pharma packaging requires different product development currently on the drawing board.
6. **Sahil Doshi asked for quantification of US de-growth and margin sustainability.** Management declined to share specific US revenue numbers, reiterating that domestic and ROW growth will cover the shortfall, and fixed-cost absorption in H2 will drive bottom-line recovery.

### One-line takeaway
Despite a severe H1 dent from factory flooding and US headwinds, Mamata Machinery expects a strong H2 rebound supported by Rs. 160 crores in order backlog, Rs. 35 crores in ready inventory, and newly certified RecTech™ catalysts.
