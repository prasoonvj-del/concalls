# Synergy Green Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong demand and revenue growth, but PBDIT margins and PAT impacted by steep input cost escalations, logistics issues, and one-off electricity expenses.

### Key numbers
- **H1 FY27 Total Income:** Rs 192 crore (vs Rs 159.76 crore for H1 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 PBDIT:** Rs 17.13 crore (PBDIT margin of 8.9%)
- **Q2 FY27 PBDIT Margin:** 10.2% (vs 15.7% in Q2 FY26)
- **H1 FY27 PBDIT Margin:** 8.9% (vs 15.6% in H1 FY26)
- **H1 Production Growth YoY:** 25.8%
- **H1 Revenue Growth YoY:** 20.2%
- **Long-term Borrowings:** Rs 176.21 crore
- **Capacity Utilization:** H1 at ~75%; FY27 projected at ~80% (H2 projected at ~85%)
- **FY27 Guidance:** Revenue growth of 33%; Export revenue growth >50% (contributing ~30% of total revenue); PBDIT margins expected to expand by around 100 bps YoY.

### Highlights
- Foundry capacity stands at 45,000 tonnes per annum; in-house machining and surface treatment capacity stands at 20,000 tonnes per annum.
- Portfolio composition: Wind castings (~70%), wind gearbox castings (~15%), non-wind space including cone crushers, coal pulverizers, mining, and pumps (remainder).
- Working with 6 out of 15 leading global wind OEMs (Vestas, Envision, Siemens Gamesa, Nordex, Adani) and gearbox leaders (Flender, ZF).
- Finalized a deal with Nordex with a confirmed export order book of more than Rs 200 crore for US exports.
- 10 MW captive solar power portfolio helped contain energy cost increases.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains FY27 revenue growth guidance of 33%.
- Export revenues expected to grow by over 50% YoY, contributing around 30% of total revenues.
- PBDIT margins for FY27 expected to expand by around 100 bps YoY over the previous financial year, with H2 margins estimated around 17%–18%.
- Revenue potential from current capacity revised to Rs 600 crore – Rs 650 crore due to inflation and price revisions.
- Greenfield project expected to take 15–18 months timeline and will be evaluated in the next 2 quarters after stabilization.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global cumulative wind capacity projected to exceed 2 TW by 2030; Indian wind capacity expected to grow from 51 GW to 107 GW by 2030.
- AI boom and data center energy demand in the US are driving higher demand for renewable energy and wind installations.
- US market relies heavily on Indian foundries due to tariff barriers against China.
- Management states demand is currently exceeding company capacity, with open book orders from major OEMs.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- PBDIT margins impacted by an almost 8% cost impact from raw material price increases (e.g., furan resin prices up by 50%+).
- Unabsorbed manpower cost during the initial capacity ramp-up phase also impacted margins.
- Customer price corrections occur with a one-quarter lag (weighted average 5% price correction secured for Q3).
- Logistics costs for exports are borne by customers, but import logistics and container/ship availability for over-dimension consignments faced disruptions and higher costs due to war/geopolitical issues.
- One-off electricity impact of ~Rs 3 crore in H1 due to a retrospective high court verdict in favor of MSEDCL on solar energy banking.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Current foundry capacity: 45,000 tonnes per annum; Machining capacity: 20,000 tonnes per annum.
- Machine shop capacity utilization is at 45%–50%; full contribution expected in the next 5–6 months.
- Greenfield expansion deferred to focus on execution stability, training personnel, and proper capitalization before adding debt or diluting equity.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Long-term borrowings stand at Rs 176.21 crore.
- Inventory increased due to export castings unable to move to ports on time owing to ship availability constraints.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Commodity price volatility and steep input cost escalations (consumables like furan).
- Logistics disruptions and high shipping/insurance costs for export consignments.
- Retrospective electricity tariff and energy banking charges in Maharashtra.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Niteen Dharmawat (Investor/Analyst) asked about the feasibility of the 33% revenue growth and 18% EBITDA margin guidance for H2.** Management stated that top-line is supported by existing capacity and built-up inventory, and targeting 17%-18% EBITDA in H2 to achieve the overall 100 bps YoY margin expansion.
2. **Rajas Joshi (Investor/Analyst) asked about the discrepancy between 25.8% volume/production growth and 20.2% revenue growth.** Management explained the variance is due to inventory buildup sitting at cost price that has not yet been billed as revenue.
3. **Rajas Joshi (Investor/Analyst) asked for quantification of the electricity one-off in H1.** Management stated it was around Rs 3 crore (about 1.5% of EBITDA) due to retrospective charges following a high court verdict.
4. **Samarth (Investor/Analyst) asked why management is not aggressively expanding capacity via greenfield despite strong demand.** Management answered that execution capability, skilled manpower availability, and prudent financial/valuation considerations prevent aggressive overexpansion without prior stabilization.
5. **Girish (Investor/Analyst) asked when the company will turn net profit positive given recent quarters showed negative PAT despite revenue growth.** Management advised looking at EBITDA and cash profit (projected to grow >50% this year) rather than absolute PAT, citing heavy depreciation charges post-capitalization.
6. **Gulam Soni (Investor/Analyst) questioned if management is being too conservative given the cyclical renewable upswing.** Management reiterated that manufacturing large castings is an art requiring skilled labor and rigorous execution training, and they prefer building customer demand before rushing into greenfield capex.

### One-line takeaway
Synergy Green Industries maintains its 33% revenue growth guidance for FY27 driven by robust US wind demand, despite near-term margin pressure from raw material inflation, logistics constraints, and electricity one-offs.
