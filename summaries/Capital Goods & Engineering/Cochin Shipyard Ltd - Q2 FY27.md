# Cochin Shipyard Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-09-18 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted steady execution, strategic JVs (Drydocks World), and a robust order book/pipeline.

### Key numbers
- **Q1 FY27 Turnover:** Rs 1,094.21 crore (vs Rs 1,068.59 crore YoY)
- **Q1 FY27 PBT:** Rs 202.49 crore (vs Rs 249.54 crore YoY)
- **Q1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 151.45 crore (vs Rs 187.82 crore YoY)
- **Q1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** ~24%
- **Q1 FY27 PAT Margin:** ~14%
- **Current Unexecuted Order Book:** Rs 22,000 crore
- **Potential L1 Order:** 5 Next-Generation Survey Vessels (NGSV) for the Indian Navy valued at ~Rs 5,000 crore (total order book to reach ~Rs 27,000 crore post-conclusion)
- **ISRF JV Slump Sale Consideration:** Rs 1,800 crore (50% in cash, 50% in equity)
- **Tuticorin Facility One-time Lease Payment:** Rs 305.76 crore for 110 acres of land and 17.29 acres of waterfront for 30 years
- **Green Maritime Propulsion JV Revenue Target:** Rs 640 crore by 5th year (2031) with an estimated EBITDA margin of ~20%
- **Green Maritime Propulsion JV Capital:** Rs 50 crore total capital (60% HBL, 40% CSL)

### Highlights
- Delivered 3 vessels in FY27 so far: 3rd Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft (ASW SWC) for the Indian Navy, 2nd multipurpose vessel for a German client, and a Ro-Ro ferry for Kochi Municipal Corporation. Subsidiary Udupi Cochin Shipyard delivered 3 vessels (2 cargo vessels to Wilson Group, Norway, and 1 tug to Polestar Maritime/Adani Group).
- Approved a 50:50 JV with Drydocks World, Dubai (a DP World company) to own, operate, and manage the International Ship Repair Facility (ISRF) at Willingdon Island, Kochi via slump sale. The JV will also add 10 more workstations. JV agreement to be signed at the BRICS Summit in New Delhi.
- Terminated JV talks with HD KSOE of Korea for the Block Fabrication Facility at Vallarpadam, Kochi; CSL will proceed independently at a reduced capacity of 60,000 tonnes/annum (plus existing 12,000 tonnes/annum).
- Vadinar ship repair facility (jointly with Deendayal Port Authority) received CCEA approval in May; awaiting environmental clearance, targeting 300-600 crore turnover post-operationalization within 36 months of clearance.
- Emerged as successful bidder in e-auction for 30-year lease of 110 acres of land and waterfront at V.O. Chidambaranar Port, Tuticorin for a hybrid shipbuilding and ship repair facility.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guides for ~12% annual revenue growth conservatively, though actual delivery may end up between 12% to 15%.
- Blended EBITDA margin guided at ~14% conservatively (Shipbuilding: 10%–12%, Ship Repair: 22%–24%).
- Targeting 10 vessel deliveries in FY27.
- Operating cash flow expected to turn positive in FY27 as tail-ended export milestone payments are realized upon vessel deliveries.
- Target ROC of around 14% to 15% for new investments.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Defense pipeline includes LPD (4 numbers, ~Rs 32,000 crore), MCMV (12 numbers, ~Rs 36,000 crore), and P-17 Bravo (7 numbers, ~Rs 49,000 crore) RFPs.
- Government of India's demand aggregation targets 432 vessels over 10 years; SCI and BCSL have issued tenders in which CSL is participating.
- Green Tug Transition Program (GTTP) targets 50% green tugs by 2035–2040, creating a ~Rs 1,000 crore investment sector over 10 years.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Higher margins in the past 3 years were driven by nominated orders (aircraft carrier shipbuilding and refits) and steady interest income from a large cash surplus (~Rs 2,000–3,000 crore).
- With cash spent on capex (ISRF and Drydock without loans), cash surplus has reduced, impacting total returns and margins.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Funding new capex projects mostly through 80:20 debt-equity, targeting a 25% capex subsidy under the Government of India Shipbuilding Development Scheme and 3% interest subvention under the Maritime Development Fund.
- Block capacity: New block fabrication facility will add 60,000 tonnes/annum, bringing total throughput to 72,000 tonnes/annum.
- ISRF revenue potential: Expected to start with ~Rs 600 crore turnover over the next 2 years, scaling to Rs 1,000–1,200 crore over 5 years.
- Tuticorin facility: Expected to start with ~Rs 650–700 crore turnover, scaling to Rs 1,800–2,000 crore in 7 to 8 years.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Operating cash flow was negative in FY26 due to tail-ended milestone payments for European export orders (30% during construction, 70% upon delivery). Cash flow is expected to normalize and turn positive in FY27 as 10 vessels are delivered.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Defense tender timelines (such as NGSV finalization or P-17 Bravo) are lengthy, subject to CCS approval, Price Negotiation Committee (PNC), and not in management's control.
- Electric-propelled tugs cost almost twice as much as conventional diesel-propelled tugs, which may cause mass adoption to take 3 to 4 years.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Garvit Goyal (Serene Alpha) on Green Maritime Propulsion JV:** Management clarified that HBL holds 60% and CSL holds 40%; HBL will manufacture the batteries while CSL provides marine expertise ("marinization"). Total capital is Rs 50 crore. Target revenue is Rs 640 crore by the 5th year with ~20% EBITDA margin.
2. **Dipen Vakil (Individual Investor) on Drydocks World JV operations:** Management clarified that ISRF handles vessels up to 130 meters and 6,000 tonnes. CSL's captive defense/government work will continue to be executed via the JV, while DDW will bring in international clients. No market share overlap with CSL's main yard operations.
3. **Mohit Chaurasiya (Individual Investor) on Negative OCF in FY26:** Management explained that export contracts have tail-ended cash flows (70% on delivery). With 10 vessel deliveries slated for FY27, cash flow will turn positive. ROC target for new investments is 14%–15%.
4. **Bhavya Gandhi (Investor) on Order Wins & Margins:** Management expects NGSV contract signing around November and a repeat dredger order from DCI (~Rs 1,300 crore) within 2 months. Blended EBITDA margin guided at 14%.
5. **Abhishek Poddar (Investor) on Margin Normalization:** Management stated historical high margins were due to aircraft carrier orders and high interest income on cash reserves. With capex spent, interest income is lower, and competitive bidding in defense reduces margins to a 14%–15% blended range.
6. **Deepak Krishnan (Kotak Institutional Equities) on Capex Revenue Potential & IAC-2:** Management stated ISRF potential is Rs 600–1,200 crore, Vadinar is Rs 300–600 crore, and Tuticorin is Rs 650–2,000 crore. On IAC-2, management stated it has not even reached the Approval of Necessity (AoN) stage with the Navy.

### One-line takeaway
Cochin Shipyard is expanding its ship repair and shipbuilding footprint through strategic JVs (such as Drydocks World for ISRF) and brownfield/greenfield expansions, anticipating positive cash flows in FY27 as a robust Rs 22,000+ crore order book is executed.
