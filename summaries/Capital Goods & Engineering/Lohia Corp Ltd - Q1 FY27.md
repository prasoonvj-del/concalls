# Lohia Corp Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-27 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong revenue growth of 60% YoY, significant margin expansion, and a robust order book.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** Rs 503 crore, up 60% YoY (from Rs 315 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 100 crore, up 276% YoY (from Rs 36 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 19.9%, compared to 11.5% in Q1 FY26.
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 66 crore, compared to Rs 17 crore in Q1 FY26.
- **PAT Margin:** 13%.
- **Order Book:** Approximately Rs 1,778 crore (or Rs 1,780 crore), up 30% since March 2026 and up 195% since June 2025; backed by ~20% customer advances.
- **Revenue Mix:** Domestic 59%, Exports 41%.
- **Net Working Capital Cycle:** ~81 days as of the end of the quarter.
- **Capacity Utilization:** ~70% to 72% currently (potential peak ideal utilization around 80% to 85%).
- **R&D Spend:** ~3% of revenue.

### Highlights
- First earnings call as a listed company.
- Rolled out new products via in-house R&D: Multi-layer coating line CoEx 1600, nova 6 plus (high-speed six-shuttle circular loom up to 1,150 picks/min), and 1-loop/2-loop FIBC bag cutting machine.
- Established a virtual remote assistance center in Kanpur for real-time customer support.
- Technical Training and Research Centre (TTRC) introduced a 3-month residential training and certification program for block bottom bag machines.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects top-line growth in the range of 20% to 25% going forward.
- Management internally targets a 20% EBITDA margin as the "new normal."
- Current order book execution period is 6 to 9 months; management indicated potential revenues closer to Rs 2,150 to Rs 2,200 crore for the year if conditions hold.
- Existing capacities can support up to Rs 2,400 to Rs 2,500 crore in turnover with minor balancing.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Growth driven by increasing non-packaging applications (geotextiles, ground cover, tarpaulins, roof underlayment) alongside traditional packaging (cement, chemicals, food grain, fertilizers, shopping bags, FIBCs).
- Government impetus against single-use plastics is driving new non-single-use plastic applications.
- Majority of the order book demand (95%+) is coming from new customer expansions, with replacement demand currently at 3% to 5% (expected to increase over time).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margins driven by higher volumes, operating leverage, better efficiencies, periodic sales price revisions, short/medium-term supplier contracts, and tweaked discount structures.
- Export prices average 12% to 15% higher than domestic market prices, though net margins are balanced by CIF freight costs and agency commissions.
- Commands a 15% to 20% price premium over Chinese competitors.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Company is asset-light with an asset turnover ratio of 3x to 4x.
- Beyond Rs 2,500 crore in turnover, major capex would be required (estimated at Rs 80 to Rs 90 crore or Rs 100 crore for every Rs 500 crore turnover increment).
- Plant expansion can be executed within 5 to 6 months using available adjacent land in Kanpur.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Company continues to be net debt-negative (net cash).
- Net working capital cycle stood at 81 days.
- Customer advances stand at approximately 20% of the order book.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Macro-level uncertainties (geopolitical conflicts/wars) impact raw material movements and supply chains.
- Company does not lock in long-term raw material prices with large steel suppliers like Hindustan Steel or Tata Steel.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kiran (Table Tree) asked about the steep jump in order book over recent years.** Management attributed it to post-COVID investment upticks, expanding non-packaging applications, and government curbs on single-use plastics.
2. **Viraj asked if rupee depreciation against other currencies helps win export orders against Starlinger.** Management stated depreciation helps in negotiations and discounts, though competition in developed markets is from Starlinger and in Southeast Asia mainly from China.
3. **Harshit Patel asked what drove the resilient margins despite commodity inflation.** Management cited regular sales price revisions, short/medium-term supplier contracts, and managed discounting structures.
4. **Akshay Satija asked about product-level gross margins and replacement revenue.** Management stated gross margins are similar across products and evaluated at the project level, while consumable spare parts contribute 2% to 3% of revenue perpetually with 2% to 3% higher gross margins.
5. **Rishi Maheshwari asked why Lohia grows consistently while its customer industries show volatile capex.** Management explained that Lohia holds technological barriers as a machinery provider, whereas bag-making end-users face lower entry/technical barriers and higher competition.
6. **Arvind Arora asked about capacity bottlenecks and capex plans.** Management clarified they operate at ~70%–72% capacity, have land available in Kanpur, and can expand within 5 to 6 months; major capex is only triggered after crossing Rs 2,400–2,500 crore revenue.

### One-line takeaway
Lohia Corp reported a strong Q1 FY27 with 60% revenue growth and a 19.9% EBITDA margin, underpinned by a robust order book of Rs 1,778 crore and rising demand across both packaging and non-packaging technical textile applications.
