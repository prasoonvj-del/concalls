# Texmaco Rail & Engineering Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-17 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Sequential operational improvement (revenue up 15% QoQ, EBITDA margin 10.2%) offset by YoY top-line contraction (down ~31%) due to lower Indian Railways wagon ordering.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained general Vision 2030 / Texmaco 2.0 targets (Rs 5,000–6,000 crore core top-line; total target Rs 9,000–10,000 crore); no specific fiscal year guidance number revised.
- **Tone and demand view:** Tone remains execution-focused but acknowledges temporary YoY revenue drag from reduced Indian Railways commodity wagon procurement; offset by private and export order momentum.
- **New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared:** US tariff fluctuations impacting exports discussed (now stabilizing); tariff regimes on foundries highlighted; Rs 700 crore risk mitigation provisions defended; defense business capex of Rs 200 crore earmarked.
- **Last quarter's promises:** Rail Infra and Bright Power execution improved (electrical infra rose to 27.8% of turnover/Rs 250 crore in Q2); credit rating upgraded to CRISIL A+ (from A).

### Key numbers
- **Standalone Revenue from Operations:** Rs 863 crore (up 14.6% / 15% QoQ, down from ~Rs 1,250 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 88 crore; EBITDA Margin at 10.2% (vs 10.8% in Q1 FY27).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 36 crore (Profit Before Tax: Rs 51.2 crore vs Rs 44.1 crore in Q1 FY27).
- **Deliveries & Production:** 1,106 wagons delivered; Steel Foundry produced 6,468 metric tonnes.
- **Order Book:** ~Rs 8,000 crore in Freight Rolling Stock (executable over this year and next, over 10 quarters total); Infra/component order deliverable around Rs 3,000–3,500 crore (Electrical Infra order backlog at ~Rs 1,600 crore).
- **Revenue Mix:** Freight cars contributed 62.3% of revenue (vs 68.8% in Q1 FY27 and 74.7% in Q2 FY26); Infra electrical business at 27.8%; non-freight car revenue reached 37%.
- **Order Mix:** Core freight rolling stock order book is 80%–85% private & export, and 15%–16% Indian Railways.

### Highlights
- CRISIL upgraded the company’s long-term credit rating to A+ (from A); ESG rating improved to 51–59 grade.
- Shift toward private and export orders de-risked operations from over-reliance on Indian Railways tenders.
- Freight Car Division orders executable over the next 10 quarters.
- Export share in order book shot up to 54% YoY (vs 9% in Q2 FY26).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to scale revenue to Rs 9,000–10,000 crore under Texmaco 2.0 (Rs 4,000–5,000 crore core manufacturing plus Rs 5,000–6,000 crore from adjacencies/diversification).
- Management expects sequential operational and profit improvement from Q3 FY27 onward.
- Electrical infra business targeting 4-figure annual top-line run-rate.
- Steel foundry dispatches expected to reach 35,000–40,000 MT in FY27, with 25%–30% coming from exports.
- South African wagon and locomotive export order deliveries expected to accrue revenue starting FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Indian Railways procurement slowed for commodity wagons YoY; recent Indian Railways option-clause allocation prioritized existing type-contract holders.
- Ministry of Railways focusing on simplifying private procurement policies and increasing rail modal share for freight (e.g., coal movement).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margins defended via supply chain cost optimization, waste reduction, power/fuel savings, freight renegotiations, and higher-margin export mix.
- Finance costs reduced QoQ and YoY due to lower interest rates and debt repayment.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Core manufacturing requires minimal incremental capex; current capacity is being sweated.
- Earmarked ~Rs 200 crore capex for defense business entry. Executed 40% of planned overall growth/maintenance capex for the year.
- Setting up a maintenance facility in South Africa; locomotives to be manufactured in India and exported.
- Steel foundry capacity expanded to 60,000 MT/year across both foundries.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt is being retired every quarter; capex requirements funded via operational surpluses without needing short-term debt.
- Private and export contracts structured with significant advance payments or LCs, mitigating receivable and working capital risks.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Customer design changes and non-standard private/export specifications require longer lead times, keeping volume output lower than commodity wagon runs.
- Geopolitical uncertainties and tariff fluctuations (particularly US market tariffs) present external headwinds.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Balasubramanian (Arihant Capital) on Wheel JV MoU and capex/debt plans:** Management stated the wheel business entry aims for heavy-haul and high-speed train applications; core business needs no major capex, growth capex (e.g., Rs 200 crore in defense) will be funded out of internal accruals while systematically retiring debt.
- **Parvez Qazi (Nuvama) on Indian Railways option order and South Africa locomotives:** Management clarified Indian Railways option order was distributed based on statutory/contractual execution history; South African locomotive and wagon execution will span FY28 plus 1 quarter, with 15-year maintenance. Locomotives will be exported from India.
- **Hari Kumar S. (Individual Investor) on RVNL JV and Nymwag JV status:** Management stated RVNL JV is awaiting larger tender opportunities (expected within FY27); Nymwag JV received initial G-105 approval from RDSO and plant is ready, awaiting prototype order allocation.
- **Viren Patel (Balaji Securities) on Rs 700 crore provision and order execution schedule:** Management reiterated the Rs 700 crore provision was a geopolitical risk mitigation step taken in prior quarters. Confirmed rolling stock order book (~Rs 8,000 crore) is executable over the next 10 quarters.

### One-line takeaway
Texmaco Rail is navigating lower short-term Indian Railways volume by expanding private/export order mix (85%), upgrading its credit rating to A+, and deploying internal cash flows toward growth adjacencies.
