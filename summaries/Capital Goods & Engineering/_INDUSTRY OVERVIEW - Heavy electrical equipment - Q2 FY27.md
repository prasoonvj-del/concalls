# Capital Goods & Engineering - Heavy electrical equipment: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Across the heavy electrical equipment and capital goods sector, management commentary for Q2 FY27 reflects robust structural demand driven by domestic infrastructure investments, the renewable energy transition, power transmission grid modernizations, and global "China Plus One" supply chain diversification. While top-line execution remains strong—with multiple companies reporting double-digit revenue growth and record order books (such as ISGEC crossing Rs 10,273 crore consolidated and Quality Power crossing Rs 2,000 crore)—profitability has faced localized headwinds. Geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East and West Asia have driven temporary spikes in steel, copper, aluminum, and freight/logistics costs. However, companies with robust pricing power or contractual variation clauses are successfully passing on inflation with a lag. 

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Managements universally agree that the secular demand environment across power transmission, renewables integration, and data center-driven power requirements is exceptionally strong. Export demand, particularly from the US, Europe, Middle East, and Africa, is robust, aided by supply chain shifts away from China.
*   **Divergence:** While firms like TD Power Systems, Karamtara Engineering, and Quality Power report unprecedented order inflow visibility extending out to 2030, others are deliberately shifting strategy. For instance, ISGEC is actively avoiding long-duration, civil-heavy projects in favor of shorter-cycle, technology-oriented products to de-risk its order book and improve cash flows. Meanwhile, Inox Wind experienced strategic realignment and withdrew full-year revenue guidance amid organizational transitions.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Agreements:** Most players (including GEE Ltd, ISGEC, Karamtara, and Quality Power) noted margin compression or temporary material cost increases in Q2 due to geopolitical Middle East conflicts driving up steel, aluminum, resin, and shipping/freight costs. 
*   **Disagreements & Mitigants:** While some companies like GEE experienced a direct squeeze on EBITDA margins due to steel hoarding and inventory timing, others like TD Power Systems and Karamtara insulated themselves via back-to-back procurement contracts and price variation formulas. Quality Power noted that standalone material costs rose from 35% to 45% due to normalized vendor drawbacks and rising metal prices, but maintained strong target EBITDA group margins around 25%. Triveni Power Transmission acknowledged a YoY margin moderation due to product mix changes.

## Capex, capacity & expansion
Capital expenditure across the sector is running at a brisk pace, primarily funded via internal accruals or recent equity raises (IPOs/QIPs):
*   **TD Power Systems:** Advancing Plant 4 (Siemens Energy 2-pole generator project), targeted for completion by calendar Q1 2028.
*   **Karamtara Engineering:** Expanding sellable capacity toward 1.4 million tons with ongoing projects in Bhachau and Saudi Arabia (trial production starting December).
*   **ISGEC Heavy Engineering:** Executing a planned Rs 502 crore capex program (deploying Rs 199 crore in H1) across presses, skids, and tube/pipe shops.
*   **Quality Power:** Commissioned the Sangli global coil factory and a new PCS factory in Turkey, while preparing a Rs 700 crore QIP to fund debt reduction, working capital, and subsidiary capacities.
*   **Tempsens Instruments:** Five capacity expansion projects remain on track for commissioning between Q4 FY27 and Q3 FY28, targeting a peak revenue potential of ~Rs 500 crore.
*   **GEE Ltd:** Planned CapEx of Rs 35–40 crore over two years for flux-cored wirelines and MIG wire capacity expansion.
*   **Triveni Power Transmission:** Completed its long-term Rs 340 crore capex program, creating a peak gear revenue potential of ~Rs 700 crore.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance Adjustments:** 
    *   *TD Power Systems:* Raised full-year FY27 revenue guidance from INR2,600 crore to INR2,900–3,000 crore and initiated FY28 guidance at INR3,400–3,500 crore.
    *   *Inox Wind:* Cut/Withdrawn specific FY27 revenue growth and EBITDA margin targets amid strategic realignment, introducing an FY28 revenue guidance of ~Rs 7,500 crore.
    *   *GEE Ltd, Karamtara, Tempsens, and Quality Power:* Maintained existing growth guidance.
*   **Tone Shifts & Management Changes:** 
    *   GEE shifted slightly to acknowledge short-term margin pressures from geopolitical conflicts, remaining structurally bullish. 
    *   Inox Wind announced the resignation of CEO Sanjeev Agarwal (transitioning to an advisory role), pivoting its tone toward balance sheet discipline over pure top-line quantum.
*   **Operational Milestones:** Hitachi Energy officially commenced revenue recognition on its large HVDC contracts (Bhadla and Khavda); GEE successfully handed over the Thane Wagle Industrial Estate land for real estate monetization.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **Margin Recovery:** Whether companies experiencing temporary Q2 margin compression (GEE, ISGEC, Quality Power) successfully restore operating margins in Q3/Q4 as raw material price volatility eases and price hikes take effect.
*   **Working Capital & Cash Flows:** Monitoring working capital elongation reported by Quality Power (due to cable backward integration) and Hitachi Energy (due to HVDC contract asset build-up).
*   **Execution of Expansion Plans:** Progress on Karamtara's Saudi facility, TD Power's Plant 4 ground-breaking, and Tempsens' new OEM production lines.
