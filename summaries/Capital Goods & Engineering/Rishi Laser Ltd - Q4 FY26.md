# Rishi Laser Ltd - Q4 FY26 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-06-09 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q4/FY26
**Tone:** Cautious - Management admitted to execution and leadership failures in capital and human resource commissioning, leading to missed targets, though they are optimistic about operational leverage kicking in.

### Key numbers
- **FY26 Revenue:** Rs 160 crore (7% YoY growth)
- **FY26 PAT:** Rs 3.67 crore (55% YoY decline)
- **FY26 EBITDA Margin:** 8.7% (down from 9.1%)
- **FY26 Exports:** Rs 22.83 crore (14.2% of revenue)
- **Total Assets:** Rs 148.7 crore
- **Property, Plant & Equipment (PPE):** Rs 94.7 crore
- **Debt-to-Equity:** 0.29x
- **Malur Facility Revenue Target (FY29):** Rs 100 crore (aiming for Rs 60 crore in FY27)
- **Company Revenue CAGR Target:** ~20% over the next three years
- **Robotics Revenue (FY26):** ~Rs 2 crore (targeting Rs 5-10 crore for FY27)

### Highlights
- Malur plant is fully operational with tooling in place; Phase 1 of the in-house paint shop went live in June 2026.
- Human capital restructuring at Malur is complete, and ramp-up costs of FY26 will not repeat at the same scale in FY27.
- Construction equipment fabrication accounts for 53% of total revenue.
- Pune plant utilization is expected to reach ~80% (targeting ~Rs 50 crore revenue) by Q3 FY27.
- Vadodara facility growth remains subdued due to a major customer's business contraction.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Guidance:** Maintained a conservative stance on overall revenue targets despite positive customer traction, citing geopolitical and raw material uncertainties.
- **FY27 Outlook:** Expects higher throughput, improving margins via operating leverage, fully loaded finance/depreciation costs, and no commissioning drag.
- **EBITDA Margin Band:** Management expects margins in the 8% to 11% range.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Construction equipment demand is strong; Caterpillar's verticals indicate a good ramp-up with expected 15-20% business volume growth for Rishi Laser.
- Global push for alternative sourcing from Western companies benefiting Indian suppliers in forging and fabrication.
- Power sector is mixed; transformer segment is booming, but Schneider (key customer in medium voltage switchgear) saw drastic drops in offtake.
- Emerging opportunities in Caterpillar's engine division (Hosur/Perkins) for power generation/data centers, and new scaffolding/aluminium formwork segments.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- **Gross Margins:** Maintained around 48-50% over the last three years.
- **Employee Costs:** Rose sharply from Rs 20 crore (FY23) to Rs 33 crore (FY26) due to duplication of work and onboarding during plant transitions; quarterly run rate expected around Rs 9.5-10 crore. Minimum wage pressures (e.g., Karnataka up to 30%) pose challenges.
- **Raw Material Pass-through:** Quarterly pass-through mechanism for the majority of customers if prices fluctuate by more than ±5%, with a one-quarter lag.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Malur plant capitalized, with a remaining max Rs 2-3 crore investment pending for Phase 2 of the paint shop.
- Tube manufacturing expansion plan put aside for the next 12 months to focus on stabilizing existing operations.
- Installing 8-10 additional robots across facilities in FY27 to manage labor costs and improve automation.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Disciplined debt-to-equity ratio of 0.29x.
- Finance costs expected to remain around Q4 levels.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Execution and leadership risks in managing large-scale capital projects and human capital readiness.
- Rising labor costs and minimum wage hikes (especially in Karnataka).
- Subdued offtake from certain key power/switchgear clients like Schneider.
- Geopolitical uncertainties and raw material price volatility.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Dhwanil Desai on Malur Revenue & Overlap:** Asked if the Rs 60 crore Malur revenue for FY27 is additive to the Rs 160 crore base. *Management clarified that Rs 25-30 crore of that business was already executed from the old Bommasandra plant, so it is not a direct addition.*
2. **Viraj Mehta on Multiple Plants and Low Utilization:** Questioned the logic of adding Malur when Pune and Vadodara had idle capacity, questioning long-term EBITDA margin stagnation at ~10%. *Management explained Malur was built to meet specific space/capability requirements demanded by Caterpillar, and Pune utilization is expected to hit 80% this year.*
3. **Rahul Jain on Employee Cost Surge:** Asked about the sharp increase in employee expenses to Rs 33 crore. *Management cited work duplication, parallel plant operations during transition, and highlighted upcoming robotic automation to counter rising labor rates.*
4. **Manoj Dua on Long-term Margins:** Challenged management on why EBITDA margins remain capped at 8-11% despite esteemed clients and robotic facilities. *Management agreed that achieving double-digit EBITDA levels is imperative and treated past margins as a failure of growth execution.*

### One-line takeaway
Rishi Laser absorbed heavy transition and human capital costs in FY26 due to commissioning delays at its Malur plant, but expects operating leverage, higher export traction, and automation to drive growth toward a 20% CAGR over the next three years.
