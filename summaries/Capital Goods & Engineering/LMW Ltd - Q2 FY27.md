# LMW Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-26 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue grew 30% YoY with strong order inflows and robust demand in the machine tool division, though management remains cautious regarding cost pressures in the textile division.

### Key numbers
- Revenue for Q2 FY27: ₹1,153 crores (vs ₹815 crores in Q2 FY25 [stated as September 2025]), up ~30% YoY.
- H1 FY27 revenue: ₹2,045 crores (vs ₹1,537 crores in H1 FY26), up 33% YoY.
- PBT for the period: ₹204 crores (vs ₹93 crores in the previous period).
- Textile Machinery Division (TMD) Q2 turnover: ₹639 crores, up 32% QoQ and 38% YoY.
- TMD H1 turnover: ₹1,121 crores (vs ₹876 crores), up ~28% YoY; H1 profit stands at ₹48 crores (vs ₹2 crores YoY).
- TMD order book: ~₹3,400 crores (active order book ~₹2,600 crores).
- TMD sales breakup: 67% domestic, 8% exports, 25% spares (domestic). Capacity utilization is ~60%.
- LMW Global (Dubai) H1 turnover: ₹100 crores (vs ₹71 crores for H1 FY26); export order book is closer to ₹163 crores.
- LMW China H1 turnover: ₹36 crores (vs ₹41 crores YoY); order book is around ₹128 crores.
- Machine Tool Division (MTD) and Foundry H1 turnover: ₹794 crores (vs ₹555 crores YoY), with 9% relating to Foundry. MTD capacity utilization is 75% to 80%. MTD mix: 23-24% machining centers, 76% turning centers.
- Advanced Technology Centre (ATC) H1 turnover: ₹135 crores (vs ₹97 crores YoY); capacity utilization is closer to 70%; order book is around ₹1,000 crores (30% composites, 70% metallics).

### Highlights
- TMD sales mix stands at 67% domestic, 8% exports, and 25% domestic spares.
- LMW Global and LMW China are consolidating overseas operations under LMW Holding in Dubai, helping reach markets like Egypt and Kenya.
- Entered a JV with Infinium Developers for a 4.2-acre land parcel development on revenue sharing, valued at ₹20 to ₹24 crores, realizable over 24 to 30 months.
- MTD is seeing strong execution with the China+1 strategy playing out on the ground and healthy demand for machining centers.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains a cautious outlook on the textile division due to uncharted territories, second-order war impacts, commodity price hikes, and electronic component availability.
- Capex for the current year will be similar to depreciation, with some leased assets also being utilized.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Textile demand is supported by government policies, such as the Karnataka ₹4,000-crore textile policy, export initiatives, and FTA policies.
- Auto sector remains the predominant driver for MTD (upwards of 50%), while Defense, EMS, and tooling are also expanding.
- ATC is experiencing good traction in the composite segment alongside metallics, with increasing focus on the Indian defense ecosystem (drones, missiles, radar systems).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- TMD operates on fixed pricing for the domestic OEM industry, with no immediate pass-through for rising costs; internal Value Analysis and Value Engineering (VAVE) is being deployed to protect margins.
- Fixed costs have been well-contained through digitization, automation, and cost-control initiatives.
- Rising costs noted in copper, aluminum, electronics, and general commodities.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- MTD capacity is being expanded via an existing unit repurposed for assemblies to support a quick ramp-up.
- ATC is planning a ₹150 crore investment (per previous MoU with the Tamil Nadu government), with timelines to be announced later.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Overseas investments through LMW Holding were initially utilized to acquire subsidiaries (like LMW China) and repatriate funds, with active investments now focused on capacity and market reach.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Challenges and financial constraints in export markets like Bangladesh and Turkey affecting overseas top-line absorption and leading to losses in overseas entities.
- Unprecedented cost increases in raw materials (copper, aluminum) and tightening availability of electronics impacting TMD margins.
- Strict penalty clauses in ATC contracts require careful matching of order intake with execution capacity.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Poojan Shah asked about ATC peak revenue and asset turns, and TMD margin expectations.** Management stated asset turns typically range from 1:1 to 1:1.3 depending on material scope, and remain cautious on TMD margins due to raw material cost pressures.
- **Manish Goyal asked about overseas losses (LMW Global) and MTD capacity expansion details.** Management explained that overseas losses stemmed from unabsorbed fixed costs due to subdued top-line growth in challenging markets like Bangladesh and Turkey, while MTD capacity additions are immediate, agile assembly ramp-ups.
- **Manish Shah inquired about the auto-corner market and 5-axis machine adoption.** Management stated auto-coners represent a ~₹1,500+ crore addressable market traditionally relying on imports, while 5-axis machines currently comprise <5% of the market but are growing due to labor scarcity.
- **Suraj Maloo asked about the potential for EMS machines and the Articles of Association expansion.** Management clarified that the AoA changes were enabling resolutions for future opportunities, while EMS-specific machining centers are seeing good demand.
- **Jay Unadkat asked about structural yarn capacity demand and MTD growth drivers.** Management noted global yarn demand remains supported by China and Bangladesh constraints, while MTD growth is driven by broad-based sector demand (Auto, Defense, EMS) and upgrading trends.

### One-line takeaway
LMW reported a robust 30% YoY revenue growth in Q2 FY27 driven by strong momentum in the Machine Tool and Advanced Technology divisions, even as management maintains a cautious stance on textile machinery margins amid rising input costs.
