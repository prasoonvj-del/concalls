# GEE Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-16 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported strong H1 YoY growth and milestone real estate land handover, viewing margin compression as a temporary result of geopolitical steel cost inflation.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (revenue target of Rs 1,000 crore by FY29-30; sustainable EBITDA margins of 12% to 13% over the next 3 to 4 years).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly to acknowledge short-term margin pressures from geopolitical war situations and oil/steel price hikes, but remains strongly bullish on India's infrastructure, defence, and clean energy tailwinds.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Container issues and Middle East belt delays impacting short-term exports were newly discussed; promoter pledge details were absent this quarter.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Handed over possession of the Wagle Industrial Estate (Thane) land to the developer in record time, meeting the monetization milestone.

### Key numbers
- H1 FY27 Turnover/Revenue: Rs 204 crore (up 24% YoY from Rs 165 crore)
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs 16 crore (up 17.5% YoY from Rs 13.5 crore)
- H1 FY27 PBT: Rs 10.7 crore (up from Rs 7 crore)
- Q2 FY28/FY29 target: EBITDA margin target of 12% to 13% in next 3-4 years (with 0.5% increase in FY27 and 0.5% to 1% in FY28)
- CapEx guidance: Rs 35 crore to Rs 40 crore over the next two years
- Working capital limits: ~Rs 100 crore sanctioned limits, with ~Rs 40 crore utilized
- Market share: Currently 6% to 6.5% of the organized market, targeting 10% to 12% at Rs 1,000 crore revenue
- Export turnover target: 15% to 20% in next 2 to 3 years
- Real estate revenue expectation: Rs 400 crore plus over the next five years

### Highlights
- Handed over possession of the Wagle Industrial Estate land in Thane to the developer for a real estate project in record time.
- Consolidated manufacturing capacity stands at 59,000 metric tonnes (38,000 MT in Kolkata, 21,000 MT elsewhere), targeted to reach 71,000 MT by FY29-30.
- Added more than 130 new dealers in the last six months, expanding penetration into southern India and the central cement/infrastructure belts.
- Flux-cored wireline capacity installation completed, with commercial production expected to begin by the last week of November.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains a top-line growth target of 25% to 30% YoY.
- Targets reaching Rs 1,000 crore revenue by FY29-30 (more realistically FY30).
- Sustainable EBITDA margins projected at 12% to 13% over the next 3 to 4 years, with a 0.5% increase expected in FY27 and 0.5% to 1% in FY28.
- Real estate project cash accruals expected to begin from the coming financial year (first quarter).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global welding consumable market projected to grow from $1.2 billion (2024) to $2.4 billion by 2032-33; Indian organized market shifting from unorganized players and substituting European imports.
- Key growth sectors highlighted: Defence and shipbuilding, power and energy (including nuclear target of 100 gigawatts by 2040), railways, steel, and infrastructure freight corridors.
- Industry demand is seasonal, with H2 typically contributing >55% of revenue compared to 40% to 45% in H1.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margin and operating profitability faced temporary pressure in Q2 due to exponential spikes in steel prices and Middle East war-driven oil inflation.
- Management expects margins to restore to 9% and above in Q3 and Q4.
- Specialized and niche value-add products (e.g., silver brazing alloy wires, nickel wires, GTX series) account for ~27% of the product basket, priced between Rs 275 to Rs 1,500/kg versus conventional products at Rs 100 to Rs 125/kg; target is 35% to 40% mix next year.
- Price increases can be passed on to customers with a lag of 15 to 25 days.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned CapEx outlay of Rs 35 crore to Rs 40 crore over the next two years, targeting an 18% to 20% return.
- CapEx directed toward flux-cored wirelines, soft flux lines, and doubling MIG wire production capacity from 12,000 MT to 24,000 MT.
- Capacity utilization targeted at >90% by FY29-30.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Working capital inventory days are currently over 100 days (127 days) due to deliberate stock-up of steel amidst price volatility; management aims to bring it sub-100 days and down to peer levels by FY28.
- Short-term borrowings/working capital utilization increased to Rs 56.6 crore as of September end due to advance payments placed with steel suppliers (JSW, SAIL, TATA), which normalized post-quarter. The company has no long-term term loans.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical war situations in the Middle East causing oil and steel price volatility and short-term container/consignment delays for exports.
- High working capital inventory holding days due to strategic steel stocking.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Praneeth Bommisetty asked about the real estate cash accrual timeline and model.** Management replied that cash accruals begin next financial year (first quarter) via an area-share revenue model handled through a mix of outright sales, long-term leases, and retained units.
2. **Mishka Dani asked about the divergence between revenue growth and profit decline in Q2.** Management explained it was due to temporary input steel cost inflation and war conditions, and guided for EBITDA margins to return to 9%+ in Q3/Q4.
3. **Neha Sharma asked about the utilization of real estate asset monetization proceeds.** Management stated proceeds will fund brownfield inorganic acquisitions in tier-2/3 companies, reward shareholders, and optimize fund utilization without adding debt.
4. **Kumar Saurabh asked how GEE will bridge the margin gap with peers like ESAB and Ador.** Management answered that the gap will be closed through higher price realization driven by strong brand goodwill, shifting product mix toward 35%-40% niche offerings, and operating leverage from scale.
5. **Kumar Saurabh asked about the visibility of 25%-30% growth without long-term order books.** Management stated visibility comes from domestic macroeconomic momentum, government infrastructure push, H2 seasonality, and market share gains from unorganized players.

### One-line takeaway
GEE Ltd. posted a 24% YoY revenue increase for H1 FY27 alongside a key real estate land handover, navigating temporary steel cost pressures while maintaining its long-term Rs 1,000 crore revenue roadmap by FY30.
