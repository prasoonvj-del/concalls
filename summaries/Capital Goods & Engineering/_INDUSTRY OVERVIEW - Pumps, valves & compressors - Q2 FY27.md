# Capital Goods & Engineering - Pumps, valves & compressors: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the pumps, valves, and compressors sector reflects a bifurcated operational environment. While most companies—such as **Kirloskar Brothers Ltd (KBL)**, **HD Fire Protect**, and **Shakti Pumps**—reported robust top-line growth and surging order books driven by strong domestic industrial and solar demand, profitability across the sector faced severe headwinds. Managements universally cited rising raw material inflation (steel, copper, aluminium) and project lumpiness or delayed government scheme execution (such as PM-KUSUM 2.0) as pressures that compressed margins, prompting strategic pivots toward backward integration and cost-optimization initiatives.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus (Agreement):** Managements across **Elgi Equipments**, **KSB Ltd**, **KBL**, and **HD Fire Protect** agree that domestic industrial demand—spanning thermal power, oil & gas, data centers, and general manufacturing—remains buoyant and structurally sound. 
*   **Divergence:** Divergence is stark in government-backed agricultural and water schemes. While **Shakti Pumps** saw steady solar pump execution (23,865 units, +7% YoY) and **Oswal Pumps** diversified into rooftop and utility-scale EPC, both companies reported headwinds from the delayed rollout of the PM-KUSUM 2.0 scheme. Meanwhile, international demand shows regional contrasts: **Elgi** noted a muted portables market in Europe due to economic downturns, whereas **HD Fire** and **KBL** highlighted strong oil & gas demand from the Middle East and the U.S.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
Commodity price inflation—specifically in copper, steel, aluminium, zinc, and gun metal—exerted downward pressure on margins across almost all players (**Elgi**, **KSB**, **KBL**, **Oswal**, **Shakti**, **HD Fire**). 
*   **Agreements & Disagreements:** While companies like **Elgi** and **HD Fire** successfully implemented price hikes or relied on pass-through mechanisms to manage input costs, **KSB** struggled with low-margin, fixed-price contracts that created a 6-to-9-month lag in price realization. **KBL** and **WPIL** attributed temporary margin dips to adverse product mix shifts (lower high-margin service revenues relative to capital goods projects). 
*   **Mitigation:** To counter margin compression, companies are leaning on internal cost reductions (**Shakti**, **Elgi**) and backward integration—such as **Oswal's** proposed 1.2 GW solar cell facility to eliminate external supplier markups.

## Capex & capacity
Capital expenditure remains focused on backward integration, scaling manufacturing, and global footprints:
*   **Oswal Pumps** reprioritized its capex, dropping certain module/frame expansions to fund a Rs 455.85 crore investment for a 1.2 GW TOPCon solar cell facility (commercial production expected April 2028).
*   **Shakti Pumps** is advancing its Rs 1,700 crore total capex plan, having spent ~Rs 200 crore on its 2.2 GW solar cell plant, with its 500 MW solar panel facility entering trial production.
*   **Elgi Equipments** continues its Rs 600 crore multi-year MK2 campus program, bringing Phase 1 (global support center) on stream.
*   **HD Fire Protect** is expanding its Jalgaon facility and completing a new production facility/warehouse at Wagle Estate, Thane, alongside establishing a trading presence in Saudi Arabia.
*   **KSB Ltd** completed its Shirwal plant expansion to support industrial pump volume growth.

## What changed since last quarter
*(Drawn from companies providing explicit sequential comparisons: Elgi Equipments, KSB Ltd, Oswal Pumps)*
*   **Elgi Equipments:** Maintained its guidance and tone, though it shifted from broad optimism to actively acknowledging specific geopolitical and commodity headwinds while retaining confidence in mitigation.
*   **KSB Ltd:** Maintained its 13%–14% EBITDA margin target (explicitly rejecting higher bands due to strategic market entries) and shifted focus toward non-nuclear order drivers to compensate for nuclear testing delays.
*   **Oswal Pumps:** Walked back its previous full-year revenue growth guidance from 20%–25% down to 10%–15% and dropped full-year margin targets, reflecting missed H1 targets, delayed PM-KUSUM 2.0 rollouts, and collection headwinds.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **PM-KUSUM 2.0 Rollout:** Clearance and execution pickup for solar pump players like **Shakti Pumps** and **Oswal Pumps**.
*   **Government Receivables & Working Capital:** Normalization of debtor days following large delayed collections (e.g., **Oswal's** expected improvement to 150–160 days by Q4 and **Shakti's** post-quarter collection drop in debt).
*   **H2 Margin Recovery:** Delivery on management promises of margin expansion through price realizations and mix normalization at **KBL**, **WPIL**, and **Elgi**.
*   **Nuclear & Major Project Milestones:** Completion of nuclear pump testing at **KSB** and execution progress on large international oil & gas/water projects at **KBL** and **WPIL**.

## Company one-liners
*   **Elgi Equipments Ltd:** Strong operational performance and successful new product rollout (Vayu) offset by persistent commodity inflation and geopolitical headwinds.
*   **HD Fire Protect Ltd:** HD Fire Protect delivered a strong Q2 FY27 with 29% revenue growth and 42% PAT growth, backed by robust industrial demand, expanding international footprints, and established global quality certifications.
*   **KSB Ltd:** KSB Ltd continues to register robust order inflows from non-nuclear segments, though near-term profitability remains constrained by fixed-price contract lags and nuclear testing delays.
*   **Kirloskar Brothers Ltd:** KBL is delivering strong top-line growth and order intake, but profitability is currently constrained by raw material costs and a temporary product mix shift toward lower-margin capital goods, with management expecting margin recovery in H2.
*   **Oswal Pumps Ltd:** Oswal Pumps faced a difficult H1 with margin compression and delayed government schemes, but management is pivoting capital toward backward integration (solar cell manufacturing) and EPC diversification to recover profitability in H2.
*   **Shakti Pumps India Ltd:** Shakti Pumps delivered steady Q2 FY27 performance driven by strong solar installations and export growth, while awaiting PM-KUSUM 2.0 rollout and ramping up in-house solar panel and cell manufacturing capacities.
*   **WPIL Ltd:** WPIL faces near-term domestic execution headwinds and revenue lumpiness, but management maintains a positive outlook for H2, driven by a strong order book and expected fund releases in West Bengal.
