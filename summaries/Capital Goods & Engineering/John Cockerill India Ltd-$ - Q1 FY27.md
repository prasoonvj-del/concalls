# John Cockerill India Ltd-$ - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 CY26
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a strong order book and strategic global consolidation, viewing current margin pressure as a transitional phase related to project mix and restructuring.

### Key numbers
*   **Standalone Revenue:** Rs 149 crore (+82% YoY).
*   **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 299 crore (+18% YoY).
*   **Order Wins (Quarter):** ~Rs 1,200 crore.
*   **Total Order Book (Consolidated):** ~Rs 4,500 crore.

### Highlights
*   **Strategic Consolidation:** Successfully consolidated Chinese, German, and Belgian entities under John Cockerill India Limited to create an integrated global platform.
*   **Operational Expansion:** Inaugurated Advanced Coating facility at Taloja (India); opened a new office in Shanghai, with a workshop for special machine assembly scheduled to open in Q3.
*   **Revenue Dynamics:** Sequential revenue decline attributed to project cycles; Q1 benefited from closing older projects, while Q2 revenue reflects early-stage execution of new orders.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Long-term Target:** Management reiterated the "North Star" target of Rs 8,000 crore top line by CY30, driven by organic growth (JVD, Volteron) and external acquisitions.
*   **H2 Outlook:** Management expects revenue generation and execution to ramp up in the second half of the year.
*   **Profitability:** Management expects profitability to improve over the medium term as new projects progress and organizational integration benefits materialize.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Europe:** Challenging environment; new tariff-quota regime (15% duty on excess imports) is driving interest in hot/cold rolling solutions and electric arc furnaces (EAF).
*   **China:** Shifting toward a "green steel pivot"; demand increasing for downstream lines (galvanizing, annealing, finishing) and EAF/hydrogen-based metallurgy.
*   **United States:** Strong revival; capacity utilization reached 82% in July 2026; strong demand for spare parts and maintenance.
*   **India:** Strong growth potential; production target of 300 million tonnes by 2030; per capita consumption remains low (93 kg vs global average 230 kg).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Margin Impact:** Profitability was affected by the early-stage execution of new orders (upfront costs) and one-time costs related to group restructuring and consolidation.
*   **Cost Outlook:** Employee costs expected to rise slightly due to manpower expansion for execution; other expenses expected to be marginally lower in the next two quarters.
*   **Cash Flow:** Management noted that shifting to share-based payments for certain transactions will reduce future cash outflows.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Taloja Facility:** Advanced Coating facility is operational with testing and trial orders underway.
*   **Acquisitions:** Management is actively investigating several external acquisition opportunities.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   Not discussed in detail.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Geopolitical:** Tensions in the Middle East are impacting energy markets, logistics, and commodity flows.
*   **Hydrogen Adoption:** Demand for hydrogen in the steel industry remains weak; current adoption is focused on natural gas cracking or reusing coke oven gas rather than green hydrogen.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   *Q: Why the gap between the Rs 2,000 crore revenue guidance and the actual Rs 960 crore reported?* A: Management clarified Rs 2,000 crore was a target/guideline, not a guarantee, and expects significant revenue improvement in H2.
*   *Q: Why did the parent company reduce its stake from 75% to 70.4%?* A: Management stated this was due to normal operational priorities and confirmed the stake will return to 72%+ via preferential convertible shares.
*   *Q: What is the cost differential between JVD and Hot-Dip Galvanizing (HDP)?* A: JVD offers zinc savings and higher speed; savings range from Rs 1,000 to 20x that figure depending on the product mix and application.
*   *Q: What was the impact of the merger on the recent JSW order?* A: The global integration allowed Indian teams to be supported by Belgian experts; part of the project will be executed in Europe.
*   *Q: Can you quantify the one-off costs?* A: Management noted these included transaction, legal, and compliance costs across multiple countries (Belgium, Germany, China) and stated many of these costs will cease in H2.

### One-line takeaway
John Cockerill India is transitioning into an integrated global entity with a robust Rs 4,500 crore order book, focusing on execution ramp-up and technology-led growth despite short-term margin pressure from restructuring costs.
