# Capital Goods & Engineering - Process & engineering equipment: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the Capital Goods & Engineering (Process & engineering equipment) sector reflects a resilient overarching demand narrative driven by robust order books, expansion into sunrise sectors (such as nuclear energy, defense, space, and clean tech/CBG), and aggressive international diversification. However, the sector presents a clear dichotomy: while companies with strong export footprints and high-margin product/consultancy mixes (such as INOX India, Engineers India, Dee Development, and Fabtech) are reporting stellar growth and record order backlogs, companies exposed to domestic greenfield segments and restructuring efforts (such as Praj Industries, GMM Pfaudler, and HLE Glascoat) face domestic policy headwinds, funding delays, and operational friction in overseas markets.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus Agreement:** Managements universally highlight strong medium-to-long-term demand driven by energy transition, pharmaceuticals/APIs, and specialized infrastructure. International markets (Middle East, Africa, US, and Europe) remain robust, particularly for high-end cryogenic, filtration, and modular piping equipment.
* **Domestic Divergence:** While Dee Development and GMM Pfaudler note strong domestic thermal and nuclear power capex (citing BHEL, NPCIL, and private players like JSW and Adani), Praj Industries flags severe domestic greenfield headwinds and funding challenges due to sudden OMC exclusions of sugarcane juice and B-Heavy molasses from ethanol procurement tenders. Furthermore, Dee Development notes that tender finalization and conversion timelines at public sector giants like BHEL and L&T are extremely slow, prompting a pivot toward private developers.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Margin Resilience vs. Bottoming Out:** Several companies reported substantial margin expansions via operating leverage and high-margin mix shifts (Engineers India achieved a 30% consultancy margin; Dee Development expanded EBITDA margins by 380 bps YoY to 20.1%). Praj Industries and INOX India noted that their margins have bottomed out or experienced temporary strategic pricing/mix dips that are non-structural.
* **Geographic & Cost Pressures:** Fabtech and INOX India cited ongoing freight volatility and geopolitical disruptions in the GCC and West Asia, adding security and logistics costs. Conversely, companies like HLE Glascoat and GMM Pfaudler are mitigating high European operational costs by leveraging Indian manufacturing bases for strategic sourcing and shifting production to low-cost hubs like Poland.

## Capex & capacity
* **Capacity Expansion:** Capital deployment is heavily skewed toward specialized, high-growth adjacencies. Dee Development is constructing a dedicated nuclear piping plant in Anjar and finalizing a global nuclear piping JV. HLE Glascoat is setting up a heavy equipment plant near Anand (Adas) for >100–1000 tonne units. INOX India is constructing a new facility near Kandla port, and Praj Industries is investing in its GenX facility and a Rs 100 crore CBG JV with BPCL. 
* **Inorganic Growth:** Fabtech remains on track to close its European (Italy) acquisition within the next six months.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Dee Development:** Maintained full-year guidance (Rs 1,500 crore revenue, >19% EBITDA margin) and shifted immediate conversion focus to private players (JSW, Adani) due to slow BHEL/L&T tender timelines.
* **Engineers India:** Order inflow momentum surpassed expectations; management confirmed they will cross Rs 8,000 crore in order inflows for FY27.
* **Fabtech Technologies:** Reported a strong sequential recovery in Q2 with EBITDA more than doubling QoQ; maintaining full-year adjusted PAT margin guidance of 8% to 9%.
* **GMM Pfaudler:** Recorded highest-ever quarterly order intake crossing Rs 1,200 crore; accelerated European restructuring (UK facility closed, German workforce reduced by 40+).
* **HLE Glascoat:** Consolidated order book crossed Rs 1,087 crore for the first time; Omeras achieved EBITDA breakeven in H1.
* **INOX India:** Order backlog reached an all-time high of Rs 2,268 crore, driven by major U.S. aerospace and space wins.
* **Praj Industries:** Navigating domestic greenfield policy headwinds by pivoting toward brownfield solutions, international markets, and new growth engines like CBG and GenX.

## What to watch next quarter
* **Execution of Lumpy Order Books:** Conversion speed of record-high order books (e.g., INOX India's US space order, HLE Glascoat's Rs 1,087 crore backlog, GMM Pfaudler's Rs 2,446 crore backlog) into dispatch-level revenues.
* **European Restructuring Progress:** Visible financial impact and cost savings from GMM Pfaudler's plant closures and workforce rationalization in Europe.
* **Domestic Policy & Tender Flows:** Progress on Praj's CBG/GenX rollouts and whether Dee Development successfully converts large private-sector pipeline orders from JSW and Adani.
* **Geopolitical & Freight Stability:** Monitoring logistics costs and site security overheads for international projects in the Middle East and Africa (Fabtech, Engineers India).
