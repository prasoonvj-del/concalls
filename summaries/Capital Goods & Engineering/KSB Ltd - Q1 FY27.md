# KSB Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-26 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 CY 2026
**Tone:** Mixed - While long-term demand drivers and order books remain robust, H1 performance was impacted by geopolitical issues affecting exports, gas availability/foundry cost pressures, and delays in the KUSUM solar scheme.

### Key numbers
- **Half-year Revenue from Sales:** Rs 1,292 crore (INR 12,920 million)
- **Half-year EBITDA:** Rs 146.5 crore (INR 1,465 million)
- **Half-year PBT:** Rs 127.9 crore (INR 1,279 million)
- **ROCE:** 22.8% (24% excluding one-time labour code impact)
- **Dividend Declared (Last Year):** 220%
- **H1 Order Intake (without Nuclear):** Rs 1,530.7 crore (INR 15,307 million)
- **Orders on Hand (as of June 2026):** Rs 2,744.5 crore (INR 27,445 million)
- **Nuclear Orders on Hand:** Rs 1,235 crore
- **Solar Revenue (H1):** Rs 50 crore to Rs 60 crore
- **Foundry/Gas supply cost impact:** Price increases given to foundries in the range of 12% to 15%
- **Capacity Utilization (Shirwal plant):** 80% to 90%
- **Planned Annual CapEx:** Rs 80 crore to Rs 120 crore annually

### Highlights
- Completed expansion at Shirwal plant by building a new 7,000 sq metre shed on a newly acquired 15,000 sq metre plot, adding ~20% capacity.
- Successfully localized LUV boiler circulation pump orders for supercritical thermal power projects (previously imported).
- First indigenous nuclear pump made in 2025; testing underway at NPCIL test bed in 2026.
- Advanced Pricing Agreement (APA) signed this year, valid for 5 years, regulating royalty and transfer pricing.
- SAP S/4HANA global rollout expected to incur cost allocations for KSB India starting next year (H1 of next year).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims for double-digit top-line and bottom-line growth for the full year, backed by a stronger H2.
- Volume growth targeted at 10% to 15%; value growth at 15% (averaging 15% to 17%).
- SupremeServ (aftermarket) business targeting 25% to 30% revenue share (excluding standard agri/domestic portfolio).
- BP&CL reciprocating pump business targeting an annual scale of Rs 40 crore to Rs 50 crore once fully stabilized.

### Demand & industry commentary
- **Energy (Conventional & Nuclear):** Leading growth; thermal power picking up with supercritical projects under discussion. Nuclear long-term target is 100 GW by 2047 (23 GW by 2031).
- **Water & Wastewater:** Upcoming municipal water supply, desalination, and rural water infrastructure projects providing strong visibility.
- **Commercial Building Services:** Opportunities in metro rails, urban infrastructure, and Data Centres (cooling infrastructure/liquid cooling).
- **Emerging Segments:** Marine/shipbuilding (Naval and commercial), green hydrogen (Magnochem), railways (Etaseco), and renewable energy (Movitec).
- **Solar:** H1 affected by delays in KUSUM 2.0; expected to recover in H2.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Foundries and castings severely impacted by gas availability and pricing issues; supply stability has now reached 85% to 90%.
- Standard product price hikes taken in single digits so far, while foundries demanded 12% to 15%.
- Project business remains under fixed-price pressure, affecting gross margins with a 3 to 6-month lag in passing costs to customers.
- Export margins generally better than domestic markets.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Annual CapEx guidance maintained at Rs 80 crore to Rs 120 crore covering sustenance, plant/machinery upgrades, office infrastructure, and service stations.
- Shirwal plant capacity increased by ~20% via the new shed.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory build-up in H1 attributed to delayed export clearances, paused FGD business, and slowed solar dispatches.
- Working capital cycle for exports noted to be similar to or shorter than domestic markets due to tighter delivery schedules.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions and Middle East conflicts disrupting export shipments and supply chains in H1.
- Fixed-price project orders limiting immediate margin pass-through for raw material/casting inflation.
- Dependency on external test-bed readiness at NPCIL affecting nuclear pump dispatch timelines.
- PSU client tendering guidelines (PTR requirements) causing slower initial scale-up for acquired entities like BP&CL.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Saurabh Mehta (East Lane Capital) on thermal/nuclear growth vs. GDP:** Management stated energy will lead growth, supported by boiler feed pump packages and newly localized boiler circulation pumps.
2. **Subham Murade on export headwinds:** Management confirmed H1 exports were held up due to geopolitical friction and war, but supply chain consistency has improved and H2 looks stronger.
3. **Nityasurya on nuclear test bed delays and revenue recognition:** Management clarified that the first pump requires 500 hours of testing (2-3 months), targeting one pump per quarter post-testing, with Kaiga testing to follow.
4. **Mohit Surana on margin pressure and order inflows:** Management explained that lower-than-planned sales volume impacted operational leverage, while gas cost increases in foundries take 3–6 months to reflect in sales books.
5. **Analyst on S/4HANA rollout impact:** Management confirmed S/4HANA is mandatory, will improve internal efficiencies, and will bring Opex cost allocations starting next year.

### One-line takeaway
KSB India experienced a subdued H1 due to export bottlenecks and foundry cost pressures, but management anticipates a stronger H2 and double-digit growth driven by energy, nuclear localization, and emerging infrastructure segments.
