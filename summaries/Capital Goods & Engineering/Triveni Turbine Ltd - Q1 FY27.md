# Triveni Turbine Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-17 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Challenging quarter with softer profitability and delivery deferrals, though management remains optimistic on full-year revenue and profit growth.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations:** ₹4.43 billion (up 19.2% YoY)
- **Domestic sales:** ₹2.4 billion (up 27.4% YoY)
- **Exports:** ₹2.03 billion
- **Export share in revenue:** 48.8% (against 49.3% in Q1 FY26)
- **EBITDA:** ₹797 million (margin of 18%, down from 25.8% YoY)
- **Profit before tax (PBT):** ₹697 million, down 20.1% YoY (margin of 15.7%, against 23.5% YoY)
- **Order booking:** ₹5.68 billion (up 6.1% YoY)
- **Export order booking growth:** 53.4% YoY (accounting for 68% of total order booking)
- **Aftermarket order booking growth:** 54% YoY (accounting for 39% of total order booking)
- **Closing order book:** ₹21.8 billion as of June 30, 2026 (up 5.1% YoY)
- **Closing aftermarket orders:** ₹6.24 billion (up 115% YoY)

### Highlights
- Revenue grew 19.2% YoY driven by domestic growth, but profitability was softer due to a lower-margin order mix from H1 FY26 and delivery deferrals.
- The NTPC CO2-based energy storage project (validation project taken at near-zero margins) is due for commissioning in Q2, with revenue expected in Q2.
- Balance sheet improved with reduced receivables, a reversion to negative working capital, and an increased cash balance.
- The COO role transitioned to focus on AI-specific business transformation initiatives.
- U.S. subsidiary continues to see growing enquiries for products and aftermarket services.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is optimistic on growth for both profit and revenue for full-year FY27, which will be back-ended.
- Management maintains a medium- to long-term PBT margin target of over 20%.
- U.S. subsidiary operations are targeted to break even this year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- International enquiry generation has improved, particularly in the U.S. and Southeast Asia, with green shoots in Europe; North Africa, Middle East, and SAARC remain depressed.
- Domestic market saw a broad-based decline in enquiries and a slowdown/longer gestation periods for order finalisations.
- Global data centre demand is surging; due to 3-to-4-year lead times for gas turbines, customers are turning to conventional/combined cycles and small modular reactors.
- Aftermarket growth is supported by utility-scale refurbishment, gas turbine MRO, geothermal, and biomass segments.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Export dispatches were pushed to Q2/Q3 because freight rates surged 3x to 4x and vessel availability tightened (costs borne by clients under FOB terms).
- Fixed-price contracts carry commodity price risks, which are factored in during order intake.
- Lower-margin legacy orders executed during the quarter impacted overall EBITDA margins.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Heavy ongoing investments into R&D for newer product ranges (Organic Rankine Cycle - ORC, heat pumps, Mechanical Vapor Recompression - MVR).
- U.S. facility is building local service/product capabilities and local registrations/licensing.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Reverted to negative working capital with a reduction in receivables and an increase in the cash balance.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical uncertainties bringing volatility in dispatches and order bookings on a quarterly/monthly basis.
- Surging freight rates (3x-4x) and vessel shortages causing client-side deferrals of export dispatches.
- Slowdown and softer enquiry generation in the domestic market.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Amit Anwani (PL Capital) asked about the NTPC project impact and commodity inflation.** Management stated the NTPC project was taken at near-zero margins for technology validation (commissioning in Q2), and margin pressure was primarily due to higher domestic revenue share and export delivery shifts rather than unexpected cost inflation.
2. **Ravi Swaminathan (Avendus) asked about domestic ordering weakness and API turbine/U.S. progress.** Management clarified that domestic enquiry dips represent general demand leaving the market or delayed finalisations, while API and U.S. markets show strong traction with delayed conversions due to geopolitics.
3. **Mohit Surana (Monarch Networth) asked about bought-out components and sustainable EBITDA margins.** Management noted bought-outs range from 30% to 70% per order, and reaffirmed the medium/long-term PBT margin target of over 20%.
4. **Chirag Muchhala (Centrum Broking) asked about data centre order conversions and FOB terms.** Management confirmed data centre enquiries are active and growing, and verified that all export logistics/freight rates are borne by clients under FOB terms.
5. **Prolin Nandu (Edelweiss) asked how to reduce business volatility given recurring geopolitical disruptions.** Management responded that building a large enough order book and expanding product ranges are the only ways to smooth out execution and booking volatility.

### One-line takeaway
Despite Q1 profitability softening due to a legacy low-margin order mix and export delivery deferrals, Triveni Turbine remains confident of back-ended top- and bottom-line growth for FY27, underpinned by robust export and aftermarket order books.
