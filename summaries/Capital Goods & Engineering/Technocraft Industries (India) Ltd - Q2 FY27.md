# Technocraft Industries (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Capital Goods & Engineering*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Driven by strong volume growth in scaffolding and drum closures, robust U.S. demand, and stable margins.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (no new numerical guidance numbers provided, but management reaffirmed demand visibility and long-term targets).
- Tone and demand view: Remained confident, noting strong near-term visibility in the U.S. construction and drum markets despite global uncertainties.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: U.S. tariff refund partial accounting (USD 350,000 booked out of USD 3 million received) was discussed in detail this quarter.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Maintenance capex continuation and Phase 2 of Aurangabad extrusion plant eyed for FY28 remained on track.

### Key numbers
- Scaffolding and Formwork total segment revenue: Rs. 502 crore (Scaffolding: Rs. 260-270 crore, Formwork: Rs. 240 crore)
- Scaffolding volumes: ~11,900 tonnes
- Drum closures volume: ~220 lakh sets (230 lakh sets mentioned later)
- Plastic drum closure 6-month turnover: Rs. 25 crore (annualized Rs. 50-60 crore)
- Defence order book: Rs. 30 crore
- Forex gain for the quarter: Rs. 11 crore
- Mach One order book: 50,000-odd square meters
- U.S. tariff refund received: USD 3 million total (USD 350,000 accounted for in books this quarter)
- Extrusion Phase 2 capex guidance: ~Rs. 120 crore (slated for latter part of FY28)

### Highlights
- Both scaffolding and drum closure segments saw significant volume and realization increases over the last two quarters.
- Management stated existing plants can support up to Rs. 2,500 crores of revenue with minimal additional capex.
- Extrusion Phase 2 expansion in Aurangabad planned for FY28 to backward integrate incremental capacity.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident of sustaining current volume levels and margins over the ensuing quarters, backed by a strong 15-18 month pipeline in the U.S.
- Drum closure margins expected to hold around 35% to 40%.
- Mach One vertical on track to achieve over Rs. 900 crores of revenue this fiscal year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- U.S. scaffolding demand driven by investments in data centers, chip manufacturing plants, and energy plants, with a healthy 12-18 month capex pipeline.
- Drum closure demand strong in the U.S. and Asia Pacific (including China); Europe remains muted though Central Europe (Poland) shows good signs.
- Aluminium formwork (Mach One) in India is seeing steady adoption, though hindered by customer and site readiness rather than a lack of orders.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Drum closure margins supported by high volumes, fixed cost absorption, and rupee depreciation gains (though rupee appreciation may moderate future currency gains).
- Price hikes in drum closures well accepted with no customer pushback.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- No significant capex planned currently other than regular maintenance.
- Second phase of aluminum extrusion capacity expansion in Aurangabad planned for FY28 involving ~Rs. 120 crore capex.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Investments primarily held in fixed income instruments, balanced funds, and highly liquid surplus cash; maintained for arbitrage against borrowing costs.
- Free cash flows fluctuate quarter-on-quarter due to inventory staging in overseas warehouses (U.S. entity running on a Jan-Dec calendar vs. Indian April-March).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global turbulent times make long-term forecasting difficult.
- Slower adoption cycles and supply chain readiness in the Indian formwork market.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Digant Haria asked about the sustainability of high volumes in scaffolding and drum closures:** Management confirmed confidence in sustaining volumes due to strong U.S. and Asia-Pacific demand.
- **Anubhav Mukherjee asked about U.S. tariff refund status:** Management clarified that while USD 3 million was received, only USD 350,000 is accounted for this quarter, with the rest spread over 6-7 months.
- **Abhinav asked about drum closure margin guidance and pricing pushbacks:** Management stated no pricing pushback is seen and guided for 35% to 40% margins.
- **Rahul Kumar asked about Mach One utilization and 3x revenue aspirations:** Management reiterated they are on track for over Rs. 900 crores in Mach One revenue this fiscal and focus on realizations over volume price wars.
- **Kumar Ramachandran asked about operating cash flows and defence numbers:** Management explained inventory staging causes cash flow lumpiness and cited a Rs. 30 crore defence order book.

### One-line takeaway
Technocraft delivered a strong Q2 FY27 powered by robust U.S. infrastructure demand, sustained high volumes in scaffolding and drum closures, and a solid near-term order outlook.
