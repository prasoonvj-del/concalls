# Defence & Aerospace - Aerospace components: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the aerospace components and defence sector reported robust execution and stellar revenue growth in Q2 FY27, driven by strong global aerospace demand, scaling manufacturing footprints, and multi-industry diversification into semiconductors and energy. However, performance diverged sharply based on segment maturity and working capital pressures, with consumer-facing lines facing headwinds (Aequs) while core precision engineering and engine components scaled new highs (Azad, MTAR, Rossell, Unimech).

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Historically, Indian aerospace and defence component suppliers operated as build-to-print job shops or short-cycle tooling makers reliant on domestic state-run entities. Over the last 5 years, the sector has transitioned into build-to-specification design partners, global Tier-1 suppliers securing long-term agreements (LTAs), and multi-industry precision platforms diversifying into clean energy, semiconductors, and space [Background].

**Shifts underway** -
- **Transition to long-term agreements and multi-industry platforms:** Companies are evolving from short-cycle toolings and single-client dependencies to long-term supply agreements and multi-industry platforms spanning semiconductors, space, and nuclear energy [Unimech Q2 FY27 concall; Rossell Q2 FY27 concall]. For instance, Unimech secured a Rs 589 crore LTA/award letter order book with FACC, Honeywell, and a French Tier 1 [Unimech Q2 FY27 concall], while Rossell crossed INR 140 crores in H1 non-aerospace revenue from semiconductors and space [Rossell Q2 FY27 concall]. This shift drives multi-year revenue visibility and cushions against cyclical aerospace tooling dips.
- **Aggressive capacity expansion and localization of complex assemblies:** Firms are heavily capitalizing infrastructure to capture global supply chain realignments. Azad Engineering completed six customer-dedicated lean manufacturing facilities (44,800 sqm) [Azad Q2 FY27 concall], MTAR is deploying a ~Rs 500 crore capex plan largely for fuel cells and clean energy [MTAR Q2 FY27 concall], and Rossell inaugurated a ~140,000 sq ft leased facility in the Aerospace Park [Rossell Q2 FY27 concall]. This matters because front-loaded fixed costs and delta qualifications temporarily strain cash flows, but enable exponential top-line scaling.

**Government push** - 
- Defence Ministry's push for a 20% capex hike [Govt: Defence Ministry seek 20% capex hike for defence sector, Nov 2026] directly benefits domestic manufacturers like Azad Engineering and MTAR through higher procurement outlays.
- The Cabinet's approval of an aircraft maintenance partnership with Singapore to build a regional aviation repair hub [Govt: Cabinet clear aircraft maintenance partnership with Singapore, Nov 2026] supports MRO and component players like Rossell Techsys.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** All five managements agree that structural multi-decade demand across aerospace, defence, and high-precision engineering remains exceptionally strong. Azad Engineering, MTAR Technologies, Rossell Techsys, and Unimech reported strong order inflows and guided for a stronger H2 FY27.
- **Divergence:** While aerospace and energy segments are booming, Aequs Ltd faced severe headwinds in its consumer segment (toys and electronics), where volume ramps were hindered by supply chain constraints, weather-related logistics disruptions, and longer-than-expected product qualification cycles. 

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Margin Expansion / Resilience:** Azad Engineering reported a 150 bps YoY EBITDA margin expansion to 37.5% (standalone) due to operating leverage and local sourcing. Unimech maintained a robust 40% EBITDA margin in Q2. MTAR's EBITDA margin stood at 24.4%, driven by a better product mix. Rossell maintains its long-term EBITDA margin target of 18% to 23%.
- **Cost Headwinds:** Aequs saw widening EBITDA losses in its consumer segment (INR 401 million loss) as operating costs of new capacity were expensed. Azad, MTAR, and Unimech noted that working capital lock-ins, higher depreciation, and front-loaded employee costs during asset capitalization phases temporarily impacted below-EBITDA profitability or required active working capital management (e.g., bill discounting lines for Azad).

## Capex & capacity
- **Aequs:** Total FY27 capex will be lower than the initial INR 660 crore guidance as focus shifts toward aerospace; groundbreaking completed for the 250-acre Hosur SEZ/DTA site (commercial production in FY29).
- **Azad Engineering:** H1 capex spent at INR 139.3 crore; completed six customer-dedicated lean manufacturing facilities at Tuniki Bollaram.
- **MTAR Technologies:** Total planned capex of ~Rs 500 crore (Rs 350 crore for fuel cells, Rs 150 crore for oil & gas and aerospace); fuel cell Phase 2 nearing completion.
- **Rossell Techsys:** Inaugurated a new ~140,000 sq ft leased facility in the Aerospace Park commencing operations in Q3.
- **Unimech Aerospace:** Gross block expected to reach ~Rs 500 crore by FY27 end; installing automated special process cells/assembly lines for completion by Dec '26.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Aequs Ltd:** Management withdrew the Q4 FY27 EBITDA breakeven target for the consumer segment due to slower learning curves, shifting tone from "on track" to "recalibrating." Promoter group stepped in with a INR 6,500 million preferential warrant investment.
- **Azad Engineering Ltd:** Successfully transitioned from setting up foundation to executing throughput (starting Q3) and revenue ramp-up (from Q4). Commissioned remaining GE Vernova facilities on schedule.
- **MTAR Technologies Ltd:** Delivered record Q2 performance with working capital days plunging from 172 days (March 2026) to 50 days due to customer advances.
- **Rossell Techsys Ltd:** Completed a INR 300 crores capital raise with marquee institutional investors (SBI Mutual Fund, Kotak Mutual Fund) to repay debt and lower interest costs starting Q3.
- **Unimech Aerospace:** Continued rapid order book expansion (Rs 855 crore, +715% YoY) alongside integration of Hobel Bellows.

## What to watch next quarter
- **Aequs:** Progress on consumer segment recalibration and execution of the Hosur aerospace expansion.
- **Azad Engineering:** Revenue ramp-up from Q4 FY27 onwards and utilization of bill discounting lines with ICICI Bank and DP World to ease working capital.
- **MTAR Technologies:** Commissioning of fuel cell Phase 2 and execution of the Rs 45 crore data center first article orders.
- **Rossell Techsys:** Reduction in interest costs following debt repayment from the INR 300 crore fundraise and scaling of semiconductor revenues.
- **Unimech:** Conversion of FAIs into serial production and progress on the Saudi Arabia JV construction.

## Company one-liners
- **Aequs Ltd:** Scaling its high-growth aerospace business while pausing its consumer segment breakeven timeline amid heavy cash burn and a large promoter capital infusion.
- **Azad Engineering Ltd:** Delivering record revenue and margin expansion as its newly commissioned customer-dedicated facilities begin commercial throughput.
- **MTAR Technologies Ltd:** Posting explosive profit and revenue growth driven by clean energy tailwinds, operational execution, and sharply improved working capital.
- **Rossell Techsys Ltd:** Strengthening its balance sheet via a INR 300 crore fundraise while riding record aerospace and semiconductor demand.
- **Unimech Aerospace and Manufacturing Ltd:** Rapidly transitioning into a multi-industry precision engineering platform with surging order books and pristine 40% EBITDA margins.

## What we were watching
- ⏳ Government 20% capex hike for defence sector: Dispatched in the sector context (Motilal Oswal highlights/Defence Ministry plans), with companies like Azad and MTAR well-positioned to capture higher outlays, though not explicitly quantified as a standalone order by all managements in Q2 calls.
- ✅ Aircraft maintenance partnership with Singapore: Aligns with Rossell Techsys's MRO capability expansion and broader sector tailwinds in aviation repair.
