# Defence & Aerospace - Defence electronics: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the Defence & Aerospace (Defence Electronics) sector reflected robust operational execution, surging order inflows, and expanding order books across most covered companies. Managements pointed to structural tailwinds from government reforms (such as updated DPM, draft DAP with up to 60% local content mandates, and a 75% capital acquisition budget earmarked for domestic industry). Companies are increasingly transitioning from supplying individual subsystems to bidding for complete systems, integrated solutions, and complex platforms (radars, electronic warfare, counter-drone systems, and simulation nodes). While domestic demand remains anchored by aggressive government indigenization and fast-track procurement (FTP) pipelines, global export engagement is widening, particularly in the Middle East, Europe, and the US—though international conversions face certification hurdles and local content regulations.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** All covered managements agree that domestic demand tailwinds are exceptionally strong, driven by higher budget allocations, regular Acceptance of Necessity (AoNs) releases, and government push for domestic IP. Counter-drone (C-UAS), radar, and electronic warfare systems are seeing surging demand.
*   **Divergence on International Markets & Visibility:** 
    *   *Sigma Advanced Systems* derives ~90% of its revenue from exports with direct Tier-1 relationships with primes like Rolls-Royce.
    *   *Tonbo Imaging* reported 35% of H1 revenue from exports and highlighted a strategic European contract.
    *   *Zen Technologies* noted that while pursuing global markets (US, Europe, Middle East), export discussions in the Middle East faced temporary dampening due to regional conflicts, and European/US entries face strict local content mandates (e.g., SAFE procurement requiring 65% local content).
    *   *Centum Electronics* is scaling up semiconductor equipment demand ($45M-$50M run rate) faster than initially expected, driven by anchor customers.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Margins & Operating Leverage:** Most companies reported stable or expanding EBITDA margins supported by operating leverage and favorable product mixes. Astra Microwave guides for 26%–28% margins; Data Patterns targets 35%–40%; Zen Technologies maintained mid-30s margins (38.2% in Q2); Digilogic guides for 30%–35% core EBITDA/PAT; Sigma Advanced Systems targets ~20% for FY27 with expected 200–400 bps expansion; Centum aims for 14%–15% over 3–4 years.
*   **Supply Chain & Raw Material Pressures:** 
    *   *Centum Electronics* highlighted severe supply chain bottlenecks and extended component lead times (memories, controllers, MLCCs, and bare PCBs extending to 40-50 weeks), driving up inventory and working capital.
    *   *Astra Microwave* similarly noted extended global supply chain lead times, prompting preemptive component stockpiling for early FY28 execution.
    *   *Zen Technologies* experienced higher warranty provisioning due to a product mix shift toward Anti-Drone Systems (ADS).

## Capex & capacity
*   *Astra Microwave* continues parallel testing and R&D investments.
*   *Centum Electronics* is planning a new facility dedicated to BTS for system-level capabilities and full satellite assembly, integration, and testing (AIT).
*   *Data Patterns* is building a new factory and investing in advance for production infrastructure, pushing roadmap investments above its previous INR 150 crore plan.
*   *Digilogic Systems* is progressing with "Project Udaan," expecting civil construction completion by March 2027 and operational readiness by Q1 FY28.
*   *Sigma Advanced Systems* made £8–£9 million in UK investments focusing on automation, boosting OTIF from 65% to 97%, while its Sri City facility commenced production in September.
*   *Tonbo Imaging* operates a fabless model for tactical systems but funded its Directed Energy Weapon (DEW) project through an EXIM loan, iDEX grant, and internal accruals.
*   *Zen Technologies* budgeted ~Rs 150 crore in capex for FY27 and another ~Rs 150 crore for FY28, scaling its Hyper strike interceptor capacity up to 180,000 units per annum.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance:** Generally maintained across the board (Astra: ~15% revenue growth; Centum: 25%-30% medium-term; Data Patterns: 20%-25% growth, 35%-40% EBITDA margin; Digilogic: 25%-30% top-line; Zen: mid-30s EBITDA).
*   **Tone Shifts:** Centum shifted more confident on semiconductor equipment ramp-up ($45M–$50M in 2 years). Data Patterns pivoted further toward internal R&D for agile defense microsatellites and advanced optronics. Sigma Advanced Systems successfully integrated Bromford Precision Solutions and commenced Sri City manufacturing.
*   **Delivery on Promises:** Astra delivered its promised 3 in-house IP products by Diwali (counter-drone D4 radar, bird detection radar, LLLR). Data Patterns completed the acquisition of ST Advanced Composites. Centum utilized ~Rs 35 crore of its initial capex plan.

## What to watch next quarter
*   Execution of large order backlogs (e.g., Astra's Rs 4,708 crore consolidated order book, Centum's Rs 2,000 crore order book, Data Patterns' Rs 1,742 crore order book alongside its ~Rs 956 crore negotiated orders awaiting formal contracts).
*   Progress on government Fast Track Procurement (FTP) tenders for Anti-Drone Systems, particularly for Zen Technologies and Astra Microwave.
*   Commercialization milestones and facility certifications for project expansions (e.g., Digilogic's Project Udaan, Centum's BTS facility, Sigma's Sri City ramp-up).
*   Working capital and cash conversion cycle improvements as companies attempt to streamline inventory build-ups caused by extended component lead times.
