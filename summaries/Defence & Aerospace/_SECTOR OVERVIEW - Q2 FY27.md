# Defence & Aerospace: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The sector delivered a broadly strong quarter with most companies reporting record order books, revenue growth and margin expansion driven by domestic procurement momentum and rising export traction. Managements are investing heavily in capacity ahead of multi‑year visibility, while flagging near‑term working‑capital pressure from inventory stocking and long qualification cycles. A few names (Aequs consumer, Millworks) highlighted execution delays, but the dominant tone is confident.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** – Indian defence & aerospace has historically relied on licence production and Tier‑2/3 component supply to PSUs/DRDO with long gestation and back‑ended revenue. Over the last ~5 years the model has shifted toward private‑sector design & development (IDDM), export orientation, systems integration and leveraging commercial aerospace/space opportunities. ## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
**Consensus:** Record order books across the board (BEL ₹68,717 cr, GRSE ~₹44,846 cr, Astra ₹4,708 cr consolidated, Sigma ₹10,000+ cr, Data Patterns ₹2,698 cr visibility, ZEN ₹1,375 cr, MTAR ₹4,102 cr H1 inflows). Domestic policy tailwinds (DAP 2025, positive indigenisation lists, 75% capital budget for domestic) and rising export enquiries (Europe, Middle East, Southeast Asia, US) underpin multi‑year visibility. Counter‑drone, radar, missile, shipbuilding, space and semiconductor equipment segments see structural demand.

**Divergence:** Aequs’ consumer segment (toys/electronics) faces slower volume ramps and supply‑chain constraints, forcing a breakeven recalibration. Millworks’ H1 revenue shifted to H2 due to accounting timing and defence approval delays, resulting in an operating loss. Bharatrohan (agri‑tech) cites El Niño‑driven farmer conservatism. Centum Electronics and Aequs flag extended component lead times (40‑50 weeks) inflating inventory. Data Patterns has ₹956 cr of negotiated orders stuck in government approval. ZEN Technologies notes C‑UAS FTP tender delays and European local‑content rules (65%) slowing export conversion.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
**Margins:** Most companies report stable or expanding EBITDA margins on operating leverage and mix improvement. BEL guides ~28%; Astra 26‑28% steady state; Azad 37.5% (+150 bps YoY); Data Patterns 35‑40% guidance; Centum 12.7% Q2 targeting 13%+ combined, 20‑22% BTS; Sigma 19.7% targeting 20% FY27 with 200‑400 bps improvement; Rossell long‑term 18‑23%; MTAR 24.4% (guided 24%); Unimech 40% but flags 1‑2% long‑term compression from mix shift; ZEN 38.2% Q2 guiding mid‑30s; Ideaforge 25.4%; Tonbo Q2 24.1% (export 60‑65%, domestic tactical 40‑45%, platform 45‑50%); Aequs aerospace ~22%, consumer loss widening; Millworks ~37% gross margin compressed from 38%; GRSE NGC margins “adequate, akin to ASW”; Digilogic core EBITDA growth 30‑35%; Bharatrohan 17.7% (+200 bps).

**Raw materials & supply chain:** Centum Electronics highlights 40‑50 week lead times for memories, controllers, MLCCs, bare PCBs, raising component prices and inventory. Sigma Advanced Systems improved UK OTIF from 65% to 97% via buffer stock investment. Millworks notes higher raw‑material prices from geopolitical situations. MTAR Technologies sees minor consumable cost increases from geopolitics. BEL states direct raw‑material cost of mechanical items/alloys is only ~1‑2% of product cost, focus on electronics indigenisation.
