# TeamLease Services Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-10 · Services & Others*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Management acknowledged results fell short of aspirations but emphasized structural resets and long-term positioning.

### Key numbers
*   **Revenue:** Rs 3,086 crore (+1% QoQ, +1% YoY).
*   **H1 Revenue:** Rs 6,142 crore (+4% YoY).
*   **Operating EBITDA:** Rs 44 crore (+17% QoQ, +5% YoY).
*   **H1 Operating EBITDA:** Rs 82 crore (+11% YoY).
*   **Reported EBITDA:** Rs 36 crore (1.2% margin).
*   **PBT:** Rs 30 crore (+2% YoY).
*   **H1 PBT:** Rs 66 crore (+19% YoY).
*   **PAT:** Rs 26 crore.
*   **Net Free Cash:** Rs 350 crore (post Rs 238 crore buyback).
*   **Headcount:** ~333,200.

### Highlights
*   **Staffing:** General staffing headcount reduced by ~5,500 (net) due to planned exits of low-margin mandates (including one 7,000-associate client).
*   **Specialized Staffing:** Revenue Rs 190 crore; EBITDA grew double digits QoQ.
*   **GCCs:** Now serve 135+ clients (up from 110+).
*   **Portfolio:** Divested 30% stake in Crystal HR (no capital loss).
*   **HR Services:** Segment EBITDA margin 3% (turned profitable vs loss in Q1).

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management expects to deliver consistent net adds in general staffing going forward.
*   Targeting 15-20% growth in HR services for the next few quarters.
*   Management expects specialized staffing EBITDA margins to improve by ~20 bps by Q4.
*   Management is confident in double-digit absolute profit growth for the general staffing vertical.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **BFSI:** Sectoral decline due to client cost optimization, despite wallet share gains in select private banks/NBFCs.
*   **Consumer/Retail:** Strong performance, especially in quick commerce.
*   **Telecom:** Shift in demand; human deployment remains effective/cheaper than automation for field roles.
*   **IT Services:** Hiring remains cautious (replacement-focused), but demand exists for AI/ML, data engineering, and cloud skills.
*   **Labour Codes:** Implementation ongoing; no P&L impact expected as costs are contractually recoverable.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Operating leverage expected in staffing as volumes recover.
*   Cost per hire reduced by ~30% in general staffing and ~40% in specialized staffing over the last 18 months via tech investments.
*   Corporate EBITDA cost: Rs 8 crore (vs Rs 4 crore YoY) due to buyback expenses and brand/tech investments.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **M&A:** Evaluating opportunities for scale or new vertical entry; looking for high-margin businesses.
*   **Tech:** Ongoing overhaul of associate lifecycle system (full rollout in Q4).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Cash:** Net free cash of Rs 350 crore.
*   **Receivables:** TDS receivable stands at Rs 160 crore.
*   **Funding:** Funding exposure in staffing is 16%.
*   **Working Capital:** Management noted a one-day spike in funding due to client ad-hoc requests, which was recovered post-quarter.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Macro uncertainty causing client tentativeness.
*   Client cost optimization programs (e.g., BFSI).
*   Labour Code implementation causing short-term budget complications for clients.
*   Furloughs expected in Q3.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Amit Chandra (HDFC):** Asked about headcount softness. Management attributed it to planned exits of low-margin mandates and macro-driven client caution.
*   **Amit Chandra (HDFC):** Questioned Crystal HR divestment rationale. Management stated it was a minority stake exit to avoid future capital commitment in a challenging SaaS environment.
*   **Bhargav Buddhadev:** Asked about M&A. Management confirmed they are building a framework for high-margin, scale-accretive acquisitions.
*   **Dipesh Mehta:** Asked about operating cash flow. Management clarified the negative cash flow was due to a one-day ad-hoc funding spike for client payrolls, which was recovered.

### One-line takeaway
TeamLease is prioritizing profitable growth and portfolio realignment over volume, expecting margin expansion through operating leverage and higher-value specialized staffing.
