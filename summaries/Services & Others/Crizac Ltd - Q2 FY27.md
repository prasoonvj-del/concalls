# Crizac Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Services & Others*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Mixed – Q2 performance was significantly impacted by external disruptions and student deferrals, but management maintained its full-year guidance expecting recovery in H2.

### Key numbers
* **Operating Income:** Rs 102.7 crore (INR 1,027 million), down 36.7% YoY.
* **Operating EBITDA:** Rs 33.0 crore (INR 330 million), down 44.0% YoY.
* **EBITDA Margin:** 32.0%, declining 418 bps YoY (up 233 bps QoQ).
* **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 36.9 crore (INR 369 million), down 23.7% YoY.
* **H1 FY27 Operating Income:** Rs 303.9 crore (INR 3,039 million), down 18.3% YoY.
* **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 84.0 crore (INR 840 million), down 10.7% YoY.
* **H1 FY27 EPS:** Rs 4.8 per share.
* **Interim Dividend:** Rs 4 per equity share declared (total FY27 dividend targeted at Rs 8 to Rs 10 per share).

### Highlights
* Q2 revenue drop was driven by student deferrals (mainly postgraduate level) following West Asia geopolitical tensions (Feb–June) and sharp currency fluctuations in source markets.
* Revenue recognition occurs 45 to 90 days post university enrollment confirmation; thus Q2 reflects applications processed in Q1 and prior.
* Global UK sponsored study visas fell 20% between FY24 and FY26, but Crizac's UK market share grew from 3.5% to 6.0% overall, and from 9.0% to 13.9% among Indian students.
* Non-UK destination applications grew to 9.5% of total applications in H1 FY27 (up from 4.7% in H1 FY26), though revenue contribution remains low at 1.4%.
* H1 FY27 cash flow from operations stood at Rs 126.9 crore (136% of operating EBITDA).

### Guidance & outlook
* Management expects Q3 FY27 revenue to grow by 10% to 12% YoY.
* Maintained full-year FY27 revenue and PAT guidance to be in line with FY26 levels, backed by strong pipeline visibility in H2 (Q3 and Q4 are peak intake seasons).
* Management expects non-UK destinations to contribute ~30% of total revenue by FY30.

### Demand & industry commentary
* UK government budget proposal (Oct 28) suggested setting graduate visas to 2 years for high-compliance universities, signaling a constructive policy shift towards international student talent.
* Tightened immigration and compliance standards (such as the UK Agent Quality Framework) are causing universities to consolidate recruitment partners, benefiting compliant, scaled platforms.
* Geopolitical events and currency movements created temporary deferrals rather than outright student cancellations.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
* EBITDA margin declined YoY to 32.0% due to a lower revenue base combined with expanded technology investments and team build-outs.
* Depreciation expense dropped to Rs 3.8 crore (vs Rs 6.1 crore in Q2 FY26) due to a accounting shift from Written Down Value (WDV) to Straight Line Method (SLM) effective April 1, 2026.
* Employee costs rose to ~7% of sales in Q2 due to lower top-line and headcount addition (tech + M&A); long-term employee costs are expected at 3% to 4% of sales.
* Steady-state EBITDA margins are expected at 25% to 26% on a full-year basis.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
* Committed USD 2.5 million in March 2026 toward in-house development of "EduMentor", an AI-led student-university matching and mentorship platform.
* Active M&A pipeline targeting EBITDA-accretive opportunities (10% to 20% target IRR) to expand source and destination geographies.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
* Net debt-free balance sheet with free net cash of Rs 595.8 crore (INR 5,958 million) as of September 30, 2026 (up from Rs 469.3 crore as of March 2026).
* Annualized H1 FY27 ROE was 24.6% and ROCE was 31.3%.

### Risks & concerns flagged
* High geographic concentration risk, with UK representing the vast majority of revenue (~98.6%).
* High institutional concentration, with top 3 university partners contributing 44% of revenues.
* Regulatory, immigration, and visa policy shifts in key destination countries (e.g., US visa availability constraints).

### Notable analyst Q&A
* **Q: What caused the 37% revenue drop in Q2, and what gives confidence in recovery?**  
  *Management:* The gap was purely timing-related due to students deferring entry to Nov/Jan intakes following West Asia geopolitical disruptions and currency swings; high visa approval rates for the pipeline provide high confidence of 10-12% Q3 growth.
* **Q: Why not execute a share buyback to deploy excess cash and optimize tax efficiency?**  
  *Management:* Under SEBI regulations, promoter holding must be reduced below 75% before a buyback can be executed; hence buyback is technically not permissible at present.
* **Q: Why haven't non-UK geographies (US/Australia/NZ) scaled up meaningfully despite years of effort?**  
  *Management:* Geographies like the US face policy/visa appointment bottlenecks; non-UK application share doubled to 9.5% in H1 FY27 following recent acquisitions (e.g., Medway Education), which will translate into revenue in H2 (e.g., Feb intake in New Zealand).
* **Q: Will universities' adoption of internal AI tools replace platforms like Crizac?**  
  *Management:* No, AI assists information processing, but students still require counselor support and reassurance. Additionally, intense competition between universities for international student market share ensures platforms remain essential.

### One-line takeaway
Crizac's Q2 was hit hard by external student deferrals, but market share gains and strong intake visibility for H2 allow management to maintain full-year flat revenue/PAT guidance.
