# Adani Total Gas Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-07-28 · Services & Others*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong revenue and volume growth are currently offset by significant margin compression due to geopolitical headwinds and reliance on spot gas purchases.

### Key numbers
*   **Revenue:** INR 1,908 crore (+27% YoY).
*   **EBITDA:** INR 281 crore.
*   **Gas Sales Volume:** 303 MMSCM (+13% YoY).
*   **Segment Growth:** CNG +18% YoY; PNG +4% YoY.
*   **Infrastructure:** 15,987-inch kilometres of steel pipeline; 707 CNG stations (5 added in Q1).
*   **Customer Base:** 11.41 lakh domestic households (38,000 added); 10,422 industrial/commercial customers (392 commercial and 56 industrial added).
*   **E-Mobility:** 5,306 charging points (58 MW capacity); 3.3 million electrons sold (+100% YoY).
*   **JV (IOAGPL):** 53 GAs, 125 districts, 13.74 lakh CNG households, 12,326 commercial/industrial customers, 1,167 CNG stations, 28,600-inch kilometres of pipeline.

### Highlights
*   Management reported resilient operational growth despite geopolitical issues in West Asia and USD/INR currency depreciation.
*   Company is transitioning from a city gas distribution entity to an integrated clean energy platform.
*   ESG ratings improved to 84 (CareEdge) and 66 (CRISIL) out of 100.

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management expects volume growth to continue in a similar manner.
*   Management aims to maintain the current rate of PNG connection additions.
*   Management anticipates margins will recover to previous levels or better once the Middle East crisis subsides and supply from the U.S. and Qatar increases.
*   Management expects potential positive news regarding the reinstatement of the pooled gas mechanism shortly.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   CNG demand is growing at ~30% YoY, supported by OEMs (Maruti, Hyundai, Tata, Eicher, Volvo).
*   Industrial and commercial customer additions were 3x and 2x higher, respectively, compared to the same period last year.
*   Industry-wide training initiatives (via ITIs and diploma colleges) are addressing the shortage of technical personnel for PNG installations.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Operating margins have compressed from ~25% to 15% over the last 6-8 quarters.
*   **Gas Sourcing Mix:** ~62% domestic (APM/NWG), ~38% imported RLNG.
*   Spot purchases currently account for ~15% of total consumption.
*   NWG prices are >$5/MMBTU (12% of Indian crude basket).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   Capex budget is slightly higher than last year.
*   Management is deploying a dedicated task force to accelerate domestic PNG connections.
*   Exploring new business models, such as converting existing reticulated LPG systems to gas.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Geopolitical tensions in West Asia causing elevated Brent-linked gas prices.
*   Withdrawal of the pooled gas mechanism by the government.
*   Permissions and regulatory coordination challenges in certain states.
*   Shortage of technical personnel for PNG rollout (though management noted this is being mitigated).

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Sridhar (Prasid Capitals):** Asked about the cause of margin compression (25% to 15%). Management attributed it to gas sourcing costs, reduced APM allocation, and reliance on spot market purchases.
*   **Arya Patel (Emkay Global):** Asked about the impact of the government withdrawing the pooled gas mechanism. Management confirmed the withdrawal and stated they are seeking midterm purchases to manage the shortfall.
*   **Nirmal Gole (Aditya Birla PMS):** Asked if the 15% spot purchase reliance is structural. Management stated it is a result of the current crisis and expects it to reduce as curtailment eases.
*   **Kirtan Mehta (Baroda BNP Paribas):** Asked about the status of the pooled gas mechanism. Management indicated industry-level representations are ongoing and expects positive news shortly.

### One-line takeaway
Strong volume growth and network expansion are currently overshadowed by margin pressure due to high spot gas reliance and geopolitical headwinds, with recovery contingent on supply normalization and government policy support.
