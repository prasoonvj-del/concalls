# WeWork India Management Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-30 · Real Estate*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong financial performance with 31.6% YoY revenue growth, expanding margins, record desk additions, and robust free cash flow generation.

### Key numbers
- **Total Desks / Capacity:** 145,300 desks across 85 centres in 8 cities (up 27% YoY); 11,700 desks added in Q2; 18,400 added in H1 FY27; 4,500 added in October.
- **Members & Occupancy:** Members grew 32.5% YoY to ~121,700; overall occupancy at 83.7% (mature centres near 88%, growth centres at 64.8%).
- **Revenue:** Core revenue at Rs 657.7 crores (up 31.7% YoY); Value-Added Services (VAS) at Rs 79.6 crores (up 35% YoY); Digital at Rs 29.2 crores (up 46% YoY); Total revenue from operations at Rs 766.5 crores (up 32.5% YoY); Total reported revenue (I-GAAP equivalent) up 31.6% YoY and 10.1% QoQ to ~Rs 768.7 crores. H1 FY27 revenue at Rs 1,466.7 crores (up 30%).
- **EBITDA & Margins:** EBITDA up 37.6% YoY and 17.8% QoQ to Rs 162.9 crores; EBITDA margin at 21.2% (up 138 bps QoQ). Centre-level EBITDA grew 38.4% YoY to Rs 214 crores (margin at 28.4%). H1 FY27 EBITDA at Rs 301.2 crores (up 50.6%).
- **PAT:** PAT grew 67.4% YoY to Rs 65.8 crores (margin 8.6%). Reported Ind AS PAT at Rs 20.5 crores. H1 FY27 PAT at Rs 119 crores (more than doubled).
- **Returns & Cash Flow:** ROCE reached 34.5% (up >12 points YoY). Free cash flow from operations (FCFO) at Rs 237.7 crores in Q2 (1.5x of EBITDA); H1 FCFO at Rs 379.8 crores. Net cash position at Rs 23.1 crores as of September (improved from net debt of Rs 310.5 crores a year ago).
- **Borrowing Costs & Credit Rating:** Average cost of borrowing down 184 bps to 8.5%; credit rating upgraded from A- to A+.
- **Guidance & Contract Metrics:** Locked-in core revenue grew ~66% to Rs 3,843 crores against Rs 1,421 crores of rent (2.7x multiple). Average commitment term up to ~28 months (larger accounts at ~32 months). FY27 capacity guidance set at 150,000 to 155,000 desks by March 2027. Capex expected around Rs 500 crores for FY27 and ~Rs 600 crores (or slightly more) for FY28.

### Highlights
- Delivered the largest investment cycle in company history while expanding margins and doubling H1 ROCE to 31.1%.
- Over half of the Q2 capacity additions were fully leased from day one, driven by demand in Chennai, Hyderabad, and Pune.
- Bangalore remains the anchor (54,000 desks), Hyderabad overtook Mumbai as the second-largest market (20,000 desks), and Pune has 17,000 desks.
- Secured pipeline includes over 9,000 desks under leases signed/fit-out and 38,000 seats under LOIs.
- Digital products operate near an 80% EBITDA margin, contributing disproportionately to profitability alongside VAS.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to reach 150,000 to 155,000 seats by March 2027, on the way to 190,000 to 200,000 seats, with supply for FY28 and FY29 already under negotiation.
- Projected FY27 capex around Rs 500 crores; FY28 capex expected around Rs 600 crores or slightly higher to support accelerated expansion.
- PAT margins expected to hover between 7% to 10% with incoming growth.
- EBITDA margin range projected between 20% to 23% for next year.
- Management plans to explore dividends, buybacks, or investments into adjacent businesses if excess cash persists, alongside ongoing promoter deleveraging.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India office absorption reached 21 million square feet in Q3 (up 6% YoY), with Global Capability Centres (GCCs) taking 41% and Flex taking 24% (5 million sq ft, up 21% YoY).
- Flex has been India's largest occupier segment for three consecutive quarters; domestic companies account for 47% of leasing (flex operators driving half).
- 5-plus year enterprise leases have collapsed from 40% to 8%; companies prefer flexible formats due to headcount unpredictability and AI/macro shifts.
- GCC demand is shifting from full-time equivalent (FTE) headcount metrics to productivity and outcome-driven metrics, increasing demand for premium amenities, wellness spaces, F&B, and collaborative spaces.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Centre-level EBITDA margin expanded 109 bps YoY to 28.4% (28.1% in H1).
- Opex per square foot increased due to a ~60% minimum wage hike in Karnataka, sizable hikes in Haryana, and backdated energy tariffs from BESCOM in Karnataka.
- Fixed costs are growing much slower than revenue, driving structural margin expansion.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 11,700 desks in Q2, taking total capacity to 145,300 desks across 85 centres in 8 cities.
- Managed office deals and larger dedicated capex deals require higher per-square-foot capex but yield higher revenue-to-rent multiples (~3x vs 2.7x historically).
- Q2 capex stood at Rs 175.8 crores; H1 capex totaled Rs 363.8 crores, fully funded by operating cash flows with a Rs 16 crore surplus.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash position of Rs 23.1 crores as of September 2026, compared to net debt of Rs 310.5 crores a year ago.
- Credit rating upgraded from A- to A+; average borrowing cost reduced by 184 bps YoY to 8.5%.
- Security deposit float and upfront customization cash collection drive strong free cash flow conversion (FCFO-to-EBITDA range of 1.2x to 1.5x).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Minimum wage spikes (e.g., ~60% in Karnataka) and retroactive energy tariff hikes increased opex per square foot.
- Large lumpy move-outs anticipated next year, such as Commonwealth Bank of Australia shifting to its own 1.2 million sq ft built-to-suit campus with Embassy in Manyata (though backfill and large incoming contracts are expected to offset this).
- Promoter share pledge remains active due to market volatility, though management stated a 100% commitment to deleveraging over the next three years without rushing block sales at depressed share prices.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Girish Choudhary (Avendus Spark) on underwriting new assets vs historical levels:** Management stated they are taking larger positions in Grade A assets, signing under-development properties, and grouping multiple properties per transaction. Return profiles and payback periods continue to improve.
2. **Adhidev Chattopadhyay (Elara Capital) on GCC headcount trends:** Management noted GCCs are insourcing work from the US/IT services to control culture and talent, shifting toward outcome-driven productivity and higher-spec collaborative spaces.
3. **Mohit Agrawal (BOB Capital Markets) on renewal economics and capex:** Management noted renewals see 6% to 7% contracted escalations with an 80%-85% retention rate. Heavy refurbishment is typically only deployed between years 5 and 7, protecting margins.
4. **Ankit Minocha (Individual Investor) on promoter share pledges:** Management acknowledged the negative impact on share valuation and stated they are on a clear deleveraging path, aiming to resolve the pledge over a three-year window when pricing and market conditions permit.
5. **Mayur Patel (Multi-Act) on FY28 capex and margin sustainability:** Management guided for FY28 capex of around Rs 600 crores (or slightly higher) and maintained an EBITDA margin guidance range of 20% to 23% for next year.

### One-line takeaway
WeWork India delivered a stellar Q2 FY27 with 31.6% YoY revenue growth, record desk additions, expanding EBITDA margins to 21.2%, and a transition to a net cash balance sheet, driven by robust enterprise and GCC demand for branded flexible workspaces.
