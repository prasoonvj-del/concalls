## Real Estate: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

### Big picture
Residential demand remains bifurcated: premium and luxury segments (>Rs 1 crore) show strong resilience across MMR, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune and Hyderabad, while affordable and mid-income (<Rs 1 crore) face headwinds from rate hikes and affordability constraints. Commercial office leasing is robust at ~21 msf in Q2 (up 6% YoY), driven by GCCs (41% share) and flex operators (24% share). Most developers report healthy collections, strong launch pipelines for H2 FY27, and a structural shift toward asset-light JDA/JV models and annuity income (rental, data centers, senior living, flex space). Balance sheets are generally strong — several large caps are net cash or low leverage — though a few mid-caps carry higher debt. The sector is executing a heavy H2 launch calendar while navigating NCR pollution/GRAP risks and approval timelines.

### Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Indian real estate has traditionally been asset-heavy with outright land purchases, project-completion accounting, and reliance on new launches for revenue recognition. Over the last ~5 years, the model has shifted toward asset-light JDA/JV structures, annuity income streams (commercial leasing, data centers, senior living, flex space), platform-based tech integration, and institutional capital partnerships. ### Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
**Consensus**
- **Premium/luxury residential (>Rs 1 crore) strong** across MMR, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad — Aditya Birla, DLF, Godrej, Lodha, Brigade, Phoenix Mills, Sri Lotus, Signatureglobal, Puravankara, Keystone, Sunteck, Suraj Estate all cite resilience.
- **Commercial office leasing robust** — GCCs 41-42% of absorption, flex operators 24-25%; Awfis, Indiqube, Smartworks, WeWork, Horizon, Phoenix Mills, Brigade, DLF all report strong pre-leasing and rent growth.
- **End-user driven, not speculative** — Embassy, Puravankara, Lodha, Mahindra Lifespaces emphasize genuine demand.
- **Price growth 3-8%** — MMR sales up 11% YoY (Aditya Birla), Pune/Bengaluru absorption up 3% with prices +3%/+8% (Aditya Birla), NCR prices +12% (Max Estates), Mumbai luxury +6.2% (Sri Lotus).

**Divergence**
- **Affordable/mid-income weakness** — Aditya Birla: "segments below Rs 1 crore show signs of weakness"; Ashiana: "affordability and own-contribution challenges"; Ganesh Housing: "affordable segment continues to shrink"; Raymond Realty: rate hikes impact affordable but not their Rs 1.5 cr+ segment.
- **Rate hike impact** — Ganesh Housing, Raymond Realty, Sri Lotus say 25-50 bps hikes weigh on affordable/mid-income but luxury buyers less loan-dependent; Kalpataru says "demand healthy everywhere, from sub-3 crore up to South Mumbai luxury"; Puravankara sees "no slowdown"; Mahindra Lifespaces notes "slight slowdown due to macro factors (war, job market, FII outflows)".

### Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Construction cost inflation 4-5%** over last 8-9 months (Arihant, Lodha); steel ~Rs 68-71k/tonne (Veegaland); cables, PVC, sanitary items up slightly (Veegaland).
- **Labor cost pressure** — Karnataka ~60% minimum wage hike, Haryana sizable hikes, backdated energy tariffs (WeWork); most developers passing on via 3-5% price hikes (Kalpataru, Lodha).
- **Project-level EBITDA margins 25-35%** typical — Birla Navya 35%, Niyaara 40%+ (Aditya Birla); Puravankara ~30%; Suraj Estate 33-35%; Sri Lotus 35-40% guided; Keystone mass market 16%→31% (Virar JDA); commercial leasing margins 70%+ (Brigade, Phoenix Mills, Horizon, DLF).
- **Approval/marketing costs pressuring near-term EBITDA** — Arkade (Rs 70 cr approval costs in inventory), Max Estates (Rs 20 cr marketing vs Rs 12 cr YoY), Godrej (consolidation adjustments), EFC (material price spikes).
