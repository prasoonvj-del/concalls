# Indiqube Spaces Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-18 · Real Estate*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Highest-ever quarterly revenue of Rs 428 crore, robust YoY growth across profitability metrics, and strong operating leverage.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs 428 crore, up 37% YoY
- EBITDA: Rs 87 crore, up 34% YoY
- EBITDA margin: 20%
- EBIT: Rs 55 crore, up from 59% (note: transcript states EBIT grew from 59% to Rs 55 crores, and EBIT margin improved to 13% from 11% YoY)
- PAT: Rs 35 crore, up 91% YoY
- PAT margin: 8%, up from 6% YoY
- Area under management added: 1.91 million square feet added during the year (with 17 new centers launched)
- Solar operational capacity: 30 megawatt (targeting additional 25-30 megawatts)
- Value-added services (VAS) contribution: Currently around 17% of total revenue (up from 12%-15%)
- Occupancy rates: 80% to 85% at the corporate level; 85% to 90% on mature centers (currently overall corporate occupancy stands at 86%)

### Highlights
- Delivered highest-ever quarterly revenue of Rs 428 crore.
- Catered to 855 clients as of June 2026, with Global Capability Centers (GCCs) contributing 53% of revenue and multi-center clients accounting for 41%.
- Nearly 90% of occupants come from clients taking more than 100 seats.
- Rebranded offerings: Bespoke renamed to DesignQube, IndiQube One renamed to IndiCare (now extending into retail), and Eco.
- Solar capacity additions require an estimated capex of Rs 100 crore to Rs 120 crore.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management reiterated the target to add close to 2 million square feet annually (~44,000 seats per year).
- Headroom of about 3.9 million square feet (~97,000 seats) already signed and in the pipeline.
- VAS revenue contribution is expected to increase further by 2% to 4% over time.
- Management aims to become the total outsourcing solution for commercial spaces.

### Demand & industry commentary
- GCCs contributed 52%-53% of revenue; startups and unicorns 23%; Indian enterprises 28% (including Indian IT services and companies like Mahindra Logistics and TVS).
- Strong demand drivers include better administration, law and order, and the upcoming Jewar airport, supporting large expansion in Noida (e.g., signing a large center expected to go live by mid-next year / Q2-Q3 FY28).
- Strategy remains "Follow the Talent," building high-density clusters in talent-rich micro-markets.
- Bangalore constitutes ~60% of the portfolio, but management expects faster growth in NCR, Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Chennai.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating leverage translating growth in scale into stronger profitability (EBIT margin at 13%, PAT margin at 8%).
- VAS standalone margin is maintained around 15% as priority remains on scale and growth over near-term margin expansion.
- Real estate acquisition costs in Tier-2 cities range between Rs 50 to Rs 70 per square foot, with seat pricing offered between Rs 6,500 and Rs 7,500 per seat.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned solar capex of Rs 100 crore to Rs 120 crore for 25-30 megawatts of additional capacity in FY27.
- New centers typically reach operating break-even (52%-57% occupancy) within 5 to 6 months and 90% occupancy within 9 to 12 months.
- Institutional supply accounts for ~20% of the portfolio (up from 12% a couple of years ago), with the rest from ultra-HNI landlords.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt increased due to loans taken specifically for solar plant expansion, not for the normal leasing business.
- Client notice periods range between 60 to 90 days.
- Straight leases with landlords; no occupancy-linked back-to-back arrangements.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- None explicitly flagged as material risks by management, though quarterly additions in rent-paying area can vary based on building ramp-up timings.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shamit Ashar (Ambit Capital) on rent-paying area growth vs annual guidance:** Management explained that rent-paying area additions are booked annually rather than sequentially due to varied center addition timings, but reiterated the 2 million square feet annual guidance backed by 3.9 million square feet of signed headroom.
2. **Shamit Ashar (Ambit Capital) on solar capex and IRRs:** Management stated that solar investments require Rs 100-120 crore for 25-30 MW and deliver robust IRRs of 18% to 22%.
3. **Yog Rajani (Omega Portfolio Advisors) on newer center break-even impact on margins:** Management clarified that new centers achieve operating break-even in 5-6 months, exerting no pressure on corporate margins.
4. **Sourabh Gilda (JM Financial Services) on the large Noida center addition:** Management stated the large supply addition is backed by positive micro-market fundamentals, administration improvements, and the Jewar airport, rather than being strictly pre-committed.
5. **Jainam Sanghvi (Dhamma Capital) on institutional vs HNI supply pricing:** Management noted that while institutional supply costs more due to superior grade and CAM charges, it attracts high-paying GCCs, keeping margins consistent across both.
6. **Vikrant Kashyap (Asian Market Securities) on VAS margin impact:** Management stated VAS margins will be kept around 15% for the next 1-2 years to prioritize scaling over margin expansion.

### One-line takeaway
Indiqube delivered a strong Q1 FY27 with a 37% YoY revenue jump to Rs 428 crore and 91% growth in PAT, driven by scale, steady enterprise/GCC demand, and expanding value-added services.
