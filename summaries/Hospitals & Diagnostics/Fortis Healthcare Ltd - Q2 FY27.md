# Fortis Healthcare Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-09-10 · Hospitals & Diagnostics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27 (Referencing earnings call held on September 4, 2026, filed September 10, 2026)
**Tone:** Cautious - Delhi High Court ordered a forensic audit regarding historical transactions, though management remains confident of full legal compliance and business continuity.

### Key numbers
- Legal expenses incurred last year: Rs 25 crore (management budgeted similar amount for the current year).
- Forensic audit timeline: 4 weeks for auditor to send questionnaire, 2 weeks to respond, 6 months to submit report.

### Highlights
- Delhi High Court issued a 200-page order directing the appointment of a forensic auditor to examine historical transactions.
- Scope of the forensic audit covers the evolution of promoter entity FHHPL (2018–2026), IHH's acquisition of a controlling stake and subsequent utilization of funds for RHT Health Trust asset acquisition, role of FHL and its officers in unencumbered share dissipation of erstwhile promoters, and the role of 17 banks/financial institutions.
- Management and IHH stated that the order does not change, limit, or impede strategic/operational plans, bed expansion, or M&A initiatives.
- IHH completed the Mandatory Tender Offer (MTO) last year with SEBI's permission and views Fortis as its growth engine for India, remaining ready to infuse growth capital.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects the forensic audit process to take approximately 6 months as per the court-set timeline.
- IHH reiterated its long-term commitment to increase its stake in Fortis to 50%-plus and support future capex/growth capital needs within legal parameters.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Competitive intensity in the healthcare sector has increased, requiring the company to step up its game, though hospital operations remain insulated from legal proceedings.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cost of the current forensic audit is to be borne by Daiichi as mentioned in the court order.
- Legal expenses were around Rs 25 crore last year, and a similar amount is budgeted for the current year depending on case intensity.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Brownfield expansion and M&A plans continue to progress as planned, primarily driven by internal accruals.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- No financial provisioning or liability has been fixed by the court order; the impact on financials is described as balanced/zero (excluding ongoing legal expenses).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Ongoing legal litigations involving Daiichi Sankyo, including the newly ordered forensic audit by the Delhi High Court and proceedings in Japan.
- Potential short-term distractions, though management stated operations and employee/patient sentiment remain insulated.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Neha Manpuria (Bank of America):** Asked about the scope and timeline of the forensic audit. Management detailed the 4-week questionnaire, 2-week response, and 6-month report timeline, noting no material adverse results are foreseen.
2. **Damayanti Kerai:** Inquired about the worst-case scenario and confidence in settling the matter. Management reiterated confidence due to internal compliance and previous Supreme Court findings that the transaction was in the normal course of business.
3. **Shyam Srinivasan:** Asked about operational impact on employees and doctors. Management stated operations are insulated, teams are transparently communicated with, and clinical services remain unaffected.
4. **Raghavendra Divekar:** Asked if Fortis plans counter-litigation against Daiichi. Management stated they are not pursuing counter-litigation currently while the matter is sub judice, but will protect company interests.
5. **Nikhil Mathur:** Inquired about financial provisioning and legal costs. Vivek Goyal stated no provisioning is needed as no liability is fixed, and legal costs are budgeted around Rs 25 crore similar to last year.
6. **Yan Wu:** Asked about the finality and worst-case scenario. Management stated the audit report submission and subsequent court steps will mark the closure, and reiterated that no significant adverse impact is expected given full legal compliance.

### One-line takeaway
Fortis Healthcare and IHH assured investors that the Delhi High Court's order for a forensic audit does not impede operational plans, capital expansion, or IHH's long-term growth commitment to India.
