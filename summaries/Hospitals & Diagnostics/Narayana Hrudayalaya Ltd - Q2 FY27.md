# Narayana Hrudayalaya Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Hospitals & Diagnostics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong operating performance and margin expansion in India and reduction in international insurance losses, offset by revenue challenges and transition costs in the U.K. business.

### Key numbers
- IP volumes (India): 4% increase YoY; 6% increase QoQ
- OP footfalls (India): 12.5% growth YoY
- ARPOB (India): INR 19.5 million (annualised)
- EBITDA margin (India/Hospital): >26% (>25% noted by analyst)
- Doctors and nursing costs (India): Decreased by 1.9% QoQ
- Oncology revenue contribution (India): ~15%
- Insurance and clinic losses (India): INR 22 crores (down from INR 33 crores previous quarter; Health insurance INR 14 crores, clinics INR 8 crores)
- Cayman insurance losses: Reduced from $3.7 million to $1.1 million
- Group ROCE: 15% to 16% (with U.K. dilution of 4% to 5%)
- Occupancy (India): Hovering at 60% to 65% (traditional calculation)
- ALOS (India): 4.3 to 4.5 days

### Highlights
- Best-in-class margins achieved in India despite lower ARPOB compared to peers, driven by operating leverage, digitization, process improvements, and supply chain efficiencies.
- Doctor and nursing costs in India declined due to operating leverage on a 9% QoQ revenue increase over a largely fixed clinical/nursing base.
- Combined losses for Indian insurance and clinics decreased to INR 22 crores from INR 33 crores.
- Cayman insurance losses dropped significantly from $3.7 million to $1.1 million due to repricing, purging poor-performing accounts, and process improvements.
- U.K. integration is progressing with supply chain/consumables changes and rollout of a Global Capability Center (GCC) for patient communication; Athma (digital platform) implementation expected in 4-5 months following regulatory certification.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management stated they do not target specific margins or guide on occupancy/run-rates, focusing instead on volume growth and accessing the largest patient pool.
- Management aims to bring India ALOS closer to 4 or 4.1 days over the next 4 to 6 quarters.
- U.K. business is viewed on a 5 to 7-year time horizon.
- Cayman insurance expects negligible losses before July 2027, exiting almost all unprofitable books by July 2027.
- Bed additions (635 beds in FY28) will be guided 1 quarter in advance of commissioning.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Q2 is historically a strong quarter across financial years.
- Eastern cluster in India experienced a transition issue in porting certain government scheme patients in Bengal, expected to settle in Q3 resulting in stronger footfall growth.
- International patients (primarily from Bangladesh) contribute 6% to 7% of hospital revenue organically.
- U.K. market faces temporary NHS slow down in contracting, while private patient acquisition requires building blocks (empinellisation and doctor recognition).
- Cayman Islands hospital growth is constrained by the small island population; integrated care strategy is focused on securing the existing patient base.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Oncology drug price caps go live on November 9; management notes potential short-term adjustments while supporting transparent and affordable pricing.
- U.K. separation costs (novating agreements, raising prices by vendors post-separation from larger group) temporarily impacted cost bases.
- Supply chain and consumable changes active in U.K. operations; GCC setup providing cost efficiencies.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Clinic business in India is being merged into the hospital business.
- U.K. digital platform undergoing regulatory certification prior to Athma rollout.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Group ROCE is 15% to 16%, impacted by Cayman, U.K., and India capex phases; U.K. causes a 4% to 5% dilution.
- U.K. net debt stands at approximately INR 1,700 crores; NHS is a prompt payer.
- H1 cash conversion from EBITDA was ~88% (compared to historical ~93%), impacted by slight collection slowness from government and insurance payers in India and Cayman, which management expects to normalize annually.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Cybersecurity risks in interconnected healthcare systems highlighted as a constant concern requiring ongoing investment.
- Regulatory price caps in oncology (effective Nov 9) and insurance distribution guidelines.
- Revenue volatility and learning curve in U.K. private patient acquisition and NHS contracting.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Prithvi Raj (Unifi Capital) on India Margins & ARPOB:** Asked if margins can expand further given the ARPOB gap with peers. **Management (Viren Shetty / R. Venkatesh):** Clarified they do not target margins; margins reflect efficiency and digitization, and volume growth is the core focus rather than ARPOB chasing.
2. **Prithvi Raj on Insurance Losses:** Questioned the timeline and losses for the insurance business. **Management (Ravi Vishwanath / Viren Shetty):** Stated claims ratios are trending down and the direct retail/SME model is building up; they will neither give up nor pursue it at any cost.
3. **Sajal Kapoor (Antifragile Thinking) on U.K. & Insurance ROCE:** Asked about the time horizon for evaluating ROCE. **Management (Sandhya Jayaraman):** Suggested a 5-7 year horizon for the U.K.; insurance is viewed as a financing continuum of care rather than a standalone ROCE business.
4. **Vinay Nadkarni (Hathway Investments) on U.K. Performance:** Inquired if the U.K. is on track despite revenue dips. **Management (Anesh Shetty):** Confirmed cost synergies and GCC rollouts are on track, but private revenue acquisition faces a learning curve and regulatory/empanelment delays.
5. **Prithvi Raj on Cayman Insurance Breakeven:** Asked about expected loss reduction post-January resets. **Management (Anesh Shetty):** Confirmed rapid loss reduction ($3.7M to $1.1M) and expects negligible losses ahead of the major July 2027 reset.

### One-line takeaway
Narayana Hrudayalaya delivered strong Q2 FY27 India margins (>26%) and reduced international insurance burn, even as it navigates revenue headwinds and transition costs in its newly acquired U.K. business.
