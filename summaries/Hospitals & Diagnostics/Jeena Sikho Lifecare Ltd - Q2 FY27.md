# Jeena Sikho Lifecare Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Hospitals & Diagnostics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Management acknowledged failing to meet expectations and growth due to strategic missteps and higher expenses, but expressed confidence in a recovery driven by new processes, product adjustments, and the restart of government portals.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained overall annual growth guidance (20%-25% sales growth, 35%-40% EBITDA margin), but explicitly walked back ambitious PAT targets (such as Rs 300 crore) calling them very difficult due to a weak Q3.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from highly positive/confident to apologetic and introspective regarding strategy experiments, ticket size drops, and margin compression.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Introduced new risks around aggressive e-commerce ad spends and low Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) experiments that compressed margins; government portal 2.0 restart became a major new discussion point.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Manali luxury wellness center soft-launched, but commercial rental operations were delayed from October to December 15 due to delayed international machinery shipments (hyperbaric oxygen and ozone therapy) caused by the Iran-Iraq war.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations (Q2 FY27): Rs 235 crore (up 24% YoY, up 5% QoQ)
- Revenue from operations (H1 FY27): Rs 460 crore (up 26% YoY)
- EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs 72 crore (margin 31%)
- EBITDA (H1 FY27): Rs 164 crore (margin 36%)
- PAT (Q2 FY27): Rs 36 crore
- PAT (H1 FY27): Rs 102 crore
- Gross margin (Q2 FY27): 88%
- Operational beds: Approximately 2,424 operational beds across 62 hospitals and 60 clinics/daycare centers in 23 states; 460 beds in the pipeline
- IPD patients volume (H1 FY27): 22,480
- IPD patients volume (Q2 FY27): 10,980 (up 14% YoY)
- OPD volume (H1 FY27): 3.1 lakh
- OPD volume (Q2 FY27): 1.59 lakh (up 14% YoY)
- OPD and consultation customers (H1 FY27): 12.1 lakh
- OPD and consultation customers (Q2 FY27): 7.1 lakh (up 174% YoY)
- Hospital bed occupancy (Q2 FY27): 55% (57% in H1)
- E-commerce product sales volume (Q2 FY27): 4.63 lakh pieces (vs 2.64 lakh in Q1) generating Rs 58 crore (vs Rs 32 crore in Q1)

### Highlights
- Management apologized for missing expectations and profitability due to unsuccesful strategic experiments in e-commerce and low-margin preventive pricing trials.
- Government portal 2.0 started post-Diwali in November, allowing the company to restart government business (CGHS, CAPF, ECHS) which had dropped from 25% in FY25 to 4% in FY26 due to stuck payments; management set a cap of 15% dependency on government business.
- Established an in-house research center in Chandigarh, headed by a retired NCISN official.
- Signed first wellness road property in Himachal Pradesh (Manali) with 108 rooms and 24 villas; launching commercially on December 15.
- Established a 300-seat call center in Noida and launched a "Certified Natural Healer" (CNH) course with 500 initial enrollments to build a direct agent/healer network and reduce reliance on paid ads.
- NABH accreditations received for Pune (September, valid till July 2029) and Raipur (October 1, valid till August 2029) hospitals, taking accredited hospitals to 52 (with 12 in pipeline).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects 20%-25% YoY revenue growth for FY27.
- EBITDA margin targeted to be maintained between 35%-40% in H2 (specifically aiming for stabilization in Q4).
- Operational beds expected to reach approximately 2,900 to 3,000 by March 31, 2027 (with Lucknow and Ahmedabad adding 150 beds each in January).
- Long-term target to reach 7,000 to 10,000 beds within 3 to 5 years.
- ARPOB targeted to return to Rs 8,200–Rs 8,300 over the next 2-3 quarters.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India faces a major lifestyle disease challenge (hypertension, diabetes, obesity, cholesterol), where prevention-first and Panchakarma Ayurveda approaches are critical.
- Consumer acquisition via e-commerce grew rapidly, but management realized low-margin product sales without immediate service conversion suppressed overall profitability.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating expenses rose 83% YoY due to investments in new product launches, hiring, technology, and network expansion.
- Advertisement and promotion expenses increased by Rs 13.4 crore YoY (total ad spend reached Rs 34 crore in Q2 vs Rs 21 crore in Q1, driven by brand building and television campaigns like Khatron Ke Khiladi). Total H1 ad spend was Rs 56 crore. Management guides for ad spend to normalize to Rs 20-22 crore quarterly by Q3/Q4.
- Employee benefit expenses increased by Rs 4.1 crore YoY due to new talent hiring.
- Estimated Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning impacted profitability by Rs 3 crore in Q2 (approx Rs 20-21 crore for the full year).
- Other income was wiped out due to negative fair market valuation adjustments on Chandan Healthcare shares.
- Manali luxury resort lease model has a fixed monthly expense of Rs 60-75 lakh, which management states is fully covered by an average daily booking of just 8 to 10 rooms.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 3 new clinics in Q2; 460 beds are in the pipeline.
- Ahmedabad (150 beds) and Lucknow (150 beds) hospitals scheduled to open in January.
- Manali luxury wellness property starting commercial operations on December 15, featuring international therapy machines (hyperbaric oxygen chamber, ozone therapy).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Capital-light hospital expansion model: bed preparation costs Rs 3 lakh to Rs 4 lakh per bed (compared to Rs 30-50 lakh in allopathy hospitals), with a 35% occupancy break-even and a 12-18 month payback period.
- Government payment realization cycle is noted at 2 to 4 months under portal 2.0.
- Management is evaluating a potential share buyback in an upcoming board meeting, subject to board approval and consultations.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unsuccessful strategic experiments and high customer acquisition burn in e-commerce compressed Q2 margins (EBITDA margin dropped to 31% from 48% YoY and 41% QoQ).
- High dependency on ad spends and low Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) on certain product lines created margin pressure.
- Government business payment delays in the past severely impacted ECL and cash flows, prompting a 15% cap rule going forward.
- Diwali holidays in Q3 disrupted operations for 7-8 days, causing temporary occupancy drops.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Aditya Chheda (InCred) / Priyam Shah:** Asked about the drop in per-patient realization and feasibility of guidance. Management explained they experimented with discounted rates for Ayushman trial runs and preventive packages, lowering ticket sizes, but confirmed portal 2.0 approval on Nov 6 will revive institutional business. Management conceded that an annual PAT of Rs 300 crore is unlikely due to a weak Q3, but guided for recovery in Q4.
2. **Bhavya Gandhi (Bajaj Alternative Investment Management):** Questioned why margins cannot bounce back immediately in Q3 and asked about buyback plans. Management stated Q3 has Diwali interruptions, and margin recovery will properly reflect in Q4 after terminating low-ROAS product campaigns. On buybacks, management noted a board meeting is scheduled that week to discuss the proposal with stakeholders.
3. **Kaushik:** Asked why negative surprises were not communicated earlier and about ARPOB drops. Management admitted the mistake of experimenting with 4,000 ROAS product models (which they stopped in November) and vowed to return ARPOB to Rs 8,200-8,300 in 2-3 quarters.
4. **Jaspreet Singh:** Pressed for a detailed breakdown of the incremental Rs 46 crore YoY jump in other expenses. CFO Nanak Chand clarified the components: Rs 13.4 crore in ads, Rs 4.1 crore in employee benefits, higher leasehold rentals, courier costs for e-commerce, and Rs 3 crore in ECL provisions.
5. **Ranvir Singh:** Asked about the OTC product run-rate and Entero partnership. Management clarified they shifted away from a traditional OTC/shopkeeper model due to low margins and heavy advertising costs, establishing their own e-commerce distribution channel instead while keeping Entero/Satkartar tie-ups for product-to-hospital referrals starting in December.

### One-line takeaway
Jeena Sikho Lifecare suffered margin compression and missed growth targets in Q2 due to costly e-commerce ad burns and low-margin preventive pricing trials, but management is recalibrating toward higher-ROAS conversions, restarting government portal 2.0 business, and maintaining 35%-40% EBITDA margin guidance for H2.
