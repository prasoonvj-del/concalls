# Hospitals & Diagnostics - Hospitals: Sector Overview (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The sector continues to exhibit strong operational momentum, characterized by robust volume-led revenue growth and successful capacity utilization in new facilities. While the underlying demand for complex tertiary and quaternary care remains resilient, the narrative this quarter has shifted to include a new regulatory headwind: the government-imposed trade margin capping on oncology drugs. Managements are generally confident in their ability to absorb these costs through operating leverage and volume growth, though some have adopted a more cautious stance on forward-looking margin guidance.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** There is broad agreement on the strength of demand for complex specialties, particularly oncology, cardiac sciences, and neurosciences. International patient business remains a key growth driver for players like Artemis (30% of revenue) and HCG (growing at 25%).
*   **Divergence:** While most players reported sustained growth, some noted seasonal fluctuations. Rainbow Children’s and Jupiter Life Line Hospitals cited a seasonally soft quarter due to the lack of monsoon-related infectious diseases. Conversely, Global Health reported a 30% YoY increase in inpatient volumes, and Fortis noted that key specialties like renal and neuro recorded revenue growth of 27–30%.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Regulatory Headwinds:** The most significant point of disagreement is the impact of the new trade margin cap on oncology drugs.
    *   **Downplaying impact:** KIMS, Shalby, and Rainbow Children’s view the impact as "insignificant" or manageable, noting that oncology is not a dominant revenue contributor or that surgeries are billed as fixed-price packages.
    *   **Cautious/Quantifying:** HCG expects a "mid-to-high single-digit" annualized EBITDA impact. Fortis has explicitly refrained from providing future margin guidance, citing the uncertainty surrounding these regulations.
*   **Wage Inflation:** HCG specifically flagged a ~Rs 60 million impact on EBITDA due to the Karnataka Wage Act 2026 revision.
*   **Operating Leverage:** Companies like Narayana Hrudayalaya and Global Health continue to emphasize operating leverage as the primary tool to offset cost pressures, with Narayana noting a decrease in doctor and nursing costs as a percentage of revenue.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Aggressive Expansion:** The sector remains in a heavy capex cycle. Global Health plans to invest over Rs 6,000 crore over the next 4–5 years. Fortis has committed Rs 3,500 crore over the next 3–3.5 years. Rainbow Children’s has 1,050 beds in the pipeline.
*   **Conservative/Maintenance:** Shalby is a notable outlier, restricting capex to replacement needs (Rs 10 crore in H1) to focus on debt reduction.
*   **Construction Risks:** Artemis and Global Health both flagged the potential impact of Delhi’s GRAP (pollution norms) on construction timelines for their South Delhi projects.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Breakeven Milestones:** Several companies hit critical operational milestones. Global Health’s Noida hospital and Max Healthcare’s Max Smart (260 beds) both achieved EBITDA breakeven within a year/months of launch.
*   **Guidance Shifts:** Fortis Healthcare shifted from providing margin guidance to a more cautious stance due to regulatory noise.
*   **Regulatory:** The introduction of oncology drug price caps is the primary new variable that did not exist in Q1.

## Company one-liners
*   **Artemis:** Strong operational performance in Gurugram and successful launch of the Raipur facility drive growth.
*   **Fortis:** Delivered strong operational growth but turned cautious on future margin guidance due to new regulatory risks.
*   **Global Health:** Achieved 34% revenue growth and EBITDA breakeven at the Noida facility within a year.
*   **Gujarat Kidney:** Delivered strong revenue growth via an asset-light acquisition model while maintaining a net cash balance sheet.
*   **HCG:** Sustained growth momentum and narrowing losses in new facilities, offset by wage revisions and regulatory headwinds.
*   **Jupiter Life Line:** Stable performance led by seasonal occupancy highs and a strategic land buy for Pune South.
*   **KIMS:** Recorded 41.3% YoY revenue growth driven by rapid ramp-ups in new units like Kondapur.
*   **Max Healthcare:** Solid 20% revenue growth while absorbing greenfield/brownfield ramp-up drags.
*   **Narayana Hrudayalaya:** Strong India margins (>26%) and reduced international insurance burn, despite U.K. transition costs.
*   **Rainbow Children's:** Robust 23% revenue growth supported by core pediatric demand as it presses ahead with pan-India expansion.
*   **Shalby:** Stable performance with 9.8% revenue growth, prioritizing internal efficiencies and debt reduction.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **Regulatory Impact:** The actual financial impact of the oncology drug price caps on EBITDA margins across the sector.
*   **Construction Timelines:** Whether pollution-linked construction bans in the Delhi/NCR region delay projects for Artemis, Global Health, and others.
*   **New Unit Ramp-ups:** The breakeven progress of HCG’s North Bengaluru unit and Shalby’s Gurugram facility.
