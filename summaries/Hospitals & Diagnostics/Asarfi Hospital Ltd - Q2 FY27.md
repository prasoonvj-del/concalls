# Asarfi Hospital Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-26 · Hospitals & Diagnostics*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Highest ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, and PAT, though tempered by high receivables and lower-than-expected initial performance in the cancer unit.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 53 crore, up 18.1% YoY (from Rs 44.89 crore in Q2 FY26).
- EBITDA: Rs 11.89 crore, up 34% YoY (from Rs 8.87 crore in Q2 FY26).
- EBITDA margin: 22.4%, up 260 bps YoY (from 19.8% in Q2 FY26).
- EBIT: Rs 9.51 crore, up 41.6% YoY; EBIT margin at 17.9% (vs 15% YoY).
- PAT: Rs 6.09 crore, up 45% YoY (from Rs 4.2 crore in Q2 FY26); PAT margin at 11.5% (vs 9.4% YoY).
- Super specialty unit occupancy: 64%; ARPOB: Rs 23,716, up 17% YoY. Bed capacity expanded from 265 to 285 beds.
- Cancer hospital occupancy: 40%; ARPOB: Rs 50,572. Bed capacity: 65 beds.
- Total IPD volume: 4,429 patients (up 13% YoY); OPD volume: 41,285 patients (up 25% YoY); Total surgeries: 2,295 (up 25% YoY).
- Trade receivables: Rs 75 crore (H1).

### Highlights
- Delivered highest-ever quarterly revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, and PAT on a consolidated basis.
- Super specialty revenue grew to Rs 41.3 crore (vs Rs 32.2 crore in Q2 FY26); pediatric/neonatology grew 78%, neurosciences 42%, obs & gynae 41%, orthopedic 36%, cardiology 14%.
- Cancer hospital revenue stood at Rs 11.6 crore; haemato oncology and head & neck oncology grew 46% and 43% respectively.
- Connected with ~1,500 doctors across Jharkhand and part of West Bengal, expanding promotional coverage from 7 to 10 districts; tied up with new insurance partners (Magma, ManipalCigna, Galaxy Health, IFFCO Tokio, Niva Bupa, MD India).
- Mainboard migration formality has started and is expected to complete before the close of FY27 (taking ~3 months).
- Declared L1 for a PPP medical college project in Dhanbad (total project cost Rs 352 crore with a Rs 200 crore capital grant from the government, funded on a proportionate 60:40 basis).
- *Record check:* Continues pattern of strong operational numbers and highest-ever profitability, while previous capacity expansion/acquisition timelines face delays (acquisition delayed/shifting to O&M, and research institute postponed to FY29).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management revised FY27 revenue guidance from Rs 260 crore down to Rs 240-245 crore (factoring in a ~Rs 15 crore impact if the planned hospital acquisition does not go through).
- If share transfer issues for the acquisition persist, management plans to take over the unit on an O&M model starting Q1 of next year.
- Aiming to become a 1,000-bed hospital before 2030.
- Targeting to increase cash business share to 60% in super specialty and 30% in the cancer hospital over the next 12 months.
- Healthcare Management Research Institute revenue realization delayed by 1 year; operations now expected to start from FY29.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Cancer hospital initial growth is constrained by a gestation period and trust-building challenges, as patients often initially seek treatment in metro cities and Dhanbad lacks an established prior cancer market.
- Insurance penetration in the region is very low; ~90% of cancer patients use cashless government schemes (Ayushman Bharat, state schemes, PSU schemes), whereas super specialty is ~50% cash/TPA and 50% cashless.
- Daycare procedures (chemo/radiotherapy) in cancer require 24-hour admission under certain PSU rules, causing fluctuations in ARPOB calculations.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA margin expanded to 22.4% due to cost curtailment, minimizing costs, and reducing inventory to offset cash recovery challenges. Management considers this margin sustainable.
- High government receivables increase the company's cost of funding.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Super specialty bed capacity increased from 265 to 285 beds (total combined beds at 350, including 65 cancer beds). Further bed expansion in super specialty is constrained by buildings.
- Healthcare Research Institute site/construction work delayed; revenue shifted to FY29.
- Participated in a PPP medical college project in Dhanbad (Total cost Rs 352 crore, with Rs 200 crore capital grant; 60% central/state funding, 40% bidder funding). Awaiting Letter of Intent (LOI).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Trade receivables stand at around Rs 75 crore out of H1 revenue of Rs 100 crore, heavily weighed down by delayed payments from the Government of Jharkhand and state PSUs (subsidiaries of Coal India). Realization takes 6 to 9 months for some government dues.
- Private insurer/TPA payments take 30 to 60 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High trade receivables tied up with government agencies and the Government of Jharkhand constrain free cash flows and increase funding costs.
- Low trust and lack of an existing cancer market in the 10-district catchment area delay the ramp-up of the cancer hospital unit.
- Potential dependence on debt or equity dilution if working capital (receivables) is not resolved.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Subash (Value Investments)** asked about the Rs 260 crore revenue guidance and acquisition status. Management stated guidance is now revised to ~Rs 240 crore, and if share transfer issues persist, they will move to an O&M model from April 1. *(Direct answers, no dodging)*
2. **Piyush Jain (Individual Investor)** questioned the high Rs 75 crore receivables and government dependency. Management explained it is a systemic issue with the Jharkhand government, but they are meeting officials and targeting higher cash-mix districts to improve collections. *(Direct answers)*
3. **Abhijeet (Paul Asset)** asked about the strategy to achieve 30% cash mix in cancer and details on doctor counts. Management clarified they are targeting patients from distant districts (like Giridih) who are more willing to pay cash, and explained that resident doctor numbers adjusted due to the introduction of DNB students. *(Direct answers)*
4. **Achuth Prabhat (Individual Investor)** inquired about the reasons for high EBITDA margins despite flat occupancy. Management cited cost control and inventory reduction. *(Direct answer)*
5. **Aditi Kasbekar (Individual Investor)** asked about the PPP medical college project status. Management confirmed they are L1, awaiting LOI, with a total project cost of Rs 352 crore backed by a Rs 200 crore government grant on a proportionate 60:40 funding structure. *(Direct answer)*

### One-line takeaway
Asarfi Hospital reported its highest-ever quarterly revenue and EBITDA margins in Q2 FY27, but lowered its FY27 revenue guidance to Rs 240-245 crore as receivables stuck with the state government and slower-than-expected cancer unit trust-building weigh on execution.
