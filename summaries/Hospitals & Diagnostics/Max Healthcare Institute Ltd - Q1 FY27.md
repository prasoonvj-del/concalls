# Max Healthcare Institute Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · Hospitals & Diagnostics*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Healthy momentum with 16% YoY growth in revenue and 15% YoY growth in operating EBITDA, alongside execution of capacity expansion plans.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs 2,982 crore (up 16% YoY, 12% QoQ)
- Operating EBITDA: Rs 704 crore (up 15% YoY, 3% QoQ)
- Operating EBITDA margin: 24.8% (vs 24.9% in Q1 FY26, 26.8% in trailing quarter)
- PAT: Rs 357 crore (vs Rs 345 crore in Q1 FY26, Rs 387 crore in previous quarter)
- ARPOB: Rs 81,900 (up 5% YoY and QoQ)
- Average occupancy: >75%
- Free cash flows generated: Rs 397 crore
- Net debt: Rs 2,384 crore (vs Rs 1,908 crore at end of March 2026)
- Max@Home revenue: Rs 78 crore (up 32% YoY)
- Max Lab revenue: Rs 58 crore (up 25%? Transcript says 20% YoY)

### Highlights
- Started operationalizing 50% capacity of the 400-bed brownfield tower at Max Smart; remaining 50% to be handed over in the current quarter.
- Nanavati Max remaining 50 beds to be operationalized in the current quarter; Phase 2 expansion has commenced.
- Acquired SPV for Pune greenfield project (now a subsidiary).
- Board approved capex of Rs 425 crore for a new brownfield tower at Max Vaishali adding 202 beds (commissioning before FY30).
- Established standalone Max Research Centre; secured several research grants and awards (ICMR, ANRF MAHA MedTech, DBT European Union, India AI Mission).
- Board granted in-principle approval to enter the medical education business following National Medical Commission guideline changes.
- Discontinuation of select high-value chemotherapy drugs for institutional patients reduced oncology share of in-patient revenues to 22% (from 26% in Q1 FY26).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects automatic 6% price revision under insurance renewals in September-October.
- Management expects oncology segment to start normalizing from Q3 FY26 (mid-Q3) / Q4 onwards.
- Expansion projects pipeline: Max Lucknow (100 beds over next 2 quarters), Sector 56 Gurgaon (500 beds, phased commissioning by end of year), Bhubaneswar (250 beds within 12 months), Nagpur (FY28), Zirakpur Mohali (FY28), Dwarka onco day care block (awaiting OC), Dwarka next phase 260 beds (FY30), Pitampura 200 beds (FY29), Patparganj 400 beds (end of FY29), Nanavati Phase 2 (FY30), Pune 450 beds (FY30).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Average network occupancy remained >75% despite a 13% increase in operational bed capacity YoY.
- International patient revenue grew 18% YoY to Rs 247 crore (9% of hospital revenue).
- Digital revenue reached Rs 941 crore (~32% of overall revenue).
- Parliamentary Standing Committee report on affordability and FDI caps: management believes policy needs to balance affordability with the need for new beds and viability.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating EBITDA margin impacted by recent commissioning of new brownfield capacities and Kalinga Hospital acquisition.
- Direct costs grew 16% (in line with 15% revenue growth) due to clinician costs.
- DSO increased from 87 days to 95 days, adding ~Rs 250 crore in accounts receivable due to CGHS/PSU liveness and new portal transition.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Deployed Rs 386 crore towards acquisitions (Kalinga Hospital and Yerawada Properties) and Rs 337 crore towards ongoing capacity expansions during the quarter.
- Estimated spend for a 150-seat medical college is ~Rs 300 crore, targeted at existing hospital campuses (starting with Lucknow), expected to be funded entirely through internal accruals with targeted ROCE of >25%-30%.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt-to-EBITDA ratio remained below 1x (stood at 2,384 crore cr).
- Operating cash flow conversion impacted by AR build-up (DSO at 95 days) and higher effective tax rate.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Institutional business (CGHS/PSU) remains loss-making/non-profitable, contributing only to fixed costs.
- Lumpsum delays and portal transitions for government scheme collections impact working capital and cash flows temporarily.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Damayanti Kerai (HSBC) asked about the turnaround headroom for Kalinga Hospital:** Management replied that with 50% occupancy and Rs 35,000 ARPOB, there is significant headroom (50% to 80% enhancement) which they plan to achieve over the next 12 months via infrastructure renovation, technology upgrades, and clinical programs alongside adding 200-250 beds.
- **Neha Manpuria questioned the EBITDA ramp-up trajectory for Max Smart:** Management explained that brownfields first ramp up occupancy with lower ARPOB/margins, followed by revenue growth, and finally profitability flow-through over the next 2-3 quarters of operations.
- **Bino Pathiparampil asked about litigation concerning Kalinga Hospital minority shareholders:** Management clarified that minority shareholders holding 39% equity based outside India have liquidation proceedings against them, and have filed court requests for Max to buy their un-transactable shares, which Max will do once court orders are secured. Promoters' shares were bought, giving full control.
- **Vivek Agrawal asked why Max's onco revenue is under pressure compared to peers:** Management stated that Max had the largest oncology franchise (>25% revenue share) and highest institutional/CGHS exposure in Delhi, making the impact of discontinuing certain institutional chemo drugs more pronounced. Normalization is expected from Q3/Q4.
- **Alankar Garude asked if net debt has peaked:** Management noted net debt-to-EBITDA is below 1x, and while ongoing capex will consume cash, net debt may marginally rise depending on future cash generation and potential acquisitions up to 2.5x net debt-to-EBITDA.

### One-line takeaway
Max Healthcare delivered 16% YoY revenue growth and 15% EBITDA growth in Q1 FY27, backed by strong occupancies and ongoing brownfield expansions, while laying the groundwork for entry into the for-profit medical education sector.
