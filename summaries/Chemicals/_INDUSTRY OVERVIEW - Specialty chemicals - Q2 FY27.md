# Chemicals - Specialty chemicals: Sector Overview (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The specialty chemicals sector in Q2 FY27 presents a dichotomy: robust volume and revenue growth across most players, contrasted with significant margin pressure caused by external headwinds. Managements are largely optimistic about long-term demand and are aggressively pursuing capacity expansions. However, the short-term narrative is dominated by geopolitical tensions—specifically in the Middle East—which have driven up ocean freight costs and crude-linked raw material prices. While some companies have successfully utilized pass-through pricing mechanisms, others are absorbing costs to maintain market share, leading to margin compression.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Domestic demand remains resilient, particularly in automotive, infrastructure, and construction segments (Apcotex, J.G. Chemicals, NOCIL, Vishnu, Yasho).
*   **Divergence:**
    *   **Strength:** Semiconductor, battery chemicals, and CRAMS/CEM businesses are seeing strong traction (Aether, Acutaas, Neogen).
    *   **Weakness:** The agrochemical sector remains under pressure due to excess capacity in China and muted pricing (Hikal, Jubilant Ingrevia, Meghmani). Europe is cited as a challenging market due to geopolitical strife and high costs (Fairchem, Hikal, Meghmani).

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Freight Costs:** This is the most cited headwind. Companies like Apcotex, Camlin, Meghmani, Platinum, Vishnu, and Yasho explicitly flagged surging ocean freight costs as a major drag on profitability.
*   **Raw Material Volatility:** Crude-linked raw material prices remain volatile (Aarti, Balaji, Clean Science, Hikal, Privi, Vinati).
*   **Pass-through Capability:**
    *   *Successful:* Acutaas, Aether, and Balaji reported success in passing on cost increases.
    *   *Struggling:* Clean Science, Meghmani, NOCIL, Vinati, and Yasho noted that competitive pressures (often from China) or contract structures prevented full pass-through, leading to margin absorption.

## Capex & capacity
The sector is in the midst of a massive expansion cycle. Almost all companies are either commissioning new facilities or have significant capex underway:
*   **Commissioning/Ramping:** Aarti (Zone IV), Aether (Magnum site), Balaji (Acetonitrile/DME), J.G. Chemicals (Dahej/Naidupeta), Neogen (Pakhajan), Privi (Phase 1 debottlenecking), and Yasho (Pakhajan).
*   **Planned/Ongoing:** Apcotex (Valia), Clean Science (Performance Chemical 2), Hikal (Panoli), Himadri (Carbon Nanotubes/LFP), Platinum (Egypt), Vinati (Rs 500cr+ plan), and Vishnu (DMSO/Chrome).

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance Upgrades:** Acutaas raised revenue growth guidance to ~30% (from 25%) and EBITDA margin guidance to 38%-40%.
*   **Guidance/Tone Shifts:**
    *   **Camlin:** Shifted to a cautious tone, noting liquidity issues and sub-scale utilization; expects turnaround only in Q4.
    *   **Hikal:** Remains cautious due to U.S. FDA remediation costs and crop protection headwinds.
    *   **Meghmani:** Suspended titanium dioxide operations due to unviable raw material costs.
    *   **Vinati:** Reported a sharp Q2 EBITDA/PAT decline due to ATBS sales shifting to H2, but maintains a positive outlook for H2 recovery.

## What to watch next quarter
*   **Freight/Logistics:** Will the surge in ocean freight costs normalize, or will it continue to compress margins for exporters?
*   **Crude Oil/Raw Materials:** Impact of Middle East tensions on feedstock pricing and pass-through success.
*   **Capacity Utilization:** Ramp-up of new plants (Aarti, Aether, J.G. Chemicals, Neogen, Privi, Yasho) and their impact on operating leverage.
*   **China Factor:** Continued monitoring of Chinese dumping and policy shifts (e.g., VAT rebate removals) affecting domestic pricing power.
