# Gujarat Fluorochemicals Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 and H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Chemical segment revenue and PAT grew significantly YoY, supported by strong demand and operating momentum.

### Key numbers
- **Chemical segment revenue:** INR 1,723 crore, up 42% YoY.
- **Chemical segment EBITDA:** INR 505 crore, up 33% YoY (up 10% QoQ).
- **Chemical segment PAT:** INR 290 crore, up 46% YoY.
- **Consolidated revenue:** INR 1,760 crore, up 45% YoY and 11% QoQ.
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** INR 473 crore, up 32% YoY and 11% QoQ.
- **Consolidated EBITDA margin:** 27%.
- **Consolidated PAT:** INR 240 crore, up 42% YoY and 10% QoQ.
- **Fluoropolymers revenue:** INR 931 crore, up 22% YoY (from INR 764 crore in Q2 FY26) and 2% QoQ.
- **Fluorochemicals revenue:** INR 607 crore, up 135% YoY (from INR 258 crore in Q2 FY26) and 33% QoQ.
- **Bulk Chemical portfolio revenue:** INR 151 crore, down 5% YoY (from INR 158 crore in Q2 FY26) and down 8% QoQ.

### Highlights
- Fluoropolymers growth driven by volume and higher-value specialty grades (FKM, PSA, modified PTFE) for semiconductors, data centers, and automotive sectors.
- Fluorochemicals growth primarily driven by refrigerants, especially R-32.
- Bulk chemical revenue impacted by lower caustic soda prices and higher captive consumption of chloromethanes for R-32.
- GFCL EV (subsidiary) progressing in battery materials: LiPF6 sales ramping up to 5 major global electrolyte players with multi-year order visibility; LFP plant samples undergoing global customer qualifications; PVDF and PTFE grades at advanced qualification stages for wet and dry electrode applications.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guided for annual fluoropolymers growth of 17% to 20%; delivered 22% YoY in Q2 and remains confident in the trajectory.
- Battery materials business total investment plan targeted at INR 6,000 crores (~INR 3,600 crores remaining); current year battery capex of INR 2,100 crores pushed out by 1.5 quarters due to shifting the Oman project to India.
- Reaffirmed battery materials guidance of 2x asset turnover and 25% EBITDA margin.
- R-134A production expected to commence by Q4 FY27.
- Commercial supplies of battery PVDF/PTFE under long-term agreements expected to commence from Q4 FY27.
- Domestic electrolyte demand expected to pick up towards the end of next financial year; revenue contribution expected in H2 FY28.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Healthy demand across fluoropolymers and fluorochemicals; R-32 demand supported by transition to lower global warming potential refrigerants.
- Dry electrode application using PTFE is seeing strong R&D traction globally, representing a huge upside for PTFE.
- Global battery materials industry represents a strong long-term opportunity driven by EV and energy storage.
- Domestic battery manufacturing in India is currently behind schedule; initial plant stabilization uses tech-supplier electrolytes before localization ramps up.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- LiPF6 pricing is dropping due to lithium price declines; formulas are mostly pass-through, keeping margins protected.
- Caustic soda prices were lower during the quarter.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Non-battery standalone capex of INR 800 crores (including fluoropolymers) progressing as planned.
- Fluoropolymer capacity additions are downstream via polymerization reactors (short cycle of 8-10 months), leveraging existing monomer capacity.
- LFP capacity expansion underway in India (shifting from Oman); significant capacity expected by Q3 of next financial year.
- Anode project is currently on slow/hold status, prioritized behind LFP, electrolyte, and salt.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Battery materials funding to date: ~INR 2,300 crores of equity raised (INR 800 crores from GFL, INR 1,000 crores from prior round, INR 500 crores from IFC) plus INR 900 crores of debt from Sumitomo (INR 600 crores) and ICICI Bank (INR 300 crores).
- Plan to raise another INR 1,200 crores in equity over the next 18 to 24 months via climate/sovereign funds, targeting INR 3,500 crores total equity, INR 1,500 crores debt, and INR 1,000 crores internal accruals for the INR 6,000 crores total outlay.
- No put option or backstop provided by parent or promoters for battery subsidiary funding.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global uncertainties: European conflict, Gulf developments, trade/tariff policies, financial market volatility, climate uncertainties.
- Pace of battery materials ramp-up is linked to customer qualification timelines, commercial approvals, and customer plant delays in India.
- Long approval cycles and gestation periods act as critical entry barriers in battery materials.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sanjesh Jain (ICICI Securities) on LiPF6 pricing and China price drop:** Management clarified that LiPF6 price drops are driven by lithium price falls, and since contracts are pass-through, margins remain protected.
2. **Ankur Periwal (Axis Capital) on capex spillover:** Management confirmed the INR 2,100 crore battery capex is pushed out by ~1.5 quarters due to shifting the Oman project to India, but the overall INR 6,000 crore plan remains intact.
3. **Archit Joshi (Nuvama) on revenue recognition sequence for battery materials:** Management outlined the sequence as Salt, LFP, Electrolyte, and lastly Anode.
4. **Rohit Nagraj (360 ONE Capital) on funding sources for the INR 6,000 cr battery plan:** Akhil Jindal provided a detailed breakdown of equity (INR 2,300 cr raised, INR 1,200 cr more needed), debt (INR 900 cr raised, INR 600 cr more needed), and internal accruals, reiterating no parent backstops.
5. **Moksh Jain (Niveshaay) on backward integration into iron phosphate:** Management stated that backward integration for FP will be timed to match the scaling up of LFP sales volumes.
6. **Hansal Thacker (Lalkar Securities) on LiPF6 contracts:** Management stated that volumes are bound via annual or longer contracts with formula-based pricing linked to lithium, with very little spot exposure.

### One-line takeaway
GFL delivered robust Q2 FY27 performance led by strong fluorochemicals and fluoropolymers growth, while its advanced battery materials platform progresses through customer qualifications toward commercialization.
