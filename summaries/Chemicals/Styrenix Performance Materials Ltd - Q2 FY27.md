# Styrenix Performance Materials Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While domestic ABS demand remains robust and capacity expansion is on track, the Thailand business continues to face structural challenges and slow recovery, and GPPS volumes remain impacted by management's decision to avoid speculative procurement.

### Key numbers
*   **Standalone Q2 FY27:**
    *   Total Income: Rs 781 crore (up 26.5% YoY)
    *   EBITDA: Rs 84.5 crore (up 3.3% YoY)
    *   EBITDA Margin: 10.8%
    *   PAT: Rs 51.2 crore (up 0.8% YoY)
    *   Sales Volume: 43.4 KT
*   **Consolidated Q2 FY27:**
    *   Total Income: Rs 997 crore
    *   EBITDA: Rs 75.6 crore
    *   EBITDA Margin: 7.6%
    *   PAT: Rs 23 crore
    *   Sales Volume: 55.1 KT (India + Thailand)
*   **H1 FY27 (Standalone):**
    *   Total Income: Rs 1,551 crore (up 15.8% YoY)
    *   EBITDA: Rs 286 crore (up 70% YoY)
    *   EBITDA Margin: 18.4%
    *   PAT: Rs 188.5 crore (up 78.4% YoY)
    *   Sales Volume: 82.4 KT

### Highlights
*   **Capacity Expansion:** Phase 1 of the expansion plan is on track to come on stream within the current financial year (FY27).
*   **Product Strategy:** Management is focusing on high-value grades, specifically refrigeration liner ABS, where they aim to capture 30,000–40,000 tonnes of the import market.
*   **New Launches:** Commercialized ASALAC (ASA product) and STYROLOY (blends of PC-ABS, Nylon-ABS, etc.) with approvals received.
*   **Polystyrene (PS):** HIPS (High Impact Polystyrene) business is performing well with 70%–80% of capacity driven by OEM demand. GPPS (General Purpose Polystyrene) volumes were lower due to a conscious management decision to avoid speculative raw material buying amid volatility.

### Guidance & outlook
*   **Management Outlook:** Expects ABS volumes to remain buoyant in H2, in line with Q2 levels.
*   **Thailand:** Management expects performance to remain consistent with last year for the next 1.5 to 2 years while restructuring projects are executed.
*   **Capex:** Phase 2 of the ABS expansion is proceeding as planned; work will commence once Phase 1 is online.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Imports:** Management noted that while dumping from China has occurred, it has not significantly impacted their ability to sell.
*   **Market Dynamics:** The Indian market for refrigeration liner ABS is 3.5–4 lakh tonnes, with imports accounting for roughly 30,000–40,000 tonnes.
*   **Global Trends:** Regional supply-demand mismatches persist due to energy/naphtha supply issues affecting refineries globally. Ocean freight volatility continues to impact raw material and finished goods pricing.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Raw Materials:** Styrene Monomer prices have been volatile, recently hovering around $1,600–$1,650.
*   **Margins:** Management believes normalized gross margins on an annualized basis will remain in line with historical levels.
*   **Thailand:** Currently facing higher cost structures; management is executing projects to improve rubber efficiency and production costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Expansion Status:** Phase 1 expansion (Nandesari, Katol, and Moxi plants) is expected to go live within FY27.
*   **Utilization:** Management is confident that new capacities will reach high utilization within a short period after commissioning.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Speculative Risk:** Management explicitly avoids speculative procurement of raw materials (Styrene Monomer) when finished product demand is uncertain or unorganized.
*   **Thailand Turnaround:** The turnaround of the Thailand asset is taking longer than anticipated due to complex restructuring requirements and revalidation processes with customers.
*   **Geopolitical Volatility:** Ongoing fluctuations in crude oil prices and supply chain disruptions remain key risks.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Can you quantify the volume guidance for FY27/28?**
    *   *A:* Management declined to provide specific forward-looking volume guidance but noted they are currently running at full capacity in India and expect to utilize new capacities quickly.
*   **Q: Why is the Thailand ramp-up taking so long?**
    *   *A:* Management explained that it involves a multi-pronged approach (cost reduction, product mix rebalancing, and customer revalidations) which takes time; they estimate 1.5–2 years for structural improvement.
*   **Q: Are you seeing a reduction in import competitive intensity due to high freight costs?**
    *   *A:* Management noted no major shift in competitive intensity; while Korean freight is higher, it is balanced by lower utilization in Taiwan/Thailand/China.
*   **Q: Will the new ABS plant allow for higher margins?**
    *   *A:* Management advised assuming a similar product mix and margin profile to historical levels, noting that while new products add value, competitive pressures and operating leverage are offsetting factors.

### One-line takeaway
Styrenix is prioritizing operational stability and high-value product substitution (refrigeration liner ABS) over speculative volume growth, while managing a multi-year, gradual turnaround of its Thailand subsidiary.
