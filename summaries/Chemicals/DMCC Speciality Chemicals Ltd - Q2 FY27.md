# DMCC Speciality Chemicals Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27 (call dated November 18, 2026)  
**Tone:** Cautious – Severely impacted by sulfur price shocks and downstream demand destruction, though speciality operations remain resilient.

---

### Key numbers
* **Revenue (Q2):** ~Rs 273 crore  
* **EBITDA (Q2):** Rs 11.6 crore  
* **EBITDA margin (Q2):** ~4.2% (down from 13.5% in Q1; H1 EBITDA margin stood at ~8%–8.5% on ~Rs 525 crore revenue)  
* **Exports share of revenue:** ~12% in Q2  
* **Sulfuric acid capacity utilization:** Operated at ~65% in Q2 (down from 95%–100% historically)  

---

### Highlights
* Topline expanded substantially due to elevated sulfur pass-through pricing, but profitability was crushed as high sulfuric acid prices caused end-user demand destruction.  
* Sulfuric acid business turned loss-making (negative) for the quarter.  
* Company rationalized output to ~65% to avoid selling below cost while sustaining downstream utility and specialty production requirements.  
* Progress made in geographic diversification into Latin America and Japan, offsetting ongoing structural weakness in Europe.  

---

### Guidance & outlook
* Management expects demand revival as sulfur and sulfuric acid prices continue to soften from peak levels.  
* Commercialized an intermediate for a polymer used in enhanced oil recovery; expected to yield Rs 25–50 crore annualized revenue across multiple customers.  
* Speciality/commodity revenue mix would normalize near 50:50 once sulfur prices revert toward pre-crisis baseline (~$500/ton).  

---

### Demand & industry commentary
* Global and domestic demand destruction occurred when equivalent sulfuric acid prices surpassed ~Rs 30,000/ton.  
* Fertilizers cracked first (e.g., GSFC halted fertilizer manufacturing and offloaded sulfuric acid into the merchant market), followed by cuts in detergents and general downstream chemicals.  
* Post-October, sulfur availability is easing and prices are tapering down.  
* Weakness in Europe’s chemical sector is viewed by management as structural rather than cyclical due to energy costs and unintegrated supply chains.  

---

### Costs, margins & raw materials
* Sulfur moved from ~$200/ton historically to $500–$600/ton (driven by Indonesian smelter consumption of ~3 million tons) and surged up to $1,100–$1,200/ton following the Iran/Hormuz crisis before cooling down to ~$800/ton.  
* Q1’s 13.5% EBITDA margin benefited from inventory gains, which reversed in Q2 as gross margins compressed.  
* Completely shutting down the sulfuric acid plant would raise downstream specialty chemical costs by ~10% due to loss of captive steam and power generation.  
* Raw material inventory is maintained at 15–20 days of stock across Roha and Dahej plants.  

---

### Capex, capacity & expansion
* Specialty downstream capacity currently has 25%–30% headroom before requiring major capital outlay; debottlenecking can be done with low capex.  
* Ongoing R&D spending is ~Rs 2 crore per annum, entirely directed toward new product development (e.g., boron-based fine chemicals for catalytic and agrochemical applications).  

---

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
* Working capital and short-term debt rose sharply to fund higher-cost sulfur and elevated receivables from inflated nominal revenues.  
* Debtor collection cycles remain within 45 days.  
* Short-term working capital borrowing interest rates are 7.5%–8.5%.  
* Term debt is being amortized as scheduled; includes ~Rs 10 crore under the government ECLGS facility at ~8.5% interest.  
* Monetization of the land parcel near Ambernath remains delayed due to pending Maharashtra government survey compliance following a Supreme Court order.  

---

### Notable analyst Q&A
* **Sulfur spread compression vs. plant slowdown impact:** Management declined to give a specific per-product gross margin breakdown, confirming that running below variable cost made overhead absorption secondary, leading to a deliberate cut to ~65% utilization.  
* **Speciality revenue contribution reality:** Management noted that although the reported revenue share was compressed to ~27% due to the inflated commodity denominator, normalizing sulfur to ~$500/ton places the underlying specialty business close to 50% of revenue.  
* **Sulfuric acid financial performance:** When pressed on the exact P&L cost impact of lost captive steam/power at reduced utilization, management dodged the specific rupee amount but admitted the sulfuric acid segment was negative for the quarter.  
* **Ambernath land monetization timeline:** Management could not commit to a timeline, stating delays stem from state government survey delays rather than company inaction.  

---

### One-line takeaway
A temporary collapse in commodity spreads and sulfuric acid demand destruction crushed Q2 margins, masking steady progress and volume expansion in downstream specialty chemicals.
