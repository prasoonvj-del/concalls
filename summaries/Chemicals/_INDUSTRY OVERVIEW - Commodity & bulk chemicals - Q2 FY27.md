# Chemicals - Commodity & bulk chemicals: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for India's commodity and bulk chemical sector was characterized by a fundamental divergence between resilient domestic volume demand and a challenging external macro environment. While companies with strong domestic footprints, value-added downstream portfolios, or high renewable energy adoption (such as **Gujarat Alkalies**, **Lords Chloro Alkali**, and **Regaal Resources**) delivered healthy top-line and operating profit growth, others faced severe margin compression. Managements universally highlighted persistent global oversupply (predominantly driven by China), aggressive import dumping, and a sharp escalation in energy (coal, furnace oil, gas) and global freight costs. Several players also encountered localized or operational headwinds, such as mining suspensions in Kenya (**Tata Chemicals**), temporary facility shutdowns or plant floods (**DCW**, **GNFC**, **Archean**, **Regaal Resources**), and sluggish realizations in core chlor-alkali and soda ash segments.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Domestic Resilience vs. Global Oversupply:** Managements across the board (including **GHCL**, **Tata Chemicals**, **Epigral**, and **DCW**) agreed that domestic demand remains fundamentally healthy, underpinned by infrastructure, construction, plumbing (CPVC), and surging solar glass manufacturing. However, this is heavily offset by a global oversupply in commodity chemicals like soda ash and caustic soda, with high inventory levels in China depressing international export realizations.
- **Segment Divergence:** Agricultural and agrochemical demand was described as tepid or uneven due to erratic monsoons by **Archean Chemical** and **Tata Chemicals**, whereas packaging, polymers, rubber, and construction chemical segments witnessed solid double-digit growth (**20 Microns**, **DCW**).
- **Export & Realization Views:** While **Gujarat Alkalies** noted that European markets are becoming import-dependent, creating export opportunities, companies heavily exposed to global trade (like **Tata Chemicals** and **GHCL**) cited soft global pricing and intense competitive intensity. **Regaal Resources** reported exceptionally strong pricing power and demand for maize starch, driven by high global tapioca starch prices.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Energy & Freight Inflation:** A dominant theme for the quarter was margin erosion driven by surging energy and logistics expenses. **20 Microns** quantified a direct EBITDA margin hit of ~200 bps from surging furnace oil (~33%), gas (~77%), and freight rates (~11%). **Epigral**, **Archean Chemical**, **GHCL**, and **GNFC** similarly cited soaring coal, sulfur, and gas prices, as well as West Asia geopolitical tensions inflating ocean freight and lead times.
- **Chlorine Realization Relief:** A notable silver lining in the chlor-alkali space was the slight recovery in chlorine realizations. **Epigral**, **GACL**, and **Lords Chloro Alkali** noted that domestic demand growth and new downstream integration have helped pull chlorine prices out of the deeply negative territory seen in previous quarters.
- **Mitigation via Green Energy:** To combat structural power cost inflation, multiple companies highlighted aggressive transitions to renewable energy. **GACL** reached 55% RE consumption, **Lords Chloro Alkali** achieved 40%, **Epigral** is investing in solar/wind hybrid SPVs, and **20 Microns** is commissioning a 9 MW captive solar plant to meet ~40% of its power needs.

## Capex & capacity
- **Ongoing Expansion Cycles:** Capital expenditure remains robust as companies prepare for structural growth. **Epigral** is nearing completion of its CPVC and ECH capacity expansions. **Archean Chemical** is progressing on its bromine debottlenecking, brine field expansion, and the SiCSem semiconductor project (INR 463 crore invested YTD). **DCW** initiated a Rs 250 crore project for SIOP expansion and power optimization. **GHCL** commenced commercial production of vacuum salt and bromine, and progressed on its major greenfield soda ash project phases. **Lords Chloro Alkali** is executing a Rs 165 crore capex plan to expand caustic soda capacity by July 2027. **Regaal Resources** has largely completed its major crushing capacity expansion (doubling to 1,650 TPD) and is pivoting toward smaller efficiency and value-added product line capex.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Guidance & Forecast Adjustments:** **Archean Chemical** revised its SOP volume guidance down to 3,500–4,000 tonnes due to feedstock limitations and trial delays. **GNFC** and **Tata Chemicals** adopted a cautious stance, with GNFC explicitly refraining from short-term guidance due to extreme geopolitical and energy volatility. Conversely, **Regaal Resources** raised its FY28 volume visibility and maintained an optimistic tone on value-added product traction.
- **Operational Disruptions:** Several companies reported unexpected operational hurdles compared to previous quarters—notably **Regaal Resources** dealing with a temporary 10-MW turbine breakdown (now resolved), **DCW** managing facility stoppages from Dhrangadhra floods, and **Tata Chemicals** facing formal suspension of Kenyan mining operations.

## What to watch next quarter
- **H2 Recovery Realization:** Multiple managements (**20 Microns**, **DCW**, **Epigral**, **GHCL**) guided that H2 FY27 will be significantly stronger than H1; actual delivery against this expectation will be closely tracked.
- **Progress on Renewable & Captive Sourcing:** Execution and initial financial benefits from upcoming captive solar projects (**20 Microns**, **Epigral**) and railway sidings (**GHCL**).
- **Regulatory Outcomes:** Progress on proposed government trade interventions, such as the DGTR-recommended countervailing/import restrictions on PVC (**DCW**) and soda ash imports (**Tata Chemicals**).
- **Greenfield & Semiconductor Execution:** Capital deployment and trial progress on long-term strategic diversification projects, such as **Archean's** SiCSem semiconductor project and **GHCL's** greenfield soda ash plant.

## Company one-liners
- **20 Microns Ltd:** Revenue and gross margins held steady, but energy and freight inflation weighed on profitability, prompting investments in captive solar and mining.
- **Archean Chemical Industries Ltd:** Delivered steady revenue and EBITDA growth despite severe cost inflation and logistics hurdles, advancing on bromine debottlenecking and semiconductors.
- **DCW Ltd:** Strong revenue growth and a Basic Chemicals turnaround were tempered by specialty chemical input cost volatility and high freight rates.
- **Epigral Ltd:** Reported 28% YoY revenue growth, but EBITDA margins were constrained by volatile raw material costs and energy inflation as key derivatives expansions neared completion.
- **GHCL Ltd:** Navigated a soft global soda ash pricing environment through strict cost discipline while successfully commercializing vacuum salt and bromine projects.
- **Gujarat Alkalies and Chemicals Ltd:** Delivered strong H1 growth driven by record sales, improved realizations, and expanded renewable energy usage.
- **Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd:** Experienced margin compression due to lower chemical realizations, coal cost spikes, and wage provisions amid volatile geopolitical conditions.
- **Lords Chloro Alkali Ltd:** Leveraged renewable energy cost advantages to expand volumes and maintain profitability while planning a strategic pivot toward specialty chemicals.
- **Regaal Resources Ltd:** Achieved record quarterly PAT and margin expansion underpinned by high capacity utilization and strategic low-cost maize inventory.
- **Tata Chemicals Ltd:** Resilient domestic demand and volume growth were neutralized by global soda ash oversupply, soaring UK energy costs, and the suspension of Kenyan mining.
