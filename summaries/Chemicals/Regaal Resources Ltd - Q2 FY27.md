# Regaal Resources Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Achieved record operating income, highest-ever quarterly PAT, and robust margin expansion driven by scale and favorable pricing, despite a temporary turbine disruption.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained annual maize crushing target at over 400,000 tonnes (and estimated FY28 volumes at 525,000 to 550,000 tonnes); interest cost guidance revised slightly from Rs. 39-40 crores to around Rs. 38-42 crores.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted more optimistic regarding realizations and value-added traction, noting strong customer acceptance of higher prices.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Temporary operational disruption from damage to a 10-megawatt turbine in the new 825 TPD facility (now recommissioned).
- Last quarter's promises: Capacity utilization is ramping up, and inventory is being progressively consumed to support H2 volumes as promised.

### Key numbers
- **Operating Income:** Rs. 316.6 crore (Core manufacturing revenue: Rs. 312.7 crore, up 64.40% YoY from Rs. 190.2 crore in Q2 FY26)
- **Value-Added Revenue:** Rs. 129.0 crore (up 64.3% YoY) with a value-add margin of 40.8%
- **Operating EBITDA:** Rs. 54.2 crore (up 54.7% YoY)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 17.1%
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 23.2 crore (up 38.7% YoY)
- **PAT Margin:** 7.3%
- **Maize Crushing Volume:** 90,489 metric tonnes (up 32% YoY)
- **Capacity Utilisation:** Running between 1,500 to 1,575 TPD against 1,650 TPD capacity
- **Net Debt:** Rs. 880.8 crore (comprising Rs. 535.7 crore term loans and Rs. 350.6 crore working capital)
- **ROCE:** 10.2%
- **ROE:** 14.2% (improved from 10.8% in Q1 FY27)
- **Cash Conversion Cycle:** 123 days (improved from 130 days in Q1 FY27)

### Highlights
- Recorded highest-ever quarterly PAT of Rs. 23.2 crore and record PAT margin of 7.3%.
- Newly commissioned 825 TPD facility experienced a temporary disruption due to a damaged 10-MW turbine, which has now been successfully recommissioned and is fully operational.
- Trading sales were negligible (close to zero / Rs. 8.5 crore opportunistic sales in October), reflecting a pure manufacturing-led model compared to Rs. 12.7 crore in Q2 FY26.
- Recovered 60% of pending GST issues from distributors, with the rest expected by December.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 crushing volumes to be around 240,000 tonnes, taking total annual FY27 crushing to easily exceed 400,000 tonnes.
- FY28 volume projected around 525,000 to 550,000 tonnes.
- Value-added products expected to contribute 10%-11% for FY27, moving upward of 22%-23% (excluding liquid glucose) by FY28.
- Interest cost for the year targeted around Rs. 38 to 42 crores.
- Sustainable steady-state EBITDA per tonne baseline viewed around Rs. 4,500 (plus/minus 5%), though current realizations are higher (Q2 EBITDA per tonne was Rs. 6,000, and H1 was Rs. 5,300).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Tapioca starch prices are high globally around USD 700, supporting large demand and pricing power for Indian maize starch.
- Exports (fiber and starch) account for about 10% of total produce.
- Demand remains extremely strong; management stated order intake exceeded targets by 150%, prompting further price hikes.
- Maize availability in Bihar, Bengal, and Assam remains secure due to direct farmer loyalty networks and strategic regional positioning, despite national supply constraints and ethanol diversion.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw maize prices surged 50% plus from Q1 levels, but finished product prices (starch up to Rs. 49-51/kg, germ at Rs. 83, fiber at Rs. 23) rose proportionally, expanding delta/value-addition margins from ~10% to ~20%.
- Inventory of maize is stocked at ~90% for the entire H2 (sufficient until mid-March 2027), allowing the company to leverage lower-cost inventory against rising market prices.
- Working capital and associated borrowings are at peak levels due to advance Rabi maize stocking and are expected to moderate by March as inventory is consumed.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Major expansion capex is largely behind; future focus is on plant stabilization, efficiency, and small debottlenecking.
- Upcoming small capex projects: 1.6 MW methane gas-based power plant (utilizing effluent treatment plant gas), liquid glucose drum manufacturing unit, and Dextrose Monohydrate (DMH) / Dextrose Anhydrous (DAH) lines slated for early FY28/28-29.
- Total total capital outlay stands at Rs. 665 crore, with approximately Rs. 275 crore remaining to be capitalized.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt stood at Rs. 880.8 crore (Term loans: Rs. 535.7 crore, Working capital debt: Rs. 350.6 crore).
- Working capital debt is expected to drop by 50% or more by March 31 as seasonal inventory is utilized.
- Cash conversion cycle improved to 123 days.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Commodity nature of the business causing raw material and finished goods price fluctuations.
- Temporary operational downtime from turbine damage (now resolved).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Diwakar Rana (Prudent Equity) asked about EBITDA per ton drivers and debt reduction:** Management explained that increased capacity utilization, lower fixed costs per unit, and higher export/domestic sales realization drove EBITDA up to Rs. 6,000/tonne, and working capital debt will halve by March as peak inventory is consumed.
2. **Rohit Sinha asked about inventory levels and value-added mix:** Management stated they hold ~90% of H2 maize requirements (until mid-March), and value-added ramp-up will be fully visible in Q3 and Q4.
3. **Gulit Singh asked about sustainable margins and future capex:** Management guided a baseline steady-state EBITDA of ~Rs. 4,500/tonne (though Q2 was Rs. 6,000) and noted only minor efficiency capex remaining.
4. **Pratik Kothari asked about maize diversion for ethanol and availability risks:** Management reassured that being located in Bihar (a net exporter of maize with direct farmer procurement) insulates them from regional shortages.
5. **Naman Maheshwari asked if debt has peaked:** Management clarified that term loans remain stable while working capital debt will decrease significantly by March 31.
6. **Rajesh Jain asked about total capacity and value-added breakdown:** Management clarified that 1,650 TPD crushing yields ~1,500 TPD finished products (~1,100 TPD starch), of which ~20% is targeted for pure value-added products.

### One-line takeaway
Regaal Resources delivered record Q2 financial performance with a 64% jump in manufacturing revenue and highest-ever PAT, underpinned by robust capacity utilization and strategic low-cost maize inventory despite a temporary turbine disruption.
