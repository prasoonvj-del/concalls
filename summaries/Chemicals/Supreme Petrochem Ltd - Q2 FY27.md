# Supreme Petrochem Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-02 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27  
**Tone:** Cautious - Strong Q2 results but management flags geopolitical uncertainty, raw material volatility, and export challenges.

### Key numbers
- Operating income: Rs 1,957 crore (vs Rs 1,100 crore YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 160 crore, margin 8.12%
- PAT: Rs 95 crore (almost doubled YoY)
- H1 FY27 operating income: Rs 3,649 crore; EBITDA: Rs 508 crore; PAT: Rs 331 crore (margin 9.08%)
- Sales volume Q2: 95,242 MT (+24% YoY, +35% QoQ); H1: 1,66,085 MT (-3% YoY)
- Debt-free with investable surplus Rs 886 crore (Sep 2026)
- Interim dividend: 125% (Rs 2.5/share)
- Trading sales: ~22% of Q2 revenue
- Styrene monomer landed price: ~$1,650 (up from $900-950)
- Spreads: GPPS/HIPS $200-250/ton after freight; GPPS/HIPS $300-350/ton
- ABS utilization: 85% of truncated capacity (65% of rated)
- Polystyrene India demand FY27-28: ~375,000 MT; market share ~60%
- EPS India demand current year: 165,000-170,000 MT
- Specialty mix: ~40% of overall
- Xmold: numbers nearly doubled YoY and QoQ

### Highlights
- Volume rebound driven by domestic demand recovery (festival season) and non-OEM segment returning after Q1 weakness
- Raw material price increases passed through with lag; no inventory loss booked (moving average valuation)
- Diversifying sourcing away from Gulf region; domestic supply meeting full requirement
- Expansions on track: XPS board line (150,000 m³), compounding (50k→80k MT), ABS Phase 1 (70k MT) and Phase 2 (140k MT total)
- Capex fully funded from internal accruals
- Entered auto components for ABS compounds, targeting EV two-wheelers

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects volume growth better than FY26 if US midterm elections settle geopolitical tensions and raw material availability eases from December onward
- Exports to restart once freight rates and raw material prices stabilize
- Compounding capacity 80k MT by Q3 FY28; full utilization by FY29-30
- ABS Phase 1 (70k MT) by Q3 FY29; Phase 2 (140k MT) by FY29-30
- XPS board commercial production Q1 FY28; full absorption in 24-30 months
- No inorganic growth plans; ABS most capital-intensive

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic demand good in October; OEM and non-OEM both strong (50-50 split)
- Duty-free imports (May-Jul) spiked PS/ABS imports; post duty withdrawal (Jul), imports fell to normal/lower levels
- EPS demand strong in India; no new applications recently
- European PS plants: no closures heard, some restructuring/reduced rates
- Competition capacity additions not yet operational

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Styrene monomer price doubled YoY ($900-950 → $1,650)
- Freight volatility: containerized vs liquid bulk differential shrinking spreads; ocean freight up ~$2,000/container in weeks
- Pentane prices up but available locally; styrene monomer 92% of EPS raw material cost
- Raw material cost increases passed through with time lag
- Inventory up ~50% mainly due to raw material price surge (includes in-transit/high-seas material)

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- XPS insulation board: 150,000 m³, commercial Q1 FY28
- Compounding: 50k→80k MT by Q3 FY28
- ABS Phase 1: 70k MT by Q3 FY29
- ABS Phase 2: 140k MT total by FY29-30
- All capex internally funded

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Zero debt; investable surplus Rs 886 crore (Sep 2026)
- Interim dividend 125% declared
- Inventory increase driven by raw material value (CIF basis includes in-transit)

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical instability (Gulf war, US elections) affecting raw material supply and freight
- Volatile styrene prices and ocean freight rates
- Export execution risk due to freight/raw material price gap between order and shipment
- Lag in passing raw material cost increases
- Potential future competition from new domestic capacities

5. **Ansh Khimavat (Capital One):** Non-OEM demand recovery, OEM/non-OEM split.

### One-line takeaway
Strong Q2 earnings on volume recovery and price pass-through, but near-term outlook clouded by geopolitical raw material volatility and freight costs; capacity expansions on track with internal funding.
