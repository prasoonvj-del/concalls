# Ester Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong improvement in BOPET film segment profitability and volume offset by demand erosion and revenue decline in the high-margin specialty polymers business.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Total Income:** Rs 449 crore (up 25.7% YoY) / H1 FY27: Rs 891 crore (up 26.5% YoY)
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 45.1 crore (up 160% YoY, margin 10%, up ~510 bps YoY) / H1 FY27: Rs 104 crore (margin 11.7%)
- **Consolidated PAT:** Rs 7.8 crore (compared to loss of Rs 15.8 crore in Q2 FY26) / H1 FY27: Rs 26.5 crore
- **Standalone Total Income:** Rs 365.8 crore (up 38.9% YoY) / H1 FY27: Rs 713.5 crore (up 30.1% YoY)
- **Standalone EBITDA:** Rs 28.6 crore (margin 7.8%, up ~250 bps YoY) / H1 FY27: Rs 68.6 crore (margin 9.6%)
- **Standalone PAT:** Rs 6.1 crore (compared to loss of Rs 4.8 crore in Q2 FY26) / H1 FY27: Rs 20.6 crore
- **Consolidated Film Sales Volume:** 24,291 metric tonnes (up 13.9% YoY) / H1 FY27: 46,412 metric tonnes (up 8.3% YoY)
- **Film Segment Revenue:** Rs 414.5 crore (up 39.6% YoY) / H1 FY27: Rs 813.9 crore (up 38.7% YoY)
- **Film Segment EBIT:** Rs 34.5 crore (margin 8.3%, compared to EBIT loss of Rs 6.2 crore in Q2 FY26) / H1 FY27: Rs 73.6 crore (margin 9%)
- **Value-Added and Specialty (VAS) Film Volume:** 6,200 metric tonnes (26% of total film volumes, up 25.8% YoY) / H1 FY27: 12,568 metric tonnes (27% of total)
- **rPET Sales Volume:** 1,437 metric tonnes (up 37.4% YoY) / Revenue: Rs 18.4 crore (up 51% YoY) / H1 FY27: 2,831 metric tonnes, Revenue: Rs 35.8 crore
- **Ester Filmtech (Subsidiary) Total Income:** Rs 168.2 crore (up 40.5% YoY) / EBITDA: Rs 16.9 crore (margin 10%) / PAT: Rs 2.2 crore / H1 FY27 Income: Rs 327.8 crore, EBITDA: Rs 36.4 crore (margin 11.1%), PAT: Rs 6.9 crore. Capacity utilization: 85.3% for H1 FY27.
- **Specialty Polymers Revenue:** Rs 29.3 crore (down 49% YoY) / Segment EBIT: Rs 9.9 crore (margin 33.9%) / Sales Volume: 711 metric tonnes / H1 FY27 Revenue: Rs 62 crore (down 41% YoY), EBIT: Rs 24.7 crore (margin 39.9%)
- **Debt & Liquidity:** Combined liquidity (cash & bank + liquid investments) stood at Rs 228.3 crore (Cash & bank: Rs 180.4 crore, Liquid investments: Rs 47.9 crore). Gross consolidated interest-bearing debt: Rs 748.8 crore as of September 2026 (Term debt: ~Rs 480 crore, Working capital debt: ~Rs 270 crore). Consolidated term loans repaid in H1 FY27: Rs 53.5 crore.

### Highlights
- BOPET film operating environment remained stable with demand growth in India at 8-10% YoY, supported by plastic waste management rules mandating 10% post-consumer recycled content.
- Exports contributed ~37% of revenue, with a revival in U.S. exports following the resolution of tariff disruptions.
- Ester Filmtech reported profitability in every quarter since Q4 FY26.
- Elite joint venture (50-50 JV with Loop Industries) completed the FEED study, appointed Toyo Engineering India for detailed engineering, and targets land acquisition by Q4 FY27 and commercial operations in Q4 CY2028. Nike signed as an anchor customer with a multiyear offtake agreement, and Loop secured an LoI from another global brand for up to 15,000 MT/annum.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management targets increasing the share of value-added and specialty films to ~35% by Q4 FY27 and to 50-60% over the next 2-3 years, aiming for a sustainable 15% EBITDA margin profile.
- Medium-term ambition for Specialty Polymers remains a ~25% CAGR in revenue over the next 3-5 years (using FY27 as the base year).
- Management expects H2 FY27 film results to be similar to or slightly better than H1 FY27, with a slight recovery in specialty polymers in H2.
- Net debt to EBITDA target is 2 to 2.25.

### Demand & industry commentary
- BOPET capacity additions globally and in India are trailing demand growth (growing at 8-10%), with only 2-3 new additions expected in the next two years, preventing overcapacity for the next 8-10 quarters.
- DGTR has recommended anti-dumping duty (ADD) on imports from China and a couple of other countries; notification is expected by the end of December.
- Specialty polymers experienced severe demand erosion in a marquee high-margin product due to a technology and process change at the customer's end.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Key raw materials (PTA and MEG) witnessed significant price increases due to crude oil supply disruptions in West Asia, which management successfully passed on through improved realizations in the film business.
- Longer transit times and higher freight/feedstock costs increased working capital requirements, impacting receivables and inventory levels.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Incurred capex of Rs 15.1 crore during H1 FY27. Apart from routine maintenance capex and marginal capex for new specialty polymer products, no major capital expenditures are planned.
- Elite project capital outlay is budgeted at Rs 1,200 crore to Rs 1,400 crore for a 45,000 MTPA capacity; additional debt will remain at the JV level and will not be consolidated with Ester Industries.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net working capital increased by Rs 66.7 crore in H1 FY27, partly funded through short-term bank borrowings of Rs 51.7 crore.
- Trade receivables rose to Rs 264.3 crore (from Rs 184.9 crore in March 2026) and inventories rose to Rs 245.2 crore (from Rs 208.7 crore).
- Total equity stood at Rs 887.6 crore as of September 30, 2026.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in crude oil, petrochemical prices, and freight costs impacting working capital and increasing receivables/inventory.
- Demand erosion in a key marquee high-margin specialty polymer product due to customer-side process/technology shifts.
- Potential land acquisition delays in Gujarat for the Elite JV project (now expected to complete by January/February).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Vishal Bhalada (Valerium Advisors) asked about the sustainability of BOPET margins and cyclicity.** Management responded that capacity additions trail demand for the next 8-10 quarters, and increasing the specialty film mix to 50-60% will insulate the business from cyclicity.
2. **Ravi Nandha (Individual Investor) questioned the financial viability and equity contribution capability of partner Loop Industries for the Elite JV.** Management stated that Elite requires equity only (not debt from Loop), Loop has private equity commitments subject to milestones, and Elite has received in-principle term loan approval from a public sector bank.
3. **Amit Kumar (Determined Investments) inquired about the sudden loss of business in specialty polymers.** Management clarified that the largest customer underwent a technology/process change, switching to an alternate chemistry.
4. **Deepak (Cap Grow Capital) asked about sequential margin drops and raw material spreads.** Management noted that the standalone margin drop was driven by Khatima's specialty polymer headwinds, while film spreads hover around 27-28.
5. **Saket Kapoor (Kapoor and Company) raised concerns over elevated debt levels and cash flow generation.** Management explained that while long-term debt is being repaid per schedule, working capital borrowings rose due to a 50% increase in raw material/finished good prices, and currency depreciation masked rupee-term debt reductions.

### One-line takeaway
Ester Industries delivered a profitable H1 FY27 driven by strong volume growth and realizations in its BOPET film business, though its high-margin specialty polymers segment faced significant headwinds from customer-side demand erosion.
