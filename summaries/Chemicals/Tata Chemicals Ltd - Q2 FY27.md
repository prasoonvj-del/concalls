# Tata Chemicals Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed / Cautious - Resilient standalone performance and healthy domestic demand were offset by global soda ash oversupply, high UK energy costs, and suspended operations in Kenya.

### Key numbers
- **Standalone Revenue:** Rs 1,303 crore (+8% YoY)
- **Standalone EBITDA:** Rs 292 crore (+22% YoY)
- **Standalone PAT:** Rs 122 crore (-31% YoY)
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 4,177 crore (+8% YoY)
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 381 crore (compared to Rs 537 crore YoY)
- **Consolidated Loss After Tax:** Rs 50 crore (compared to profit YoY)
- **H1 Standalone Revenue:** Rs 2,584 crore (+9% YoY); EBITDA: Rs 656 crore (+29% YoY); PAT: Rs 465 crore (-4% YoY)
- **H1 Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 8,432 crore (+11% YoY); EBITDA: Rs 936 crore (vs Rs 1,186 crore YoY); PAT before exceptional NCI: Rs 10 crore (vs Rs 535 crore YoY)
- **Net Debt (without leases):** Rs 6,127 crore
- **Capex Budget:** Around Rs 1,000 crore for current year and next year.
- **Proceeds from Sale of Investments:** Rs 796 crore (from selling listed group company shares).

### Highlights
- India operations outperformed with healthy demand and higher volumes in salt, FOS, and silica, though capitalization of projects increased depreciation and finance costs.
- TCNA won the bid in Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings for Searles Valley Minerals, acquiring customer contracts for half a million tonnes over two years.
- UK operations impacted by higher gas/energy costs and supply chain disruptions.
- Kenya operations remained suspended due to a notice from the Ministry of Mining; legal and administrative engagements are ongoing.
- DGTR recommended quantity restrictions on soda ash imports into India for two years.
- Sold listed shares in group companies worth Rs 796 crore in July to pay down debt in the US and UK.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects near-term market conditions to remain range-bound due to Chinese capacity additions and sustained export activity.
- Capex is budgeted at around Rs 1,000 crore for the current year and expected to remain at a similar level next year.
- Management remains committed to long-term specialty growth and non-cyclical value-added businesses under the LIFE framework.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Living Essentials (salt, bicarbonate, FOS) demand outlook is supported by premiumization and gut health trends.
- Global soda ash industry faces persistent oversupply, subdued industrial demand in China, elevated inventory (1.89 million metric tons in China by September end), and intense export competition.
- India's demand remains healthy, supported by solar glass manufacturing and infrastructure investments.
- Farm Essentials demand was affected by uneven rainfall and delayed sowing, though Kharif acreage subsequently recovered.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Elevated energy, logistics, and CO2 costs weighed on margins.
- UK variable costs spiked sharply due to higher gas costs.
- Soda ash pricing remains under pressure globally and softened in India towards the later part of the year due to improved import availability and domestic supply.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capex is budgeted at approximately Rs 1,000 crore for FY27, including growth projects.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt without leases stood at Rs 6,127 crore.
- Rs 796 crore raised from selling non-current investments (group company shares) was deployed to reduce debt in the US and UK.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Structural demand-supply imbalance and persistent low export realizations in global soda ash.
- Middle East conflict driving up freight, logistics, and CO2 input costs.
- Suspension of mining operations in Kenya and regulatory uncertainties.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Saurabh Jain (HSBC)** asked about the status of Kenya operations and legal recourse. Management answered that they are engaging administratively and legally, citing rights under the UK-Kenya Bilateral Investment Treaty, and awaiting Environment and Land Court proceedings.
- **Saurabh Jain (HSBC)** asked about the strategic benefit of acquiring Searles Valley customer contracts. Management replied that proximity to California and neighboring markets improves logistics efficiency and plant netbacks compared to exports.
- **Abhijit Akella** asked about UK segment losses and accounting treatment for Searles contracts. Management clarified that UK losses stemmed from high gas/energy costs and feedstock pricing, and the $22 million paid for Searles contracts is treated as an intangible asset amortized over five years.
- **Kartik Chawla** suggested monetizing larger Tata Sons stakes to reward shareholders. Management declined to comment on the matter.

### One-line takeaway
Resilient domestic demand and volume growth were neutralized by global soda ash oversupply, soaring UK energy costs, and the suspension of Kenyan mining operations, leading to a consolidated net loss for the quarter.
