# Kotyark Industries Ltd - Q4 FY26 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-06-24 · Chemicals*

**Quarter:** Q4 and FY26
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported steady operational progress, capacity expansion, and improving industrial sales and margins.

### Key numbers
- FY26 Revenue: Rs 314.9 crore
- FY26 EBITDA: Rs 48 crore
- FY26 PAT: Rs 19.4 crore
- Capacity utilization: Around 7% to 8% (FY26)
- Rajasthan biodiesel production capacity expanded from 500 KLPD to 1,500 KLPD; cumulative annual capacity at approximately 4,80,000 KL
- Executable order book: Approximately Rs 80 crore (to be executed over the next three months)
- Revenue pipeline: Nearly Rs 215 crore
- New capacity addition at Jhajjar (Haryana) and Kanpur (Uttar Pradesh): 200 KLPD each (total 400 KLPD), expected by December 2026
- Operating margins currently: 8% to 10%
- Carbon credits generated: Over 57,000 (Vera carbon certification)

### Highlights
- Proactively expanded Rajasthan facility capacity to 1,500 KLPD during FY26.
- Became the first Indian biodiesel company to receive Vera carbon certification.
- Setting up two new 200 KLPD manufacturing facilities in Jhajjar and Kanpur, funded through internal accruals.
- Promoter group share sale proceeds have been entirely infused into Kotyark Industries to move towards a zero-debt position.
- Expanded footprint into industrial and retail channels, supplying to clients like Modern Insulators and E-Diesel Biofuels.
- ARAI certifications have helped increase supplies to the mining industry and generator operators.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue and capacity utilization to increase by 15% to 20% (potentially up to 30%) in the upcoming year.
- Margins are expected to improve slightly as plant utilization increases.
- Aiming for 60% to 80% growth over the next four to five years.
- Export of biodiesel operations expected to start within 4 to 6 months after obtaining necessary licenses (EOU conversion and ISCC certification).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Biodiesel blending is growing gradually; management notes it follows government policy and OMC requirements.
- Change in OMC tender allocation policy from location-based to total quantity is viewed as beneficial for Kotyark.
- Management stated that isobutanol blending is currently not viable due to high prices (70%-75% more expensive than biodiesel) and limited manufacturing plants.
- Raw material prices move in tandem with OMC rates, keeping margins relatively stable.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating margins currently range between 8% to 10%.
- Flexible multi-feedstock platform capable of processing 10 to 15 non-edible oils based on availability and economics.
- Yield achieved is over 99%.
- In-house plant design and manufacturing allows Kotyark to set up facilities at roughly 30% of competitors' investment costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Rajasthan capacity expanded to 1,500 KLPD (cumulative annual capacity ~4,80,000 KL).
- Upcoming plants in Jhajjar and Kanpur (200 KLPD each) will add 400 KLPD by December 2026, increasing capacity by ~75% and strengthening presence in North India.
- Funded via internal accruals.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Promoter share sales proceeds used entirely to reduce debt and target a zero-debt position.
- Sufficient storage capacity currently available; no additional storage planned for now.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Legal case regarding seized raw material is ongoing with a stay order permitted sales; next hearing is next month.
- Association-level case regarding penalties imposed by the Revenue Department is pending in the Supreme Court.
- Biodiesel export is currently banned, subject to obtaining an EOU conversion and licenses.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Himanshu Agarwal asked about OMC tender quantities and the new allocation policy.** Management replied that blending increases gradually per government policy, and the new total-quantity allocation policy benefits Kotyark since it avoids strict location-based L1 constraints.
2. **Udit Sehgal asked about current margins and the long timeline to utilize expanded capacity.** Management stated current margins are 8-10% and will improve with utilization, noting that industrial targets will drive faster volume growth of 15-20%.
3. **Ashish Thakur asked about export timelines for biodiesel.** Management stated export is currently banned, but after obtaining licenses and certifications (EOU, ISCC) in 4 to 6 months, exports will begin this year.
4. **Shubham Mehtre asked about the government's focus on isobutanol vs biodiesel and status of legal cases.** Management dismissed isobutanol as unviable due to high cost and lack of plants, and clarified that their personal legal case has a stay order allowing sales with a hearing next month.
5. **Anshul Sharma asked what differentiates Kotyark from competitors regarding costs and yield.** Management highlighted their ability to build plants internally at ~30% of industry capital cost and achieve >99% product yield.

### One-line takeaway
Kotyark Industries reported steady FY26 performance with Rs 314.9 crore revenue, aggressively expanded its biodiesel capacity, and is directing funds toward debt reduction while scaling industrial sales and export readiness.
