# Pune E - Stock Broking Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Capital Markets & Wealth*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Consolidated revenue, EBITDA, and net profit achieved their highest-ever levels for the first half of a year, driven by strong resilience in the retail segment and newer business verticals.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated Revenue from Operations: Rs 34.2 crore (up 29.6% YoY from Rs 26 crore; up 24% QoQ from Rs 27.5 crore)
- EBITDA: Rs 22.3 crore (up 31% YoY)
- Profit After Tax (PAT): Consolidated Rs 14.3 crore (up 36.8% YoY from Rs 10.4 crore; up 51% QoQ from Rs 9.5 crore); Standalone PAT at Rs 13.4 crore
- Net Profit Margin: 41.7% (improved 220 bps YoY)
- Profit Before Tax (PBT): Rs 19.1 crore (up 38.8% YoY from Rs 13.7 crore)
- Total Equity: Rs 225 crore as of 30 September 2026 (against Rs 180.6 crore in March)
- Short-term Borrowings: Rs 65 crore (against Rs 47 crore in March); Long-term borrowings at Rs 0.6 crore
- Margin Trading Facility (MTF) Book: Crossed Rs 100 crore
- Mutual Fund AUM: Over Rs 240 crore across 36 AMCs, 500+ schemes, and 1,600+ investors; SIP book over Rs 1.5 crore
- PESB Alpha Fund (Cat III AIF): Manages around Rs 55 crore (PESB invested Rs 15 crore)
- Client Base: Over 65,000 clients (17,000+ traded clients); total demat assets held over Rs 9,000 crore
- Return Ratios (FY26): Consolidated ROE at 12.2%, ROCE at 14.2%

### Highlights
- Equity capital increased via conversion of warrants into 18 lakh equity shares and issuance of 16 lakh new convertible warrants at Rs 234 each (25% subscription received).
- Paid a final dividend of Rs 1 per share for FY26.
- Subsidiary Pune Finvest received IRDAI corporate agency approval in July 2026; advisory operations began under the brand "QURA Insure" in September 2026.
- Merchant banking division earned its first fees in 2026; first SME IPO mandate (Apar Advertisers) received approval with listing planned in the next couple of months.
- Gift City presence established to provide clients access to international markets (US stocks).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to sustain double-digit profit growth and improve return ratios.
- Target to reach over 100,000 customers by 2027.
- Expect wealth, merchant banking, and insurance to contribute meaningfully to the bottom line and drive capital-light fee-based growth.
- Management plans to open flagship branches in high-visibility locations, grow the MTF book carefully, scale retail algo trading, and build Gift City, NBFC, and insurance businesses.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Secondary market impacted by global uncertainties (West Asian conflict, high Brent crude around USD 110/barrel, US Fed rate hikes, highest G-Sec yields since 2007, FII net selling of over Rs 2.3 lakh crore in cash markets, and Nifty down ~6% in September).
- Domestic resilience noted: DIIs bought close to Rs 3.9 lakh crores; monthly SIP contributions hit a record >Rs 32,400 crore in September (~Rs 3.9 lakh crore annual run-rate); demat accounts crossed 24 crores.
- Primary market booming: Mainboard IPOs raised a record Rs 94,200 crores across 78 issues in H1 (35% higher YoY); total IPO fundraise including SMEs crossed Rs 1 lakh crore.
- Regulatory headwinds: SEBI measures on F&O halved option volumes in 2026 and reduced individual F&O traders by ~20%; higher STT on derivatives added pressure. Industry MTF book grew from >Rs 1.4 lakh crore (June) to ~Rs 1.6 lakh crore (September).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Shift towards fee-based, capital-light businesses (merchant banking, insurance, AIF) expected to structurally improve net profit margins and return ratios.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capital deployment prioritized towards MTF book expansion and merchant banking/IPO pipeline.
- Ongoing investments in technology, open API-based algo trading platforms, and flagship branches on a need basis.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- MTF book is fully collateralized by client stock holdings and cash.
- Short-term borrowings of Rs 65 crore primarily fund the MTF book.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Regulatory risk: Government/regulatory changes in stock broking, exchanges, and insurance create short-term margin/profitability pressures, though management views them as transparent and beneficial for the long term.
- Market volatility and concentration risks in the MTF book managed via automated MTM tracking, staff follow-ups for margin shortages, and mandatory stock liquidation after 5 days of continuous shortage.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Mukul Darda asked about the merchant banking outlook and IPO pipeline.** 
   * *Management:* Stated the first SME IPO (Apar Advertisers) is approved and will list in a couple of months; multiple SME and mainboard IPOs are in the pipeline. *(Straight answer)*
2. **Ishan Kadam asked about the group structure (Pune Finvest, PESB Alpha Fund, IFSC) and primary growth drivers.** 
   * *Management:* Explained Pune Finvest is an 79.8% subsidiary housing the NBFC and insurance corporate agency. The AIF is a trust managed by PESB Asset Management LLP (a wholly-owned subsidiary). *(Straight answer)*
3. **Anand Ahuja questioned the choice of an insurance corporate agency license over a broking license.** 
   * *Management:* Explained that a corporate agency license was quicker to obtain via consultants and aligns with standard NBFC operating models without major structural disadvantages. *(Straight answer)*
4. **Anand Ahuja asked about warrant fund deployment and dilution concerns.** 
   * *Management:* Stated funds will be deployed into MTF and merchant banking; promoters participated equally in the warrant round, so no major structural dilution occurred for existing shareholders. *(Straight answer)*
5. **Harshal Sharma asked how IRDAI commission caps and SEBI MTF rules affect their business.** 
   * *Management:* Acknowledged margin compression from IRDAI rules but expects volume expansion; viewed SEBI's MTF regulations as positive long-term safeguards against speculation in weak scripts. *(Straight answer)*
6. **Harshal Sharma asked about AIF fee lumpiness and growth scaling.** 
   * *Management:* Clarified that AIF income is consistent (tied to 3-year lock-ins/thresholds) rather than lumpy; noted the fund delivered ~25% post-fee/tax returns in its first year. *(Straight answer)*

### One-line takeaway
Pune E-Stock Broking delivered its highest-ever half-yearly financial performance (revenue up ~30% YoY, PAT up ~37% YoY), scaling its MTF book above Rs 100 crore while expanding into capital-light, fee-based verticals like insurance, AIF, and merchant banking.
