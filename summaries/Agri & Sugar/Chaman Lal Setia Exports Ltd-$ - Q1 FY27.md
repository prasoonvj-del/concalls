# Chaman Lal Setia Exports Ltd-$ - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-10 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Smooth operations despite geopolitical challenges (Iran-USA war), with strong profitability and margins driven by low-cost inventory procurement.

### Key numbers
- **Q1 FY27 Domestic Selling Price:** Rs 64 per kg
- **Q1 FY27 Export Selling Price:** Rs 98 per kg
- **Q1 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** 12.59% (or 12.6%)
- **FY26 Export Revenue Percentage:** 91% of total revenue (management estimate; noted ambiguity with presentation data)
- **Potential Packing Capacity:** Up to 800 tonnes per day (average current packing around 500 tonnes per day)
- **Inventory Mix:** 30% to 45% paddy, rest is rice

### Highlights
- Operations remained smooth with no impact from the Iran-USA war, zero stuck containers, and zero shipments to Iran.
- Received strong praise from MSC shipping line regarding global port diversification and business model.
- Initiated business relationship with Saudi company Al-Muhaidib (buying ~2.5 lakh tonnes from India); initial trial shipment of 500 tonnes moving successfully.
- Management highlighted continuous efforts by a 20+ member team dedicated to new buyer development.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue to reach Rs 1,800 crore to Rs 2,000 crore this year.
- Management expects EBITDA margins and gross margins to sustain at current levels in future quarters.
- Q2 demand outlook remains positive, though logistics and war developments are monitored.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Basmati demand remains robust as an essential product despite geopolitical tensions and ocean freight increases.
- Current Basmati prices are high, though below peak levels.
- Early Basmati crop (C9 variety from UP) arrivals are normal and prices are high; farmers report expectations of a good crop due to favorable monsoon rains.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Ocean freight costs increased due to geopolitical situations; company quotes FOB and adds actual ocean freight to make it CIF, passing costs to buyers.
- Gross and EBITDA margins expanded in Q1 primarily due to low-cost inventory built up from the beginning of the season until mid-January.
- Company maintains a policy of ensuring profitability on every single container/transaction rather than operating on breakeven.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Milling and packaging capacity operate efficiently, with packing capacity able to scale up to 800 tonnes per day.
- Increased strategic focus on expanding domestic sales alongside the export portfolio.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory procurement operates largely on a "just in time" model (processing and packing within 24 hours when needed).
- Receivables and money recovery are normal with no heavy credit risks reported.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical disturbances (war situations affecting Middle East / Red Sea routes, ocean freight volatility).
- Slowdown in movement to certain Middle East destinations if logistics do not normalize.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Manish Kela on ocean freights & inventory:** Management stated they pass freight costs via CIF pricing and still hold reasonable low-cost inventory procured earlier in the season.
- **Rajesh on top-line growth stagnation:** Management explained that top-line tends to remain flat for a few years before breaking out, highlighting the new engagement with Al-Muhaidib and renewed domestic focus.
- **Mrunal Kadam on realizations:** Management confirmed Q1 average selling prices reached Rs 98/kg for exports and Rs 64/kg for domestic, marking high levels.
- **Love Gupta on EBITDA and flat export volumes:** Management reiterated that profitability per transaction and overall EBITDA margin (12%-14%) take priority over mere revenue scale.
- **Vedant Rane on capacity and customer sizing:** Management noted packaging capacity can scale to 800 tonnes/day and emphasized they prioritize transaction profitability over blindly fulfilling large volume orders without margins.

### One-line takeaway
Chaman Lal Setia Exports delivered a strong Q1 FY27 with a 12.59% EBITDA margin, driven by low-cost inventory procurement and resilient global demand, while maintaining strict profitability thresholds over volume chasing.
