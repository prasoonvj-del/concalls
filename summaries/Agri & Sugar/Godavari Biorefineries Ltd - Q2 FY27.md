# Godavari Biorefineries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-13 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong revenue growth and EBITDA turnaround in bio-based chemicals were offset by seasonal losses and government policy restrictions in sugar and ethanol.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained target of Rs 240 crore quarterly revenue in bio-based chemicals by Q3 FY28 and the planned Rs 25 crore debottlenecking investment.
- Tone and demand view: Bio-based chemicals demand beat internal expectations (Rs 204.2 crore actual vs Rs 190 crore expected) due to the Hormuz region crisis lifting product realizations; however, the sugar and ethanol outlook tightened due to drought conditions in Maharashtra/Karnataka and strict government restrictions on sugarcane juice and B-heavy molasses diversion.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Drought in key growing states (Maharashtra and Karnataka); government policy restrictions on sugarcane juice/B-heavy molasses diversion to ethanol; rising maize prices exerting pressure on grain-based ethanol margins. Secured additional patents in the US and Mexico.
- Last quarter's promises and delivery: Exceeded Q2 bio-based chemicals revenue guidance (Rs 204.2 crore vs Rs 190 crore guided); filed for TNBC preliminary efficacy trial approvals with CDSCO, which are now expected during Q3 FY27.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations:** Rs 549.2 crore, up 27.5% YoY.
- **Total income:** Rs 549.8 crore, up 26.6% YoY.
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 10.9 crore (margin 2.0%), compared to negative Rs 4.4 crore (margin -1.0%) in Q2 FY26.
- **Consolidated Net Loss:** Narrowed to Rs 15.2 crore vs Rs 41.6 crore loss in Q2 FY26.
- **Bio-based Chemicals Segment:** Revenue Rs 204.2 crore (up 38.8% YoY); Segment EBITDA Rs 22.1 crore (up 47% YoY); Segment EBITDA margin 10.8% (up 60 bps YoY from 10.2%).
- **Sugar, Co-generation & Ethanol Combined Revenue:** Rs 334.2 crore vs Rs 275.7 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **Sugar & Co-generation EBITDA:** Loss of Rs 19.3 crore vs Loss of Rs 23.1 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **Ethanol Segment EBITDA:** Rs 10.6 crore vs Rs 4.7 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **H1 FY27 Performance:** Total income Rs 1,109.7 crore (up 14.6% YoY); EBITDA Rs 13.4 crore vs Rs 2.1 crore in H1 FY26; Net Loss narrowed to Rs 34.6 crore vs Rs 57.6 crore loss in H1 FY26.

### Highlights
- Bio-based specialty chemicals accounted for 52% of the chemical segment's revenue in H1 FY27.
- H1 FY27 bio-based chemical EBITDA reached 86% of the full-year FY26 chemical segment EBITDA.
- Secured European patents for anti-cancer compositions and bio-based rubber enforcement, a Chinese patent for antiviral compounds, and US and Mexico patents for antiviral/anti-cancer research.
- Recognized as "Manufacturing Process Innovator of the Year" at the FICCI Chemicals and Petrochemicals Awards 2026.

### Guidance & outlook
- Target bio-based chemical quarterly revenue of Rs 240 crore by Q3 FY28 post-debottlenecking.
- Management expects Q3 and Q4 to be strongly profitable at the consolidated level as sugarcane crushing and processing operations resume.
- Preliminary efficacy trials for the triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) candidate budgeted at Rs 15-20 crore max, aiming to out-license the molecule post-trial success. Patient trials will run for 33 months, with initial readout available in 6-9 months.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global bio-based chemicals demand supported by supply disruptions (Hormuz region crisis) and higher fossil fuel costs narrowing the cost gap with green alternatives.
- Sugar prices expected to remain firm due to lower regional sugarcane availability and policy restrictions on ethanol diversion.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Drought conditions in Maharashtra and Karnataka present agricultural supply challenges.
- Government policy restricts sugarcane juice and B-heavy molasses diversion to ethanol, leaving C-heavy molasses, grain, and surplus food grains as permitted feedstocks.
- Higher maize prices are putting margin pressures on grain-based ethanol production.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Additional Rs 25 crore capex dedicated to bio-based specialty chemical debottlenecking, expected to start commercial operations from April 2027.
- Total distillery capacity stands at 800 KLPD with multi-feedstock flexibility (sugarcane, C-molasses, maize, surplus rice).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory reduced from Rs 775 crore in March to ~Rs 260 crore in September due to standard seasonal drawdown of sugar inventory.
- Generated ~Rs 65 crore in operating cash flows in H1 FY27.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Weather disruptions and drought conditions in Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- Changing government policy restrictions regarding ethanol feedstocks.
- Elevated raw material costs for maize impacting grain distillery margins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Vivek Gupta (Star Investments):** *What drove Q2 bio-based chemical revenue past the Rs 190 crore guidance?*  
  Management stated that past debottlenecking combined with higher volume and price realizations driven by the Hormuz region crisis boosted revenues to Rs 204.2 crore. The eventual target of Rs 240 crore per quarter by Q3 FY28 will be driven by the current Rs 25 crore debottlenecking investment starting April 2027.
- **Kranti:** *What are the timelines for the DME and drug discovery projects?*  
  Management confirmed that CO2-to-DME pilot trials with ICT Mumbai will conclude by March 2027. CDSCO trial permission for the TNBC drug candidate is expected in Q3 FY27, with preliminary patient trial readouts anticipated within 6 to 9 months of trial commencement.
- **Nivesh Verma:** *How will clinical trial developments be funded?*  
  Management clarified that preliminary efficacy trials require Rs 15-20 crore max and will be funded internally; following positive efficacy data, the company intends to out-license the candidate to larger pharmaceutical companies.
- **Varun Mishra:** *How will sugar and ethanol economics play out given feedstock restrictions?*  
  Management explained that reduced sugarcane juice/B-heavy molasses diversion will channel more cane into sugar, where realizations are expected to be stronger, while ethanol capacity will be supported by C-heavy molasses and grain feedstocks.

### One-line takeaway
Godavari Biorefineries delivered an operational EBITDA turnaround driven by bio-based chemicals growth while relying on its multi-feedstock ethanol distillery to navigate regulatory and weather-related sugar industry headwinds.
