# Triveni Engineering & Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-10 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong profitability turnaround in sugar and alcohol driven by higher realizations and lower costs, offset by unseasonal rainfall risks in UP, challenging new ethanol tender terms, and slower water business execution.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: None given (maintained stance of cautious watch on cash generation and upcoming tender finalisation).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from cautious optimism about ethanol flexibility to concerns over the ESY 26-27 tender (exclusion of juice/B-heavy, lower maize prices, firm maize feedstock costs) and late October unseasonal rainfall posing a near-term risk to UP cane recovery.
- New topics or risks: Unseasonal heavy rainfall in October across UP, Haryana SAP increases for SS 26-27 and 27-28, and strict limits on FCI rice (30%) in the new ESY tender. Old risks like top borers shifted to post-rainfall recovery uncertainty.
- Last quarter's promises: TPTL listing application was noted as pending in 4-6 weeks last quarter; the call noted the power transmission business demerger became effective April 1, 2026.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenues from Operations:** ₹1,731 crore (up 8% YoY); H1 FY27 at ₹3,311 crore (up 5% YoY from ₹3,145 crore).
- **Operating (EBITDA) Margin:** H1 FY27 operating margin improved by 383 bps to 6.9% (from 3.1% in H1 FY26).
- **Profit Before Tax (PBT):** ₹131 crore for H1 FY27 (vs loss of ₹25 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Finance Cost:** H1 FY27 finance cost 20% lower YoY; average cost of funds at 6.8% for H1 and 6.78% for Q2.
- **Net Debt:** ₹157 crore as of September 30, 2026 (vs ₹702 crore a year ago).
- **Sugar Segment Revenue:** ₹1,262 crore in Q2 (up 4% YoY); H1 revenue ₹2,497 crore (up 5% YoY). Segment PBIT at ₹113 crore for H1 (vs loss of ₹5 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Sugar Realisations:** Average Q2 realisation at ₹4,728 per metric tonne (up 16% YoY); H1 average realisation up 9% to ₹4,428 per metric tonne. Dispatches down 8% in Q2; H1 dispatches stable at 5.33 lakh metric tonnes.
- **Sugar Inventory:** 1.03 lakh metric tonnes as of September 30, 2026 (valued at ₹38.6 per kg) vs 1.69 lakh metric tonnes a year ago.
- **Alcohol Segment Revenue:** Q2 net revenue up 19% supported by 16% higher sales volume; H1 revenue up 3%. Segment PBIT for Q2 at ₹43 crore (vs ₹28 crore YoY); H1 PBIT up 44%. H1 production at 1,085 lakh litres (down 13% YoY).
- **Water Business Revenue:** H1 revenue dipped 17%; Q2 revenue declined 13%. PBIT at ₹3 crore for H1 (vs ₹13 crore YoY) and broadly breakeven in Q2. Closing order book at ₹1,452 crore (comprising ₹404 crore EPC/equipment and ₹1,048 crore O&M).

### Highlights
- Sugar segment turnaround driven by significantly higher realisations, lower finance costs due to lower inventory, and disciplined off-season costs.
- Ethanol production constrained by lower operating days, storage constraints, and a shift away from B-heavy molasses.
- Company doubled down on field surveillance for chronic diseases and fertilizer/irrigation control in Western UP.
- First sugar plant (Chandanpur) commenced crushing operations on November 3, 2026.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management estimates national net sugar production for SS 2026-27 at ~27.5 million metric tonnes with downside risks due to diversion to the unorganised sector and zero diversion to juice/B-heavy molasses.
- Management expects domestic sugar prices to remain firm supported by low opening stocks (estimated at ~3.5 million metric tonnes) and weather uncertainties.
- Management notes ESY 26-27 tender economics are challenging due to the exclusion of sugarcane juice and B-heavy molasses, a ₹3/kg reduction in maize ethanol prices, and firm maize prices (~₹26-27/kg).
- Management expects water business execution to pick up in Q3 and Q4 FY27 to return to budgeted levels.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic annual sugar consumption estimated at ~28.5 million metric tonnes against very low opening stocks of ~3.5 million metric tonnes.
- Haryana increased SAP for SS 2026-27 by ₹15 to ₹430/quintal and announced SS 2027-28 SAP at ₹450/quintal (up ₹20).
- International raw sugar prices moved between $0.1331 and $0.213/pound (currently ~$0.195/pound); white sugar moved between $376 and $561/tonne (currently ~$520/tonne).
- Government allowed import of 1 million metric tonnes of raw sugar (of which ~300,000 tonnes imported to date); management notes imports could increase towards 2 million tonnes.
- ESY 26-27 tender invites 1,100 crore litres with no offers for juice/B-heavy molasses, limiting FCI rice-based ethanol to 30%.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Prevailing Triveni sugar prices: ~₹5,800/quintal for institutional and ~₹5,400/quintal for trade.
- DDGS realisations strengthened and remain stable with minor upside potential.
- Maize prices increased to ₹26–27/kg; management noted current prices completely erode standalone maize-based ethanol profitability, though Triveni is cushioned by lower-cost advance procurement for the near term.
- Employee costs reflected wage bill revisions under the new labor code covering 4,000+ employees.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- No meaningful capex planned for the year aside from minor steam/efficiency improvements. Capital allocation focus remains on debt repayment and cash generation.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt reduced significantly to ₹157 crore as of September 30, 2026, compared to ₹702 crore a year ago, driven by higher profitability, lower working capital utilisation, and term loan repayments.
- Closing sugar inventory reduced to 1.03 lakh metric tonnes from 1.69 lakh metric tonnes YoY.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unseasonal heavy rainfall in late October across Uttar Pradesh posing risks to cane maturity, initial recovery, field access, and season start dates.
- Severe drought and irrigation shortages in Maharashtra and Karnataka impacting crop size and planting for subsequent seasons.
- High maize feedstock costs and unfavorable pricing/tender terms in the ESY 26-27 ethanol policy.
- Slow execution timing in select EPC water projects.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Aman Kumar Sonthalia (AK Securities) on UP rainfall impact and cane recovery:** Management stated sampling data indicates a 50-70 bps decline in recovery due to late rains, but true lab data upon factory startup is required; lodging is minimal in Western UP due to binding practices.
2. **Tanuj Nangalia (SKP Securities) on realisation sustainability and import parity:** Management noted domestic trade prices (~₹5,400-5,500) align closely with raw sugar import parity landed costs (~₹54.5/kg without margin) and expect prices to hold subject to government quotas/stock limits.
3. **Sanjay Manyal (DAM Capital) on maize ethanol profitability at ₹26-27/kg:** Management answered that current maize prices completely erode standalone maize-based ethanol margins, leaving profitability dependent on DDGS and conversion efficiency, though integrated players are better cushioned.
4. **Chetan Sharma (Systematix Shares) on UP SAP and import volumes:** Management stated ~300,000 tonnes have been imported out of the permitted 1 million tonnes, and expects the UP government to announce cane prices within 3-4 weeks.
5. **Somnath Saha (360 ONE Capital) on employee cost increase:** Management clarified that employee costs reflect wage revisions under the new labor code for 4,000+ employees, noting last year's power transmission costs are classified under discontinued operations.

### One-line takeaway
Triveni Engineering achieved a strong H1 FY27 profitability turnaround and net debt reduction, entering H2 with tight domestic sugar supply balances offset by unseasonal UP rainfall risks and margin pressures in the new ethanol tender.
