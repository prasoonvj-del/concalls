# EID Parry India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - While sugar prices and realisations improved, segments like CPG saw revenue declines and ethanol production moderated.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained CPG breakeven target for FY28.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from weather/crop concerns to more concrete estimates of a ~10% lower crop for next year and higher cane prices in Karnataka due to farmer agitations.
- New topics or risks: Intense farmer agitations in Karnataka and Maharashtra leading to higher cane payments (INR3,800s vs FRP of INR3,300-3,400); potential sugar imports needed for SY '26-'27.
- Last quarter's promises: PSRIPL SEZ exit in-principle received in April, duty workings submitted in June, with final duty payment now expected in Q3 (slightly pushed out from Q2).

### Highlights
- PSRIPL inventories were fully sold and money realized by September 30, 2026; final duty payment for SEZ exit expected in Q3.
- CPG revenue declined due to intentional drop of low-margin bulk dal and white sugar, while VAP saliency and CM1 margins improved.
- Nutraceuticals US business (Valensa) saw strong growth, expanding into new derm and hair care segments alongside pet joint health.
- *Breaking pattern:* Unlike steady past guidance, management noted a ~10% lower crop expected for next year due to El Nino and subpar monsoons, alongside mounting farmer pressure for higher cane prices.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects CPG to break even in FY28.
- Management expects next year's crop to be ~10% lower due to weather and water availability.
- Full-year ethanol/alcohol volumes expected around 14 crores to 15 crores litres.
- CPG targeted to achieve INR500 crore revenue this year, followed by 20% to 25% growth next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global sugar market shifting from surplus to marginal deficit in SY '26-'27.
- Subpar southwest monsoon in India led to lower inventories and elevated prices, prompting government interventions (duty-free raw sugar imports, stock limits, frequent quotas).
- India likely to need sugar imports in SY '26-'27 to bridge the supply gap.
- Karnataka cane prices settling around INR3,800/MT due to farmer agitations, significantly higher than state FRPs (INR3,300-3,400).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Cane landed cost increased to INR3,798 per MT.
- CPG contribution margins (CM1) moved up significantly through focus on high-margin products (jaggery, brown sugar).
- Tamil Nadu cost of production remains higher than Karnataka.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- No capex or inorganic plans in the sugar and biofuel segments.
- No capital allocation plans for Nutra expansion; FMCG investments are primarily operating expenses (A&SP, manpower, new product development).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Standalone balance sheet strengthened through asset monetization and physical asset divestment of the refinery.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Subpar southwest monsoon impacting crop yields and sowing in Karnataka and Maharashtra (estimated 8-10% downside in sowing).
- High volatility in sugar spot prices leading to a shift away from long-term institutional contracts (limiting contracts to 15-24 days).
- Farmer agitations forcing mills to pay above FRP, necessitating a fair, uniform policy framework.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sanjay Manyal (DAM Capital) on Karnataka/Maharashtra crop and pricing:** Management explained that crushing will be shorter by 10-15 days, sowing could drop 8-10%, and mills are forced to pay ~INR3,800/MT (above FRP of INR3,300-3,400) due to agitations.
2. **Rajakumar on CPG profitability and capital allocation:** Management stated FY28 remains the target for CPG break-even, driven by distribution expansion and VAP launches, funded via operating expenses rather than heavy capex.
3. **Gautam on CPG revenue drivers:** Management clarified that the targeted INR500 crore revenue for this year and 20-25% growth next year will be driven by distribution expansion and new launches like jaggery, while shedding low-margin bulk products.
4. **Gautam on institutional sugar contracts:** Management noted they have eliminated long-term contracts due to price volatility, keeping commitments to 15-24 days to mirror spot prices.
5. **Rajakumar on biochemical/bio-CNG diversification:** Management stated they are not exploring biochemicals currently, but continue to focus on substrate monetization (green grow media and bagasse).

### One-line takeaway
EID Parry delivered a mixed Q2 FY27 performance as higher sugar realisations and strong US nutraceuticals growth offset lower ethanol volumes, CPG revenue rationalisation, and rising cane cost pressures in Karnataka.
