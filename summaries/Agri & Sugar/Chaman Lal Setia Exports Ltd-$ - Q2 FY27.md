# Chaman Lal Setia Exports Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-17 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Achieved historically best quarterly PBT of Rs 75 crore, though management remains cautious regarding high paddy prices and logistics costs.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained (reiterated industry target of 6.5 million tonnes).
- **Tone and demand view:** Shifted from "smooth operations" to "catastrophic" regarding logistics costs due to Middle East conflict; demand remains robust but price-sensitive.
- **New topics:** Discussion on shifting to eco-friendly packaging due to potential plastic bans; management open to considering share buyback given cash reserves.
- **Last quarter's promises:** Saudi trial order (500 tonnes) progressed to a 200-tonne repeat order.

### Key numbers
- **PBT:** ~Rs 75 crore (historically best quarter).
- **Ocean Freight:** Increased from $200–$250 to $5,500–$6,000 per container due to Middle East conflict.
- **Cash Reserves:** Rs 500 crore.

### Highlights
- Management reported record PBT of Rs 75 crore, attributed to low-cost inventory procurement from the previous year and operational efficiencies.
- Confirmed zero exposure to Iran for the last 25 years.
- Management is prioritizing automation and new plant setups to reduce labor reliance.
- Confirmed a 200-tonne repeat order from a Saudi customer.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects the Indian industry to achieve a 6.5 million tonne export target this year.
- Management expects potential for profit if the rupee weakens (projected range Rs 96–98).

### Demand & industry commentary
- High paddy prices are currently driven by heavy buying from exporters with Iran exposure.
- Buyers are increasingly cautious, holding back orders to wait for price corrections.
- Basmati demand remains robust as an essential product, despite high freight and commodity prices.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Ocean freight costs are being passed to buyers via CIF pricing; company mandates insurance for all exports.
- Procurement strategy: Buying slower compared to last year to maintain margins; inventory levels are lower than the previous September quarter.
- Management noted that shipping lines are increasingly accepting ocean freight payments in USD, providing cost savings.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Focus on replacing old machinery with new, automated plants to improve grain handling and reduce labor dependency.
- No immediate plans for diversification; focus remains on the core rice business and potential domestic market expansion.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Company holds Rs 500 crore in cash reserves.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- **Geopolitical:** Middle East conflict causing "catastrophic" ocean freight spikes.
- **Commodity:** High paddy prices may lead to buyer resistance and volume deferrals.
- **Regulatory:** Potential bans on plastic pouches in export markets.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **On Iran exposure:** Management clarified they have had zero exposure for 25 years and avoid the market due to payment risks.
- **On inventory gains:** Management confirmed profitability was bolstered by low-cost inventory procured last year.
- **On share buyback:** Management acknowledged the suggestion and will discuss it internally given the Rs 500 crore cash position.
- **On Saudi order:** Management confirmed a 200-tonne repeat order but noted they are not confirming prices yet due to market volatility.
- **On volume growth:** Management noted that while volumes are currently lower, they are prioritizing transaction profitability over volume chasing.

### One-line takeaway
Chaman Lal Setia Exports delivered a record PBT of Rs 75 crore by leveraging low-cost inventory and passing on logistics costs, though management remains wary of high paddy prices and geopolitical freight volatility.
