# Apex Frozen Foods Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Encouraging top-line volume growth and strong H1 performance were offset by elevated farm gate prices, higher freight costs, and an exceptional antidumping duty provision impacting quarterly profitability.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained FY27 total volume target of around 12,000 metric tonnes, while noting potential minor adjustments due to recent cyclones.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly more cautious due to supply constraints from cyclones hitting Andhra Pradesh, higher freight costs from ongoing Middle East conflicts, and incremental retroactive antidumping duty provisions.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Added exceptional antidumping duty provision (Rs 10.10 crores in Q2, total approx Rs 30 crores / ~Rs 94 mn) for earlier years and recent cyclone disruptions in Andhra Pradesh. Labor shortages from Q1 were not mentioned.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Progressed on Japan market entry with first RTE shipments expected in early 2027 (Jan/Feb); Russia listing expected anytime soon with shipments anticipated in Q4.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue (Q2 FY27):** Rs 274 crore vs Rs 238 crore in Q2 FY26 (up 15% YoY).
- **Revenue (H1 FY27):** Rs 530 crore vs Rs 497 crore in H1 FY26 (up 7% YoY).
- **EBITDA (Q2 FY27):** Rs 18 crore vs Rs 18 crore in Q2 FY26 (flat YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin (Q2 FY27):** 6.4% vs 7.2% in Q2 FY26.
- **EBITDA (H1 FY27):** Rs 51 crore vs Rs 36 crore in H1 FY26 (up 40% YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin (H1 FY27):** 9.4% vs 7.1% in H1 FY26.
- **PBT before exceptional items (H1 FY27):** Rs 42 crore vs Rs 26 crore in H1 FY26 (up 60% YoY).
- **PAT (Q2 FY27):** Rs 2 crore vs Rs 12 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **PAT (H1 FY27):** Rs 24 crore vs Rs 21 crore in H1 FY26 (up 13% YoY).
- **Shrimp Sales Volume (Q2 FY27):** 2,973 metric tonnes (up 14% YoY and 13% QoQ).
- **Average Shrimp Realization (Q2 FY27):** Rs 874 per kg vs Rs 870 per kg in Q2 FY26.
- **Geographical Mix (Q2 FY27):** USA 62%, EU and UK 32%, Other markets 6%.
- **Geographical Mix (H1 FY27):** USA 66%, EU and UK 29%, Other markets 5%.
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** 0.04x as of September 2026.
- **Antidumping Duty Provision (Q2 FY27):** Rs 10.10 crores recognized (totaling approximately Rs 30 crores).

### Highlights
- Total shrimp sales volumes grew 14% YoY and 13% QoQ to 2,973 metric tonnes in Q2 FY27.
- USA sales volumes grew 59% YoY and remained flat QoQ, contributing 62% of total shrimp sales in Q2 FY27, supported by improving trade visibility and stabilization of tariff rates.
- EU and UK sales volumes declined 33% YoY due to earlier transportation disruptions, but recovered strongly by 46% QoQ in Q2 FY27 as logistics improved.
- Volumes to other markets (mainly Japan) grew approximately 1,000% YoY and 43% QoQ on a small base.
- Recognized an exceptional provision of Rs 10.10 crores in Q2 FY27 towards incremental antidumping duty liability (totaling ~Rs 30 crores) following a revision of rates from 1.35% to 3.76% and 5.53% by the U.S. Department of Commerce; the company has appealed to the Court of International Trade in the U.S.A.
- Farm gate prices experienced a significant correction downward in October following recent cyclone impacts and panic harvests.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains positive on reaching the 12,000 metric tonnes volume guidance for FY27, noting potential minor adjustments if supply takes a drastic hit from massive cyclone damages.
- Expects the EU free trade agreement / FDA benefit to be effective tentatively by early 2027 (end of this financial year or start of next financial year) with 0% tariffs on shrimp imports.
- Expects first shipments of Ready-to-Eat (RTE) products to Japan in early 2027 (January or February).
- Expects Russia market listing anytime soon with shipments anticipated in Q4.

### Demand & industry commentary
- USA demand improved due to stabilization of tariff rates (10%), though a 10% tariff continues to restrict stable demand growth.
- EU and UK sailing times remain extended as vessels route around the Cape of Good Hope due to Middle East conflicts.
- Non-tariff barriers and issues with Indian regulatory agencies stretch shipment execution times post-production to 2 to 3 weeks for the EU.
- Farm gate prices increased significantly in August and September due to subdued supply conditions, but corrected downwards significantly in October.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Q2 EBITDA margins were impacted by elevated farm gate prices (driven by subdued supply in August and September) and higher freight costs due to crude oil price increases and war-led routing around the Cape of Good Hope.
- The company has passed on some farm gate price increases to overseas customers.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- The company continues to utilize available manufacturing capacity and maintain a lean balance sheet.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt levels are prudently managed with a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.04x as of September 2026.
- Overall inventory holding periods are stretched due to export shipment execution delays for the EU.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Exceptional antidumping duty liability provision of Rs 10.10 crores in Q2 (totaling ~Rs 30 crores) due to rate revisions to 5.53%.
- Cyclones hitting the East Coast of India (Andhra Pradesh) causing crop damage, premature harvests, and short-term supply disruptions.
- Higher ocean freight costs and extended sailing times caused by Middle East conflicts and vessel rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope.
- Regulatory delays and non-tariff barriers in India and the EU extending post-production shipment execution times to 2-3 weeks.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Saiguhan (Individual Investor) asked how rupee appreciation against the USD (to Rs 89-90) would affect revenues.** Management replied that currency fluctuations impact both sales prices and farm gate prices, stating that an appreciating rupee primarily forces farm gate prices down as the export industry cannot absorb it, while volume growth remains the key focus to offset revenue impacts.
2. **Nilesh Patil (Share India Securities) asked whether the company will maintain the 12,000 metric tons volume guidance for FY27 given H1 performance and recent cyclones.** Management stated they remain positive on reaching 12,000 metric tons (or between 11,500 to 12,000 tons), but warned that drastic supply destructions from massive cyclones could impact the final number.
3. **Nilesh Patil asked if freight costs might see reductions in Q3.** Management stated they have no foresight on the war ending and shipping lines have not provided positive news, but noted that a drop in crude oil prices could help lower ocean freight costs.
4. **Devansh Mehta asked about the expected contribution from existing vs. new customers.** Management explained that existing customers and distributors will continue to contribute the major part of sales, while newer markets (East Europe, Japan, Russia) will add newer customers through ongoing product development.
5. **Deepak Karwa asked about the key factors driving margins and their sustainability.** Management explained that margins were impacted by elevated farm gate prices in August-September and ocean freight costs, though a much-needed correction in farm gate prices occurred in October.

### One-line takeaway
Apex Frozen Foods posted 15% YoY revenue growth in Q2 FY27 to Rs 274 crore, but PAT dropped to Rs 2 crore due to an exceptional Rs 10.10 crore antidumping duty provision, higher farm gate prices, and elevated freight costs.
