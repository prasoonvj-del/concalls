# Agri & Sugar - Agri processing (rice, tea, spices): what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the agri-processing sector (rice, tea, spices, cashew, and protein platforms) reported a robust top-line performance in Q2/H1 FY27, largely driven by resilient domestic demand and accelerating consumer-pack volumes. However, profitability and working capital faced significant headwinds due to geopolitical friction, Middle East conflicts, severe ocean freight spikes, and rising raw material costs (such as a 22–25% jump in opening paddy prices and green coffee volatility). While companies with strong balance sheets (such as LT Foods, KRBL, and CCL Products) absorbed these pressures or passed costs through CIF pricing, others experienced margin compression, inventory bloat, and transit delays.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Domestic demand remains strong, with urban consumer adoption, e-commerce, and modern trade driving healthy volume growth (e.g., KRBL’s consumer-pack volume growth accelerated to 18% YoY; LT Foods reported a 140 bps domestic market share gain). Global demand for specialized categories like freeze-dried coffee (CCL Products) and basmati rice remains resilient.
*   **Divergence (Exports & Middle East):** Middle East shipping routes and export channels faced severe friction. Companies like KRBL and LT Foods cited ongoing Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz disruptions, while DSM Fresh Foods experienced container shortages for Middle East exports of its Meevaa brand, forcing a pivot to domestic quick-commerce. Conversely, Chaman Lal Setia noted robust basmati demand despite freight spikes, and Aelea Commodities targeted the Middle East to lower logistics costs.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Freight & Logistics:** Widely cited as a major pressure point. Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb conflicts extended transit times (e.g., Aelea's transit times surged from 35–45 to 110–112 days) and caused ocean freight spikes (Chaman Lal Setia reported container freight jumping from $200–$250 to $5,500–$6,000). 
*   **Raw Materials:** New crop paddy prices opened 22–25% higher YoY (LT Foods, KRBL). Green coffee prices remained volatile in a USD 3,400–3,800/tonne range (CCL Products). 
*   **Margin Impact:** Gross and EBITDA margins moderated across several players due to higher input costs, increased freight absorption, and step-ups in brand investments (e.g., LT Foods consolidated EBITDA margin at 11%, DSM Fresh Foods gross margin down to 28.9%). CCL Products maintained its EBITDA per kg in the Rs 135–140 range.

## Capex & capacity
*   **Aelea Commodities:** Board approved a 60 MT shelling capacity expansion and Cardanol unit (Rs 114 crore total project cost over 30 months).
*   **CCL Products:** Evaluating brownfield expansion (~6,000 tonnes for freeze-dried) or strategic JVs/partnerships as FD capacity nears full utilization.
*   **DSM Fresh Foods:** Scaled its newly acquired Chandigarh manufacturing facility for Meevaa Foods (peaking at Rs 120–140 crore revenue potential) and expanded retail partner stores to 200.
*   **LT Foods:** First phase of the new Madhya Pradesh facility for Nature Bio Foods starts operations this FY.
*   **Pajson Agro:** Vizianagaram greenfield facility (35,000 MT addition) delayed by two months due to three Andhra Pradesh cyclones (trials expected in February); additionally acquired Pacific Global FZCO (60,000 MT facility in Cote d'Ivoire) via a share swap, bringing total capacity to 113,000 MT.

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Guidance & Tone Shifts:** CCL Products shifted from a "wait and watch" tone to seeing medium and long-term contracts return, maintaining its 15% volume growth guidance. KRBL updated its full-year EBITDA margin guidance to 17%–19% (vs 17%–18% in Q1). LT Foods and Chaman Lal Setia maintained their volume/industry targets.
*   **Delivery on Promises:** KRBL successfully delivered domestic volume acceleration, with consumer pack volume growth jumping from 7% YoY in Q1 to 18% YoY in Q2. CCL Products continued deleveraging, reducing net debt to Rs 742 crore from Rs 963 crore in Q1. Chaman Lal Setia progressed its Saudi trial order to a 200-tonne repeat order.

## Company one-liners
*   **Aelea Commodities:** Prioritizing capacity expansion and absolute EBITDA per kg while navigating logistics-driven working capital pressure and project delays.
*   **CCL Products (India) Ltd:** Delivered resilient volume growth and deleveraging, maintaining 15% growth guidance while evaluating capacity expansion options.
*   **Chaman Lal Setia Exports Ltd:** Leveraged low-cost inventory to post a record PBT of Rs 75 crore while remaining wary of high paddy prices and freight volatility.
*   **DSM Fresh Foods Ltd:** Achieved 73% revenue growth to Rs 165 crore, though subsidiary investments and a higher B2B mix created short-term operating deleverage.
*   **KRBL Ltd:** Posted record Q2 revenue of Rs 1,727 crore fueled by domestic consumer-pack acceleration, while export recovery awaits West Asian shipping normalization.
*   **LT Foods Ltd:** Delivered 22% H1 revenue growth on strong core basmati demand and better working capital, despite freight inflation and brand investments.
*   **Pajson Agro India Ltd:** Reported steady H1 growth and announced a transformational 60,000 MT capacity acquisition in Cote d'Ivoire via a share swap.
