# Apex Frozen Foods Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-22 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Improved global shrimp realizations, USD-INR exchange rate, and cost efficiencies drove strong profit growth, offset by labor shortages and war-led transportation disruptions affecting sales volumes.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 257 crore vs Rs 258 crore in Q1 FY26 (flat YoY).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 33 crore vs Rs 18 crore in Q1 FY26 (up 79% YoY).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 12.7% vs 7.1% in Q1 FY26 (expanded by 560 bps YoY).
- **PAT:** Rs 22 crore vs Rs 9 crore in Q1 FY26 (up 138% YoY).
- **PAT Margin:** 8.4% vs 3.5% in Q1 FY26.
- **Shrimp Sales Volume:** 2,624 tonnes vs 3,015 tonnes in Q1 FY26 (lower YoY).
- **Average Shrimp Realization:** ~Rs 930 per kg vs ~Rs 812 per kg in Q1 FY26 (up 15% YoY).
- **Geographical Mix (Q1 FY27):** USA contributed 70% (vs 54% in Q1 FY26); EU and UK together contributed 25% (vs 39% in Q1 FY26).
- **Ready-to-Eat (RTE) Share:** 16% of total volume (vs 15% in Q1 FY26); RTE realization moved to $12.05 per kg from $11.20 per kg in Q1 FY27.
- **Capacity Utilization:** 38% vs 39% in Q1 FY26.

### Highlights
- Average shrimp realizations grew 15% YoY, aided by improving global prices, a depreciated rupee, and a higher share of value-added/RTE products.
- USA sales grew 13% YoY and 121% QoQ due to easing tariff-related uncertainties (10% tariff finalized, 50% tariff removed).
- EU and UK sales volumes declined due to war-led transportation disruptions and origin-testing/certification delays in India, resulting in shipments spilling over into subsequent quarters.
- Order book is currently strong up to the middle of Q3.
- Initiated business ties with Japan; exploring Australia and Russia for later in the fiscal year.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects a recovery in sales volume in Q2 FY27, subject to the normalization of global transportation.
- Maintains a target of around 12,000 metric tonnes of total volume for FY27.
- Expects stable profit margins supported by volume growth, cost efficiencies, and current realization levels, despite rising farm gate prices and higher ocean freight costs.
- Expects RTE volume share to reach a minimum of 18% to 20% for the full year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Farm gate prices were stable through Q1 FY27 and have risen 6% to 7% over the past 3 to 4 weeks, providing good comfort to farmers.
- USA buyers increased order positions due to greater certainty following the stabilization of tariffs at 10%.
- Non-tariff barriers (such as 50% mandatory testing of Indian shrimp consignments) continue to impact shipments to the UK and EU, pending resolution by the Government of India.
- The India-UK FTA became effective on July 15, 2026; full benefits and volume impacts are anticipated over a minimum 1-year implementation window. The EU FTA is expected by the end of calendar 2026 or early 2027.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Profitability was supported by higher shrimp realizations, stable farm gate prices, and ongoing cost-efficiency measures.
- Ocean freight expenses have more than doubled between Q4 and Q1 due to war-led disruptions.
- Margin differential between Ready-to-Cook (RTC) and Ready-to-Eat (RTE) is a minimum of $0.50 per kg.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capacity utilization is planned to be consistently maintained over and above 35% to 40% through the year.
- Next-phase volume target for FY28/FY29 is between 14,000 to 15,000 metric tonnes, subject to global scenario improvements.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- The company maintains a lean financial profile with prudently managed debt levels and a disciplined focus on working capital.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Labor shortages (mainly in April and May due to summer heat) hampered Q1 production.
- War-led transportation disruptions have more than doubled ocean freight costs and caused container equipment shortages and shipping delays.
- Non-tariff barriers in the UK and EU (e.g., mandatory testing at origin) remain a bottleneck.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Nilesh Patil (Share India Securities) asked about the full-year volume guidance of 12,000 MT versus Q1 performance and the spike in US sales.** Management clarified that Q1 volumes were impacted by summer labor shortages and delayed EU shipments (spilling into Q2), while US growth reflected order certainty after tariff stabilization.
2. **Bala Murali Krishna (Oman Investment Advisors) asked if the company expects any tariff refunds from the US.** Management stated that no refunds have been received yet, there is no clarity on when or if they will be received, and tariffs were initially compensated by customers.
3. **Abhishek (SNS Capital) asked about the capacity utilization trajectory.** Management stated utilization was 38% in Q1, expects to maintain 35%–40% through the year, aims for ~12,000 MT total volume in FY27, and targets 14,000–15,000 MT for FY28/FY29.
4. **Shubhroy Tripathi (Krish Capital) asked about the timeline for the US CVD and ADD review.** Management stated ADD determination is expected around September and CVD review around December, with potential for duty reduction if the US accepts India's explanations regarding duty drawback and RoDTEP schemes.

### One-line takeaway
Apex Frozen Foods reported flat revenues (Rs 257 crore) but strong EBITDA (Rs 33 crore, +79% YoY) and PAT (Rs 22 crore, +138% YoY) in Q1 FY27, driven by 15% higher realizations and stable farm gate prices, despite volume headwinds from labor shortages and logistical disruptions.
