# Jain Irrigation Systems Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-05 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management delivered double-digit revenue growth, improved profitability, and significant working capital reduction despite severe drought conditions.

### Key numbers
*   **Revenue:** Rs 1,576 crore (+10% YoY).
*   **EBITDA:** Rs 207 crore.
*   **EBITDA Margin:** 13.2% (vs 13.9% in Q2 FY26).
*   **Adjusted PAT:** Rs 40 crore.
*   **H1 Revenue:** Rs 3,100 crore (+4% YoY).
*   **H1 EBITDA:** Rs 372 crore (12.1% margin).
*   **H1 Adjusted PAT:** Rs 43 crore.
*   **Working Capital Cycle:** Reduced to 179 days (from 183 days in Q1 and 205 days in Sept last year).

### Highlights
*   **Segment Performance:** Hi-Tech Micro-Irrigation (MI) maintained high-teen margins at 18.8%; Food segment at ~12%; Plastics segment at ~9%.
*   **Cash Flow:** Strong EBITDA-to-cash conversion (Rs 206 crore cash generated from Rs 208 crore EBITDA).
*   **Debt Repayment:** Successfully repaid Rs 230 crore of NCDs using operational cash flow.
*   **Strategic Shift:** Management is intentionally reducing project business; active projects down to 7-8.
*   **Operational Improvement:** 25-day improvement in working capital cycle YoY (15 days from DSOs, rest from inventory/payables).

### Guidance & outlook
*   **H2 Outlook:** Management expects "flattish" growth for H2 due to drought and raw material price uncertainty.
*   **Receivables:** Management expects a net reduction of Rs 300 crore+ in receivables by March 2027.
*   **Liquidity:** Management anticipates Rs 100-150 crore from land monetization and Rs 100 crore from Maharashtra incentives in H2.
*   **Project Business:** Management expects project business and associated recoveries to reach near-zero after next September.
*   **FY28:** Management expects the balance sheet to look "much better" with legacy issues resolved.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   **Monsoon Impact:** Worst monsoon in 35-50 years in West and South India (where 80-85% of company business is concentrated), impacting Kharif and Rabi crops.
*   **Government Policy:** In some areas, government is prioritizing drinking water over agriculture, limiting irrigation demand.
*   **Growth Drivers:** Strong demand for large diameter pipes for infrastructure projects and consistent demand for solar water pumps.
*   **Global:** Food processing and beverage business demand is picking up globally.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   **Polymer Prices:** Volatile due to West Asian conflict; management does not anticipate margin impact when prices decline as pricing is based on average inventory costs.
*   **Margin Targets:** Medium-term targets are 18-19% for Hi-Tech MI and ~12% for the Plastics segment.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **Tomato JV:** Expected Rs 30-40 crore capex in March quarter (subject to asset acquisition closing).
*   **Beverage Business:** Phase 2 capex of ~Rs 100 crore planned between March and June quarter.
*   **Biochar:** Growth capex planned for next fiscal year.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   **Receivables:** Total ~Rs 1,860 crore (Rs 800 crore project-linked, Rs 400 crore MIP-linked).
*   **Interest Savings:** Expected ~Rs 20 crore/year savings in FY28 due to debt reduction.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   **Drought:** "Super El Niño" is significantly impacting India, creating uncertainty in demand.
*   **Liquidity:** Tight liquidity in states like Andhra, Telangana, and Tamil Nadu led to the company letting go of ~Rs 200-250 crore of business in H1.
*   **Geopolitics:** West Asian war impacting energy needs, polymer availability, and costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Q: Why is piping growth lagging?** A: Management noted rural demand is weak; expects 8-10% growth for the full year driven by non-agricultural pipe applications.
*   **Q: How much business was let go due to liquidity?** A: ~Rs 200-250 crore (Rs 100 crore in MI, Rs 150 crore in solar pumps/projects).
*   **Q: What is the status of project business?** A: Active projects reduced to 7-8; expects project business and recovery to hit zero after next September.
*   **Q: Will lower polymer prices hurt margins?** A: Management stated no, as pricing is based on average inventory cost, not spot prices.

### One-line takeaway
Jain Irrigation delivered a profitable quarter with strong cash conversion and working capital improvement, while strategically de-risking the balance sheet despite severe drought headwinds.
