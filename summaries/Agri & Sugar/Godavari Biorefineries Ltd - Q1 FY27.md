# Godavari Biorefineries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-13 · Agri & Sugar*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong performance and margin expansion in bio-based chemicals, offset by near-term pressures from elevated inventory carrying costs, higher raw material prices, and a wider EBITDA loss in the integrated sugar, cogeneration, and ethanol segment.

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** Rs 559.9 crore (up 4.9% YoY); Revenue from operations: Rs 557.9 crore (up 4.6% YoY).
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 2.6 crore (vs Rs 6.5 crore in Q1 FY26); EBITDA margin: 0.5% (vs 1.2% in Q1 FY26).
- **Consolidated Net Loss:** Rs 19.3 crore (vs net loss of Rs 16 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Bio-based Chemicals Revenue:** Rs 168.7 crore (up 19.4% YoY); EBITDA: Rs 19.2 crore (up 53% YoY); EBITDA margin: 11.4% (vs 8.9% in Q1 FY26). Expected Q2 revenue: Rs 190 crore. Post-debottlenecking target: Rs 240 crore per quarter by early FY28.
- **Integrated Sugar, Cogeneration & Ethanol Revenue:** Rs 377.9 crore (vs Rs 382.8 crore in Q1 FY26); EBITDA loss widened to Rs 14.6 crore (vs Rs 4.5 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Distillery Capacity:** Reached 800 KLPD following the successful commissioning of a 200 KLPD grain-based distillery at Sameerwadi.
- **Sugar Inventory:** Closing sugar inventory at quarter-end was approximately 65,000 tonnes (15,000 to 20,000 tonnes under long-term contracts).
- **R&D Outlay:** Estimated Rs 20 crore over the next 2 to 3 years to take the triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) drug candidate through preliminary efficacy trials.
- **Planned Capex:** Rs 25 crore for further debottlenecking in the bio-based specialty chemical business, expected to be completed by early FY28.

### Highlights
- Commissioned a 200 KLPD grain-based distillery at Sameerwadi, bringing total distillery capacity to 800 KLPD.
- Bio-based chemicals segment benefited from prior debottlenecking, showing strong growth in sales and profitability.
- Filed an application with the CDSCO in July for preliminary efficacy trials for a novel triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) molecule, with trials expected to commence in Q3 FY27 subject to approvals, following a secured Japanese patent and safety trials.
- Secured an Indian patent for a cost-effective process to manufacture branched alcohols.
- Signed an MoU with Synthomer for bio-butanol as a chemical intermediate.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Q2 FY27 bio-based chemical revenue to be around Rs 190 crore.
- Full market penetration post-debottlenecking is projected to yield around Rs 240 crore per quarter by early FY28.
- Management plans to evaluate adding a 160 KLPD fungible capacity (front-end maize preparation) within the existing 800 KLPD capacity to optimize between sugar and ethanol.
- Overall debt levels are projected to remain similar or see only slight changes, with no major changes to the debt-equity structure.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global supply tightness and rising fossil/oil prices are increasing preference for captive, bio-based production and supply chain reliability.
- In India, the rollout of E20, draft guidelines for E85 and E100, and the launch of flex-fuel engines by auto companies support long-term demand for renewable fuels.
- Grain/maize played a dominant part in recent ethanol tenders.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Profitability was impacted by elevated inventory carrying costs and higher raw material prices.
- Bio-based chemicals margin expanded to 11.4% from 8.9% due to a favorable product mix and debottlenecking benefits.
- Integrated segment faced pressure from challenging feedstock conditions and higher manufacturing costs, partially offset by improved sugar realizations.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Invested in a 200 KLPD grain distillery (Sameerwadi), taking total capacity to 800 KLPD.
- Announced an additional Rs 25 crore capex for debottlenecking bio-based specialty chemicals, targeting completion by early FY28.
- Evaluating a 160 KLPD maize preparation facility to utilize idle sugarcane fermentation and distillation capacity if sugar diversion increases.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt levels expected to remain stable with no major changes to the overall debt-equity structure.
- Operating cash flows in the chemical sector are improving on better gross margins.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Monitoring climate effects, specifically El Niño, rainfall, and dam water levels in Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- Geopolitical volatility and raw/energy market fluctuations.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Santosh Shetty (LGC Capital) asked about the DME and bio-butanol commercialization timeline:** Management stated DME pilot trials are ongoing, with final results expected by March 2027; bio-butanol is being explored for chemical intermediates (via Synthomer MoU) and green fuel transition pathways.
- **Dhananjai Bagrodia (Individual Investor) asked about TNBC drug market potential and funding:** Management noted it is a very large global market given the difficult prognosis; funding (estimated at Rs 20 crore over 2-3 years) will be met internally without capital raising, and out-licensing is planned post-efficacy success.
- **Soumya (Analyst) asked about sugar vs. ethanol prioritization and feedstock flexibility:** Management explained they monitor sugar prices and ethanol juice realizations closely; if sugar diversion increases, the 160 KLPD maize front-end preparation addition will utilize freed-up sugarcane fermentation/distillation capacity.
- **Soham (Analyst) asked about closing sugar inventory and monetization:** Management stated closing inventory was 65,000 tonnes (15,000 to 20,000 tonnes in long-term contracts) and noted rising sugar prices will improve sector economics.
- **Nimish Verma (Analyst) asked about leverage and finance costs:** Management confirmed debt levels will be kept similar or with minor changes, avoiding major shifts in the debt-equity structure.

### One-line takeaway
Godavari Biorefineries delivered a mixed Q1 FY27 marked by robust growth and margin expansion in bio-based chemicals offset by inventory and feedstock pressures in sugar/ethanol, while expanding capacity to 800 KLPD and advancing pharmaceutical R&D.
