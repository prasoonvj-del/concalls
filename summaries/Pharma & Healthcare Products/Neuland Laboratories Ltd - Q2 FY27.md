# Neuland Laboratories Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Pharma & Healthcare Products*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Financial performance was below recent trends due to lumpiness in commercial shipments and cost pressures, though growth expectations remain intact for the year.

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** Rs. 457.2 crore (vs. Rs. 516.1 crore in Q2 FY26); H1 FY27 total income stood at Rs. 1,107.2 crore (vs. Rs. 816.7 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Gross Margin:** 59.4% (vs. 60.1% in Q2 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs. 104.6 crore (vs. Rs. 156.9 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 22.9% (vs. 30.4% in Q2 FY26).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs. 51.1 crore (vs. Rs. 96.5 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **EPS:** Rs. 39.85 per share for the quarter.
- **Working Capital:** 104 days of sale (vs. 84 days in the previous quarter).
- **Capex Outflow (H1 FY27):** Rs. 261 crore.
- **Free Cash Flow (H1 FY27):** Rs. 99 crore.
- **Net Debt:** Negative Rs. 208 crore.
- **Peptide Module 1 Capex:** Approximately Rs. 250 crore.
- **Land Acquisition:** 134 acres acquired at Kakinada, Andhra Pradesh.

### Highlights
- Revenues were predominantly driven by commercial molecules in Custom Manufacturing Solutions (CMS) and the Prime portfolio within General Drug Substances (GDS).
- No new product launches occurred in the last two to three quarters; previous launch was about a year ago.
- Commissioned the new peptide block at Unit One, showing encouraging traction.
- Filed 2 DMFs during the period: Fezolinetant and Ivacaftor.
- Acquired approximately 134 acres of land at Kakinada for future long-term capacity expansion; board also approved expanding Unit I land footprint.
- New process R&D center at Genome Valley, Hyderabad expected to be fully operational by early next year.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management stated that expectations of growth remain intact for H2 FY27 as well as the following financial year (FY28).
- Long-term capex guidance over three years remains around Rs. 1,700 crore to Rs. 1,800 crore, subject to periodic reviews including Kakinada scaling.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Request for Proposal (RFP) activity in FY27 has been the strongest to date, driven by long-term customer relationships and big pharma engagements.
- Global synthetic peptide pipeline features around 1,000 peptides between IND and commercial stages.
- Management noted that while global peptide demand is rising (spurred by GLP-1s), India lacks an overcapacity issue in peptides, unlike China, though peptide talent for CDMO remains a challenge.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Experienced increases in input costs during H1 FY27 due to geopolitical conflicts causing volatility in raw material prices and escalation in freight and logistics costs.
- Staff costs moved up during Q2, driven by peptide unit additions and R&D/manufacturing headcount.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Peptide Module 1 is commissioned; Module 2, 3, and 4 configurations will be finalized over the next 3 to 6 months based on project visibility.
- Kakinada land acquisition (134 acres, with potential to reach 200 acres) is intended for long-term return and flexibility rather than immediate-term returns.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt stands at negative Rs. 208 crore (net cash position).
- Working capital increased to 104 days of sales due to business scale and composition changes.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Quarterly lumpiness: Reliance on a handful of commercial CMS molecules creates uneven quarterly shipments and unpredictable revenue timing.
- Intrinsic development risks: Molecule progression can shift, and customer timelines can move.
- Supply chain and cost pressures: Ongoing monitoring required for raw materials, freight, and supply chain volatility.
- Technical execution risks in peptide manufacturing: Downstream mistakes in peptide synthesis can result in complete batch rejections due to irreversibility.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sajal Kapoor (Antifragile Thinking) on peptide idle cost and customer concentration:** Management clarified that idle time for the peptide facility (expected over the next 1-1.5 years) will be borne by Neuland unless built for a specific project. If a negative impact occurred in CMS, peptides would continue, but Kakinada expansion could be slowed down.
2. **Chirag Shah (White Pine) on peptide revenue timing and CMS lumpiness:** Management stated it is 6 to 9 months away from seeing PPQ-related peptide revenues and subsequent MSA transitions. On CMS, lumpiness is due to the lack of recent launches and non-even commercial shipment schedules among a handful of key molecules.
3. **Karan Vora (Goldman Sachs) on peptide technical configuration and patent cliffs:** Management explained that Module 1 equipment (columns, lyophilizers) is sized specifically for current project needs rather than oversized batches. For key CMS molecules, no patent expiries are seen before 2030 in meaningful markets.
4. **Gourav Bhama (JM Financial) on specialty segment drop:** Management attributed specialty segment dips to customer manufacturing difficulties, expecting normalization by Q4.
5. **Prateek Shrivastava (Nivesh Wisdom) on AI impact on drug discovery:** Management views AI as a potential tailwind for discovery, but sees no direct threat to Neuland's IND-onward development and GMP manufacturing model.

### One-line takeaway
Neuland Laboratories reported a softer Q2 due to commercial shipment lumpiness and input cost pressures, but management remains confident in long-term growth driven by robust RFP activity, upcoming peptide commercialization, and expanding CDMO capabilities.
