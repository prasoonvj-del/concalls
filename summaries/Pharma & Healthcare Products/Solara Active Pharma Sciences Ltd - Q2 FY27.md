# Solara Active Pharma Sciences Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-23 · Pharma & Healthcare Products*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Best quarter in about five years across all profitability metrics (gross margin, EBITDA, and PAT), driven by strong base business growth and better-than-expected ibuprofen performance.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 418 crore, up 33% YoY.
- **Base Business Revenue:** Rs 327 crore, up 37% YoY.
- **Ibuprofen Revenue:** Rs 91 crore, up 21% YoY and 18% QoQ.
- **EBITDA:** Rs 77 crore, up 118% YoY.
- **Base Business EBITDA:** Rs 77 crore, up 68% YoY (margin up 440 bps YoY to 23.6%).
- **Consolidated EBITDA Margin:** 18.4%, up 720 bps YoY and up ~200 bps QoQ.
- **PAT:** Rs 28.5 crore (compared to a loss of ~Rs 10 crore YoY).
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** ~Rs 140 crore, up 51% (margin 17.5% vs 15% YoY).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** ~Rs 45 crore (vs breakeven YoY).
- **Net Debt:** Reduced to ~Rs 500 crore from Rs 614 crore as of March '26 (Rs 100 crore from rights issue, Rs 14 crore from operational cash flows). 
- **Net Debt to EBITDA:** Improved to ~1.8x from 3.2x last year.
- **Gross Margin:** Consolidated gross margin at 47.2%; Base business gross margin at 50%+.
- **Depreciation (Q2):** Rs 266 million.
- **Net Finance Cost:** Dropped by ~Rs 32 million (~16% reduction QoQ).
- **Exceptional Loss:** Rs 45 million from selling unutilized/idle equipment at the Vizag facility.

### Highlights
- Regulated markets contributed ~78% of revenues.
- Filed the second DMF of the financial year (target is 5 DMF filings in FY27).
- Mangalore facility successfully completed an ANVISA audit in August (Cuddalore facility completed ANVISA audit in May).
- CRISIL upgraded Solara's ESG rating from adequate to strong.
- Trade receivables increased due to a ~50% YoY revenue jump, lack of billing linearity (most billing in the last 7-10 days), and container/shipping availability delays extending billing-to-shipment SLAs from 2-3 days to 5-7+ days. Management stated payment terms have not been adversely extended.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to close the strategic review of the Ibuprofen business in 12 weeks and will provide an update then.
- Management aims to complete 3 more DMF filings before the end of FY27 (totaling 5 for the year).
- Operating costs expected to remain flattish (plus/minus 2-3% from current levels).
- Capex commitment expected around Rs 40-50 crore yearly (~Rs 10-15 crore per quarter).
- Net debt to EBITDA target to remain in the ~1.8x range over the medium term.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Base business continues to lead growth; general health of the ibuprofen business has improved, though overall ibuprofen environment remains commodity-priced.
- Ibuprofen performance supported by better price realizations and higher volumes driven by market dynamics (West Asia crisis, solvent price rises, raw material availability issues, and competitor exits).
- Solara remains therapeutic area agnostic; product selection is driven by chemistry fit, regulatory complexity, and customer demand. Anti-infectives, cardiovascular, neurology, nephrology, anesthesia, and CNS account for ~70% of offerings.
- KSM procurement: ~60-70% from India, ~20-25% from China, and balance from Europe.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating costs sustained around Rs 120-odd crores, remaining flat over previous quarters.
- Gross margin impacted by ~200 bps YoY due to incremental raw material and input costs driven by the West Asia crisis. 
- Price increases/pass-throughs contributed ~Rs 25-30 crore to total revenue (Base business: ~Rs 20-25 crore; Ibuprofen: ~Rs 10-12 crore); rest is volume-driven. Management cannot fully pass on cost increases to every customer/product yet.
- Inventory increased by ~Rs 20 crore in Q2 due to strategic stocking for raw material security.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Vizag facility remains mothballed; idle ibuprofen-related equipment was monetized/sold as an exceptional item.
- Incremental capex and debottlenecking programs are focused on high-margin products.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt reduced by ~Rs 114 crore during H1 to ~Rs 500 crore. Sequential net debt moved up by ~Rs 20 crore vs June '26 due to working capital investments and maintenance capex.
- Strides accounts for an average of ~25% of the business annually (can fluctuate to 30-35% in quarters based on production campaign mix).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- West Asia crisis continuing, causing pricing pressures, solvent price rises, and raw material availability challenges leading to intermittent production stoppages.
- Ibuprofen market remains challenging and commodity-driven; sustainability of ibuprofen breakeven/profitability in Q3 depends on unpredictable macroeconomic factors.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sajal Kapoor (Antifragile Thinking) on Ibuprofen Review & Vizag:** Management stated options for ibuprofen remain repurposing into a multiproduct/intermediate facility or finding a buyer, and asked for 12 weeks of patience. The Rs 45 million Vizag loss is a one-off monetization of idle ibuprofen equipment.
2. **Kushal Goenka (Mangal Keshav) on Related Party & Receivables:** Management clarified Strides contributes ~25% annually, but campaign-based billing can shift quarterly concentrations; higher receivables are due to overall revenue growth, lack of billing linearity, and shipping delays, not extended credit terms.
3. **Ameya Deosethali (Value Equity) on DMF/R&D:** Management confirmed targeting 5 DMF filings in FY27 (2 done in H1, 3 planned for H2), marking a major turnaround from zero filings in the prior three years.
4. **Maulik Varia (36ONE Capital) on Gross & EBITDA Margins:** Management stated comfortable sustainable base business gross margins are 52-55% (reported ~50.5%, impacted by unrecovered raw material costs), and incremental gross margin expansion will flow directly into EBITDA given flat opex.
5. **Pranav Chawla (JM Financial AMC) on Value vs. Volume Growth:** Management broke down that out of Rs 418 crore revenue, ~Rs 25-30 crore is price-driven (pass-throughs) and the rest is volume expansion. Procurement is 60-70% India, 20-25% China, balance Europe.

### One-line takeaway
Solara delivered its best quarter in five years with strong 33% YoY revenue growth and a 118% surge in EBITDA, driven by base business momentum and temporary tailwinds in ibuprofen, while strengthening its balance sheet and advancing its R&D/DMF pipeline.
