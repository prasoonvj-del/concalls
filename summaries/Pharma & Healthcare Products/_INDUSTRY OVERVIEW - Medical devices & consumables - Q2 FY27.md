# Pharma & Healthcare Products - Medical devices & consumables: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the medical devices and consumables sector reflected robust top-line momentum, with most companies posting double-digit revenue growth (Amanta Healthcare up 17% YoY, Poly Medicure consolidated up 31% YoY, SMT up 24.7% YoY, and Tarsons standalone up 25% YoY) alongside record-setting quarterly revenues for Laxmi Dental. Growth was primarily propelled by strong domestic healthcare demand and a rebound in international exports. However, managements across the board highlighted input cost pressures stemming from West Asia geopolitical tensions, crude oil volatility affecting polymer prices, and logistical/container bottlenecks.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus:** Managements universally reported healthy and resilient domestic demand. Poly Medicure noted domestic revenue growth of 22% in Q2, Laxmi Dental saw broad-based expansion across dental labs and aligners (with Tier 2/3 cities driving aligner adoption), Amanta highlighted strong high-value therapy demand for SteriPort, and Tarsons observed robust private sector demand from CROs, CDMOs, and pharma players.
* **Divergence:** International market performance varied significantly. Poly Medicure's European organic business grew nearly 40% in Q2, whereas its ROW market declined 7% due to West Asia conflicts and freight hurdles. Conversely, Tarsons witnessed a 37% surge in international exports driven by restocking and wallet-share gains, while Laxmi Dental reported a 32% jump in international labs. SMT noted steady European growth (23%) and fast ROW growth (37%). Domestically, Tarsons highlighted a notable divergence, noting that while private demand is strong, government and semi-government research institute demand has yet to recover.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Agreements:** Crude oil and polymer price volatility—exacerbated by West Asia conflicts—put upward pressure on raw materials across players like Amanta (which absorbed 70%+ volatility in polymer prices), Poly Medicure (which faced potential 200–300 bps headwinds), and Tarsons (where raw material prices remained 25% to 35% higher than pre-crisis levels). Companies utilized calibrated price hikes (4–5% at Amanta and Poly Medicure) and inventory management to mitigate the impact.
* **Disagreements/Varying Performance:** Gross and EBITDA margin trajectories diverged based on product mix. Laxmi Dental expanded gross margins to 76.9% due to a favorable product mix, and Poly Medicure raised its standalone EBITDA margin guidance to 26%-27.5%. In contrast, SMT's gross margins declined by ~170 bps YoY to 74.3% due to a mix shift toward traded products, and Tarsons/Nerbe experienced compressed EBITDA margins due to overhead costs from newly established facilities awaiting revenue scale-up.

## Capex & capacity
* **Amanta Healthcare:** Commissioned SteriPort Line 3 in September 2026 (increasing capacity by ~75%) and is planning an SVP capacity expansion (+52%) for Q1 FY28.
* **Laxmi Dental:** Deployed INR 16.7 crore in H1 FY27 capex and plans to utilize INR 48.1 crore of unutilized IPO proceeds across FY27 and FY28 for land, construction, and machinery.
* **Poly Medicure:** Incurred H1 capex of INR 124 crore, maintaining its full-year guidance of INR 200 crore to INR 225 crore.
* **Sahajanand Medical Technologies (SMT):** Incurred INR 44 crore in H1 capex and guided for an annual capex of INR 70 crore for FY27 and FY28, focused on vertical integration and automation.
* **Tarsons Products:** Restricted annual capex to INR 30 crore to INR 40 crore as its Panchla and Amta facilities near completion, with cell culture lines commissioning pushed to H2.

## What changed since last quarter
* **Tarsons Products:** Management's tone on demand momentum and enquiry levels shifted slightly more positive; however, container availability and transportation logistics emerged as major new export bottlenecks. Commissioning of cell culture lines slipped from Q2 into H2.
* **Poly Medicure:** Reaffirmed full-year consolidated revenue guidance (INR 2,300-2,400 crore) and raised standalone EBITDA margin guidance from 25%-27% to 26%-27.5%.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Historically, the Indian medical devices and consumables sector relied on contract manufacturing of low-cost commoditized products and traditional dealer-driven distribution models. Over the past five years, the model has transformed toward direct-to-clinician digital platforms, specialized high-margin therapies (structural heart, cardiology), and global inorganic expansions [Background].

**Shifts underway**
* Digital adoption and direct-to-clinician distribution models in dental solutions (Stage: Scaling). Laxmi Dental reached ~78% digital penetration in its domestic lab business and introduced a scanner subscription model [Laxmi Dental concall Q2 FY27].
* Transition into high-margin advanced therapeutic segments (structural heart, cardiology, orthopedics) via global regulatory approvals and international footprint expansion (Stage: Scaling). SMT secured EU MDR certification for Supraflex Cruz and expanded structural heart sales (~40% growth), while Poly Medicure established a direct U.K. presence and optimized its Brazil subsidiary [Poly Medicure concall Q2 FY27; SMT concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push**
* India extended the medical devices PLI scheme until FY36 with a INR 2,700 crore outlay, rewarding domestic manufacturing and advanced device capabilities [Govt: India extends medical devices PLI scheme until FY36 with 2,700 crore outlay, Nov 2026].
* DGHS issued a circular mandating single-use medical devices (dialyzers), structurally boosting domestic demand for single-use consumables [Govt: DGHS circular mandating single-use medical devices, Oct 2026].
