# Manorama Industries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-18 · Pharma & Healthcare Products*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered strong 39.5% YoY revenue growth, crossing the INR 4,000 million milestone in quarterly revenue and EBITDA, supported by value-added specialty offerings.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: INR 404 crore (up 39.5% YoY)
- EBITDA: INR 106 crore (up 42.2% YoY)
- EBITDA Margin: 26.3% (expanded by 49 bps YoY)
- Profit After Tax (PAT): INR 79 crore (up 67.6% YoY)
- PAT Margin: 19.5% (expanded by 326 bps YoY)
- Export to domestic revenue mix: 60:40
- Capacity utilization: ~80% for the quarter
- Debottlenecking capacity addition: 4,500 tonnes (taking capacity to ~52,000 tonnes per annum)
- Capex guidance for FY27: INR 225 crore to INR 250 crore (out of INR 460 crore proposed capex plan)
- Other income: INR 16 crore (includes INR 13 crore forex gain and INR 3 crore FDR income)

### Highlights
- Crossed the INR 4,000 million quarterly revenue and EBITDA milestone for the first time.
- Incorporated a wholly owned subsidiary, Manorama Savannah Agro Chad Sarl, in Chad to expand West African sourcing footprint.
- Acquired ~10 hectares (24 acres) of land in Burkina Faso for a shea nut and mango nut processing facility (regulatory approvals in progress).
- Completed a Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP) raising INR 500 crore to strengthen the balance sheet.
- Trial production started at the Brazil partnership facility.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects full-year capacity utilization to be around 80% to 85%.
- Proposed capex of INR 460 crore (including solvent fractionation, refinery in India, and Burkina Faso extraction facility) targeted for commissioning around Q3 FY28.
- Full top-line impact of the new capex expected to be more visible in FY29.
- Management expects margins to hold broadly stable in the current range.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Sustained demand across end-use industries (confectionery, chocolate, cosmetics).
- Nigeria's temporary export ban on shea nuts does not materially impact sourcing due to the company's vast multi-country presence across Africa (operating in 10 different regions).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Employee cost run rate expected to normalize to INR 14 crore to INR 15 crore per quarter.
- Other expenses increased primarily due to higher freight and container costs.
- Raw material procurement largely completed in the previous quarter for domestic seeds; pricing largely stable.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Debottlenecking of fractionation capacity: Portion of 7,500 tonnes operational; balance 4,500 tonnes expected around Q3 FY27 (total capacity moving to ~52,000 tonnes).
- Burkina Faso facility capex: ~INR 120 crore to INR 130 crore, with an expected payback period of 3 years once operational.
- Total FY27 capex planned at INR 225 crore to INR 250 crore.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- QIP raised INR 500 crore.
- Company holds ~INR 150 crore in fixed deposits (FDRs) excluding the QIP amount.
- Working capital requirements are supported by existing banker limits (Lead banker: State Bank of India).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions and macroeconomic factors may cause indirect impacts on freight, logistics, and supply chain.
- Business involves normal operational risks, seasonal dynamics, and foreign exchange fluctuations.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kumar Saumya (AMBIT Capital) asked about debottlenecking timelines.** Management stated the balance capacity addition of 4,500 tonnes is expected around Q3 FY27.
2. **Rishabh (Demeter Advisors) asked about the Burkina Faso facility payback and impact of Nigeria's shea ban.** Management stated a 3-year payback on the ~INR 120 crore capex and clarified that Nigeria's ban doesn't impact them due to multi-country African operations.
3. **Roshan Nair asked about the ramp-up of the Brazil partnership.** Management noted trial production started last quarter and commercial volumes will ramp up gradually over 2 to 4 quarters.
4. **Akhil asked about subsidiary losses and other income normalization.** Management explained that West African entities are cost-sourcing vehicles without standalone revenue, drag will reduce over time, and other income was driven by INR 13 crore forex gains.
5. **Utkarsh Chanana asked about shipping route dependency and supplier liability recovery.** Management stated they use multiple geographies/routes globally and are claiming the full amount via a debit note from the supplier while maintaining a diversified supplier base.

### One-line takeaway
Manorama Industries delivered a robust Q1 FY27 with 39.5% YoY revenue growth and 67.6% PAT growth, while advancing strategic backward/forward integration projects in Africa and India.
