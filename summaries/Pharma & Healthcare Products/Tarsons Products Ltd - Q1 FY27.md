# Tarsons Products Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-17 · Pharma & Healthcare Products*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Margins are under pressure due to higher raw material and operational costs, though demand and export volumes are recovering.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated revenue: Rs. 110.2 crore (growing by 20.7% YoY) / Rs. 110 crore (reported as "almost 21% Y-o-Y").
- Standalone revenue: Rs. 86.1 crore (growing 21% YoY, highest ever Q1 standalone revenue) / Rs. 86 crore.
- Domestic sales growth (standalone): 17% YoY.
- Export business growth: 29% YoY.
- Standalone gross margin: 67.1%.
- Consolidated EBITDA: Rs. 26 crore.
- Consolidated EBITDA margin: 23.6%.
- Standalone EBITDA: Rs. 24.2 crore.
- Consolidated cash profit: Rs. 25.6 crore (growing ~18% YoY).
- Standalone cash profit: Rs. 25.2 crore (growing 18% YoY).
- Standalone depreciation (full year estimate): Rs. 105 crore to Rs. 110 crore (includes Rs. 14 crore from Panchla and Rs. 4 crore from Amta).
- Standalone depreciation (current): Rs. 24.5 crore.
- Capital work in progress (CWIP): Rs. 160 crore.
- Capital advances: Rs. 20 crore.
- Gross debt: ~Rs. 380 crore.
- Net debt: ~Rs. 330 crore to Rs. 340 crore.
- Annual loan repayment target: ~Rs. 70 crore (net debt reduction target of Rs. 40 crore to Rs. 50 crore for the year).
- Annual interest run rate: ~Rs. 20 crore.
- Planned contribution from Panchla and Amta in FY28: 20% to 25% of standalone revenues.
- Expected revenue from new product portfolio next year (FY28): Rs. 65 crore to Rs. 70 crore.

### Highlights
- Growth in Q1 FY27 was driven predominantly by the existing product portfolio, with negligible contributions from incremental capacities and newly introduced product categories.
- Exports rebounded after previous disruptions caused by West Asia geopolitical tensions and U.S. tariff uncertainties.
- German subsidiary (Nerbe) grew ~6% YoY in constant currency terms.
- Panchla and Amta capacity expansion is in its final phase; remaining facilities are expected to be fully commissioned during Q2, with revenue contributions beginning in H2.
- Management noted that fixed costs and depreciation from new CAPEX have peaked, which will be absorbed as utilization ramps up.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects profitability to remain relatively moderate in FY27 as new CAPEX costs are absorbed.
- Management expects long-term sustainable annual growth of at least 15% or more going forward, backed by larger facilities and expanded product lines.
- Panchla and Amta facilities are expected to contribute 20% to 25% of standalone revenues in FY28.
- New product portfolio (including cell culture) is expected to bring in Rs. 65 crore to Rs. 70 crore in FY28.
- Net debt is targeted to reduce by Rs. 40 crore to Rs. 50 crore by the end of the year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic demand is showing a meaningful revival and improvement, with increasing customer inquiries and a return toward traditional late single-digit pre-COVID growth rates.
- Government focus on biopharma via the "Biopharma Shakti scheme" provides positive tailwinds.
- International competition from Chinese players remains intense in Europe and the U.S.
- The life sciences industry has a clearly demarcated usage for glass and plastics without significant cannibalization.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Key input costs (polymers) spiked by 25% to 50%, impacting gross and EBITDA margins.
- Margins were further impacted by operating costs and depreciation associated with newly commissioned facilities.
- Partial price increases were implemented in the domestic market with a one-quarter lag benefit; price hikes in international markets were close to zero due to competitive pressures and currency depreciation benefits.
- Input costs showed extreme volatility, peaking around 6 weeks prior to the call, dipping briefly, and moving up sharply over the preceding two weeks.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- The company is concluding its largest-ever multi-year CAPEX program.
- Remaining select lines at Panchla are undergoing trial runs and will be fully commissioned in Q2, with commercial revenue contribution starting in H2.
- No major new CAPEX is planned for this year apart from maintenance CAPEX (expected around Rs. 20 crore, not exceeding INR 25 crore).
- Total peak asset turnover on a fixed asset basis is expected to be ~0.8x, translating to incremental revenue of ~Rs. 400 crore (total peak potential of Rs. 750 crore to Rs. 800 crore).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gross debt stands at ~Rs. 380 crore; net debt is ~Rs. 330 crore to Rs. 340 crore.
- Loan repayments for the year are slated at ~Rs. 70 crore, targeting a net debt reduction of Rs. 40 crore to Rs. 50 crore.
- CWIP stands at INR 160 crore and capital advances at INR 20 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in raw material (polymer) prices and inability to fully pass on cost increases internationally.
- Geopolitical tensions, vessel/shipping availability constraints in West Asia, and looming threats of U.S. tariffs.
- Slower adoption timelines for complex production-grade lines (like cell culture and bioprocess containers) due to existing customer SOPs and incumbent switching costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Jasdeep Walia (Clockvine Capital) asked about the delay in Panchla's commercial commissioning.** Management explained that the delay is due to the sheer volume and scale of machines and lines requiring external engineering teams, pushing remaining lines to H2.
2. **Aditya (Securities Investment Management) asked about raw material trends and gross margin bottoms.** Management stated that RM prices remain highly volatile, making it difficult to call a bottom, and margins in upcoming quarters will likely look similar to Q1 unless external input costs change drastically.
3. **Rahul Jain (Credence Wealth) asked about peak sales potential from the total CAPEX.** Management stated that total peak revenue potential from the four-year CAPEX cycle is around Rs. 750 crore to Rs. 800 crore at ~0.8x fixed asset turnover.
4. **Kiran (TableTree Capitals) asked what is required to exceed the 15% growth rate.** Management responded that 15% is baseline sustainable growth, and hitting 20% to 22% depends on the successful launch of new products (like cell culture) and supportive international market conditions.
5. **Nishitha (Sapphire Capital) asked about expected revenue contribution from Panchla and Amta in FY28.** Management guided that these facilities should contribute 20% to 25% of standalone revenues in FY28.

### One-line takeaway
Tarsons reported a healthy 21% YoY revenue growth in Q1 FY27 driven by core portfolio strength and export recovery, but profitability faced headwinds from a 25–50% surge in raw material prices and higher depreciation/interest from its concluding major CAPEX cycle.
