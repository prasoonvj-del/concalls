# Techknowgreen Solutions Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-23 · Power & Utilities*

**Quarter:** H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted robust revenue and profit growth, an expanding order book, and strong traction in key verticals, while addressing working capital and cash flow concerns as temporary transition impacts.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations (Consolidated):** Rs 24.60 crore, up 46.11% YoY (from Rs 16.80 crore in H1 FY26). Operating revenues stood at Rs 24.56 crore.
- **Profit Before Tax (Consolidated):** Rs 6.36 crore, up 63.61% YoY.
- **Profit After Tax (Consolidated):** Rs 4.36 crore, up 45.48% YoY. (Standalone PAT was Rs 4.30 crore).
- **Earnings Per Share (EPS):** Rs 5.90, compared to Rs 4.06 in H1 FY26.
- **PBT Margin (Consolidated):** 25.90%, compared to 23.13% in H1 FY26.
- **PAT Margin (Consolidated):** 17.70%, compared to 17.79% in H1 FY26.
- **Segment Revenue:** Knowledge Consultancy at Rs 15.66 crore (up 24.48% YoY from Rs 12.58 crore); Technology at Rs 8.79 crore (compared to Rs 2.75 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Confirmed Order Book:** Rs 46 crore in hand (with Rs 24.56 crore already billed).
- **Net Operating Cash Flow:** Negative Rs 2.98 crore for H1 FY27 (compared to negative Rs 2.04 crore in H1 FY26).
- **Trade Receivables:** Standalone trade receivables at Rs 22.86 crore (including Rs 6.23 crore of unbilled revenue; Rs 6.43 crore realized in October alone).
- **Legacy Outstanding:** Rs 4 crore under NCLT and judicial proceedings.
- **EMD Expenses:** Rs 1.6 crore invested in earnest money deposits for government tenders.
- **Dividend:** Interim dividend of 50% per equity share declared on November 3, 2026.

### Highlights
- Added approximately 35 new customers during the half-year.
- Approximately 90% of active work orders are valued below Rs 1 crore, providing steady, diversified execution.
- Closed a corporate loan of Rs 3 crore using internal cash in hand.
- Received NABL approval for the TRL9 environmental testing and climate technology research center.
- Introduced 'Sushti', a coin-based tokenization system for client incentives on sustainability efforts.
- International presence updates: U.S. office completed 5 out of 7 due diligence projects (particularly around LNG pipelines in Texas); Singapore office signed an NDA with A*STAR for R&D and is pursuing a government school project for CEWT technology.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 to be historically stronger than H1, following past trajectories.
- Targeting sustained year-on-year top-line growth in the range of 30% to 40%.
- Aiming to maintain annual PAT margins around the 25% ballpark.
- Target to nullify long-term borrowings by March 2027, leaving only working capital CC limits.
- Confident of turning full-year operating cash flow positive.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Business is driven by three macro pillars: population (people), pollution, and policies.
- National Clean Air Program (NCAP) targets 131 non-attainment cities across India, creating robust demand for 'Yuka Yantra' air pollution control equipment.
- Data centers and infrastructure construction represent major sustained client sectors, with data center environment clearances typically ranging from Rs 70 lakh to Rs 2 crore.
- Expanding into marine and coastal areas, including a new branch in Alang for ship-breaking ESG services and port/harbor clearances.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Short-term borrowings (cash credit limit) carry an interest rate of 9%.
- Cash flow is constrained by credit periods (typically 60 to 90 days), upfront project investments (30% to 33% unbilled revenue before milestone billing), and the need for bank guarantees (10% of project values) and EMDs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Regional offices are active in Assam, Gujarat (Vadodara), and Karnataka, while also working via associates in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Haryana, and Punjab.
- Assam operations secured consulting revenue of Rs 60 lakh and are targeting the 7 sister states (including Arunachal Pradesh and Tripura).
- Gujarat operations feature a major Rs 10 crore order over three years with Deendayal Port Authority (including AMC).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Long-term borrowings decreased from Rs 5.6 crore to Rs 4.5 crore following the closure of a Rs 3 crore corporate loan.
- Short-term borrowings (CC limits) increased from Rs 3 crore to Rs 8.5 crore to fund EMDs, bank guarantees, and unbilled project stages.
- Legacy dues of Rs 4 crore include Rs 3 crore stuck with the Delhi Government (confirmed by the PMO office, awaiting fund reallocation) and NCLT/MSME proceedings against entities like Premier Limited and Ambey Valley; management sees no need for provisions as recoverability is assessed as positive.
- Cash flows turned positive in October 2026 following realization of Rs 6.43 crore.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High customer credit periods (60 to 90 days) and upfront working capital requirements lead to temporary working capital crunches and negative interim operating cash flows.
- Legacy receivables of Rs 4 crore are stuck in judicial, NCLT, or government budgetary extension processes.
- Long sales and due diligence cycles for government tenders delay revenue realization despite order wins.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Dalpat Mehta (Cyclicality, Cash Flows, and Valuations):** Asked about business seasonality and negative cash flows despite profits. Management replied that H2 is historically much stronger due to project billing cycles, and negative H1 cash flow is a temporary transition driven by CC utilization for bank guarantees and unbilled revenue; cash flows turned positive in October.
2. **Lakshman Easwaran (Borrowing Mix and Regional Traction):** Inquired about the shift from long-term to short-term debt and regional office contributions. Management clarified that the Rs 3 crore corporate loan was paid off, short-term debt represents CC limits for project funding, and traction is improving in Assam, Gujarat (Deendayal Port), and Karnataka.
3. **Paras Chedda (Margins, Receivables, and Growth Drivers):** Questioned receivable aging, debt targets by March 2027, and growth drivers. Management stated that weighted average receivable days are 60–90 days (excluding legacy), long-term debt is targeted for a 70–80% reduction by March, and primary growth drivers are NCAP policies, urban infrastructure (STP audits), and data center clearances.
4. **Darshadeep Singh (Tenders and Government Orders):** Asked about specific large tenders like DPCC. Management clarified that the DPCC project is a pilot revenue-sharing model rather than a direct equipment sale, and trials are ongoing across multiple state governments.

### One-line takeaway
Techknowgreen reported robust H1 FY27 revenue growth of 46.11% and PBT growth of 63.61%, driven by its consulting and technology segments, while management expects stronger H2 performance and normalization of working capital pressures.
