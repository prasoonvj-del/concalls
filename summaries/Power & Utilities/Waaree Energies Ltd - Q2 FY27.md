# Waaree Energies Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Power & Utilities*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY '27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong revenue and operating cash flow growth, robust retail momentum, and clear execution of backward/horizontal integration roadmaps.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue:** Rs. 7,517.7 crores (up 23.9% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 Operating EBITDA:** Rs. 1,335.2 crores (margin: 17.8%)
- **Q2 FY27 PAT:** Rs. 753.6 crores
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs. 15,449.5 crores (up 47.3% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 Operating EBITDA:** Rs. 2,775.1 crores (up 15.5% YoY, margin: 18%)
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs. 1,645.5 crores (vs Rs. 1,651.1 crores YoY)
- **H1 FY27 Retail Revenue:** Rs. 5,753 crores (up ~2x YoY, accounting for 37% of H1 revenue; Q2 retail was Rs. 3,464 crores, up 252% YoY)
- **US Domestic Revenue (H1 FY27):** Increased 113% YoY to Rs. 1,300 crores (from Rs. 732 crores)
- **Total Order Book:** Rs. 59,200 crores as of October 26, 2026 (Module order book: 25.6 GW; geographical mix: 48.4% domestic India, 43.8% domestic US, 7.8% exports from India)
- **Net Cash:** Rs. 3,224 crores (Net debt-to-equity ratio: -0.2x)
- **ROCE:** 28.7%; **ROE:** 24.2%

### Highlights
- Management stated that operating cash flow surpassed 5x of total operating cash generated YoY.
- Sold 1 lakh kits since the kit business started in April 2025.
- Installed module manufacturing capacity stands at 25.8 GW (including 1.6 GW in the USA). US module production reached 0.3 GW with an annualized utilization of 71%.
- Solar cell capacity stands at 5.4 GW in India; 10 GW expansion at the Unn facility is on track for commissioning in FY27 (5 GW in Q3, 5 GW in Q4), raising cell-to-module capacity ratio from ~22% to ~65%.
- BESS plans: 5 GWh containerization/pack facility commissioning in Q3 FY27 and 3.5 GWh battery cell manufacturing in FY27.
- Outlined capex program of ~Rs. 30,000 crores: 35% deployed, 28% under execution, 37% under planning stage.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management reaffirmed FY27 operating EBITDA guidance of Rs. 7,000 to Rs. 7,700 crores.
- Management expects H2 EBITDA to be around 55% to 60% of the full-year guidance.
- Non-solar revenue contribution is projected to rise from 15%-20% to 30%-35% over the next 2-3 years, and potentially ~50% in 4-5 years towards a Rs. 90,000 to Rs. 1 lakh crore top-line ambition.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India installed ~38 GW of solar in CY25 and 35.3 GW in the first 9 months of CY26 (20.8 GW in H1 FY27). USA installed ~43 GW in CY25 and 19.2 GW in H1 CY26.
- Green shoots seen in solar plus battery for data centers, green ammonia, and green hydrogen.
- US Section 232 enactment set a minimum import price (MIP) framework: module price fixed at 38 cents + 15% duty, cell at 22 cents + 15% duty, wafer at 14-15 cents + 3% duty, and polysilicon at $21 + 15% duty.

### Costs, raw materials & margins
- Q2 EBITDA margin was 17.8% (vs 18.2% in Q1). Q1 included Rs. 349 crores in tariff refunds (with an additional Rs. 11.4 crores recorded in Q2, totaling Rs. 360.4 crores).
- US local manufacturing EBITDA margin is estimated at 8-9 cents/W (including ~7 cents of IRA incentives at >70% utilization), compared to 3-4 cents/W for exports from India.
- DCR spot realization: Utility ~19-20 cents, retail ~22-23 cents. Non-DCR spot realization: Utility ~12-12.5 cents, retail ~13.5-14 cents. US local manufactured pricing: ~29-30 cents pre-232, moving to ~46-47 cents post-232 (including local cell content).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- US solar manufacturing capacity to reach 4.8 GW (Brookshire factory adding 3.2 GW, with 1.6 GW operational in Q3 FY27 and trial production for another 1.6 GW by March 27).
- Ingot wafer 10 GW facility at Nagpur progressing towards commercial production by September 2027 (capex: Rs. 5,500 crores).
- Solar glass facility planned near Navsari, Gujarat, with commercialization expected in FY29 (Rs. 3,900 crores total capex).
- Inverter (4 GW), transformer (20,000 MVA capacity targeted), and electrolyzer investments totaling ~Rs. 1,200 crores are progressing.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Maintained net cash-free status with net cash of ~Rs. 3,224 crores.
- Increase in current liabilities driven primarily by customer advances and capital goods advances.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Rationalized/discontinued reporting ~3 GW+ of US export orders due to Section 232 implementation risks without assuming liabilities under these contracts.
- Temporary lower domestic module capacity utilization (45%) in Q2 due to supply chain replanning around the 6-month extension of ALMM II.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sweta Jain (Anand Rathi):** Asked about US order cancellations and drop in domestic module CUF. Management clarified that ~3 GW of export orders were rationalized/discontinued from the reportable order book due to Section 232 MIP transitions, and domestic CUF dipped temporarily due to ALMM II extension supply chain transitions.
2. **Sumit Kishore (Axis Capital):** Inquired about sequential EBITDA margin dip and tariff refund impacts. Management stated that Q2 was a clean underlying margin quarter with minimal refund impact (only Rs. 11.4 crore addition vs Q1's Rs. 349 crore), backed by higher cell/US utilization.
3. **Aritra Banerjee (Nomura):** Asked about margin differences between US exports and local US manufacturing. Management stated local US manufacturing yields ~8-9 cents/W EBITDA (including IRA benefits), more than double export margins.
4. **Sourabh Arya (Oaklane Capital):** Asked about data center strategy and capital allocation. Management explained that data centers require heavy renewable integration (5 GW solar per 1 GW data center), and the Rs. 3,250 crore RE infrastructure commitment caters to this without being incremental to announced capex plans.
5. **Dhruv Muchhal (HDFC AMC):** Questioned US anti-dumping/countervailing duties (ADD/CVD) post-Section 232. Management stated Waaree does not use Indian-made cells for US exports (sourcing non-Chinese, FEOC-compliant cells), thus avoiding India-specific AD/CVD, though subject to the 38 cents MIP.

### One-line takeaway
Waaree Energies delivered a strong H1 FY27 with 47% revenue growth and robust retail momentum, while proactively rationalizing US export orders and advancing major backward-integration capex across cells, ingots, and BESS.
