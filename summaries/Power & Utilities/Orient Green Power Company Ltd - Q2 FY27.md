# Orient Green Power Company Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-18 · Power & Utilities*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlights healthy H1 profit over Rs. 100 crores without extraordinary income, successful solar commissioning, and an upgraded credit rating.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (management expects to do something strategic/significant by the end of the fiscal year; no specific quantitative revenue/EBITDA numbers were guided last quarter or this quarter).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted slightly to acknowledge a surplus of solar capacity in the country and grid absorption issues in the afternoon, making future organic growth more wind-focused.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: New topics included discussing Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) for FY28 and the impact of the US political developments on stock markets; old disputes regarding Andhra Pradesh interest were not mentioned.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: The 17.6 MW solar plant was successfully commissioned in September 26, delivering on the previous quarter's expectation.

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** Rs. 146.57 crore (up 8% YoY) for Q2; Rs. 232.81 crore (up 2% YoY) for H1.
- **EBITDA:** Rs. 116.55 crore (up 12% YoY) for Q2; Rs. 176.56 crore (up 4% YoY) for H1.
- **PAT:** Rs. 81.57 crore for Q2 (vs. Rs. 80.94 crore YoY); Rs. 105.51 crore for H1 (vs. Rs. 109.56 crore YoY, which included a Rs. 16 crore exceptional gain).
- **Total Outstanding Debt:** Approximately Rs. 562.9 crore.
- **Net Debt:** Approximately Rs. 440 crore (accounting for an Rs. 81 crore DSRA and available cash).
- **Weighted Average Cost of Debt:** 9.09% (ranging from 8.2% to 9.25%).
- **PLFs:** Beta subsidiary PLF was 33.11% for Q2 and 26.82% for H1; solar PLF was 25%.
- **Solar Incremental Revenue & EBITDA:** Revenue of ~Rs. 15.35 crore and EBITDA of ~Rs. 14.5 crore from the 17 MW project.
- **Repowering Generation Impact:** Incremental generation of ~1.7 crore units translating to ~Rs. 9 crore of revenue from the 7.8 MW repowering project.
- **Carried Forward Tax Losses:** Approximately Rs. 800 crore.

### Highlights
- Successfully commissioned a 17.6 MW solar plant on September 26, funded by rights issue proceeds, taking total solar capacity to 24.6 MW.
- Main subsidiary (Beta) and the solar subsidiary received credit rating upgrades to BBB.
- 7.8 MW wind repowering project is almost complete, awaiting statutory approvals.
- Entered into an EPC contract for an additional 6.6 MW of new wind capacity, partly funded by tied-up debt.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to execute significant strategic actions or transactions before the end of the fiscal year.
- Planning Phase 2 of repowering for ~30 megawatts (10 MW in one subsidiary, 20 MW in another) once the formal repowering policy is approved in Tamil Nadu.
- Planning a BESS (Battery Energy Storage System) project of 10 to 15 megawatt-hours during FY28.
- Future organic capacity growth will be more wind-focused due to solar generation surpluses and grid absorption issues during afternoons.

### Demand & industry commentary
- C&I (Commercial & Industrial) demand is strong, with over 70% of power sold to industrial customers in Tamil Nadu.
- Industrial customers are keen on 100% renewable power to meet export and ESG obligations.
- Data centers are emerging as a new demand stream looking to go green.
- Collections from state electricity boards have improved significantly across the country.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Additional depreciation from the 17 MW solar plant expected to be around Rs. 3 crore per quarter starting Q3.
- Target to reduce borrowing costs by another 25 bps, depending on RBI rate actions.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Total operational solar capacity stands at 24.6 MW (including 7 MW commissioned in Dec '25 and 17.6 MW in Sep '26).
- Evaluating a second repowering project in advanced stages of planning.
- Holding over 1,000 acres of land, partly non-core or pledged, valued around Rs. 5 lakhs per acre on average.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total debt stands at ~Rs. 562.9 crore, offset by an Rs. 81 crore DSRA and total available cash/reserves of Rs. 110-120 crore.
- Surplus cash flows are being utilized as equity for new capacity and repowering.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Formal approval of the wind repowering policy in Tamil Nadu is still pending due to the reconstitution of the regulatory commission.
- Grid connectivity approvals and churn in government departments in Tamil Nadu have delayed operationalizing ready wind assets.
- Inherent weather volatility (wind patterns and El Niño impacts affecting Q3/Q4 generation).
- Listed company status creates valuation and mark-to-market challenges for private equity partnerships.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Faisal Hawa (H. G. Hawa and Co.):** Asked about PLF, debt, and the 1 GW target. Management stated Beta's H1 PLF was 26.82% (Q2 33.11%), debt is ~Rs. 563 crore at 9.09% weighted average cost, and organic growth to 1 GW is slow, necessitating inorganic actions which stalled due to market conditions but are now reopening. *(Management did not dodge)*
2. **Manoj Bagadia (Equicorp Partners LLC):** Asked if unlisting or promoter buying could help valuation and strategic transactions. Management noted that being listed has mixed advantages/disadvantages (PE mark-to-market issues) and they are working hard on strategic options. *(Management did not dodge)*
3. **Rahul Kumar (Individual Investor):** Asked about reasons for delayed commissioning of Beta and Clarion wind projects. Management explained that delays are entirely due to bureaucratic churn and grid approval slowdowns from the Tamil Nadu government, though construction is 100% complete. *(Management did not dodge)*
4. **Shreya Kumari (Individual Investor):** Asked about further repowering opportunities across the 381.7 MW portfolio. Management stated there are about 75 MW of repowering opportunities currently (including 25 MW in Andhra Pradesh), alongside 40-50 MW ripe for repowering. *(Management did not dodge)*
5. **Vivek Gupta (Research Analyst):** Asked about the status of the Croatia asset. Management stated there is no plan to sell it as it generates and returns cash, manages itself via a local team, and is too small (~5 MW effective) to warrant a formal sale process. *(Management did not dodge)*

### One-line takeaway
While Orient Green Power reported healthy H1 profits and advanced its solar and repowering projects, growth remains tethered to regulatory approvals in Tamil Nadu and the search for strategic inorganic catalysts.
