# PTC India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Power & Utilities*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong volume growth of 24% YoY, but standalone PAT declined 15% YoY and consolidated PAT dropped 28% YoY due to lower net surcharge income and impairment provisions at PFS.

### Key numbers
- **Traded Volume (Standalone/Consolidated Q2 FY27):** 32.5 billion units (up 24% YoY from 26.2 billion units)
- **Traded Volume (H1 FY27):** 58.25 billion units / 58.3 billion units (up 18% YoY from 49.2 billion units)
- **Standalone Operational Income (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 134 crore (down 2% YoY from Rs. 137 crore)
- **Standalone PBT (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 152 crore (down from Rs. 180 crore YoY)
- **Standalone PAT (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 113 crore (down 15% YoY from Rs. 134 crore)
- **Standalone EPS (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 3.83 (vs. Rs. 4.52 YoY)
- **Consolidated PBT (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 171 crore (down 43% YoY from Rs. 298 crore)
- **Consolidated PAT (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 159 crore (down 28% YoY from Rs. 222 crore)
- **Consolidated EPS (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 4.85 (vs. Rs. 6.46 YoY)
- **HPX Revenue (Q2 FY27):** Rs. 18.13 crore (vs. Rs. 9.89 crore YoY)
- **Cash on Hand (as of September 30, 2026):** Rs. 2,431 crore
- **Trading Margins (Q2 FY27):** Short-term: 0.69 paisa/unit; Medium-term: 3.17 paisa/unit; Cross-border: 6.98 paisa/unit; Long-term: 7.98 paisa/unit; Overall: 2.89 paisa/unit.

### Highlights
- Traded volumes grew >18% in H1 FY27, outpacing the 10.64% growth in total national energy generation. Exchange products comprised 60% of traded volume.
- Signed 1,200 MW of solar PPA with NTPC Renewable Energy and floated a 500 MW / 2,000 MWh long-term renewable energy storage procurement tender.
- Cross-border power flows to Bangladesh remain stable with regular payments; signed a 1,200 MW winter power supply contract with Bhutan; commenced import/export with Nepal.
- Management stated that the PFS disinvestment process is on track with a transaction advisor/consultant appointed, though no hard deadlines are set due to regulatory approvals and valuation considerations.
- Planning to enter the mineral exchange ecosystem to capture new revenue streams.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management (MD & CEO) provided no specific volume or financial guidance for Q3 or the full year, citing no dramatic changes in the current market environment.
- Management expects power prices to remain firm during peak demand hours due to growing RE penetration and low storage capacity (<20 GWh), but anticipates policy measures for energy storage over the next 12–18 months to moderate price volatility.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Power market exhibits diurnal price variation from <Rs. 1 to the Rs. 10 regulatory ceiling.
- Middle East crisis has spiked gas prices (making gas-based power cost ~Rs. 30/unit for RTC) and increased raw material prices/lead times for RE projects, causing commissioning slippages.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating income and PAT decline driven by a drop in net surcharge income (from ~Rs. 42 crore to Rs. 26 crore) due to improved DISCOM liquidity and lower outstandings, alongside reduced treasury income following the special dividend payout.
- Establishment expenses increased slightly (~Rs. 2 crore on a low base of Rs. 10 crore) due to staff absorption post-PEL divestment.
- Finance cost includes working capital interest (~Rs. 7 crore for the quarter, Rs. 14 crore for H1) and back-to-back surcharge expenses.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- JV with NCL (NLC India) has held its first Board meeting to formulate a business plan, but near-term execution (1-2 years) will be limited due to transmission connectivity timelines (3-4 years).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash and bank balance stood at Rs. 2,431 crore as of September 30, 2026.
- Receivables and DISCOM outstandings have improved, leading to lower net surcharge collections.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Impairment provision of Rs. 51 crore at PFS during the quarter (compared to a Rs. 57 crore reversal YoY) impacted consolidated earnings.
- Transmission connectivity delays pose bottlenecks for new RE and JV capacity build-outs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Abhinav Goswami (Apollo Tyres) asked about the PFS stake sale timeline and mineral exchange prospects:** Management replied that transaction advisors are appointed and the process is on track, but specific deadlines cannot be given due to regulatory approvals (RBI, SEBI) and the goal of realizing fair value; the mineral exchange is viewed as a first-mover synergy play.
- **Channamallu Halagodi (Individual Investor) raised concerns over delayed PFS monetization and historical stock performance:** Management reiterated commitment to the divestment process, denying any internal management hindrance and emphasizing that sales will not be rushed at distressed valuations.
- **Rahul Kumar (Individual Investor) asked about the reasons for standalone PAT drop despite operational income/volume growth:** Management clarified that lower net surcharge income (due to better DISCOM collections) and reduced treasury income (post-special dividend payout) impacted profitability.
- **Vivek Nayak (Individual Investor) asked if PFS asset book is fully provided for:** PFS MD Rajiv Malhotra stated that the book is well-provided for with no hidden surprises beyond disclosed NPAs.
- **Vivek Nayak asked about PTC trading on HPX given its 22.5% equity stake:** Management noted that to trade on HPX, equity must be reduced to 5%, an option currently under evaluation pending market coupling developments.

### One-line takeaway
PTC India reported robust 24% volume growth in Q2 FY27, but profitability contracted due to lower net surcharge recoveries and PFS impairment provisions, while management continues to weigh strategic monetization of its PFS stake and expansion into new exchange verticals.
