# Borosil Renewables Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Power & Utilities*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Operating margins compressed QoQ due to furnace maintenance downtime and lower fuel surcharge collection, but management expects a strong rebound in H2 FY27 driven by record production levels in October and fuel surcharge reinstatement.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Net Revenue:** Rs 396.7 crore vs Rs 378.88 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 116.22 crore vs Rs 120.42 crore in Q2 FY26.
- **Standalone Total Sales:** Rs 396.2 crore (Solar glass: Rs 382.1 crore; Solar kits: Rs 14.1 crore).
- **Standalone Solar Glass EBITDA:** Rs 116.34 crore (30.4% of solar glass sales) vs Rs 125.5 crore (33.0%) in Q2 FY26 and Rs 142.0 crore (33.8%) in Q1 FY27.
- **Average Realization (Ex-factory):** Rs 155.9 per sq meter in Q2 FY27 vs Rs 147.5 in Q2 FY26 and Rs 160.3 in Q1 FY27.
- **H1 FY27 Standalone Revenue:** Rs 801.8 crore, up 12.8% YoY vs Rs 710.7 crore in H1 FY26.
- **H1 FY27 Standalone EBITDA:** Rs 258.3 crore, up 18.5% YoY vs Rs 218 crore in H1 FY26.
- **Overseas Subsidiaries:** Net revenue Rs 0 crore and EBITDA of negative Rs 0.11 crore in Q2 FY27.

### Highlights
- Production dipped by ~4.5% in Q2 FY27 due to maintenance and parameter fine-tuning, resulting in an estimated Rs 15 crore revenue loss.
- Production recovered sharply from mid-September, achieving historical high output levels in October 2026.
- Fuel surcharge was rolled back partially in July due to a temporary dip in gas prices, impacting QoQ realizations, but has been raised back in October to offset renewed gas price increases.
- Anti-dumping duty on solar glass imports from China and Vietnam remains in place until December 2029.
- Captive power generation saved close to Rs 10 crore in costs over the last 6 months.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects margins to return to historical levels of 30%+ EBITDA in Q3 FY27 on higher volumes and surcharge recovery.
- Management foresees no demand challenge in absorbing additional volumes from the new expansion capacity.
- The company expects to remain net cash positive by the end of March 2027.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic module manufacturing capacity reached 233 GW (supported by PLI, BCD, ALMM-I) against annual domestic installations of 62 GW on a DC basis (45 GW AC basis).
- Domestic solar cell capacity is expected to reach ~75 GW by 2027 from ~38 GW currently under ALMM-II.
- Domestic solar glass manufacturers currently supply ~18 GW equivalent, while domestic demand stands at 62 GW, leaving a substantial domestic supply deficit met via imports.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material basket costs increased due to a ~10% price hike in soda ash (representing 30%–32% of RM basket) and higher freight rates on imported dolomite and limestone.
- The company is actively shifting dolomite sourcing from imports to domestic suppliers (targeting a reversal of its current 30%-40% domestic mix) to reduce costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Ongoing 600 TPD (60%) capacity expansion project at the existing site revised total capex from budgeted levels up to Rs 1,100 crore (an increase of Rs 150 crore).
- Capex revision causes: 20% from Rupee devaluation vs USD; balance from fuel cost saving equipment additions and adding power redundancy infrastructure (~Rs 25 crore).
- Expansion project is fully funded via Rs 800 crore internal accruals/equity and Rs 300 crore pre-tied debt (debt undrawn as of Q2).
- Commercial firing of new furnaces scheduled for February and March 2027 (end of Q4 FY27).
- Furnaces 1 and 2 (SG1 and SG2) are due for 75-day rebuild shutdowns each in H1 FY28, which will be executed sequentially without disrupting customer supplies.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP) reached Rs 349 crore as of September 2026, up from Rs 107 crore in March 2026.
- ~Rs 600 crore of unused project/equity funds are temporarily deployed in liquid mutual funds and AAA debt securities yielding 7.0%–7.5%.
- Warrants issuance and promoter equity infusions are fully converted and paid up; no further equity dilution is planned in the foreseeable future.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High volatility in Brent crude and natural gas prices directly impacts power and fuel expenses.
- Elevated global freight rates driving up the cost of imported raw materials (dolomite, limestone).
- Planned 75-day maintenance downtime for Furnaces 1 and 2 in H1 FY28.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Q: What caused the project cost increase to Rs 1,100 crore?**  
  *Management:* The Rs 150 crore increase was driven by Rupee devaluation (20% of increase), addition of energy cost-saving equipment, and installing Rs 25 crore of power redundancy assets to prevent furnace power supply disturbances. The extra cost will be met internally without extra debt.
- **Q: How will the 75-day shutdowns of SG1 and SG2 in H1 FY28 affect revenue growth?**  
  *Management:* Furnaces will give higher post-rebuild yields. Additional output from the new 600 TPD capacity coming online in Q4 FY27 will ensure continuous supply to customers and prevent revenue loss.
- **Q: Are raw material cost increases (soda ash, imported RM) pass-through to customers?**  
  *Management:* No, unlike the fuel surcharge (which was an exceptional pass-through), raw material cost increases cannot be directly passed on. Borosil relies on operational efficiency, productivity gains, and indigenous RM sourcing to protect margins.
- **Q: What is the plan for future growth beyond FY28?**  
  *Management:* Energies are focused on stabilizing the new 600 TPD capacity. External consultants are aiding evaluation of specialty glass/adjacent growth areas, with concrete plans to be announced before the end of FY27.

### One-line takeaway
Borosil Renewables faced temporary operational downtime and margin compression in Q2 FY27, but strong October production recovery, reinstated fuel surcharges, and an on-track Q4 FY27 600 TPD expansion support a return to 30%+ EBITDA margins.
