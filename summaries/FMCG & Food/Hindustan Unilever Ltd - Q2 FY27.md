# Hindustan Unilever Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered highest USG in 14 quarters with broad-based strong volume growth and disciplined execution.

### Key numbers
- **Turnover:** Rs. 18,071 crore (15% USG YoY)
- **Underlying Volume Growth (UVG):** 7% YoY
- **H1 Turnover:** Rs. 35,255 crore (12% USG YoY, 6% UVG YoY)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 22.6% (within guided range of 22% to 24%)
- **Absolute EBITDA:** Rs. 4,089 crore (up 8% YoY)
- **PAT before exceptional items (PAT bei):** Rs. 2,795 crore (up 9% YoY)
- **Interim Dividend:** Rs. 19 per share
- **Home Care USG:** 22% (with double-digit UVG)
- **Beauty & Wellbeing USG:** 16% (with double-digit UVG)
- **Personal Care USG:** 9%
- **Foods USG:** 9% (with low-single digit UVG)
- **General Trade Outlet Addition:** 30,000 outlets added in Q2

### Highlights
- Delivered highest USG in 14 quarters (15%) and strong UVG of 7%.
- Unveiled ‘Winning In New India’ (WINI) strategy focusing on four growth pools: consumption, premiumisation, market making, and new spaces.
- Home Care and Beauty & Wellbeing delivered strong double-digit USG driven by double-digit UVG.
- Personal Care and Foods saw sequential step-ups, delivering 9% USG each.
- Extended Horlicks into the protein segment with the launch of Horlicks Protein Powder.
- HUL named ‘Top Supply Chain Icon’ by the Institute of Supply Chain Management (2026 ranking).
- Board approved interim dividend of Rs. 19 per share.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects medium-term EBITDA Margin to be within the current guided range of 22% to 24%.
- Management remains confident of growing ahead of the market, driven by the WINI strategy and resilient underlying demand.
- Commodity inflation is expected to remain elevated.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand conditions remain positive, supported by healthy macro indicators and resilient GDP growth (7.8% in Q1).
- Rural demand has seen a sequential increase, and growth is broad-based across urban and rural markets.
- Categories are everyday essentials and staples, tending to be much less elastic compared to discretionary categories.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Commodity prices remain elevated, with JQ and SQ bearing the brunt of input cost inflation.
- Pricing strategy focuses on passing on absolute cost fully to protect consumer franchises and sustain volumes.
- Gross margin contracted by ~400 bps YoY, while EBITDA margin dropped by 40 bps QoQ (140 bps YoY).
- A&P spending is managed dynamically; Home Care A&P was rationalised (cut by ~Rs. 250 crore), while spend increased across other categories. AI-powered tools (Sangam) and scale buying drive A&P efficiencies.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Added 30,000 outlets in general trade with double-digit assortment growth.
- Nearly doubled asset deployment for category development in modern trade over the last 2 quarters.
- Expanded Simple, Dove, Vaseline, and Minimalist into emerging demand spaces; Minimalist expanded offline presence.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Mentioned financial resilience as a competitive advantage; no specific debt figures or working capital numbers reported in the call.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatile global geopolitical environment and elevated commodity inflation.
- Need to monitor crop impacts from monsoon and inflation trends, though agricultural GDP impact is cushioned by non-agri sources and government support.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Latika Chopra (JP Morgan) on volume growth sustainability and rural demand:** Management stated that sequential volume momentum is improving despite price increases, and rural demand is rising and broad-based.
- **Mihir Shah (Nomura) on gross margin recovery:** Management stated that structurally, the portfolio is positioned to improve gross margins over the medium term, despite short-term cyclicality.
- **Amit Sachdeva (UBS) on earnings growth versus revenue growth:** Management clarified that 9% profit growth alongside 15% top-line growth is in line with their strategy of volume-led profit growth, prioritizing market share over immediate margin recovery.
- **Arnab Mitra (Goldman Sachs) on segment divergence (Home Care/B&W vs. Personal Care/Tea):** Management noted sequential improvements in Personal Care (soaps), while mass tea was impacted by high inflation.
- **Percy Panthaki (IIFL) on Lifebuoy performance:** Management highlighted that the overall soaps business saw double-digit sequential growth and that the portfolio is shifting toward premium and beauty formats where HUL is well-positioned.

### One-line takeaway
HUL delivered its highest USG in 14 quarters (15%) with 7% UVG, balancing price hikes with volume growth amidst elevated commodity inflation.
