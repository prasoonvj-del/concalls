# FMCG & Food: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Indian FMCG and Food sector presented a story of strong top-line delivery offset by severe commodity margin pressures in Q2 FY27. Underlying demand stayed volume-led and resilient overall, led by urban modern trade and quick-commerce scaling rapidly into Tier-2/3 regions. However, widespread raw material inflation—spanning crude oil derivatives (LABSA, SLES, packaging plastics), edible oils, pulses/grains, and fresh milk—sharply squeezed gross margins for staples, personal care, and packaged food players alike. In response, managements across the board deployed calibrated pricing, grammage reductions (shrinkflation), and aggressive internal cost-savings programs (such as Dabur’s Project Samriddhi/IRIS and Mrs. Bectors’ Project IMPACT). Meanwhile, alcobev companies experienced strong volume expansion driven by structural premiumisation, despite dealing with packaging (glass) inflation and state-level excise transitions.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** – Historically, Indian FMCG and food businesses relied heavily on general trade distribution, tier-based wholesaler networks, and broad-spectrum mass media advertising. Over the last ~5 years, companies have aggressively pivoted towards direct distribution expansion, technology-led continuous replenishment systems, asset-light manufacturing/contracting models, and hyper-local fulfillment through digital and quick-commerce channels [Background].

* **Quick-Commerce as a Primary Growth Channel:** Rapidly scaling beyond tier-1 cities, quick-commerce is evolving from a pure convenience platform into a critical engine for launching premium SKUs, driving high impulse consumption, and improving trade margins [Dabur India concall Q2 FY27; Honasa Consumer concall Q2 FY27; Sundrop Brands concall Q2 FY27]. Emami noted quick-commerce contributes ~15% of its digital sales [Emami concall Q2 FY27], while Mrs. Bectors reported a 22% quick-commerce contribution across its B2C business [Mrs. Bectors Food concall Q2 FY27]. 
* **Premiumisation & Format Innovation:** Mass consumer brands are stepping up value creation by launching specialized high-margin SKUs (e.g., protein-enriched food, liquid detergents, D2C-incubated skincare lines) while premiumising traditional categories [Tata Consumer concall Q2 FY27; Milky Mist concall Q2 FY27; Zydus Wellness concall Q2 FY27]. Honasa highlighted focus categories contributing >80% of revenue [Honasa Consumer concall Q2 FY27], and Dabur’s premium portfolio reached 20% of revenue, growing 4x faster than its core business [Dabur India concall Q2 FY27].
* **Alcobev Portfolio Premiumisation:** Spirits and wine producers are aggressively shifting volumes from regular/mass products into Prestige & Above (P&A), luxury malt spirits, and Ready-to-Drink (RTD) categories to drive margin expansion [Radico Khaitan concall Q2 FY27; Allied Blenders concall Q2 FY27; IGL Spirits concall Q2 FY27]. Radico Khaitan upgraded its full-year P&A volume growth guidance to 30% [Radico Khaitan concall Q2 FY27], while IGL Spirits increased its IMFL revenue share to 18% in Q2, targeting ~30% by Q4 [IGL Spirits concall Q2 FY27].
* **Backward Integration & Operational Efficiency:** Driven by commodity price volatility, companies are investing in captive raw material processing (e.g., besan mills, malt distilleries, PET packaging lines) to lower logistics costs and insulate operating margins [Gopal Snacks concall Q2 FY27; Allied Blenders concall Q2 FY27; Bikaji Foods concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push** – Government mandates on alcohol blending targets have created multi-year order visibility for grain-based ethanol producers (e.g., Modi Naturals securing Rs 494 crore in OMC orders) [Govt: Achieved 20% ethanol blending target, Nov 2026; Modi Naturals concall Q2 FY27]. Additionally, the India-UK Free Trade Agreement reduces Scotch whisky import tariffs to 75%, providing direct margin tailwinds for domestic alco-bev bottlers and importers [Govt: India-UK trade deal cuts Scotch whisky duty to 75%, Nov 2026; Allied Blenders concall Q2 FY27]. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme continues to support foreign market branding and local export processing capabilities [PLI Scheme 2021, Background; Adf Foods concall Q2 FY27].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
### The Consensus
* **Urban & Alternate Channels Lead:** Urban markets, modern trade, e-commerce, and quick commerce saw sustained, robust volume momentum across categories. Companies like Godrej Consumer, Honasa, Dabur, HUL, and Tata Consumer all highlighted double-digit growth in alternate trade channels.
* **Resilient Snacking & Impulse Consumption:** Snacking, confectionery, and impulse foods registered stable volume expansion. Bikaji (ethnic/western snacks up 20%+ value), Gopal Snacks (+22.9% revenue growth), and Prataap Snacks (+17% transaction volume growth) confirmed healthy end-consumer appetite.
* **Premium Spirit Volumes Outperforming Mass:** Spirits companies (Radico Khaitan, Allied Blenders, Globus Spirits, Tilaknagar Industries, IGL Spirits) agreed that Prestige & Above (P&A) categories are vastly outperforming regular mass-market alcohol volumes.

### The Divergence
* **Rural Demand Recovery Trajectory:** HUL, Bajaj Consumer Care, and Zydus Wellness reported that rural demand growth is sequentially stepping up and outperforming urban growth. Conversely, AWL Agri Business, Patanjali Foods, and Mrs. Bectors noted rural demand sluggishness driven by persistent food inflation and regional rainfall deficits.
* **International & Export Logistics:** Export-focused players split on international demand. Sundrop Brands, Adf Foods, and Milky Mist experienced robust demand abroad, while Foods & Inns, Emami, and Mrs. Bectors cited significant export revenue friction caused by container shortages, shipping delays, and geopolitical tension in West Asia.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Severe Multi-Commodity Inflation:** Broad-based input cost spikes hit companies hard. Crude-linked inputs (LABSA, HDPE/packaging laminates, PET resin) surged 18% to 60% YoY, severely impacting Home Care and Personal Care segments (HUL, Jyothy Labs, Godrej Consumer, Tilaknagar).
* **Edible Oils & Agri Commodities:** Edible oils, pulses (chana, maize), coffee beans, and spices (chilli, coriander) saw inflation (AWL Agri, Bikaji, Orkla India, Prataap Snacks). Grain prices reached record highs (~Rs 30,000/ton for maize), dampening margins for alcohol/ethanol producers (Associated Alcohols, IGL Spirits).
* **Dairy Procurement Inflation:** Dairy players (Dodla Dairy, Heritage Foods, Parag Milk Foods, Milky Mist) reported an 11% to 18% YoY rise in raw milk procurement costs, squeezing quarterly gross margins prior to executing consumer price hikes.
* **Mitigation Strategies & Pricing:** Managements utilized calibrated price hikes (HUL +8%, Parag Milk, Bikaji +3% post-Diwali, Bajaj Consumer +5%, Dodla +INR 2.5/L) and grammage cuts on LUP (INR 5/10) packs to protect absolute EBITDA pools while preserving consumer affordability.

## Capex & capacity
* **Food & Dairy Expansion:** Parag Milk Foods approved INR 205 crore capex to quadruple paneer capacity and double cheese capacity. Sheetal Cool Products planned INR 10 crore capex and 10,000 freezer deployments. Milky Mist commissioned a Rs 40 crore Greek yogurt facility in Perundurai.
* **Snacks & Beverage Processing:** Prataap Snacks acquired land for a Rs 421-422 crore greenfield processing facility near Indore. Varun Beverages projected CY27 India capex of ~Rs 1,000 crore (including a greenfield plant at Suratgarh) alongside $90–100 million in international capex. Hindustan Foods is commercializing >Rs 300 crore in assets across soft drinks, ice creams, and footwear.
* **Spirits & Alcobev Infrastructure:** Allied Blenders is executing a ~Rs 1,600 crore 3-year capex strategy, commissioning a captive malt distillery in Rangapur. Vintage Coffee is deploying Rs 550 crore for its 5,500 MT Freeze-Dried Coffee expansion. Sula Vineyards approved a Rs 25 crore 35-key hotel project at York Estate.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Dabur India:** Pulled forward its mid-term strategic roadmap by 1.5 years (Vision 2031) and highlighted an internal "red" rating on strategic M&A targets due to high market valuations.
- **Radico Khaitan:** Upgraded its FY27 Prestige & Above (P&A) volume growth guidance to 30% (from earlier mid-20s) following 36% volume growth in Q2.
- **Adf Foods:** Downwardly revised FY27 revenue guidance from >Rs 900 crore to Rs 850 crore strictly due to container constraints and shipping route disruptions in the Middle East.
- **Som Distilleries:** Narrowed FY27 revenue guidance to ~INR 1,000 crore (from INR 1,000-1,100 crore) following operational disruptions and plant suspension in Bhopal, though operations are now restored.
- **AWL Agri Business:** Shifted tone from "resilient" rural demand to noting rural sluggishness and potential risks from Rabi season rainfall deficits.

## What to watch next quarter
1. **Winter Season Demand & Festive Spillover:** Extent of post-Diwali demand recovery for seasonal portfolios (Emami’s BoroPlus, Patanjali’s Ghee, Bikaji’s packaged sweets dispatches shifting to Q3).
2. **Implementation of Price Hikes:** Execution and market acceptance of announced price increases (Bikaji’s 3% post-Diwali hike, Bajaj Consumer’s 5% hike, and Dodla Dairy’s milk price adjustments).
3. **Logistics & Ocean Freight Normalization:** Tracking export shipping delays and container availability for Adf Foods, Foods & Inns, and Mrs. Bectors.
4. **Commodity Price Stabilization:** Raw material price trends in crude derivatives, packaging laminates, raw milk (post-buffalo flush), and new grain crops (maize, rice, spices) arriving in November/December.
5. **Regulatory & Policy Updates:** Progress on FSSAI front-of-package labelling deadlines, state-level alcohol pricing/policy changes (Karnataka, UP, Tamil Nadu), and bulk Scotch import duty clarifications under the India-UK FTA.
