# FMCG & Food: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The FMCG and Food sector presents a contrasting dual reality in Q2 FY27: top-line revenue and underlying volume growth (UVG) remain healthy, but bottom-line margins are facing severe cross-currents from input cost inflation. Large, diversified players and agile brand owners (such as HUL, Marico, Tata Consumer, and Honasa) are leveraging pricing power, volume scale, and channel shifts to protect profitability. Conversely, mass-market and regional players (such as Jyothy Labs, Prataap Snacks, Dodla Dairy, and AWL Agri Business) have suffered gross and operating margin compressions, as steep raw material inflation could not be fully passed on without impairing consumer volumes.

Across supply chains, raw material volatility—specifically in crude oil derivatives, packaging (laminates, HDPE, glass), edible oils, pulses, and raw milk—emerged as the central operating headwind. On distribution, quick commerce continues to redefine urban channel dynamics, transitioning rapidly from an impulse fulfillment mechanism to a strategic driver of product innovation and premiumization.

---

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence

### Consensus
* **Resilient Underlying Volumes:** Managements report healthy consumer volume demand. Standard mass staples and impulse snacks are seeing steady volume expansion (e.g., HUL UVG at +7%, Marico India volume at +10%, Britannia volume at +13%, Honasa UVG at +31.3%).
* **Quick Commerce Outperformance:** Across the board, quick commerce is cited as the fastest-growing sub-channel, unlocking high-margin impulse sales and helping premium variants scale efficiently (e.g., Tata Consumer, Honasa, Sundrop Brands, Orkla India, Zydus Wellness).
* **International Demand Resilience:** Export demand for value-added foods, frozen items, and ethnic offerings remains firm (e.g., ADF Foods, Godrej Consumer GAUM, Vintage Coffee), despite global logistics bottlenecks.

### Divergence
* **Urban vs. Rural Dynamics:** 
  * *Rural Leading/Outperforming:* Jyothy Labs, Bajaj Consumer Care, HUL, and Zydus Wellness report rural demand growing faster than or outperforming urban markets.
  * *Rural Sluggish/Pressure:* AWL Agri Business, Mrs. Bectors, and Emami flag rural demand pressure, driven by rainfall deficits in specific pockets and high food inflation squeezing lower-income household budgets.
* **General Trade (GT) Recovery:** Britannia and Dabur report strong recoveries in GT (Dabur posting its highest GT growth in 19 quarters), whereas Jyothy Labs notes that GT remains subdued relative to modern trade and e-commerce.

## Costs, raw materials & margins

* **Input Cost Headwinds:** Widespread cost spikes were reported in crude-linked derivatives, packaging materials (laminates, glass, PET, plastics), edible palm oils, pulses, sugar, and raw milk.
* **Margin Compression:**
  * **Jyothy Labs:** Gross margin contracted heavily to 34.8% (down from 48.1% YoY) due to crude-linked input cost inflation; EBITDA margin fell to 2.5%.
  * **Prataap Snacks:** EBITDA margin dropped to 2.4% due to an immediate ~8 percentage point negative gross margin hit from palm oil and laminate spikes.
  * **Dodla Dairy:** EBITDA margin compressed to 5.4% due to early front-loaded milk procurement costs (+11% YoY).
  * **AWL Agri Business & Emami:** Gross margins were hit by raw material price swings and adverse product/business mix shifts.
* **Margin Expansion & Inflation Buffers:**
  * **Marico:** Gross margin expanded ~600 bps YoY, driven by softer copra prices (-35% from last year's peak) and portfolio mix improvements.
  * **Radico Khaitan:** EBITDA margin expanded 462 bps YoY to 20.4%, propelled by a 36% volume growth in its Prestige & Above (P&A) portfolio.
  * **Milky Mist & Zydus Wellness:** Expanded margins through a higher contribution from value-added high-protein products and operational leverage.
* **Pricing Action Strategies:** Most companies (including Britannia, Bikaji, Bajaj Consumer, Heritage Foods, Orkla India, and Tata Consumer) implemented staggered 3% to 8% price hikes or grammage cuts (shrinkflation) post-Diwali to offset cost pressures without disrupting festive volume demand.

## Capex & capacity

* **Value-Added Dairy & Snacking Expansions:**
  * **Parag Milk Foods:** Approved a Rs 205 crore capex to expand paneer capacity 4x (to 80 MT/day by June 2027) and double cheese capacity (to 120 MT/day by Q2 FY28).
  * **Milky Mist:** Commissioned a 150 MT/day Greek/Skyr yogurt plant in Perundurai (Rs 40 crore investment) and acquired 50 acres in Baramati, Maharashtra.
  * **Prataap Snacks:** Acquired 50 acres near Indore for a Rs 421-422 crore greenfield integrated manufacturing unit to consolidate 7 existing standalone plants.
* **Spirits & Beverages Bottling & Integration:**
  * **Allied Blenders and Distillers (ABD):** Target capex of ~Rs 1,600 crore over 3 years; commissioned Rangapur malt distillery, UP bottling plant, and announced a 66 million bulk litre dual-mode distillery.
  * **Varun Beverages:** Projected CY2027 capex of ~$90-100M for international markets and ~Rs 1,000 crore for India (including a greenfield plant at Suratgarh, Rajasthan).
  * **Vintage Coffee:** Incurred Rs 220 crore of an estimated Rs 550 crore capex for its first 5,500 MT Freeze-Dried Coffee (FDC) facility.
* **Third-Party & Contract Manufacturing Scaling:**
  * **Hindustan Foods:** Commercializing >Rs 300 crore worth of assets starting December 2026 across soft drinks (Guwahati), ice cream cones (Nashik), and brownfield expansions (Coimbatore, Baddi, Chennai).
  * **Sundrop Brands & Bajaj Consumer:** Continuing capital-efficient asset-light strategies by utilizing 3P capabilities and outsourcing Guwahati factory production.

## What changed since last quarter

* **Guidance Revisions:**
  * **ADF Foods:** Lowered FY27 revenue guidance from >Rs 900 crore to Rs 850 crore due to severe ocean freight delays and container shortages.
  * **Marico:** Upgraded full-year EBITDA growth guidance from ~20% to mid-twenties.
  * **Radico Khaitan:** Upgraded Prestige & Above (P&A) volume growth guidance for FY27 from earlier levels to 30%.
  * **Emami:** Raised Strategic Investments annual revenue target to Rs 800-850 crore.
* **Operational & Tone Shifts:**
  * **Hindustan Foods:** Turned its footwear division profitable following the resolution of legacy fixed-price contracts and input cost revisions.
  * **Britannia:** Recovered from Middle East shipping turbulence to deliver double-digit international growth; eliminated fractional pricing issues (Rs 4.5 pack) by standardizing on Rs 5 and Rs 10 price points.
  * **Foods & Inns:** Shifted strategy by discontinuing forward currency hedging after recording a notional forex loss of Rs 10.6 crore.
  * **Honasa Consumer:** Shifted tone from Q1 caution regarding a Q2 dip to reporting its highest growth quarter post-IPO (+36% revenue).

## What to watch next quarter

* **Pass-Through Efficacy of Price Hikes:** Watch whether post-festive price hikes and grammage cuts announced by Bikaji (3%), Bajaj Consumer (5%), Heritage Foods, and Britannia successfully protect gross margins in H2 without denting lower-ticket volume demand.
* **Global Shipping & Ocean Freight Normalization:** Track container availability and transit times for export-heavy players like ADF Foods and Foods & Inns, alongside packaging material price pressures linked to West Asian geopolitical conflicts (impacting Allied Blenders, Radico Khaitan, and Varun Beverages).
* **Winter Flush & Dairy Procurement Costs:** Watch if the post-Diwali buffalo/cow milk flush in North and South India stabilizes raw milk procurement prices for Dodla Dairy, Heritage Foods, Milky Mist, and Parag Milk Foods, enabling guided EBITDA margin recoveries (e.g., Dodla targeting 7.5%-8%).
* **Execution of Greenfield/Brownfield Commissioning:** Monitor the rollout of commercial operations at Hindustan Foods' seasonal F&B plants, Parag's paneer/cheese expansions, and ABD's Minakshi Agro bottling unit.
