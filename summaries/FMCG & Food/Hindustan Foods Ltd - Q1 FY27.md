# Hindustan Foods Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-11 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management delivered record quarterly PAT and reaffirmed FY27 guidance despite temporary cost headwinds in the footwear segment.

### Key numbers
*   **Total Income:** Rs 1,207 crore (up 18% YoY).
*   **EBITDA:** Rs 106.3 crore (up 26% YoY).
*   **PBT:** Rs 56.6 crore (up 33% YoY).
*   **PAT:** Rs 42.8 crore (up 33% YoY).
*   **New Project Wins (FY27):** Rs 340 crore.
*   **Total FY27 Capex Plan:** ~Rs 500 crore (Rs 340 crore new + Rs 150 crore carry-forward).
*   **Footwear Cost Impact:** Rs 6 crore (RM/freight + wage hike).

### Highlights
*   Achieved highest ever quarterly PAT.
*   Footwear business faced temporary margin pressure due to Middle East geopolitical crisis (logistics/RM costs) and a 30% minimum wage hike in Haryana.
*   Silvassa facility experienced flooding in July; production partially restored, expected to be fully operational by end-August.
*   Successfully integrated Aurangabad Personal Care facility.
*   Expanding into Greek yogurt (Goa) and Class III medical devices (Chennai).

### Guidance & outlook
*   **FY27 PAT Guidance:** Reaffirmed at Rs 200 crore to Rs 220 crore.
*   Management is optimistic about sustaining growth in FY28 and beyond.
*   Project pipeline: Management indicated a pipeline of ~Rs 1,000 crore in potential inquiries.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   FMCG contract manufacturing remains a compelling long-term opportunity due to outsourcing, premiumization, and asset-light preferences.
*   Home & Personal Care (HPC) division seeing double-digit volume growth.
*   Beverages and Ice Cream divisions reported strong seasonal performance.
*   Footwear order book is full for H2 FY27.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Most business segments utilize a cost pass-through mechanism for inflation.
*   Footwear contracts are fixed-price (6-8 months in advance), making mid-season pass-through difficult; management expects return to profitability in coming months.
*   GST inversion is impacting cash flows; management noted that revenue growth may lag profitability growth due to customer-supplied raw material models.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   **FY27 Capex Breakup (Rs 340 crore):** Food & Beverages (Rs 210 crore), Ice Cream (Rs 80 crore), HPC (Rs 50 crore).
*   New capacities: Greek yogurt (Goa), Panipat (Ice Cream), Lucknow (HPC).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   Maintaining a 1:1 debt-equity ratio.
*   GST inversion is causing cash flow constraints in some units.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Geopolitical risks (Middle East crisis) impacting freight rates and raw material prices (e.g., polymers).
*   Natural calamities (e.g., Silvassa flooding).
*   Footwear segment's fixed-price contract structure limits ability to absorb sudden cost spikes.
*   Regulatory: FSSAI cleanup of food sector may cause short-term brand disruption.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   **Abneesh Roy (Nuvama) on footwear pass-through:** Management clarified that footwear is unique due to 6-8 month lead times, unlike other segments with pass-through mechanisms.
*   **Surbhi (Bellwether Capital) on footwear utilization:** Management stated utilization is 80-90% from August onwards.
*   **Rahul (Ambit) on gross block vs. sales growth:** Management explained that revenue growth may appear lower than gross block growth because they are increasingly adopting customer-supplied raw material models to manage GST inversion.
*   **Mayur Parkeria (Wealth Managers) on risk management:** Suggested creating a formal risk management committee; management agreed it is an excellent suggestion.
*   **Faisal Hawa (Investor) on GST inversion:** Management noted that while GST inversion is a challenge, it is not the primary driver for outsourcing; scale and diversification remain the key growth drivers.

### One-line takeaway
Hindustan Foods delivered record PAT and reaffirmed FY27 guidance, supported by a strong project pipeline and diversified manufacturing, despite temporary footwear-specific cost headwinds and logistics disruptions.
