# Sula Vineyards Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-13 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue grew 3% YoY with strong growth in Wine Tourism and Elite & Premium brands, but margins were impacted by higher blended grape costs and softer Economy & Popular segments.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 121 crore (up 3% YoY vs Rs 118 crore in Q1 FY26).
- Own Brands growth: 2% YoY.
- Elite & Premium portfolio growth: 6% YoY; share in Own Brands expanded 310 bps to 78%.
- Wine Tourism revenue: Rs 15.5 crore (up 12% YoY); contributes ~13% to overall revenue.
- Resort occupancy: ~63% overall; >70% excluding The Haven.
- B2C wine sales from bottle shops: Rs 10 crore (up 7% YoY).
- Gross profit: Declined 5% YoY.
- Net debt (as of June 2026): Rs 319 crore (vs Rs 345 crore as of June 2025).
- WIPS outstanding receivable: Rs 88 crore as of June 2026 (reduced to ~Rs 80 crore in July after Rs 10 crore payment).

### Highlights
- Marked six consecutive months of positive sales growth.
- Flagship Elite brands 'The Source' and 'RASA' saw excellent double-digit growth, now accounting for an all-time high of 16% of Own Brands sales. Introduced two new wines: The Source Chardonnay and The Source Grenache Red.
- Sula Merlot and Sula Muscat Blanc grew over 100% YoY on a small base. Sula Shiraz Cabernet recorded high single-digit growth.
- Telangana grew over 50% YoY following the route-to-market disruption resolution in December 2025.
- Completed acquisition of the former Chandon estate for Rs 20 crores, renamed 'Domaine RASA' (tasting room, bottle shop, and banquet operational; winery operations to start in Q4 harvest 2027).
- Nashik amphitheater expansion completed in July; Domaine Dindori bottle shop opening next week; 5,000 sq. ft. events pavilion in Nashik on track for Q3 completion.
- Received preliminary approval for 5 additional brand listings in CSD (taking total to 14), expected to be operational by Q3 FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to recover to last year's EBITDA margin levels shortly and surpass them before the end of FY27.
- Net debt levels expected to trend lower by the end of FY27 compared to FY26.
- Confident that positive momentum in Telangana will continue in Q2.
- Expects Karnataka market to turn the corner in the second half of FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Overall wine industry (domestic and imports) per IWSR has not seen significant growth over the past 2 years, remaining at low single-digit growth following a post-COVID spurt.
- Economy & Popular portfolio faced pressure due to unsustainable discounting by competitors.
- Karnataka market remained soft for the entire wine category; Sula maintained or improved market share in Elite & Premium but gave up share in Popular & Economy.
- Strong double-digit growth seen in Telangana, Haryana, Chandigarh, CSD, and Exports.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- EBITDA impacted by higher blended grape costs (procuring 100% wine grapes vs 80% previously and avoiding open-market table grapes to clear carryover liquid), impacting gross margin by ~150 bps. This is temporary and expected to subside in Q4 FY27, fully normalizing in Q1 FY28.
- Table grape prices during harvest 2026 more than doubled (up to Rs 35/kg), but management expects them to moderate to less than Rs 20/kg for the next harvest, benefiting Popular & Economy margins in FY28.
- Adverse geographic mix (stronger growth in lower-margin states like Telangana, Haryana, CSD vs core Maharashtra/Karnataka) reduced gross margin by ~200 bps, though offset by lower selling/distribution costs.
- Operating costs reduced by 3% YoY; employee benefit expenses reduced by 6% YoY due to organizational optimization and lower ESOP costs.
- Interest cost declined 4% due to lower average debt levels and borrowing costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Growth capex is increasingly allocated towards Wine Tourism.
- Resort expansion is largely asset-light via management contracts (70 of 150 keys are owned). Management is studying the possibility of investing in constructing its own resorts going forward.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt stood at Rs 319 crore as of June 2026 (down from Rs 345 crore in June 2025).
- WIPS outstanding stood at Rs 88 crore as of June 2026 (Rs 9 crore accrued, Rs 8 crore received); further reduced to ~Rs 80 crore in July after a Rs 10 crore payment.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unsustainable discounting by competitors in the Economy & Popular segment.
- Continued softness and industry-wide degrowth in the Karnataka market.
- Weather dependency (monsoon duration impacting grape yields and prices).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Abneesh Roy (Nuvama) on raw material outlook and past competitor behavior:** Management stated that competitor production volumes are declining as players realize the unsuitability of excess crushing and discounting, making management hopeful for lower table grape prices (< Rs 20/kg) in harvest 2027.
2. **Abneesh Roy (Nuvama) on flat revenues over the last 3-4 years and white spirits diversification:** Management acknowledged industry stagnation (low single-digit growth per IWSR) over the last 2 years of consolidation. They currently have no white spirits plans but are looking at interesting new segments in the pipeline.
3. **Aditya (CLSA) on gross margin impact from grape mix shift:** Management confirmed gross margins would have been better without the shift to 100% wine grapes, but the negative impact will reverse starting Q4 FY27 depending on climate conditions.
4. **Nikhil (SiMPL) on divergence between Sula's performance in Maharashtra/Karnataka versus other alco-bev players benefiting from state policies:** Management explained that state policies (like MML in Maharashtra and duty revisions in Karnataka) primarily target spirits and beer, leaving the wine category and duties completely unaffected.
5. **Ayush (Consortium Securities) on Wine Tourism capital employed and resort lease terms:** Management stated Wine Tourism is integral without a separate balance sheet, operates at higher EBITDA margins than standalone wine, and typically utilizes leases of 10 years or more.

### One-line takeaway
Sula Vineyards delivered 3% revenue growth driven by its Elite & Premium portfolio and Wine Tourism, while temporary high blended grape costs weighed on gross margins before expected normalization in FY28.
