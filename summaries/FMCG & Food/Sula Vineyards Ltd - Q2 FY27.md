# Sula Vineyards Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-17 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Net revenue grew 4% YoY, EBITDA grew 8% YoY, and net profit surged 34% YoY backed by strong premiumization and cost efficiencies.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (reiterated margin recovery timeline starting Q4 FY27; targeted EBITDA margins of 22%-23% over the next couple of years).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted more positive due to 34% YoY profit growth; Maharashtra demand improved vs Q1, Goa showed double-digit recovery, while Karnataka remained subdued.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Upcoming launch of wine-based RTD beverages early next month; potential drought impact on post-April 2027 Maharashtra irrigation; delay in EU FTA implementation to May 2027; plan to bring Maharashtra super distribution logistics in-house saving Rs 1 crore annually from Q4 FY27.
- Last quarter's promises: Vintage Room event pavilion (5,000 sq ft) in Nashik completed and operational; finalized plans for 4th hotel (35 keys at York); relocating York winery operations to Domaine Rasa by end of Q3 FY27.

### Key numbers
- **Net Revenue:** Rs 136 crore, up 4% YoY (vs Rs 131 crore in Q2 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 27.5 crore, up 8% YoY; EBITDA margin expanded 85 bps YoY to 20.3%.
- **Net Profit:** Rs 8 crore, up 34% YoY (H1 FY27 PAT at Rs 9.2 crore, up 15% YoY).
- **Elite & Premium Portfolio:** Revenue grew 9% YoY; share in Own Brands reached a record 82% (up 420 bps YoY).
- **Brand Standouts:** The Source grew >30% YoY (contributing >11% of Own Brands); Sula Merlot grew >100% YoY (contributing ~5% of Own Brands).
- **Wine Tourism Revenue:** Rs 16.3 crore, up 24% YoY (contributing ~20% of net revenue, up 220 bps YoY). Room revenue grew 32% YoY; visitor footfalls grew 7% YoY; D2C bottle shop sales grew 7% YoY to Rs 9.8 crore.
- **Finance Costs:** Interest cost declined 16% YoY; average borrowing cost reduced to 7.3% (vs 7.6% last year).

### Highlights
- Marked three consecutive quarters of positive revenue growth.
- Telangana grew ~40% YoY, becoming Sula's second-largest market in the Elite & Premium segment (surpassing Karnataka), with market share exceeding 70%.
- Domestic Indian wine is gaining market share over imported wines, with Sula leading the shift.
- Achieved 97% solar energy powering winery needs and 80% for total company H1 energy needs; doubling battery energy storage capacity from 2 MW to 4 MW.
- In-housing Maharashtra super distribution logistics facility at Domaine Dindori, delivering Rs 1 crore annual savings starting Q4 FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects to sustain earnings momentum and close H2 FY27 on a strong note.
- Targets YoY EBITDA growth in every quarter over the next year, aiming for 22%-23% EBITDA margins over the next couple of years.
- Raw material cost impact will continue into Q3 FY27 and moderate progressively from Q4 FY27 as procurement mix normalizes with Harvest 2027.
- WIPS accrual expected at Rs 49–50 crore for FY27 (Rs 19 crore accrued in H1; ~Rs 31 crore expected in H2).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Industry experiencing a K-shaped trend: Elite & Premium growing strongly, while Popular & Economy remains under pressure due to indiscriminate competitor discounting.
- Core market demand: Maharashtra showed improving demand (high single-digit growth in Elite & Premium); Goa, Rajasthan, West Bengal, and exports recorded robust double-digit growth; Karnataka remained soft following excise policy changes favoring beer.
- Total Indian premium wine market size (domestic + imported) estimated at ~Rs 1,000 crore, with Sula holding 70%-75% market share in domestic premium wines.
- EU FTA duty reductions on EUR >2.50 CIF wines expected to take effect around May 2027.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margin declined 280 bps YoY due to: (1) higher blended grape cost (150 bps impact from Harvest 2026 using 100% wine grapes and zero open-market table grapes), and (2) adverse geographical mix (stronger growth in lower-gross-margin state Telangana).
- Reduced operating expenses by 4% YoY through overhead discipline.
- Raw material inventory reduced by ~80 days YoY from Q2 FY26 to Q2 FY27.
- Expected bumper 2027 grape harvest in Maharashtra due to dry conditions will lower table grape prices (down from Rs 30/kg in 2026 toward historical Rs 15–20/kg levels), boosting gross margins for affordable wines from Q4 FY27 / Q1 FY28.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Vintage Room (5,000 sq ft indoor banquet facility for 300 guests at Nashik campus) completed and operational; expected to boost H2 FY27 and FY28 tourism revenue.
- Approved 4th hotel project: 35 keys at York Estate involving Rs 25 crore capex (5–6 year payback), expanding total room capacity by ~20% to 185–190 rooms by Q4 FY28.
- Relocating York winery operations to Domaine Rasa by end of Q3 FY27, enabling low-cost conversion of the York winery building into a hotel starting Q4 FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt decreased by Rs 49 crore YoY to Rs 306 crore as of September 30, 2026 (down 14% YoY). Net debt to trailing 12-month EBITDA remains under 3x.
- Cash generated from operations after working capital increased ~20% YoY to Rs 5 crore.
- WIPS receivables stood at Rs 88 crore at September end (reduced to ~Rs 85 crore post-quarter end following a Rs 4.2 crore payout received in November 2026).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- State agriculture drought in Maharashtra could lead to government irrigation water restrictions after April 2027, potentially affecting future harvest yields.
- Adverse excise policy shifts in Karnataka favoring beer over wine.
- Unsustainable discounting and non-compliant low-priced products in the Economy & Popular category.
- Geopolitical issues in the Middle East causing minor price increases in glass packaging, fuel, and oil.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kaustav Bubna (Kamana Holdings) on drought impact:** Management stated dry conditions are causing a bumper 2027 grape crop that will lower table grape prices and benefit margins. State irrigation restrictions post-April 2027 may affect farmers, but Sula does not expect material impact on its grape procurement or costs.
2. **Keshav Garg (Counter Cyclical PMS) on demerging Wine Tourism:** Management noted that while wine tourism commands strong growth and valuations, there is no immediate reason to demerge it, though structural options are regularly reviewed.
3. **Nikhil Upadhaya (SiMPL) on gross margin recovery drivers:** Management emphasized that raw material inventory is down 80 days YoY, and the return to buying cheaper table grapes in Harvest 2027 will drive gross margin recovery starting Q4 FY27/Q1 FY28.
4. **Siddharth Bhattacharya (IDFC) on high grape costs root cause:** Management explained that 100% wine grape procurement in Harvest 2026 was a deliberate one-time action to clear excess carryover liquid caused by slower historical growth. Procurement will normalize to 75%-80% wine grapes and 20%-25% table grapes in 2027.

### One-line takeaway
Sula Vineyards delivered 34% profit growth as premiumization and cost cuts offset temporary grape cost pressures, while setting the foundation for margin expansion from Q4 FY27.
