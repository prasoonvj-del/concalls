# FMCG & Food - Packaged foods: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Managements across the packaged foods sector report robust underlying consumer demand and healthy volume growth in Q2 FY27, despite persistent headwinds from multi-commodity inflation (edible oils, pulses, spices, tea) and global logistics bottlenecks. Companies with strong brand equity, diversified distribution (including quick commerce), and agile pricing models successfully defended margins through price hikes and grammage adjustments, while export-oriented players experienced transit delays due to Middle East and Red Sea shipping disruptions.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
*   **Consensus:** Urban demand, modern trade, and quick commerce remain strong structural drivers. Quick commerce is proving to be gross-margin accretive due to higher convenience-food mix (noted by Orkla, Sundrop, Tata Consumer, Mrs. Bectors).
*   **Divergence on Rural Sentiment:** While Marico (via read-across) and others noted resilient consumption, **Mrs. Bectors** and **Bikaji** flagged caution regarding rural sentiment due to below-normal monsoons and high food inflation. Meanwhile, mass snacking and biscuits saw strong impulse pack growth (e.g., Bikaji's INR 5-10 packs up 20%, Britannia's 13% volume growth).

## Costs, raw materials & margins
*   **Raw Material Pressures:** Significant inflation was reported across pulses and bad crops (Bikaji), milk (+16%) and packaging laminates (+18%) (Britannia), palm oil and laminates (Prataap Snacks, Gopal Snacks), spices like chilli and coriander (Orkla, Tata Consumer), and tea inflation of 8–10% (Tata Consumer).
*   **Margin Mitigations:** Companies deployed calibrated price increases (e.g., Mrs. Bectors ~3%, Britannia 3.5%, Tata Consumer 8–10% on tea, Bikaji 3% post-Diwali) and grammage cuts to protect profitability. However, gross margins were compressed for companies unable to pass on inflation immediately (e.g., Prataap Snacks EBITDA margin fell to 2.4%, Foods & Inns posted a forex/logistics loss).

## Capex & capacity
*   **Adf Foods:** Surat greenfield utilization at 15%, targeting 35-40% by FY27 end; Rs 40-45 crore capex remaining for FY27.
*   **Bikaji Foods:** Expanding retail store count (THF) to 40 by year-end, experimenting with smaller 700-800 sq ft formats.
*   **Foods & Inns:** Spray drying capacity expansion (+120 MT/annum) targeted for January 2027 startup; Gujarat warehouse completion by December.
*   **Hindustan Foods:** Commercializing >Rs 300 crores in assets across projects (beverages, ice cream, malt beverages) starting December.
*   **Krishival Foods:** Added 10 MT/day nut kernel capacity (total 20 MT/day), targeting 40 MT/day by March 2028; ramping up Sambhajinagar ice cream plant.
*   **Prataap Snacks:** Acquired ~50 acres near Indore for a greenfield integrated facility (Rs 421-422 crore project) to consolidate 7 smaller units over 15 months.
*   **Tata Consumer:** Expanding direct distribution reach (~1.9M+ stores) and adding Starbucks stores (reached 505).
*   **Vintage Coffee:** Incurred Rs 220 crore toward Phase 1 of its 5,500 MT freeze-dried coffee (FDC) project (commercial production expected H2 FY28).

## What changed since last quarter
*   **Adf Foods:** Lowered FY27 revenue guidance from >Rs 900 crore to Rs 850 crore due to persistent shipping constraints.
*   **Bikaji Foods:** Shifted tone from Q1 operational disruptions to strong snacking momentum; announced a 3% post-Diwali price hike to counter pulse inflation.
*   **Britannia Industries:** Volume growth accelerated from 9% (Q1) to 13% (Q2); General Trade growth accelerated to 2.6x the FY26 rate.
*   **Foods & Inns:** Management declared "the worst is over" following Q2 forex/logistics losses, with 60% of unshipped backlog cleared in October.
*   **Hindustan Foods:** Footwear division successfully turned profitable after clearing legacy fixed-price orders, overcoming a 1.5-month Silvassa flood disruption.
*   **Prataap Snacks:** Faced severe margin compression (~8% negative impact from sudden palm oil/laminate spikes) but saw market share gains as unorganized competitors struggled with RM supplies.

*   **Hindustan Foods:** Demonstrated resilience with 18% revenue growth and a footwear turnaround, absorbing temporary flood disruptions at Silvassa.
