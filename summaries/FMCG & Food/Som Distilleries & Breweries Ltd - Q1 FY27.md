# Som Distilleries & Breweries Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Operational and regulatory disruptions in Madhya Pradesh severely impacted volumes and financial performance, though offset by strong recoveries in Karnataka and Odisha and the commissioning of the Uttar Pradesh unit.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Volumes:** 45.79 lakh cases (Beer: 45 lakh cases, representing ~98.9% of total volume).
- **Beer Revenue Share:** 93% of total revenue.
- **Total Income:** INR 268.8 crores.
- **EBITDA:** INR 15.2 crores.
- **Operating Cash Flow:** ~INR 28 crores generated during the quarter.
- **Gross Debt:** Increased by INR 10 crores during the quarter; Gross debt-to-equity moved from 0.3x in March 2026 to 0.31x in June 2026.
- **UP Facility Investment:** ~INR 300 crores invested in Phase 1 (funded without external debt).
- **IMFL Realization:** Improved by ~3% YoY to INR 1,047 per case.
- **Finished Goods Stock in Bhopal:** ~INR 25 crores.
- **Fixed Cost of Bhopal Plant:** INR 6 crores to INR 7 crores per quarter (including interest, salaries, and electricity).
- **Raw Material Cost Increase:** 7.5% to 8% YoY increase in prices of cans, malt, and bottles.
- **Capacity Utilization:** Hassan facility at ~60%; Odisha facility at ~70%.
- **Annual Beer Capacity Added (UP):** ~10 million cases.
- **Revenue Loss (MP closure):** INR 250 crores to INR 260 crores.

### Highlights
- Q1 was an extremely difficult quarter due to operational and regulatory disruptions and the suspension of the Madhya Pradesh (Bhopal) plant.
- Strong rebound and volume growth seen in Karnataka (30% increase in cases sold) and Odisha (close to 40% increase).
- Successful commissioning and commencement of commercial production at the Uttar Pradesh brewery on June 9, adding ~10 million cases of annual beer capacity.
- Maintained a disciplined balance sheet with minimal increase in leverage despite heavy capital deployment in UP.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management revised revenue guidance down to INR 1,000 crores – INR 1,100 crores for FY27 (previously implied higher based on context of prior expectations).
- Management remains very hopeful that the Madhya Pradesh license issue will be resolved in the current month (August 2026).
- Plans to enter the Indian single malt category before the end of the financial year.
- Phase 2 (distillery) of the UP project execution will have a firmer timeline in 3 to 4 months.
- Andhra Pradesh market entry expected to commence via export from Karnataka by the first week of September.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Beer demand remains robust in functioning markets; Karnataka and Odisha showed strong volume recoveries.
- UP market offers huge growth opportunities due to low per capita consumption and expanding demand.
- The company maintains a portfolio of 5-6 beer brands (Hunter, Black Fort, Power Cool, Sunny Beaches, Legend, Woodpecker) to cater to different market segments and pricing tiers.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material and packaging costs (cans, malt, bottles) saw an average increase of 7.5% to 8% YoY.
- Packaging cans are dependent on a single industry-wide supplier, Ball Corporation.
- Fixed costs for the suspended Bhopal plant run at INR 6 crores to INR 7 crores per quarter; excess manpower is being absorbed into other units like UP, Hassan, and Odisha.
- Excise duty increased due to higher sales mix in Karnataka and Odisha, where excise duty is paid prior to dispatch.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- INR 300 crores invested in Phase 1 of the UP brewery (10 million cases capacity); no pending capex for Phase 1. Peak capacity utilization of the UP plant expected to take another 3 to 4 years.
- Phase 2 (distillery) in UP is currently awaiting permissions.
- Importing from other states to serve Madhya Pradesh is economically unviable due to heavy import fees.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gross debt-to-equity ratio inched up marginally from 0.3x to 0.31x.
- Generated INR 28 crores in operating cash flow during a challenging quarter.
- Receivables risk is minimal as most operations are in markets where the wholesaler is the government.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Suspension and ongoing closure of the Madhya Pradesh manufacturing facility.
- Single-supplier dependency for aluminum cans (Ball Corporation).
- Raw material price volatility (malt, glass bottles, cans).
- Potential loss of consumer mindshare in Madhya Pradesh due to prolonged non-availability of products.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sucrit Patil (Eyesight Fintrade) asked about operational playbook/guidance and managing raw material/regulatory risks.** Management noted that apart from MP being suspended, Hassan and Odisha are operating normally, and cost-control measures are being emphasized to counter industry-wide raw material volatility.
2. **Manoj Pal asked how the company plans to regain market share in Bhopal once operations resume given consumer switching.** Management acknowledged it is a long haul to bring back consumers who moved due to non-availability, but expressed confidence that brand strength, taste, and quality will attract them back over time.
3. **Nagaraj Tripali asked about UP Phase 1 capex, capacity utilization, and status of Phase 2.** Management stated INR 300 crores was spent with no pending capex for Phase 1, commercial production started June 9, utilization is too early to measure, and Phase 2 details will be clear in 3-4 months.
4. **Jitaksh Gupta asked about revenue lost due to the MP plant closure.** Management estimated a revenue loss of INR 250 crores to INR 260 crores.
5. **Hiten Boricha asked about fixed costs incurred on the closed Bhopal plant.** Management stated the fixed cost burden is about INR 6 crores to INR 7 crores per quarter (including interest, salaries, and electricity), part of which is mitigated by redeploying staff to other plants.
6. **Sunil Jatakia asked about revised FY27 revenue and EBITDA margin expectations.** Management guided for INR 1,000 crores to INR 1,100 crores in revenue for FY27, while deferring margin specifics to the next call.

### One-line takeaway
Despite a severe revenue and volume dent from the ongoing suspension of its Madhya Pradesh plant, Som Distilleries remains liquid, expanded its footprint with a new UP facility, and anticipates a swift operational revival.
