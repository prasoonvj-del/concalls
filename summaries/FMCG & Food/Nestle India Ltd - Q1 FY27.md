# Nestle India Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-07 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Analyst & Institutional Investor Meet 2026 (August 2026)  
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a volume-led growth flywheel, accelerated premiumization, distribution expansion, and long-term consumption headroom despite macro/inflation headwinds.

### Key numbers
- **Volume Growth:** FY2026 volume growth was close to 11%; recent quarters consistently delivered double-digit volume growth (5-year volume CAGR was 4.2%).
- **Distribution & Outlets:** Added ~500,000 retail outlets in the last 5 years; rural direct distribution points expanded ~4x since 2021 (from ~13,500 points). Out-of-home business serves ~535 million consumers outside home.
- **Portfolio & Spends:** Premium portfolio contribution expanded from 11% to 14% (growing ~500 bps ahead of overall business). Ad spend increased ~40% YoY in recent quarters (55–60% allocated to digital). Cost savings program reached 2.6% in 2025 (vs 1.8%–1.9% historical run-rate).
- **Capex:** Invested >Rs 6,400 crore (Rs 64 billion) over the last 5 years, including ~Rs 170 crore for a new MAGGI line and ~Rs 225 crore for a Munch line in the last calendar year.
- **Shareholder Return:** Total Shareholder Return (TSR) >15% over the last one year.
- **Operations:** ~96% of ingredients sourced domestically; total workforce stands at ~8,500 employees (with over 500,000 shareholders).

### Highlights
- India is the largest market globally for MAGGI and KITKAT; NESCAFÉ logged its 20th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth.
- Expanding high-growth incubator categories ("Acons"): NESPRESSO (4 boutiques across 3 cities), Purina Pet Care (gaining market share in cat food), and Munch breakfast/snack portfolio.
- Recent key innovations: Nestlé NAN EXCELLAPRO (5 HMOs + probiotics), LACTOGEN relaunch with pre/probiotics, CERELAC ZAS (Zero Added Sucrose), NESCAFÉ Duo Gusto hot/cold dispensing machine, Ready-to-Drink Ice Cap/Vietnamese Latte, and KITKAT Delight/Pops.
- End-to-end supply chain integration completed on SAP S/4HANA using heuristic, ML, and AI models.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident in medium-to-long-term volume-led secular growth driven by low category penetration and growing middle/affluent households.
- Prioritizing volume growth and market penetration over price hikes; cost savings programs will be utilized to sustain historic operational margin levels (management stated they will not chase growth at the expense of margins).

### Demand & industry commentary
- India's consumption story is accelerating with expanding middle/affluent tiers expected to drive spend through 2034.
- Category penetration headroom remains huge: monthly noodle penetration is 35–36% (vs ~100% for biscuits); chocolate/confectionery penetration is 23% (vs 63% overall chocolate category).
- Worksite beverage consumption is shifting from tea to a 50:50 tea-to-coffee ratio; cold coffee accounts for 1 in 4 summer cups (1 in 5 in winter). Out-of-home consumption frequency has doubled over the past decade.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Accelerated structural cost savings (2.6% of sales in 2025) directly funded the ~40% step-up in ad spend.
- Management noted short-term commodity/food inflation, currency fluctuations, energy, packaging, and shipping/geopolitical disruptions, but relies on internal efficiency programs and scale to absorb costs.
- Maintaining entry price points (Rs 2, Rs 5, Rs 10, Rs 20) is treated as vital for rural/mass penetration.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- 5-year capex spend surpassed Rs 6,400 crore; recent additions include Rs 170 crore+ for MAGGI and Rs 225 crore for Munch. 
- Capacity expansions will continue to be deployed to prevent supply bottlenecks behind volume growth.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Disciplined cash-generative flywheel model reinvests operating cash back into brand building and manufacturing capacity.
- Flawless supply chain and packaging execution during GST adjustments prevented sales downsides in Q3 and Q4 of the prior calendar year.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Macro slowdown in general FMCG market growth (as reported by Nielsen).
- Input cost volatility across oil, packaging, energy, and elevated food inflation.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Abneesh Roy (Nuvama):** What drove the 40-50% step-up in ad spends over three consecutive quarters?
  - *Management Answer:* Spends are backed by high ROAS/ROI (55-60% spent on digital) behind core brands and funded by internal structural cost savings (2.6%). Spend growth will normalize from 40% as base effects kick in, but investment support will continue.
- **Latika Chopra (Goldman Sachs):** Can double-digit volume growth and margins be sustained?
  - *Management Answer:* Low category penetration provides secular headroom. Efficiency programs will allow the company to maintain historical margin profiles without compromising volume expansion.
- **Arnab Mitra (Goldman Sachs):** What is the outlook for the Milk & Nutrition segment?
  - *Management Answer:* Volume growth in dairy/nutrition has turned positive in recent quarters, supported by execution and global R&D (HMOs/probiotics).
- **Avi Mehta (Macquarie):** What is consumer traction on CERELAC following the sugar discussions?
  - *Management Answer:* CERELAC always met FSSAI standards, but Nestlé introduced a Zero Added Sucrose (ZAS) option to give mothers a choice; both ZAS and standard portfolios are tracking well.
- **Nitin Gupta:** Is management open to inorganic M&A vs organic growth?
  - *Management Answer:* While open to evaluating opportunities, 90% of focus remains on organic growth given the substantial headroom in current categories.

### One-line takeaway
Nestlé India is driving a volume-led flywheel powered by distribution reach, digital ad reinvestments, and high-margin premiumization while protecting profitability through structured cost savings.
