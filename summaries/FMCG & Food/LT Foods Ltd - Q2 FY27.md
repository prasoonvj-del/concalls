# LT Foods Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered healthy broad-based revenue and volume growth across key geographies, supported by strong core basmati performance and improved working capital efficiencies, despite headwinds from higher freight costs and increased brand investments.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** Rs 3,253 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 17% YoY, 3% QoQ); H1 FY27 revenue at Rs 6,414 crore (up 22% YoY).
- **Gross Profit:** Rs 1,058 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 12% YoY); Gross margin at 32.5% (vs 34.2% in Q2 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 358 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 13% YoY); EBITDA margin at 11% (vs 11.4% in Q2 FY26).
- **PAT:** Rs 180 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 10% YoY); H1 FY27 PAT at Rs 364 crore (up 9% YoY).
- **Basmati & Specialty Rice H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 5,764 crore (up 27% YoY, volume up 11%), contributing 90% of consolidated revenue.
- **Geographical H1 FY27 Growth:** India revenue up 24% YoY (volume up 10%, 29% revenue share); North America revenue up 21% YoY (50% revenue share); Middle East & ROW revenue up 25% YoY (9% revenue share); Europe contributed 12%.
- **Organic & Ingredient Segment H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 520 crore (vs Rs 583 crore in H1 FY26); Q2 EBITDA at Rs 22 crore (vs Rs 10 crore in Q1).
- **Health & Convenience H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 102 crore (up 8% YoY).
- **Balance Sheet:** Net cash position of Rs 247 crore as of September 26. Working capital days improved to 143 days (vs 174 days in H1 FY26). Inventory days reduced to 159 days (vs 187 days). ROCE improved to 22.4% (vs 22% YoY).

### Highlights
- India branded portfolio grew ~8% against category growth of 1.3%, driving a 140 bps market share increase on a year-to-date basis. Household reach expanded to 67.5 lakhs from 56.2 lakhs.
- Project Vistara expanded direct distribution, feet-on-street capabilities, and assortment productivity in India. Biryani kits grew 38% in H1.
- Nature Bio Foods is undergoing strategic transformation into a globally integrated consumer-focused organic platform, consolidating manufacturing via a new Madhya Pradesh facility (first phase starting this FY).
- Europe secured new mainstream private label business in Germany and Nordic markets, and reduced debt by EUR 1.8 million in H1.
- Standalone H1 FY27 revenue grew 14% to Rs 2,417 crore, with EBITDA up 34% to Rs 223 crore (margin 9.2%).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management is confident in the business model and expects H2 performance to maintain H1 momentum.
- Full-year advertisement spend is guided at 2.3% of revenue.
- Organic business is targeted to deliver a 10% to 11% EBITDA margin over the coming 2 years.
- Consolidated EBITDA margin is targeted to reach 13% to 13.5% to 14% over the medium term as investing markets (Europe, UK, Middle East, Organic) achieve scale (matured markets already yield 15%+ EBITDA margins).
- UK business is expected to turn profitable next year as scale builds.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global basmati category is growing at 5% to 7% annually. Basmati accounts for ~10 million tonnes out of a total global rice market of ~500 million tonnes (~2%).
- Management noted no negative demand impact from price increases or sharp freight cost hikes.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margin moderated (32.5% vs 34.2%) due to shipment term changes (CIF to FOB shifting freight to COGS, EBITDA neutral) and higher shipping costs (rising ~40 bps as % of revenue).
- EBITDA margin moderated to 11% due to gross margin factors and planned step-up in brand investments (ad spend increased by ~70 bps YoY to 2.5% of revenue in Q2).
- New crop '26 paddy prices opened higher by 22% to 25% on average compared to last year. Current inventory: paddy at Rs 43/kg (vs Rs 36 in March '26), rice at Rs 60/kg (vs Rs 54 in March '26).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- First phase of the new Madhya Pradesh facility for Nature Bio Foods is expected to commence operations in FY27.
- Investments ongoing in RTH facility in the U.S. and building health/convenience portfolios.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash positive at Rs 247 crore as of September 26.
- Inventory days improved to 159 days; trade payable days at 26 days. Working capital days improved to 143 days.
- Europe business reduced debt by EUR 1.8 million in H1. Interest coverage stands at 13.8x.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Middle East operations were significantly impacted by supply disruptions constraining inventory flow and sharp escalation in freight costs, weighing on profitability.
- Operating environment in Europe and UK remains challenging with lower-than-planned mainstream/industrial volumes and elevated operating costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Bhavi Chauhan (Care PMS) asked about current inventory levels, costs, and crop pricing trends.** Management stated paddy inventory is 82,000 tonnes at an average rate of Rs 43/kg and rice inventory is 260,000 tonnes at Rs 60/kg. Crop '26 paddy prices are higher by 22% to 25% YoY, but existing inventory is sufficient for business plans.
2. **Saurabh Beria (Motilal Oswal) asked about the timeline and targets for EBITDA margin recovery.** Management stated they are confident in achieving a consolidated EBITDA margin of 13% to 14% over the medium term once investing regions (Europe, UK, Middle East, Organic) scale up, noting mature markets already deliver 15%+ EBITDA.
3. **Vipul Kumar (Sumangal Investment) asked if the substantial working capital release was a seasonal phenomenon.** Management confirmed it is seasonal as Q2 is a non-peak procurement period.
4. **Premal Shah asked about the basmati crop outlook and finished product pricing.** Management stated crop prices opened ~25% higher, and finished product prices will proportionately reset (premium products already reflecting ~15% YoY increase).
5. **Saurabh Beria asked about forex impact on growth.** Management stated forex impact accounts for roughly 4% to 5% of the value growth due to a 50-50 hedging policy and rupee depreciation benefits.

### One-line takeaway
LT Foods reported healthy H1 FY27 revenue growth of 22% driven by strong core basmati demand and working capital improvements, though profitability faced near-term pressure from freight inflation and higher brand investments.
