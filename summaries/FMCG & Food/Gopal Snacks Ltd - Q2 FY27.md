# Gopal Snacks Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue and profitability improved due to restored manufacturing capabilities and wider distribution.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations (Q2 FY27):** Rs 461.5 crore, up 22.9% YoY and 9.3% QoQ.
- **Revenue from operations (H1 FY27):** Rs 883.9 crore, up 26.7% YoY.
- **Gross profit (Q2 FY27):** Rs 125 crore, up 26.1% YoY; Gross margin at 27.1% vs 26.4% in Q2 FY26.
- **Gross profit (H1 FY27):** Rs 238.6 crore, up 30.5% YoY; Gross margin at 27% vs 26.2% in H1 FY26.
- **EBITDA (Q2 FY27):** Rs 41.6 crore, up 72% YoY and 32% QoQ; EBITDA margin at 9% vs 6.4% in Q2 FY26.
- **EBITDA (H1 FY27):** Rs 73 crore, up 85.7% YoY; EBITDA margin at 8.3% vs 5.6%.
- **Profit before tax before exceptional items (Q2 FY27):** Rs 25.9 crore, up 93% YoY.
- **Profit after tax (Q2 FY27):** Rs 45.9 crore (includes net exceptional gain of Rs 35.3 crore); PAT margin at 9.9%.
- **Profit after tax (H1 FY27):** Rs 58.7 crore vs Rs 28.2 crore in H1 FY26; PAT margin at 6.6%.
- **Category growth (Q2 FY27 YoY):** Gathiya sales up 25.6%, wafers up 40%, Snack Pellets and extruded snacks up 23%.
- **Distributor network:** 1,057 at end of September vs 1,007 at end of June and 953 at end of FY26 (130 distributors added in H1). Additionally 150 micro distributors under the super stockist model.
- **Insurance claim:** Full and final claim of Rs 36.4 crore received in October 2026 relating to fire incident; total recovery is Rs 77.4 crore.
- **Capacity utilisation:** Around 37%-38% at the company level.
- **A&P spending (H1 FY27):** Close to 1% of revenue.

### Highlights
- Restoration of manufacturing capabilities and expanded distribution network drove revenue and profitability.
- Besan manufacturing unit at Nagpur commenced operations in September, reducing transportation costs and bringing key inputs closer.
- Dual-servicing of outlets in Gujarat reached 43.5% by October end (up from 38% previously); targeted dealers saw run-rate increases of 15% to 30%.
- Non-palm oil-based products contributed 3.25% of top line in H1 FY27, targeted to reach 4.5% by year-end and 6.5% next year.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains FY27 top-line guidance of Rs 1,800 crore to Rs 1,900 crore with an EBITDA margin of 8.9%.
- Management aspires to achieve double-digit EBITDA margins once the war scenario eases and raw material inflation normalises.
- Management expects minimum 20% annual growth going forward, driven by network expansion (contributing 6% to 7%) and organic growth/new products.
- New channels (Exports, Quick Commerce, Modern Trade, Railways) expected to reach Rs 30 crore - Rs 35 crore this year and Rs 75 crore - Rs 90 crore by next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Demand remains strong; current trend in October/recent months shows 22%-23% growth momentum.
- In focus states (Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan), wafers grew 59% and Snack Pellets grew 43%.
- Namkeen H1 volume growth is in single digits at 8.6% (value growth 18.3%), impacted by grammage reductions where Gopal Snacks is not the market leader.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material inflationary impact on gross margin from Q1 to Q2 was 2.3%, offset by 1.1% grammage correction, 0.4% price increase, and 0.4% from lower trade discounts/product mix.
- RM basket breakdown in H1: Palm oil 27%, Laminates (packaging) 22%-23%, Chana 19%. Potato and chana inventories last until mid-December and mid-January respectively.
- Nagpur besan plant expected to save approximately Rs 3 crore per annum in transportation costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Joint ventures: Manendragarh JV doing Rs 3.5 crore - Rs 4 crore per month (run out of capacity); Kashipur doing Rs 1.25 crore per month; Hiriyur doing Rs 0.85 crore per month.
- Planning to add 25-30 ready-stock distributors for Western snacks in Gujarat in Q4.
- Total distributor additions expected around 220 for the full calendar year.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Inventory currently around Rs 200 crore, expected to reduce to Rs 100 crore - Rs 120 crore in H2.
- Cash flow from operations (CFO) in H2 expected to be better than H1 due to insurance proceeds (Rs 36.5 crore received in October) and inventory liquidation.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- High laminate and palm oil prices continue to pose inflationary pressures on margins, particularly troubling for smaller unorganised players.
- Competition intensity involves aggressive cash burn by some local players, though management believes it is unsustainable long-term.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Anuj D. (Antique Stock Broking) on Gujarat run-rate:** Management stated they are not satisfied with the sub-Rs 100 crore per month run-rate in Gujarat due to competitive intensity and prudent pricing, but will not chase revenue at the cost of margins.
- **Nitin (HDFC Securities) on volume vs value growth:** Management explained that Gathiya (hero product) absorbs grammage corrections well without hurting volume, unlike certain Namkeen sub-segments where they are not leaders.
- **Soham Samanta (Motilal Oswal) on 20% growth sustainability:** Management clarified that 6%-7% growth comes predictably from scaling newly added distributors (adding ~130 in H1), creating a compounding base for future years.
- **Resham Mehta (Green Edge Wealth) on new channels:** Management expects new-age channels (Quick Commerce, Modern Trade, Railways, Exports) to scale from Rs 30-35 crore currently to Rs 75-90 crore next year, backed by dedicated healthier snack launches in December.
- **Saurabh Beria (Sameeksha Capital) on cash-burning competitors:** Management noted that smaller regional players are feeling the pinch of sustained inflation, citing past closures of brands like Star and Miraj, making cash burn unsustainable.

### One-line takeaway
Gopal Snacks delivered robust H1 revenue growth of 26.7% and margin expansion via selective price/grammage adjustments and backward integration, while maintaining full-year guidance of Rs 1,800-1,900 crore top-line.
