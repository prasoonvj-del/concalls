# Hindustan Foods Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a resilient performance, return to profitability in the footwear division, and healthy volume growth despite a 1.5-month operational disruption from flooding at the Silvassa facility.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained (FY27 PAT target of more than Rs 200 crores).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from dealing with footwear cost pressures and raw material spikes to recovery and profitability in footwear, with an order book full till March '27.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Silvassa flooding impact quantified (1.5 months disruption, Rs 1.3 crore exceptional loss); transition of select customer businesses from gross revenue to net revenue model to address GST inversion; new projects announced (carbonated soft drinks in Guwahati, ice cream cones/sleeves in Nashik). Old Middle East crisis/polymer raw material cost spike issues in footwear were resolved via revised pricing on fresh orders and completion of legacy fixed-price orders.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Footwear returned to profitability in Q2 as promised; projects faced minor weather/geopolitical-related timing shifts pushing some revenue contribution from Q3 to Q4.

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** Rs 1,269 crore (up 18% YoY)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 115 crore (up 26% YoY)
- **PBT (before exceptional items):** Rs 61 crore (up 25% YoY)
- **Adjusted PAT:** Rs 46 crore (up 22% YoY)
- **Reported PAT:** Rs 45 crore (after Rs 1.3 crore exceptional loss from Silvassa flooding)
- **H1 FY27 Cash Flow from Operations:** More than Rs 57 crore
- **H1 FY27 Adjusted PAT:** Rs 89 crore (up 30% YoY)

### Highlights
- Silvassa facility faced heavy flooding resulting in ~1.5 months of operational disruption; operations have since normalized.
- Footwear division returned to profitability in Q2 after completing legacy fixed-price orders and securing fresh orders at revised prices; October '26 recorded the highest-ever monthly turnover for the division.
- Appointed dedicated business heads across all divisions for sharper focus and ownership.
- Transitioned select customers to a net revenue model to reduce GST input tax credit accumulation and lower working capital requirements without impacting absolute profitability.
- Capital Markets Day planned for early December 2026.
- *Continues pattern from record:* Overcoming temporary operational disruptions (Silvassa flood) via platform diversification, similar to how past segment-specific headwinds were absorbed.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects a strong second half of FY27 and sustained earnings growth into FY28 and beyond.
- Starting from December, assets worth more than Rs 300 crores will be commercialized across various projects.
- Management is confident of closing the year with more than Rs 200 crores PAT.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Healthy volume growth across all verticals supported by a favorable demand environment and full order books (footwear order book full till March '27).
- Broad project pipeline remains strong and expanding.
- Recent retail-level food safety inspections are viewed by management as a positive structural driver that will cause brands to migrate to organized contract manufacturers.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Footwear division addressed cost pressures from higher raw materials and revised wage rates by completing legacy fixed-price orders and shifting new orders to revised pricing alongside shop floor efficiencies.
- Inverted duty structure under GST caused input tax credit accumulation and locked up working capital; transitioning customers to a net revenue model helps reduce material-related GST outflow and inventory requirements.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Seasonal product category projects (beverages and ice creams) across Aurangabad, Mysuru, Hyderabad, and Panipat are progressing to be ready for the season.
- Brownfield expansion for malt beverages at Coimbatore and Ayurvedic supplement project at Baddi progressing for Q4 FY27 commercialization.
- New carbonated soft drinks line at Guwahati expected to commence production in Q4 FY27.
- Ice cream manufacturing capabilities expanded with an additional line for cones and sleeves at Nashik, expected in Q1 FY28.
- Additional capacity expansion work started at the existing Chennai plant for sports shoes.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- H1 FY27 cash flow from operations stood at >Rs 57 crores, showing significant improvement over H2 FY26 negative operating cash flow.
- Further cash flow improvements expected in H2 FY27 from additional customer transitions to the net revenue model starting Q4 FY27 and recent GST changes easing inverted duty structures.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unforeseen geopolitical and weather-related issues leading to minor project commissioning delays.
- Temporary operational disruption and one-time exceptional losses due to flooding at the Silvassa facility (Rs 1.3 crore impact).

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Adithya Srinivasan (ithought PMS) asked about shared vs. dedicated EBITDA margins and ROCE:** Management clarified that the increase in shared EBITDA share was primarily due to the footwear business turning profitable, and normalized dedicated ROCE is expected around 16% to 22% with shared being higher.
- **Mayur Parkeria (Scripbox Wealth Managers) asked for quantification of lost revenue/EBITDA from Silvassa, project delays, and net revenue model:** Management stated that project delays and Silvassa disruption cost around Rs 3 crores to Rs 4 crores in extra EBITDA for Q2, while the net revenue model transition is EBITDA-neutral.
- **Mayur Parkeria asked about footwear outlook for FY28 and BIS regulations:** Management stated BIS regulations are behind them, order books are full till March '27, and they are expanding capacity in Chennai by ~20% based on structural confidence.
- **Mayur Parkeria asked about risks from retail-level food and beverage regulatory inspections:** Management viewed this as a structural tailwind that will drive brands toward organized contract manufacturers like Hindustan Foods.
- **Rahul Maheshwary (Ambit Investment Advisors) asked about pricing power across categories:** Management explained pricing power depends on product sophistication and capex intensity rather than broad categories.
- **Riddhansh Chandak (Unifi AMC) asked why the healthcare segment gross block hasn't grown as fast:** Management stated they have large headroom and existing capacity in Baddi and Chennai that needs to be fully utilized before expanding gross block.

### One-line takeaway
Hindustan Foods reported resilient Q2 performance with an 18% revenue and 22% adjusted PAT growth, driven by the footwear division's turnaround and portfolio diversification, despite temporary flooding disruptions at Silvassa.
