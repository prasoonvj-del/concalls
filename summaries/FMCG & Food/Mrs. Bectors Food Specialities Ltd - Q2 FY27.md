# Mrs. Bectors Food Specialities Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · FMCG & Food*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered highest-ever quarterly revenue and robust profit growth, successfully mitigating steep input cost inflation through pricing and cost controls.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained mid-teens revenue growth for the full year.
- Tone and demand view: Slightly more cautious on rural sentiment due to below-normal monsoons and steeper food inflation, though urban and quick commerce demand remained resilient.
- New topics or risks: West Asia conflict severely impacting vessel availability and freight rates for exports; new product launches in sourdough and zero-maida pav.
- Last quarter's promises: Kolkata and Khopoli units scaled up well as planned.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 630.5 crore, up 14.3% YoY and 14.9% QoQ (highest-ever quarterly revenue).
- EBITDA: Rs 82.5 crore, up 19.1% YoY.
- EBITDA margin: 13.1%, up 50 bps YoY and flat QoQ.
- PAT: Rs 45.7 crore, up 25.2% (or 25.3%) YoY; PAT margin at 7.3%.
- H1 FY27 Revenue: Rs 1,179.3 crore, up 15.1% YoY.
- H1 FY27 EBITDA: Rs 154.6 crore, up 21.2% YoY (margin 13.1%, up 70 bps YoY).
- H1 FY27 PAT: Rs 84.5 crore, up 25.4% YoY (margin 7.2%).
- Outlet additions: Added over 30,000 outlets toward a 40,000 target, reaching ~345,000 outlets in the defined geography.

### Highlights
- Biscuit business (domestic and exports, excluding agencies) grew in low teens; bakery business sustained high-teens growth.
- New product launches: English Oven entered Mumbai pav segment with Zero Maida Pav and peanut butter on-the-go brownie; NaturBaked launched pre-sliced whole wheat sourdough; Cremica launched coconut cream.
- Quick commerce contribution reached 22% across B2C businesses; organized trade in bakery reached 43%-44% in Q2.
- Kolkata and Khopoli bakery units scaled up well.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects full-year revenue growth in mid-teens (mid-to-high teens for H2).
- EBITDA margin target: maintain around current levels in Q3, and improve towards 13.5%-14% in Q4.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Below-normal monsoon weighed on rural sentiment, and sharp food inflation squeezed household budgets.
- Domestic demand supported by premium/value-added portfolio, modern trade, quick commerce, and institutional business recovery.
- International export growth tapered slightly due to freight and vessel constraints from the West Asia conflict, but U.S. momentum sustained.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Headline consumer inflation rose to 5.5% in September 2026, driven by edible oils, sugar, cereals, and crude oil (impacting packaging and freight).
- Gross margin dipped slightly from Q1 (1% gap) due to RM and PM inflation; price increase of ~3% implemented in Q2.
- Project IMPACT and price actions offset most input cost pressures.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Kolkata and Khopoli plants scaling up; ongoing focus on distribution expansion and regional penetration.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- *Not discussed in detail.*

### Risks & concerns flagged
- West Asia conflict impacting vessel availability and freight rates for exports.
- Below-normal monsoon and sharp food inflation putting pressure on consumer wallets.
- Intense competition in the biscuit category across North and Central India.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Binay Shukla (PhillipCapital India) asked about biscuit competition and succession planning:** Management noted high competition from large and regional players, added 30,000 outlets, and stated Suvir Bector will oversee domestic biscuits alongside exports.
- **Harit Kapoor (Investec) asked about bread growth breakdown and export slowdown:** Management replied English Oven volume grew in mid-teens and value in high-teens; exports slowed temporarily due to West Asia shipping/freight delays, not relationship issues.
- **Aachal Pal (Monarch Networth Capital) asked about price hikes and outlet reach:** Management stated a ~3% price hike was taken in Q2, expects 5%-6% for the full year, and targets 900,000 to 1 million biscuit outlets by 2030.
- **Amit Purohit (Elara Capital) asked about quick commerce margins:** Management confirmed quick commerce margins are better due to operational efficiencies and technology-led procurement, despite higher marketing investments.
- **Saurabh (LKP Securities) asked about geographic mix and down-trading risks:** Management stated North India will remain 75%-80% of revenue, and down-trading is mitigated by offering price points from Rs 10 to Rs 70+ across portfolios.

### One-line takeaway
Mrs. Bectors delivered its highest-ever quarterly revenue of Rs 630.5 crore with a 19.1% jump in EBITDA, successfully countering steep raw material inflation through agile pricing and cost controls.
