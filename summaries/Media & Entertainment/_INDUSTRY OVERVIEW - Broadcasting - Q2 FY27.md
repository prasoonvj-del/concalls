# Media & Entertainment - Broadcasting: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Indian broadcasting, radio, and cinema distribution sectors reported a mixed Q2 FY27. Topline growth was heavily driven by non-traditional streams, digital expansion, and strategic inorganic acquisitions, as traditional linear radio and TV advertising faced subdued demand due to macroeconomic headwinds, high inflation, and a shift in festive dates into Q3. In response, players across radio and cinema relied heavily on structural cost-rationalization measures to protect and expand EBITDA margins, while cable and digital streaming platforms aggressively deployed capital into OTT bundling, high-profile sports acquisitions, and regional M&A to drive subscription revenues.

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian media and broadcasting relied on linear advertising revenues (FCT - Free Commercial Time) and physical cable/box distribution networks. Over the past 5 years, the model has shifted dramatically toward subscription-led revenues, digital OTT monetization, experiential events, and digital distribution aggregation. [Background]

**Shifts underway**
- **Digital streaming & sports rights driving subscription scaling:** Large networks are using marquee global sports properties and digital platform upgrades to drive paid subscriber acquisition and higher retention. Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd doubled ZEE5 revenues YoY (to INR 6.3 billion) and grew subscription revenues by 30% YoY, leveraging sports rights like the FIFA World Cup (which reached 400M+ viewers across platforms) alongside 35-40% subscriber retention post-tournament [Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. (Stage: Scaling)
- **Pivot to non-FCT, experiential, and regional non-traditional monetisation:** Radio broadcasters are reducing dependence on traditional airtime (FCT) by expanding into branded content, concerts, and digital audio platforms. Entertainment Network (India) Ltd now derives 29% of domestic revenues from digital and sees events contributing INR 20 crores [Entertainment Network (India) Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. Music Broadcast Ltd is actively driving concerts and experiential events in Tier 2/3 markets to bolster its 18-20% non-FCT share [Music Broadcast Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. (Stage: Scaling)
- **Broadband-cable bundling and HITS migration to curb churn:** Cable operators are completing fiber network migrations (GPON/FTTH) and leveraging Headend-in-the-Sky (HITS) platforms to aggregate cable, broadband, and OTT, prioritizing churn reduction over pure ad monetization. GTPL Hathway converted >60% of its network to HITS, expanded broadband ARPU to Rs 470, and used bundling primarily to retain subscribers [GTPL Hathway Ltd concall Q2 FY27]. (Stage: Mainstream)
- **Inorganic consolidation in regional cable assets:** Sector leaders are acquiring smaller regional MSOs to consolidate subscriber bases and scale margins. GTPL Hathway acquired the ACT Group cable TV business (Rs 35.55 crores, 1.25M primary subscribers) and the remaining 74% stake in DL GTPL Cabnet (INR 83.25 million) [GTPL Hathway Ltd concall Q2 FY27; News report 21 Nov 2026]. (Stage: Scaling)

**Government push**
- *Regulatory reforms for radio:* Broadcasters reported ongoing positive discussions with the government regarding regulatory reforms to improve radio business return ratios [Entertainment Network (India) Ltd concall Q2 FY27].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
- **Consensus:** Managements universally cited a soft or subdued traditional advertising environment in Q2 FY27, impacted by high inflation, a weaker monsoon, and a shift in the festive calendar (Diwali moving late into Q3), which deferred ad spending into the second half of the year. However, all expect H2 FY27 demand to pick up sharply due to festive tailwinds and content slates.
- **Divergence:**
  - *Radio vs. Digital/Cable Growth:* Traditional radio advertising volume/utilization fell YoY (ENIL inventory utilization dropped 6% to ~70%; FCT revenue down 12.6%), whereas cable TV/broadband subscription revenues saw expansion (GTPL Hathway total income rose 19% YoY; Zee subscription revenue grew 30% YoY).
  - *Cinema Audience Behavior:* UFO Moviez noted highly selective audience behavior in theaters—where sleeper hits succeeded while major titles underperformed—making advertising revenue delivery dependent on bundled multi-film packages rather than individual film bets.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Radio Broadcasters (ENIL, Music Broadcast):** Both aggressively implemented structural cost controls to offset top-line weakness. Music Broadcast cut total expenses by 20% YoY in Q2 (saving INR 1.6 crores in other expenses), achieving a positive operating profit before tax of INR 1.5 crores (vs a loss of INR 13.3 crores YoY). ENIL expanded EBITDA by 9.5% YoY despite a 16.1% drop in domestic revenue by cutting digital losses at Gaana by 23.3% YoY.
- **Digital & TV Networks (Zee Entertainment):** Zee's operating expenses rose 18% YoY due to elevated programming costs for marquee sports (FIFA World Cup) and heavy digital ad spends, resulting in a low overall EBITDA margin of 3.2% (though digital business delivered INR 416 million in EBITDA).
- **Cable & Broadband (GTPL Hathway):** Operational EBITDA margin stood at 20% (down from 22% YoY) due to initial lower margins of acquired assets (ACT operating at 12% margin). Transponder amortization (Rs 30 million) and notional forex losses (Rs 45 million) dragged net profit to a negative Rs 50 lakh.
- **Cinema Distribution (UFO Moviez):** Direct operating costs jumped 28% YoY, primarily due to a 66% spike in COGS for equipment sales (INR 32 crore vs INR 19 crore) alongside a INR 1 crore increase in manpower performance incentives.

## Capex & capacity
- **GTPL Hathway:** Finished network migration to GPON/FTTH; broadband home passes reached 6 million. Management noted that current user data consumption (436 GB/month) requires no fresh capex, and doubling traffic in the future would only require minimal incremental spend (~10%).
- **Entertainment Network (India) Ltd:** Deployed capex primarily into digital technology for Gaana. Radio capacity utilization dropped to ~70%. Cash reserves decreased to INR 371.5 crores due to event vendor advances, dividends, and seasonal digital tech capex.
- **Zee Entertainment:** Invested heavily in content rights (FIFA World Cup through 2034, Bundesliga, Serie A) and tech upgrades (revamped ZEE5 UI/UX, Trinetra AI filmmaking platform). Received INR 7.4 billion in fresh capital from promoter warrant allotments to support investments.

## What changed since last quarter
- **Tone Shifts & Guidance Shifts:**
  - *GTPL Hathway:* Broadband subscriber additions slowed down temporarily QoQ (10,000 additions vs historical >20,000/quarter) due to ground-level FTTH network migration and leadership restructuring, but management explicitly committed to reverting to >20,000 additions per quarter in H2.
  - *Entertainment Network (India) Ltd:* Delayed its Gaana D2C EBITDA breakeven target by 1 to 2 quarters due to elevated customer acquisition costs driven by AI search changes reducing referral traffic.
  - *Music Broadcast Ltd:* Withdrew directional medium-term guidance for the next 1–2 years, citing market and geopolitical uncertainties, despite maintaining a flat INR 39-40 crore quarterly cost run-rate.

## What to watch next quarter
- **Zee Entertainment:** Sustainability of ZEE5 subscription growth and viewer retention (target: 35–40%) post the FIFA World Cup, alongside the commercial performance of theatrical acquisitions ("Drishyam 3").
- **GTPL Hathway:** Integration progress of the ACT Group cable TV business (scaling margins from 12% to target 25-26%) and post-migration rebound in broadband net additions (>20,000/quarter).
- **UFO Moviez:** Announcement of an official board-approved capital distribution policy (dividends or share buybacks) in Q3/Q4.
- **ENIL & Music Broadcast:** Q3 festive ad spend rebound in radio FCT and margin contribution from non-FCT/concert events in Tier 2/3 cities.
