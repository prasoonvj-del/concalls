# Media & Entertainment - Print media: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
Print media companies navigated a festive season shift (Navaratra and Diwali moving ~20 days into Q3), displacing significant advertising revenue from Q2 into Q3. Despite this timing headwind, core operating revenues grew modestly across news publishers (HT Media and HMVL up 5% YoY; D. B. Corp stable on a like-to-like basis), while educational publisher Dachepalli Publishers recorded strong top-line expansion (+74% YoY in H1 FY27). Profitability across newsprint operators was constrained by a ~20–21% YoY spike in newsprint costs and currency depreciation, prompting strict cover-price discipline, flat circulation copy targets, cost rationalization, and the closure or surrendering of cash-draining non-print operations (e.g., HT Media and HMVL shutting OTTplay and surrendering non-viable radio station licenses).

## Where the industry is heading
**Business model so far** - Traditionally, Indian print media companies depended heavily on high-volume advertising revenues cross-subsidizing physical circulation built through low cover prices, while educational publishers relied on third-party trading of supplementary goods. Over the past five years, the model has pivoted toward profitability-focused circulation (avoiding low-margin/unprofitable copy pushing), direct digital monetization (apps and digital subscriptions), and pruning non-core legacy assets [Background].

**Shifts underway**
- **Pruning loss-making digital and peripheral businesses**: Scaling. Publishers are surrendering non-viable radio licenses and exiting loss-making digital experiments to turn non-print segments operationally profitable. HT Media and HMVL stopped their OTTplay business, surrendered non-viable radio licenses to achieve positive radio EBITDA (Rs 2 crore vs loss previously), and brought digital operations to breakeven/positive Rs 1 crore EBITDA [HT Media Ltd concall Q2 FY27; Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
- **In-house manufacturing & ecosystem bundling**: Scaling. Educational publishers are shifting from low-margin trading to in-house manufacturing and direct bundled school offerings. Dachepalli Publishers launched its "PageCraft" notebook brand via acquired machinery to improve PAT yield from 6–7% (trading) to ~12% (in-house), bundled textbooks and supplies via "Pelican EduSupply" across 100 schools, and partnered with Wiki Kids for smart classroom displays [Dachepalli Publishers Ltd concall Q2 FY27].
- **Direct digital subscription pricing over physical cover price hikes**: Early. News publishers are experimenting with direct app price hikes while freezing physical cover prices to prevent circulation copy erosion. D. B. Corp increased its app subscription price from INR 149 to INR 199 on an experimental basis while intentionally reducing marketing spend to test organic sustainability, keeping physical circulation copies flat at ~3.8 million [D. B. Corp Ltd concall Q2 FY27].

**Government push**
- **DAVP Ad Price Revisions**: Pricing revisions in government advertising (DAVP) provided an ad revenue impetus across English and Hindi titles [Govt: DAVP price increase, Nov 2026; HT Media Ltd concall Q2 FY27; Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd concall Q2 FY27].

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
**Consensus:** All news print managements (D. B. Corp, HT Media, HMVL) agree that underlying advertising demand is stable, with Q2 revenue trends impacted by the 20-day festive shift into Q3. They expect stronger ad delivery in H2 FY27. Managements also agree on maintaining flat physical copy circulation rather than aggressively pushing low-margin copies during elevated newsprint cost cycles.

**Divergence:**
- **Category Trajectory**: D. B. Corp highlighted double-digit advertising growth in jewelry, healthcare, and BFSI/IPOs (~50%), single-digit growth in real estate and education, but declines in FMCG (-2%), lifestyle, and hypermarkets due to festive timing. In contrast, HT Media and HMVL reported broader print ad growth across both English (+5%) and Hindi (+11%) titles, aided heavily by government DAVP rate hikes and volume consolidation.
- **Publisher Growth Differential**: News publishers delivered low-single-digit revenue adjustments or growth (+5% YoY), whereas educational publisher Dachepalli Publishers experienced 74% YoY revenue growth in H1 FY27, driven by multi-state geographic expansion, school onboarding, and repeat military school orders.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
- **Paper & Newsprint Inflation**: Raw material costs remain significantly elevated YoY across the sector. D. B. Corp reported average newsprint costs up ~21% YoY (from INR 47,000/tonne to INR 57,000/tonne). Dachepalli Publishers noted market paper prices at ~Rs 93/kg (with potential to hit Rs 100). HT Media and HMVL noted margin compression from newsprint costs compounded by rupee depreciation.
- **Cost Mitigation Strategies**:
  - *Advance Buying*: Dachepalli secured 90% of its annual paper requirement in advance at Rs 78–81/kg, insulating margins against spot rates of Rs 93.
  - *Price Softening Outlook*: D. B. Corp expects newsprint prices to soften 2% to 3% in Q3 and Q4, though remaining 18% to 20% higher YoY. HT Media and HMVL view newsprint prices as having peaked out, though lower freight and currency relief are needed for margin expansion.
  - *Internal Cost Rationalization*: HT Media and HMVL lowered consolidated employee expenses to Rs 95 crore (vs Rs 99 crore QoQ and Rs 105 crore YoY). Dachepalli plans a 10% MRP price hike.

## Capex & capacity
- **D. B. Corp Ltd**: No major capex planned for the next 4 to 5 quarters as plant and equipment are fully in place.
- **Dachepalli Publishers Ltd**: Expanding in-house notebook capacity to Rs 30–40 crore output. In talks to acquire Hara Communications for Rs 10–15 crore (asset value Rs 10+ crore) funded via receivables and a Rs 7–8 crore bank loan to gain land, software, and production capacity.
- **HT Media Ltd / Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd**: Capex was not discussed during the call.

## What changed since last quarter
- **HT Media Ltd / Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd**: Closed down the OTTplay business to eliminate cash burn. Surrendered loss-making radio station licenses, moving Radio operating EBITDA to a positive Rs 2 crore. Reduced consolidated employee expenses sequentially to Rs 95 crore (vs Rs 99 crore QoQ). Management acknowledged active conversations with ~10% shareholder Matthew Cyriac.
- **D. B. Corp Ltd**: Increased app subscription price from INR 149 to INR 199 on an experimental basis; intentionally curtailed digital marketing spends to verify organic app MAU retention.
- **Dachepalli Publishers Ltd**: Transitioned notebook operations from third-party trading to in-house manufacturing under the "PageCraft" brand, and launched the "Pelican EduSupply" direct school bundling platform across 100 schools.

## What to watch next quarter
- **Festive Rebound (Q3 FY27)**: Quantum of advertising revenue recovery in Q3 following the ~INR 40 crore festive shift at D. B. Corp and the 20-day shift at HT Media/HMVL.
- **Newsprint & Paper Pricing**: Realization of D. B. Corp’s guided 2–3% sequential newsprint price softening and paper price movement relative to Dachepalli's Rs 100/kg risk threshold.
- **In-House Notebook Margins & M&A**: Margin realization on Dachepalli’s "PageCraft" brand (~12% PAT target vs 6–7% trading) and closure of the Hara Communications asset acquisition.
- **Capital Allocation Decisions**: Board decisions on cash distributions given D. B. Corp’s ~INR 1,120 crore cash balance and HT Media/HMVL’s Rs 930 crore net cash balance alongside shareholder engagements.

## Company one-liners
- **D. B. Corp Ltd**: DB Corp's Q2 results were impacted by a festive calendar shift and a ~21% newsprint cost spike, but underlying ad momentum and ~INR 1,120 crore cash back its H2 outlook.
- **Dachepalli Publishers Ltd**: Dachepalli generated 74% H1 top-line growth by locking in paper requirements at low rates and shifting notebook sales to higher-margin in-house manufacturing.
- **HT Media Ltd**: HT Media increased Q2 revenue by 5% YoY while bringing its Radio and Digital units into positive EBITDA by shutting OTTplay and surrendering weak radio stations.
- **Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd**: HMVL delivered 17% EBITDA margins supported by 11% Hindi print revenue growth, government price revisions, and lower employee expenses.


*Based on: D. B. Corp Ltd, Dachepalli Publishers Ltd, HT Media Ltd, Hindustan Media Ventures Ltd*
