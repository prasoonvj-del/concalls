# Saregama India Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Media & Entertainment*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue from operations grew 37% YoY and adjusted EBITDA grew 39% YoY, driven by strong music vertical performance.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained music vertical revenue CAGR guidance of 20% to 23% and content spend guidance of INR300 crores to INR350 crores for FY27.
- Tone and demand view: Maintained a highly bullish stance on subscription growth and India being the most under-penetrated music market, noting paid subscriptions are growing faster than ad-based.
- New topics or risks: Detailed a strategic shift in live events toward IP-based events (resulting in initial uncapitalized costs and losses) over artist-based concerts (e.g., declining to work with Diljit Dosanjh due to financials); detailed deployment of two AI teams for content/podcasts and process optimization.
- Last quarter's promises: Successfully executed the Ilaiyaraaja US tour across 11 cities.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: INR3,143 million (Up 37% YoY)
- Adjusted EBITDA: INR1,180 million (Up 39% YoY)
- Operational PBT: INR858 million (Up 43% YoY)
- Music vertical revenue: INR2,513 million (Up 35% YoY)
- Music vertical EBITDA: INR1,540 million (Up 37% YoY)
- Music vertical net margin: INR1,165 million (Up 46% YoY)
- Video vertical revenue: INR183 million (Down 16% YoY)
- FY27 new content spend guidance: INR300 crores to INR350 crores (projected at ~INR1,200 crores cumulatively over FY28 to FY30)
- Total catalog size: 180,000+ songs, growing by 4,500 to 5,000 new releases annually
- Artist management: 340 artists with >450 million combined followers on Instagram and YouTube

### Highlights
- Music vertical growth was aided by a low base in H1 FY26.
- 60% of FY26 music revenue came from post-2000 releases.
- Live events expanded: 54 Carvaan Live shows, an 11-city Ilaiyaraaja US tour (20,000 attendees), and 17 devotional shows.
- Brand partnerships executed 20+ brand deals in Q2 with partners like Apple, Airbnb, Google, Cadbury, and Tanishq.
- Pocket Aces reached break-even in FY26 and is moving towards profitability in FY27.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management maintains a short-to-medium-term music vertical revenue CAGR guidance of 20% to 23%.
- Management maintains a short-to-medium-run music EBITDA margin guidance of 60% to 65%.
- Content budgets post-FY27 are projected to increase at a nominal level of ~10% per annum (~INR1,200 crores total over FY28–FY30).
- Guidance remains for a 5-year payback period on new music, followed by 55 to 75 years of returns.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India represents the most under-penetrated large music market globally, holding just a 1% share of the USD31.7 billion global recorded music market despite housing over 18% of the world population.
- An EY and IMI consumer study indicated 64% of free music customers are ready to shift to a reasonably priced paid service if free service stops.
- Paid subscriptions are growing much faster than ad-based consumption; subscription pricing growth is expected 2 to 3 years ahead.
- Universal Music agreed to a content windowing concept in India, releasing newer content first to paid tiers.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Content bought 2, 3, or 4 years ago is now entering a cycle where revenue increases far outpace cost charge-offs, driving positive margin expansion.
- IP-based live events involve higher upfront investments that are charged off immediately rather than capitalized, impacting short-term event profitability.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- New content spend is guided between INR300 crores and INR350 crores for FY27.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Current liabilities increased due to customer advances received, to be recognized as revenue over time.
- Other income declined by 55% YoY because QIP funds were redeployed into business investments (Pocket Aces, Haryanvi catalog, Bhansali stake).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unlicensed AI cover versions and remakes of songs; management is actively tracking and pulling them down or buying them out.
- Initial financial losses in IP-based live events due to high upfront uncapitalized investments.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Govindarajan (CSIM) on event losses and unallocable costs:** Management explained that IP-based events carry higher upfront uncapitalized investments, and unallocable cost increases are solely due to lower other income from redeployed QIP funds. Management did not dodge.
- **Akshay Kolekar (Dalal & Broacha) on music margin expansion:** Management clarified that margins are expanding because content bought 3–4 years ago now generates revenues well above its cost charge-off. Management did not dodge.
- **Naveen Bothra (Shubh Lab Research) on Carvaan app:** Management stated the app is currently in a testing phase with hardware owners and will be pushed more broadly after evaluating results by year-end. Management did not dodge.
- **Harshh on artist management growth:** Management noted they do not give individual vertical growth guidance but include artist management within the overall music vertical (20–23% CAGR). Management did not dodge.
- **Ranjeet Verma on digital/non-digital split and current liabilities:** Management declined to quantify the exact digital split citing competitive sensitivity (noting bulk is digital) and explained current liabilities rose due to customer advances. Management did not dodge.

### One-line takeaway
Saregama delivered robust Q2 FY27 performance (revenue up 37% YoY, adjusted EBITDA up 39% YoY), driven by compounding new-age music IP, scaling live events, and expanding subscription demand.
