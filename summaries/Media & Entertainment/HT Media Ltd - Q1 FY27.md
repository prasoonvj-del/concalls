# HT Media Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-12 · Media & Entertainment*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Operating revenue and profitability improved significantly YoY, driven by advertising performance and cost discipline.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated total revenue: Rs 497 crore (up 15% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 90 crore (up nearly 3x YoY)
- EBITDA margin expansion: 12 percentage points YoY
- PAT: Rs 47 crore (substantially improved YoY)
- PAT margin: 9%
- Net cash position (consolidated): Rs 922 crore
- Print segment revenue: Rs 376 crore (up 16% YoY)
- Print segment advertising revenue: Rs 295 crore (up 15% YoY)
- Print segment circulation revenue: Rs 52 crore (virtually flat YoY)
- Print segment operating EBITDA: Rs 50 crore (13% margin)
- English Print advertising revenue: Rs 156 crore (up 12% YoY)
- English Print circulation revenue: Rs 13 crore (up 14% YoY)
- Hindi Print advertising revenue: Rs 139 crore (up YoY)
- Radio operating EBITDA: Negative Rs 3 crore (topline flat YoY)
- Digital operating revenue: Down 28% YoY (operating EBITDA negative Rs 3 crore, margin -12%)

### Highlights
- Profitability growth achieved through steady advertising revenue and disciplined cost management.
- Print remained the anchor business with growing ad revenue and resilient circulation.
- Radio operating on a leaner footprint following the surrender of licenses for certain non-viable stations.
- Digital portfolio deliberately reset around leaner, more focused offerings for sustainable growth.
- Board approved a preferential issue of equity shares (subject to regulatory and shareholder approval) to strengthen capital structure, streamline debt profile, and fund general business requirements.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains hopeful of keeping up a good set of numbers going into the future.
- Management believes newsprint prices have peaked at $650–$700 per metric ton and should plateau before declining.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Commercial ad revenues held up from a volume perspective alongside yield improvements.
- Government ad revenues benefited from a rate increase implemented by the Government in November of last year (after seven years).
- Newsprint prices are at the highest post-COVID peak ($650–$700 per metric ton), combined with a lifetime-high US dollar exchange rate.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Newsprint is the single biggest cost line item, varying between 25% to 40% of the entire bill of material.
- Organizational right-sizing and efficiency drives have reduced employee costs (consolidated employee expenses dropped from Rs 111 crore to Rs 99 crore YoY).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- *Not discussed*

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated net cash stands at Rs 922 crore, but this is parked entirely in HMVL.
- HT Media Limited and Digicontent Limited carry net debt, which the proposed preferential issue aims to retire (targeting a 30% to 50% debt reduction).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated newsprint prices, a weaker rupee (lifetime high US dollar), and global supply chain uncertainties.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Ranga Prasad asked why the company chose a preferential issue over a rights issue when net cash is Rs 922 crore and flagged the steep dilution for public shareholders.** Management replied that cash is locked in HMVL, whereas HT Media and Digicontent carry debt. A preferential issue is faster (2-3 months quicker), offers higher certainty, and avoids underwriting risks associated with rights issues.
- **Mehul Pathak criticized the preferential issue pricing of Rs 24.7 relative to the book value and intrinsic value, calling it a governance and ethical concern.** Management defended the pricing, stating it strictly follows the SEBI formula (higher of 10-day or 90-day VWAP) without a premium, and promoters are subscribing on the same terms to retire debt.
- **Rohan Agarwal inquired about the sustainability of Print EBITDA margins given high newsprint costs.** Management stated that if newsprint prices and the USD do not rise further, the Q1 margin of 13% can be used as a baseline for modeling, though newsprint has no forward market.
- **Yash R. asked what drove the 15% Print ad revenue growth.** Management stated it was driven by both volume resilience and yield/pricing improvements, alongside the government rate hike from November.
- **Mahima suggested conducting separate conference calls for HT Media and HMVL for better clarity.** Management stated they prefer holding a single combined call for shareholders of both companies.

### One-line takeaway
HT Media reported a strong recovery in Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue and profitability, driven by yield-led print advertising growth and cost discipline, even as management faced intense shareholder pushback over a planned debt-reducing preferential equity issue.
