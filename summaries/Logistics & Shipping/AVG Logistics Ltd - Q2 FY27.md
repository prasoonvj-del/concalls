# AVG Logistics Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-27 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue grew 6% YoY and PAT rose 39.2% YoY, supported by improved asset utilization, new contract wins, and cost discipline.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained 15% to 20% revenue growth for FY27.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted more strongly toward green logistics traction, with heavy electric trucks and alternative fuel adoption scaling up in cement and industrial segments.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Entry into liquor logistics (targeted at ~Rs 24 crores in FY27 and ~Rs 100 crores next year) was newly highlighted.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Haldiram rollout and Dalmia electric fleet deployment commenced in Q2 as targeted.

### Key numbers
*   **Consolidated Revenue from Operations:** Rs 151.6 crore (up 6% YoY, up 14.4% QoQ).
*   **Consolidated Total Income:** Rs 154.2 crore (up 6.3% YoY).
*   **Consolidated EBITDA:** Rs 23.6 crore (down 10.3% YoY, up 11.8% QoQ).
*   **Consolidated EBITDA Margin:** 15.6% (vs 18.4% in Q2 FY26 and 15.9% in Q1 FY27).
*   **Consolidated PBT:** Rs 10.1 crore (up 53% YoY, up 16.1% QoQ).
*   **Consolidated PAT:** Rs 7.10 crore (up 39.2% YoY, up 9.2% QoQ).
*   **Consolidated PAT Margin:** 4.7% (vs 3.6% in Q2 FY26 and 4.9% in Q1 FY27).
*   **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 284.1 crore (up 6% YoY).
*   **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** Rs 44.7 crore (down 11% YoY).
*   **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 13.5 crore (up 33.7% YoY).
*   **Standalone Q2 Revenue:** Rs 154 crore (up 6.6% YoY).
*   **Standalone Q2 PAT:** Rs 7.3 crore (up 46% YoY).
*   **Total Borrowings / Debt:** Approx Rs 189 crore total.
*   **Vision 2030 Targets:** Revenue of Rs 1,250 crores, PAT of around Rs 120 crores.

### Highlights
*   **New Contracts & Verticals:** Entered liquor logistics in July with long-term contracts with 2 companies (expected to contribute ~Rs 24 crores in FY27 and scale to ~Rs 100 crores next year). Signed a 5-year contract with a North East cement company (deploying 30 heavy electric trucks in Assam). Secured a 5-year transportation contract worth ~Rs 60 crores with a multimodal beverage company.
*   **Green Fleet Expansion:** Running 30 heavy electric trucks for cement in Assam (carrying ~38 to 41 tons, 50 trips/month per vehicle). Planning to add around 200-250 electric vehicles by March '27.
*   **Accounting Policy Impact:** Management noted that the decline in reported EBITDA and corresponding decreases in depreciation and finance costs are due to Ind AS lease accounting changes (moving lease rentals from depreciation/interest to operating expenses), while actual operational expenses reduced or remained stable.

### Guidance & outlook
*   Management maintained its objective of delivering around 15% to 20% revenue growth for FY27.
*   Full-year PAT is expected to be around Rs 34 crores to Rs 35 crores.
*   Vision 2030 roadmap targets revenue of Rs 1,250 crores and PAT of around Rs 120 crores.
*   Management expects stronger performance in H2 as new contracts (Haldiram, Dalmia, liquor, and beverage) contribute for the full period.

### Demand & industry commentary
*   Strong demand for green logistics and alternative fuel vehicles (EV, CNG, LNG) from heavy industries like cement and steel due to carbon reduction targets.
*   Logistics cost in electric vehicles is lower because of optimized routing, higher trip counts, and lower-cost solar electricity usage for charging.
*   Routes like Mumbai-Delhi and Delhi-Kolkata now have 100% electric charger availability, enabling long-distance trials.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
*   Fuel volatility is mitigated through fuel surcharge mechanisms and periodic freight rate revisions across 75% to 80% of business (corporate clients), with a short 10-15 day adjustment lag.
*   Alternative fuel vehicles (electric/CNG) yield better profit margins than traditional diesel fleets.
*   Over the next 4 years, ~900 to 1,000 vehicles will become EMI-free, drastically reducing interest burdens and improving profitability.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
*   Charging station setup costs approx Rs 1 crore per station, with 4-5 chargers needed at origin and destination locations.
*   Utilizing a mix of owned and leased models for fleet expansion.
*   Khurda facility in Odisha is targeted to become operational in the next financial year. Land is also parceled in Himachal Pradesh and Odisha for warehousing and express services.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
*   Rights issue proceeds were primarily utilized for working capital (fuel, drivers, advances) due to the working-capital-intensive nature of the industry, and 25% for general corporate purposes.
*   Total debt stands at approx Rs 189 crores. Management stated the debt-to-equity ratio is very comfortable compared to peers.

### Risks & concerns flagged
*   Working capital intensity requires upfront payments for fuel, drivers, and advances before invoice recoveries.
*   Liquor logistics is tightly regulated, requiring strict compliance, secure handling, GPS monitoring, and trained drivers.

### Notable analyst Q&A
*   *Q: How will the 15-20% revenue growth be met given H1 single-digit growth?* A: Management stated that H3 and H4 are seasonally stronger, and recent contracts (Haldiram, Dalmia, liquor, beverage) will contribute for full periods starting October, supported by new implementations in November and December. (Management did not dodge).
*   *Q: How do the economics and charging infrastructure work for the Dalmia electric fleet?* A: Management explained that charging stations cost ~Rs 1 crore each (4-5 at origin and destination). Using solar power (~Rs 4-5/unit vs industrial Rs 8-10/unit) reduces operational costs below diesel, enabling better margins and more trips. (Management did not dodge).
*   *Q: How are equity proceeds from the rights issue utilized when borrowings increased?* A: CFO clarified that borrowings increased by only Rs 30-40 crores (totaling ~Rs 189 crores), and rights issue proceeds were mainly deployed into working capital and general corporate purposes. (Management did not dodge).
*   *Q: What is the strategy regarding potential acquisitions?* A: Management is looking at 2-3 targets, but noted valuations demanded (e.g., 15x EBITDA multiples) are unreasonable when they can build capabilities organically. (Management did not dodge).
*   *Q: What percentage of business is covered by fuel pass-throughs?* A: 75% to 80% of corporate business is covered by diesel increase/decrease clauses, with only a 10-15 day adjustment lag. (Management did not dodge).

AVG Logistics delivered steady H1 growth and stronger profitability expansion, driven by green fleet scaling, new contract wins in liquor and cement, and strict cost discipline.
