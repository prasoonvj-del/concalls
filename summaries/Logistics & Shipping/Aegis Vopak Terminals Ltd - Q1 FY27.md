# Aegis Vopak Terminals Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-19 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Reflects healthy business momentum with strong growth in liquid terminaling, robust operating profitability, and strong cash generation despite geopolitical challenges.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from operations:** INR233.8 crores (up 12.4% YoY)
- **Liquid terminaling revenue:** INR126.5 crores (up 31% YoY, 54.1% mix)
- **Gas terminaling revenue:** INR107.2 crores (down 3.5% YoY, 45.9% mix)
- **Operating EBITDA:** INR179.4 crores (up 15.6% YoY)
- **EBITDA margin:** ~76.7%
- **Cash PAT:** INR124.9 crores
- **Gas throughput (quarterly):** ~0.9 million metric tons
- **Liquid storage capacity expansion (Kochi):** 49,577 cubic meters approved (total to reach 132,122 cubic meters)
- **LPG storage capacity expansion (JNPA):** 51,998 metric ton refrigerated double-wall steel tank approved
- **Long-term capex objective:** $5 billion by 2030-2031

### Highlights
- Announced two major projects: a ~52,000 MT refrigerated double-wall steel LPG storage tank at JNPA and 49,577 cubic meters of liquid capacity at Kochi.
- Commissioned specialized ammonia storage and terminaling facility at Pipavav (36,000 MT static capacity) with a 15-year take-or-pay agreement with Hindustan Zinc.
- Four pipeline connections coming online during the year: Jamnagar-Loni (operational), Kandla-Gorakhpur (at Kandla and Pipavav), and Haldia-Panagarh.
- Secured a 15-year take-or-pay agreement with a leading conglomerate for petroleum product handling at Pipavav exceeding 0.5 million MT per annum (operations starting by end of year).
- Acquired an additional 3 acres of land at Haldia for future liquid storage and allied handling expansion.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management aims to grow LPG volumes by at least 25% YoY.
- Management expects to reach INR10,000 crores of gross block/capex by March or worst-case June 2027.
- Reaffirms the $5 billion capex objective by 2030-2031.
- Mandatory promoter equity dilution to 25% by June 2, 2028, to support net worth targets.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India's LPG demand and per capita energy consumption are low, leaving a large headroom for substitution of dirty fuels (wood, cow dung).
- Industrial LPG demand is expected to grow strongly due to its high calorific value (25% higher than alternatives), portability, and low carbon footprint.
- Geopolitical disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz caused national oil companies to ration supplies and constrained competitors, but Aegis Vopak remained unscathed via diversified sourcing (e.g., ITOCHU, Argentina, USA, Nigeria).
- Gas throughput charges remain volume-driven (~INR1,175 per metric ton) and do not fluctuate with product availability or scarcity.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating EBITDA margin remained robust at ~76.7%.
- Liquid margins benefited from newer, higher-realization capacities maturing (such as JNPA plot 1 realization of ~INR6,000/year vs. blended average of ~INR3,000).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Liquid storage capacity expected to jump from 1.7 million to 2.2 million CBM this year, and close to 3 million CBM by FY28 end.
- Mangalore LPG rail gantry and bottling infrastructure investment ongoing at INR52.5 crores; evaluating an additional 60,000 CBM of liquid storage at Mangalore.
- Vadhavan Port MoU involvement under evaluation with potential investment of ~INR20,000 crores subject to approvals.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Financial position remains strong with prudent leverage.
- Debt gearing capped at 0.6 (net debt to EBITDA cap of 3.5x).
- $5 billion capex plan to be funded via a mix of debt (max $3 billion) and equity/internal accruals (targeting $2 billion net worth).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions and Middle East conflicts (Strait of Hormuz) causing vessel delays and supply chain bottlenecks for national oil companies, though Aegis mitigated this through diversified sourcing.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Siddharth Chauhan (360 ONE Capital) asked about gas throughput pricing power versus distribution margins.** Management answered that throughput is a fixed-rate infrastructure usage fee (~INR1,175/MT) focused on volume, whereas distribution involves sourcing and inventory risks.
- **Priyankar Biswas (Indiv. / Institutional) asked about sourcing advantages during geopolitical crises.** Management attributed it to diversified global sourcing (ITOCHU, non-Middle East routes) and flexible vessel deployment.
- **Priyankar Biswas asked about post-INR10,000 crore capex plans.** Management noted expansion into inland depots, strategic national storage, and industrial terminals alongside port assets.
- **Koundinya (Jefferies) questioned how 25% volume growth is achieved against a 4-5% industry growth rate.** Management explained that their new, high-turnover cryogenic terminals capture incremental demand and replace inefficient legacy operator throughput.
- **Kunal Mehta asked about potential capacity constraints at Kandla given upcoming pipelines.** Management stated they follow demand and will readily expand if sustainable.

### One-line takeaway
Aegis Vopak delivered a resilient Q1 FY27 with 12.4% revenue growth and 76.7% EBITDA margins, driven by diversified sourcing, expanding liquid and gas capacities, and multimodal evacuation integration.
