# Western Carriers (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-19 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a 10% YoY growth in H1 container volumes, a 17% QoQ rise in EBITDA, and strong domestic traction overcoming EXIM headwinds.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained FY27 capex guidance at approximately Rs 100 crore (with Rs 30 crore completed in H1).
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from dealing with severe EXIM stress to seeing early green shoots in SAARC/Bangladesh trade and robust domestic growth from Morbi and the newly launched Calcutta terminal (GCD Yard).
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: Added discussion on severe Gujarat and Maharashtra rains/floods in July causing temporary train disruptions, and Indian Railways' four new tariff rationalization notifications for bulk cement, fertilizer, fly ash, and food grains.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: The General Goods Terminal (GCD Yard) at SMP Port Trust, Kolkata, was successfully inaugurated and made operational in September as promised.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 449 crore (vs Rs 440 crore in Q2 FY26)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 21.9 crore (up 17% QoQ from Rs 18.7 crore in Q1 FY27; up 17% YoY)
- **PAT:** Rs 9 crore (up 4.25% QoQ from Rs 8.7 crore in Q1 FY27); **PAT Margin:** 2.02% (expansion of 15 bps QoQ from 1.87%)
- **Container Volumes:** Total 59,343 TEUs (vs 58,261 TEUs in Q1 FY27); H1 total 1,17,604 TEUs (up >10% YoY from 1,06,621 TEUs in H1 FY25-26).
  - Domestic TEUs: 28,933 (up 21% QoQ from 23,909 in Q1 FY27; up ~34% in H1 YoY)
  - EXIM TEUs: 30,410 (vs 34,352 in Q1 FY27)

### Highlights
- Inaugurated and operationalized Phase 1 of the General Goods Terminal (GCD Yard) at SMP Port Trust in Kolkata in September, providing a base in India's largest consumption market.
- Handled a bulk-tiles train flag-off by the Railways Minister at the 42-acre Devaliya MMCT railhead (WCGD) in Saurashtra, Gujarat, expanding services to 6 routes including CMLK, Bangalore, Kolkata, and Guwahati.
- Metal dependence has shifted toward a more balanced mix, now approaching a 50-50 split between metals and non-metals (tiles, ceramics, chemicals, food, etc.).
- Unlike previous quarters where EXIM bottlenecks dragged overall sentiment, the company demonstrated resilience with broad-based customer additions among MSMEs.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management envisages a capex program of approximately Rs 100 crore for FY27, with timing and scale linked to customer commitments, volume visibility, and market conditions.
- Management remains optimistic that normalizing EXIM routes, combined with strong domestic momentum, will drive stronger performance in H2.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic macro remains robust, supported by government infra initiatives (National Logistics Policy, MMLPs, Sagarmala, DFCs) and strong consumption.
- EXIM trade continues to face headwinds from erratic vessel schedules, Middle East instability, port congestion, and shortages of empty containers, though break-bulk exports and SAARC trade (Nepal and Bangladesh) showed positive traction.
- Indian Railways announced four tariff rationalization notifications for bulk cement, fertilizer, fly ash, and food grains, creating future domestic conversion opportunities.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Margin profile is improving via operational excellence and asset turnarounds despite external freight and fuel pressures.
- Road transport utilizes Diesel Price Variation Clauses (DPVC) and rail movements utilize railway haulage linkages as pass-throughs to customers.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Completed Rs 30 crore of capex in H1 toward specialized containers, commercial vehicles, and equipment.
- GCD Yard (Kolkata) Phase 1 is fully operational from September; Morbi MMLP Phase 2 expects full operationalization over the next couple of quarters.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Internal target for debtor days is to bring them below 120 days and sequentially to 110 days over the next four to six quarters.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global geopolitical uncertainties, Middle East conflicts, and erratic vessel schedules impacting predictability and container availability.
- Severe rains and floods in Gujarat and Maharashtra during July caused temporary disruptions in train services.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Kaustav Bubna (Kamana Holdings) on Receivable Days:** Asked about high receivable days and guidance for FY27. *Management stated internal targets are to bring receivables below 120 days and sequentially to 110 days over 4-6 quarters, noting progress with smaller non-corporate bookings.*
2. **Kartik Jain on Rail Transportation Share & Margins:** Asked about rail revenue contribution and margin benefits. *Management stated 75%-80% of work is linked to multimodal logistics, and multimodal operations enable profitable, incremental growth rather than chasing low-rate volume.*
3. **Madhur Rathi on Margin Decline & Customer Mix:** Asked why margins historically dropped to 4%-5% and customer exposure shifts from metals. *Management explained that supply chain disruptions cause cargo bunching and asset idling; non-metal mix has improved to 50% through Morbi and Kolkata expansions and is targeted to reach 45:55 (metals:non-metals) in 4-6 quarters.*
4. **Yash Bhandari on CONCOR Partnership & Competition:** Asked if CONCOR's terminal at Rafaleshwar competes with Devaliya. *Management clarified that CONCOR is their primary business associate and partner at Devaliya, and the Morbi cluster's 1,000+ manufacturing units provide ample cargo without direct cannibalization.*
5. **Jalpa Shah on Domestic Volume Sustainability:** Asked about drivers of the 34% H1 domestic growth. *Management stated growth is driven by Morbi and Calcutta terminals, new MSME customer additions across tiles, chemicals, and food, and expects continued momentum.*

### One-line takeaway
Western Carriers delivered an H1 volume growth of over 10% YoY and a 17% QoQ jump in Q2 EBITDA, successfully leveraging a strategic pivot toward domestic multimodal hubs to offset ongoing EXIM trade headwinds.
