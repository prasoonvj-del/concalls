# Great Eastern Shipping Company Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-16 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management reported their second-highest quarterly profit historically, despite a late-quarter correction in freight rates due to geopolitical developments.

### What changed vs last quarter
- **Guidance:** Maintained (no formal earnings guidance provided).
- **Tone and demand view:** Shifted from "highest ever" in Q1 to "second highest" in Q2; management noted a significant correction in crude tanker rates (Suezmax/Aframax) in the last 10-12 days of the quarter.
- **New topics:** Detailed discussion on "shuttle service" inefficiencies in the Strait of Hormuz; specific refusal to operate in high-risk routes (Hormuz/Novorossiysk) due to crew safety; new Suezmax new-build order (a departure from the historical "replace, not expand" strategy).
- **Last quarter's promises:** Buyback is ongoing (75% of minimum threshold met).

### Key numbers
- **Average TCY (Crude):** $73,000/day (Q2) vs $93,000/day (Q1).
- **Average TCY (Aframax):** ~$50,000/day (Q2) vs ~$85,000/day (Q1).
- **Average TCY (Product):** ~$32,000/day (Q2) vs ~$45,000/day (Q1).
- **LPG Spot Rates:** ~$140,000/day (H1) vs $55,000/day (H1 previous year).
- **Interim Dividend:** Maintained at Rs 14.40/share.
- **Net Cash:** Rs 8,500 crore.
- **Fleet Break-even:** ~$13,000/day (blended).

### Highlights
- Second-highest quarterly profit in historical terms.
- NAV is at its highest ever.
- Fleet additions: Sold 2009-built LR2 (Jag Lokesh), replaced with modern LR2 (Jag Laxman). Added Kamsarmax (Jag Amit, 2015-built) and large gas carrier (Jag Vikrant, 2015-built). Committed to a 2019-built Kamsarmax and a new-build Suezmax (delivery in ~2 years).
- Offshore: 2 rigs secured 3-year contracts in India (employment until FY30).
- *Record Check:* The order of a new-build Suezmax breaks the previous pattern of purely "replace, not expand," though management frames it as portfolio construction/insurance.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management does not provide earnings or revenue guidance.
- Strategy: Continue to wait for right opportunities to deploy capital; willing to wait for asset prices to align with return thresholds.
- Management expects positive fleet growth in calendar 2027.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Tanker market: Rates corrected significantly in the last 10-12 days of the quarter due to renewed traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.
- Inefficiency: "Shuttle service" (loading inside Hormuz, offloading to another ship) adds 8-9 days to voyage time, reducing efficiency by 18-20%.
- Order book: Crude tankers (28%), Product tankers (22%), LPG (40%), Dry bulk (16%).
- Scrapping: Minimal due to high earnings.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Blended break-even rate for the fleet is ~$13,000/day.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Management is "ready to buy every day" if prices align.
- New-build Suezmax order is a departure from historical preference for second-hand ships; viewed as "insurance" if cheap second-hand assets aren't available.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net cash position: Rs 8,500 crore.
- Buyback: Intention to complete as soon as possible; 75% of minimum threshold met.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical uncertainty (Iran/US war) makes forecasting impossible.
- Normalization of supply chain (cessation of hostilities) would remove inefficiencies and likely lower rates.
- High order book (rear-ended) is a long-term supply risk.
- Crew safety: Management refuses to operate in high-risk zones (Hormuz/Novorossiysk) despite higher premiums.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- *Q: Why not fix more ships on long-term charters?* A: Time charter rates were at a 60-70% discount to spot rates; not attractive.
- *Q: Why order a new-build Suezmax?* A: High second-hand prices make it difficult to replace ships; this is "insurance" and portfolio construction.
- *Q: Impact of war ending?* A: Inefficiencies would vanish, rates would drop, but likely remain above historical norms due to inventory replenishment.
- *Q: Buyback strategy?* A: It's a capital allocation decision (buying fleet at discount), not a dividend replacement.
- *Q: Why not operate in high-risk zones?* A: Crew safety is paramount; management refuses to take the risk.

### One-line takeaway
Strong profitability continues, though freight rates corrected late in the quarter; management remains disciplined on capital allocation while making a tactical pivot to new-builds as "insurance" against high second-hand prices.
