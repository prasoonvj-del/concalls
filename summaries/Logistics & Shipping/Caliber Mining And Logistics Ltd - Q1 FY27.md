# Caliber Mining And Logistics Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-14 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted highest-ever coal extraction, overburden removal, and revenue, attributing margin compression to a temporary, extraordinary diesel price spike from the Iran war.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs 657 crore (vs Rs 393 crore in Q1 FY26)
- EBITDA: Rs 110 crore (vs Rs 95.67 crore in Q1 FY26)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 20.02% (reported EBITDA margin at 16.80%, impacted by Rs 10.57 crore diesel escalation revenue)
- Cash profit: Rs 68.54 crore (vs Rs 55.37 crore in Q1 FY26)
- Order book: Rs 9,124 crore including GST as of June 30, 2026 (average order book period of 46 months)
- Coal extraction: 1.54 million metric tonnes (vs 1.21 million metric tonnes in Q1 FY26)
- Overburden (OB) removal: 43.37 million cubic meters (vs 28.56 million cubic meters in Q1 FY26)
- Coal transported by road: 2.42 million metric tonnes (vs 2.98 million metric tonnes in Q1 FY26)
- Coal loaded on rakes: 4.77 million metric tonnes (vs 4.46 million metric tonnes in Q1 FY26)
- Fleet size: 2,033 as of June 2026
- Gross debt: Started the year at Rs 1,024 crore; expected to close around Rs 750 crore by year-end

### Highlights
- Achieved highest-ever coal extraction and overburden removal for a quarter.
- Coal mining services contributed Rs 597 crore (approximately 91%) to total revenue.
- Received a rating upgrade from CRISIL from BBB positive to A minus with a positive outlook post-IPO.
- IPO proceeds of Rs 208 crore utilized for debt rundown, Rs 167 crore for new equipment addition, and Rs 125 crore for day-to-day expenses and liquidity.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management guided for a revenue growth of about 45% to 50% YoY, driven by full-year execution of recently won orders.
- Management targeted EBITDA growth of 35% plus YoY and PAT growth of 35% plus YoY (aided by interest cost savings).
- Expected OB removal volume in Q2 FY27 is approximately 34 million cubic meters due to monsoon seasonality.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Management stated there is no slowdown or weakening demand in the coal sector, driven by growing power sector demand.
- More than 80% of revenue comes from Coal India.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Reported EBITDA margin was 16.80%, but adjusted EBITDA margin stood at 20.02% after accounting for Rs 10.57 crore in diesel escalation revenue.
- Margin compression was attributed to an extraordinary spike in diesel costs due to the Iran war and timing mismatches in fuel escalation pass-throughs.
- Average cost of debt is 8.5% to 9%, expected to decrease following the credit rating upgrade.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Planned total capex addition of INR 450 crore for the year for current orders (INR 167 crore via cash, INR 283 crore via debt if no new tenders are added).
- Operating 7 ongoing and operational sites across Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh.
- Exploring opportunities in MDO coal, MDO iron ore, and critical mineral blocks (secured one in Maharashtra).

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gross debt stood at Rs 1,024 crore at the start of the year and is expected to close around Rs 750 crore by year-end.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Sudden spikes in fuel and lubricant costs impact margins due to timing mismatches in pass-throughs.
- Monsoon season creates a seasonally weak quarter (Q2) for volumes.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Yash asked about the reason for margin compression.** Management explained it was primarily due to the Iran war causing a sudden spike in diesel costs, leading to an adjusted EBITDA margin of 20.02% instead of the normal 23%+ steady-state.
2. **Rushin asked about fuel escalation clauses across sites.** Management clarified that coal mining projects have fuel escalation arrangements, but timing mismatches and extraordinary price spikes caused a temporary hit.
3. **Arvind Arora asked if the margin dip is temporary.** Management stated it is cyclical/temporary, and diesel price trends are currently stabilizing.
4. **Ajit Sethi asked about the current gross debt and cost of debt.** Management stated gross debt started at Rs 1,024 crore and is expected to end the year near Rs 750 crore, with an average cost of debt at 8.5% to 9% expected to drop.
5. **Vishal asked about the risk of weakening coal demand from Coal India.** Management clarified there is no slowdown in coal demand, and contracts are not dependent on coal off-take since they are paid for OB removal and extraction volumes.

### One-line takeaway
Caliber Mining and Logistics delivered its highest-ever quarterly revenue and operational volumes in Q1 FY27, backed by a robust Rs 9,124 crore order book, while navigating temporary margin headwinds from a spike in diesel costs.
