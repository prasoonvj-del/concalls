# Western Carriers (India) Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-22 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted a healthy 12% YoY revenue growth, sequential profit growth, and strategic pivot toward domestic container streams amidst ongoing global EXIM disruptions.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue:** Rs 465 crore (up 12% YoY from Rs 416 crore)
- **EBITDA:** Rs 19 crore; **EBITDA Margin:** 4.1%
- **PAT:** Rs 9 crore (up 13% QoQ from Rs 8 crore in Q4 FY26); **PAT Margin:** 1.9% (expansion of 20 bps QoQ)
- **Container Volumes:** Total 58,261 TEUs (up ~15% YoY from 57,084 TEUs; higher sequentially than Q4 FY26's 57,754 TEUs).
  - Domestic TEUs: 23,909 (up 37% YoY from 17,498)
  - EXIM TEUs: 34,352 (up 3.2% YoY from 33,286)
- **Debt:** Total debt reduced from Rs 217 crore at March end to Rs 197 crore.
- **Working Capital & Debtors:** Working capital days reduced from 120 days in the previous quarter to 111 days; debtor days reduced from 139 days to 135 days.
- **Cash Flows:** Net positive cash flow of ~Rs 13 crore in Q1 (compared to Rs 9.2 crore in Q4).

### Highlights
- Total domestic vs. EXIM mix has shifted to roughly 40:60 (previously 30:70 six quarters ago).
- Placed an order for 150 specialized 40-feet containers (first 50 delivered, next 100 scheduled for delivery in the current quarter).
- 42-acre Devaliya MMCT in Saurashtra, Gujarat, running exclusively with Container Corporation, is providing seamless pan-India multimodal services.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Capex Guidance:** Management envisages a capex program of approximately Rs 100 crore for FY27, though timing and scale remain linked to customer commitments, volume visibility, and market conditions.
- Management remains optimistic about future top-line and bottom-line growth, anticipating a strong rebound in EXIM volumes once geopolitical and supply chain situations stabilize.

### Demand & industry commentary
- India’s FY27 GDP is projected at approximately 6.6%; freight and logistics market is expected to reach USD 476 billion in 5 years (8.6% CAGR).
- EXIM market remains under stress due to erratic ship schedules, port congestion (e.g., 30,000 TEU backlog at JNPT), longer Cape of Good Hope routes adding ~15 days, and surging freight rates (up 3-4x, nearly USD 9,000/TEU on certain routes).
- Domestic demand is robust, driven by consumption, infrastructure spending, and manufacturing.
- Higher diesel and fuel prices are accelerating a structural shift toward multimodal/hybrid road-rail logistics.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Fuel price volatility, shipping disruptions, and cost inflation continue to impact logistics operating expenses.
- Road transport faces immediate fuel cost pressures, leading to fuel-linked freight contracts and adjustment mechanisms.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capital allocation focuses on specialized equipment, container assets, multi-modal infrastructure, and tech-led solutions.
- Capex is guided by supply chain robustness and customer commitments, targeting healthy double-digit returns on assets.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total debt decreased by ~10% to Rs 197 crore from Rs 217 crore at March end.
- Working capital days dropped from 120 to 111; debtor days dropped from 139 to 135.
- SME collection cycles have improved, driving steady cash inflows.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Geopolitical tensions (Middle East conflict, Russia-Ukraine war) leading to erratic vessel schedules, port congestion, and inflated shipping rates.
- Vulnerability to fuel price volatility impacting road and ocean transport economics.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Keshav Garg (Counter Cyclical PMS) on Pricing & Profitability:** Asked why the company absorbs cost pressures without taking price hikes despite long-term contracts. *Management answered that supply chain disruptions cause non-linear cargo flows and hit margins, but long-term contracts provide stability; profitability and EBITDA are improving as the company pivots to domestic growth.*
2. **Keshav Garg on Working Capital & Cash Flow:** Highlighted high debtor days (142 days) and negative cumulative operating cash flows historically. *Management countered that debtor days reduced to 135, working capital days fell to 111, debt decreased to Rs 197 crore, and Q1 generated positive cash flow of ~Rs 13 crore.*
3. **Kaustav Bubna (Kamana Holdings) on Contingency Planning for Prolonged War:** Suggested planning for a multi-year war scenario and questioned expanding leverage vs. capex. *Management stated that debt has actually reduced by 10% since March, working capital is improving, and the company has successfully pivoted to the domestic segment (now 40% of top-line) to insulate itself.*
4. **Suhani Singh (ROS Capital) on Capex Return Thresholds:** Asked about return criteria and payback timeframes for asset investments. *Management stated primary criteria is ensuring supply chain robustness for clients to prevent massive losses, alongside targeting healthy double-digit returns on specialized assets.*

### One-line takeaway
Western Carriers delivered 12% YoY revenue growth and a 13% sequential profit jump in Q1 FY27 by successfully pivoting toward domestic multimodal operations, even as EXIM trade faced severe geopolitical and freight headwinds.
