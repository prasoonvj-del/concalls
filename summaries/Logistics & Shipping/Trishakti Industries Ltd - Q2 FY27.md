# Trishakti Industries Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-26 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Rapid scaling of operations, robust revenue and profit growth, and proactive capacity expansion.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue from operations:** INR 1,551 lakhs (grew 133% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA:** INR 944 lakhs (grew ~141% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 Profit After Tax (PAT):** INR 519 lakhs (grew 223% YoY)
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** 60.89%
- **H1 FY27 Revenue from operations:** INR 2,989 lakhs (grew ~178% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA:** INR 1,790 lakhs (grew ~170% YoY)
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** INR 949 lakhs (grew ~277% YoY)
- **CapEx Program (FY25-FY27):** INR 400 crores (over 90% or approx. INR 370 crores deployed inclusive of GST)
- **Mission 2030 Target:** Cumulative gross block > INR 1,000 crores by FY30
- **Peak Net Debt Guidance:** INR 400-450 crores (long-term basis)

### Highlights
- Business is scaling rapidly driven by a larger equipment base and robust demand from industrial and infrastructure projects.
- Announced "Mission 2030", aiming to build a cumulative gross block of over INR 1,000 crores by FY30.
- Current INR 400 crore CapEx program is more than 90% completed and six months ahead of schedule.
- Wind energy expansion equipment is scheduled to arrive from November 2nd week onwards (INR 125 crore CapEx).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects revenue run rate (ARR) to increase further as new fleet from CWIP and upcoming wind energy/data center deployments go live.
- Peak revenue potential post achieving the INR 1,000 crore gross block is estimated at around INR 300 crores by FY29 or FY30.
- Net debt is expected to be capped around INR 400-500 crores, supported by regular debt repayments and cash flows freeing up after 2028.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Fleet utilization remains exceptionally high at nearly 100% (and consistently above 90%), driven by longer-term (11-12 month) contracts rather than short-term spot rentals.
- Strong domestic CapEx activity is concentrated primarily in Gujarat and Odisha due to major manufacturing and plant expansions by clients like Reliance, L&T, Tata Steel, and KEC.
- Crawler cranes (above 150 tonnes) and lower-tunnage truck-mounted cranes are experiencing very high demand.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Company maintains fixed IRR loans for equipment financing; working capital limits (floating rate) have not been utilized recently due to strong cash flows.
- Debt repayment runs at approximately 2.5% of net debt monthly.
- Operating expenses (OpEx) on machines are deferred as older debt gets paid off and newer machines come with initial maintenance-free periods (FOCs).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- INR 370 crores (inclusive of GST) already deployed out of the INR 400 crore CapEx target.
- CWIP includes approximately INR 77-78 crores (excluding GST) of machines awaiting deployment in Q3.
- Wind energy segment expansion involves INR 125 crores of CapEx, constrained slightly by OEM manufacturing/delivery speeds (4-5 machines per month).
- International expansion into UAE and KSA is kept on hold temporarily as internal accruals are prioritized for domestic CapEx and debt reduction; paperwork is 60-70% complete.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Debt-to-equity ratio is targeted to stay under 2 during this hyper-expansion phase.
- Trade receivables include about INR 3 crores related to an older family settlement (provisioned/partially written off); core rental debtor cycle is expected to normalize to around 60 days by the end of the year.
- Unbilled revenue at quarter-end relates to the final month's billing cycle and operator overtime calculations.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Sector cyclicality and potential moderation in fleet utilization if industry demand slows down, though offset by product diversification.
- Managing debt levels and ensuring timely repayments during aggressive expansion phases.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Lavya Baldota (Zenflow Finance) asked about debtor days and geographic decentralization.** Management explained that family settlement receivables are being resolved/provisioned (approx INR 75 lakhs provisioned this quarter), and debtor days will return to ~60 days by year-end. Geographically, expansion follows customer CapEx hubs like Gujarat and Odisha.
2. **Ashish Mehta (LM Equity) asked about peak debt and debt-to-equity comfort.** Management stated peak net debt will be capped around INR 400-450 crores, maintaining debt-to-equity under 2 during the expansion phase, leveraging fixed IRR loans.
3. **Pranay (Prime Investments) inquired about CWIP deployment and full-year revenue run rate.** Management clarified that ~INR 77-78 crores (ex-GST) in CWIP will deploy in Q3, pushing ARR higher towards INR 120 crores once new fleets are fully operational.
4. **Ishan Modi (Individual Investor) questioned the 100% fleet utilization assumption and international expansion.** Management defended high utilization via 11-12 month sticky contracts and diverse product offerings (crawlers, AWPs, upcoming port/wind equipment), noting UAE/KSA expansion is paused to focus on domestic opportunities.
5. **Satya Mehta (Individual Investor) asked why Trishakti's fleet utilization outperforms the industry (80%).** Management attributed this to securing 11-12 month long-term contracts instead of short-term taxi jobs, avoiding transit/commissioning downtime gaps.

### One-line takeaway
Trishakti Industries delivered stellar Q2 FY27 performance with triple-digit revenue and PAT growth, completing 90% of its current CapEx cycle and officially launching "Mission 2030" to reach a gross block of over INR 1,000 crores.
