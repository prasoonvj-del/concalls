# Allcargo Global Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-20 · Logistics & Shipping*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Macro environment remains flattish and geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East negatively impact volumes, though sequential improvements are seen across trade lanes.

### Key numbers
- **Consolidated Revenue:** INR3,522 crore (up 5.8% YoY, 20.8% QoQ)
- **Gross Profit:** INR733 crore (up 2.5% YoY, 6.5% QoQ)
- **EBITDA:** INR33 crore (compared to a loss of INR31 crore in Q1 FY26 and EBITDA of INR17 crore in Q4 FY26)
- **EBIT:** Loss of INR18 crore (vs. loss of INR77 crore in Q1 FY26 and loss of INR33 crore in Q4 FY26)
- **Pre-exceptional PBT:** Loss of INR24 crore (vs. loss of INR92 crore in Q1 FY26 and loss of INR36 crore in Q4 FY26)
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Loss of INR28 crore (vs. loss of INR87 crore in Q1 FY26 and loss of INR45 crore in Q4 FY26)
- **Standalone Borrowings:** Reduced to INR272 crore as of June 30, 2026 (from INR314 crore as of March 31, 2026)
- **Consolidated Gross Debt:** INR942 crore as of June 30, 2026
- **Consolidated Net Debt:** INR570 crore as of June 30, 2026
- **Volume growth QoQ:** Incremental volumes in LCL and air grew by ~5%; FCL grew by ~1% QoQ.

### Highlights
- First quarterly earnings call subsequent to listing as an independent entity.
- Sequential volume improvements observed across transatlantic, transpacific, and intra-Asian trade lanes.
- Gross profit expansion driven by yield management, procurement efficiencies, process optimization, cost control, and productivity improvements.
- Digital platform accounts for 70% of export bookings.
- Cost initiatives include technology-led automations, Agentic AI adoption, and offshoring resources to lower-cost geographies.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management assumes no significant improvements in the macroeconomic environment for strategy planning over the next 12 months.
- Intent is to keep SG&A and employee costs flat in U.S. dollar terms.
- Net debt is expected to decrease significantly over the next two to three quarters via working capital reduction and non-core asset divestments.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Crisis in the Middle East continues to impact trade and volumes negatively (especially for FCL and Middle East/Africa region) over the last 5 to 6 months.
- Inventory corrections have led to momentum in freight flowing through other trade lanes.
- Carrier capacity remains constrained due to disruptions, keeping freight rates on the higher side.
- LCL volumes are ~10% below levels from a couple of years back due to a subdued macro environment.
- LCL business typically grows at ~2x the FCL growth rate.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Gross margin percentage is not viewed as relevant due to volatile ocean freight pass-through costs; management focuses on absolute gross profit and yield (gross profit per cubic meter for LCL or per TEU for FCL).
- Intent is to maintain current yields and grow volumes to expand absolute gross profit.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Business is extremely asset-light with minimal capital assets owned; capex is a tiny fraction of the P&L.
- No further major acquisitions needed for new markets or products in the near term; strategy is to grow organically by investing in people.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Consolidated gross debt stands at INR942 crore and net debt at INR570 crore as of June 30, 2026.
- Debt is primarily for working capital and historical acquisitions.
- Non-core asset divestments (warehousing and office assets) targeted at USD10 million to USD15 million combined.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Unpredictable geopolitical events (Middle East crisis, Russia-Ukraine war) causing global economic flattishness and volume pressure.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Business model & LCL vs FCL economics:** Explained by management (Ravi Jakhar), noting LCL consolidation operates like an airline seat booking with high entry barriers and highest profit margins, whereas FCL is full container chartering with lower margins. (Answered clearly)
- **In-sourcing threat from major shipping lines:** Clarified that large lines handle high-volume FCL, while Allcargo focuses on high-margin LCL where shipping lines hold a tiny fraction. (Answered clearly)
- **Cyclicality of the business:** Management noted it shadows economic/trade environment and geopolitical shocks rather than following traditional cyclicality. (Answered clearly)
- **Volume degrowth vs global trade:** Management clarified that LCL industry and FCL regional exposure (Middle East) account for the reported volume variance. (Answered clearly)
- **Debt levels and asset-light nature:** Management explained debt is largely working capital and past acquisitions, which will be reduced via cash generation and asset monetization. (Answered clearly)

### One-line takeaway
Allcargo Global reported sequential revenue and gross profit recovery in Q1 FY27 alongside narrowing losses, leveraging tech-driven cost controls and yield management despite ongoing Middle East trade disruptions.
