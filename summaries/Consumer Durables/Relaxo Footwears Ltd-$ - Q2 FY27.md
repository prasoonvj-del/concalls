# Relaxo Footwears Ltd-$ - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Consumer Durables*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Revenue grew double digits, but EBITDA and margins remained under pressure due to raw material volatility and statutory wage hikes.

### Key numbers
- Revenue from operations: Rs 702 crore (Q2 FY27) vs Rs 629 crore (Q2 FY26), up 11.6% YoY.
- H1 FY27 revenue: Rs 1,407 crore vs Rs 1,283 crore, up 9.6% YoY.
- EBITDA: Rs 85 crore, up 5.1% YoY from Rs 81 crore (Q2 FY27); H1 FY27 EBITDA at Rs 193 crore (+7.1% YoY).
- EBITDA margin: 12.2% (Q2 FY27); H1 FY27 EBITDA margin at 13.8%.
- Profit after tax (PAT): Rs 37 crore, up 2.2% YoY from Rs 36 crore (Q2 FY27); H1 FY27 PAT at Rs 92 crore (+8.0% YoY).
- PAT margin: 5.3% (Q2 FY27); H1 FY27 PAT margin at 6.5%.
- EBO network: 445 stores (added 16 during Q2; targeting 500 by year-end).
- JV investment (Relaxo Kenafric Footwears Ltd): Total investment of Rs 70 crore; Relaxo holds 51% majority stake up to Rs 35.7 crore. Kenafric facility capacity eyed at 50,000 pairs per day, operational by Q1 FY28-29 (call states FY 2027-2028 first quarter).
- Working capital: Inventory days increased by 10 days from 134 days to 144 days.
- Price hikes taken: 15%-18% in open portfolio; 6%-7% in Sparx shoes.
- Reimagined stores capex: Roughly Rs 22 lakh per store.

### Highlights
- Formed a joint venture named Relaxo Kenafric Footwears Limited with Kenafric Group to manufacture locally in Kenya (targeting East Africa) to bypass tariff barriers and logistical challenges.
- Appointed celebrity brand ambassadors: Tamannaah Bhatia and Lakshya for Sparx; Ali Fazal and Sanya Malhotra for Flite.
- Divided the general trade sales team into two separate divisions (Sparx vs. Hawaii and Flite) effective April 1, 2026.
- Opened 51 "Reimagined Stores" with better merchandising and prominence on premium articles; targeting 50 more in the next six months.
- *Note vs record:* Volume dip in the mass category continues despite industry tailwinds, contrasting with past expectations of immediate volume surges post-BIS.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains cautiously optimistic for H2 FY27, expecting better demand in summers next year once price increases are fully absorbed by trade.
- Expects double-digit growth in retail and e-commerce channels to continue, with Q4 onwards expected to see double-digit growth.
- Reiterates target to reach 500 EBO stores by year-end.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Mass market category (Hawaii slippers) faces volume dips and pressure due to high price sensitivity, though value growth is sustained.
- Strong double-digit growth seen in sports shoes, sneakers, and retail channels.
- Industry faces challenges from unorganized players who are exempt from BIS quality control standards (90% industry exemption) and circumvent duties via routes like Bangladesh.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Raw material prices remain volatile (e.g., rubber touched an all-time high of Rs 300/kg) due to West Asian conflicts and geopolitical risks.
- Margins faced pressure from raw material volatility, statutory minimum wage revisions (Haryana focus), and investments in brand building and retail expansion.
- Price increases have been largely passed on across categories; further price hikes are difficult to predict.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Optimized overall capacity configuration (reported capacity numbers recalibrated based on complex premium product mix/time required rather than absolute capacity reduction).
- Kenya JV plant capacity targeted at 50,000 pairs/day, operational in Q1 FY28.
- Capex per reimagined EBO store is roughly Rs 22 lakh.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Balance sheet remains strong and well-capitalized.
- Working capital inventory days increased by 10 days to 144 days due to lower-than-expected volume growth and inflation effect on inventory values.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Volatility in input costs (especially rubber) and ongoing geopolitical conflicts.
- Margin pressure from statutory wage hikes and indirect costs.
- Unorganized competitors benefiting from 90% BIS exemption and duty-free routing via Bangladesh.
- Volume sluggishness in price-sensitive mass-market Hawaii slippers.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Sameer Gupta (IIFL Securities) on price hikes vs. market share risk:** Management stated price hikes were taken gradually and accompanied by 150 new article launches, though acknowledge volume pressure in mass Hawaii slippers while sports/sneakers grew in double digits.
2. **Devanshu Bansal (Motilal Oswal) on volume dips despite structural tailwinds:** Management admitted performance has not met investor expectations, noting that changing consumer preferences, intense competition, and the shift toward differentiated/innovation-led products require strategic portfolio changes.
3. **Prerna Jhunjhunwala (Elara Capital) on the rationale for the Kenya manufacturing setup:** Management explained that heavy import duties and uncertain shipping timelines from India necessitated local manufacturing in Kenya to leverage East African trade pacts.
4. **Jay Gandhi (JM Financial) on working capital increase:** Management clarified that inventory days rose by 10 days (to 144 days) due to subdued volume growth relative to expectations and inflation impact on inventory pricing.
5. **Sameer Gupta on Reimagined Stores and organizational changes:** Management shared that 51 reimagined stores have been opened (costing ~Rs 22 lakh per store) with a separate sales team for Sparx created from April 1, 2026, to drive focus.

### One-line takeaway
Relaxo is navigating input cost volatility and mass-market volume pressure by pushing a premiumization, sneakerization, and retail expansion strategy, alongside establishing a local manufacturing footprint in Africa.
