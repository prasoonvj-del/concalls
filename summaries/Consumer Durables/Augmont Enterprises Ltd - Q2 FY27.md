# Augmont Enterprises Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-11 · Consumer Durables*

**Quarter:** Q2 & H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Strong PAT growth driven by margin expansion despite lower enterprise revenues due to a shift towards gold recycling.

### Key numbers
- **Core business (Augmont Enterprises Ltd / SPOT platform) PAT:** Rs 85 crore in Q2 FY27 (up 2.5x YoY from Rs 34 crore; 3.9x QoQ from Q1 FY27).
- **Core business EBITDA:** Rs 113 crore in Q2 FY27 (up from Rs 50 crore YoY).
- **Core business PAT margin:** 0.65% in Q2 FY27 (vs 0.18% in Q2 FY26).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** Rs 107 crore (75% higher than FY26 full year PAT of Rs 153 crore, reaching ~70% of it).
- **Consumer revenue growth:** Up 47% YoY.
- **Enterprise revenue growth:** Down 32% YoY.
- **Gold recycling contribution:** Increased from ~3% to 24.5% (an 8x YoY increase).
- **Gold for All segment revenue contribution:** Increased to 13% of overall revenue in Q2 FY27 (vs 7% in FY26).
- **Registered customers / members:** Registered customers increased to 52.3 million (from 50.6 million); registered SPOT members increased to 5,309 (from 5,271).
- **Network metrics:** Gold For All stores increased to 122 (from 118); fintech partnerships to 245 (from 231); retail point distribution centers to 751 (from 731); retail touch points to 5,558 (from 5,517).
- **IPO fresh issue proceeds:** Rs 625 crore raised, fully utilized for working capital as per RHP objectives.
- *(Note on record continuity: Investor deck noted a record Q2 FY27 PAT of ₹120 crore, while the transcript highlights core business PAT at ₹85 crore.)*

### Highlights
- Maiden earnings conference call following the company's listing in August 2026.
- Shift towards gold recycling caused enterprise revenues to drop by 32% (due to a longer working capital cycle of ~4 days vs <2 days for imports), but significantly expanded margins and profitability.
- Expanded SPOT delivery centers from 20 to 35 are planned over the next couple of years, with 10 new centers identified for launch in FY27.
- Aspiration to achieve LBMA-accredited refinery status.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management provided no specific numerical guidance on revenue growth or margins for FY27, stating they can vary based on metal prices, business mix, sourcing opportunities, and market conditions.
- Focus remains on profitable growth, efficient capital deployment, and maintaining healthy return ratios rather than revenue growth in isolation.
- Electronic Gold Receipts (EGR) platform expected to go live in the current quarter (Q3).

### Demand & industry commentary
- India’s gold demand is around 700 tonnes annually; Indian households hold approximately 31,000 tonnes (~$3.4 trillion).
- Consumer behavior is increasingly shifting towards exchanging old gold for new jewellery, boosting domestic scrap procurement.
- Digital gold market expected to grow to 55 tonnes (per Technopak report); lab-grown diamond market expected to reach Rs 6,300 crore by FY30.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Employee costs have decreased as a percentage from 0.06% in FY24 to 0.03% due to scale and amortization of technology costs.
- Higher margins achieved in Q2 FY27 due to scrap procurement (earning double margins on procurement and final sale) compared to imported doré bars.
- Working capital cycle increased from ~1.7 days to ~4 days due to higher share of scrap sourcing.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Entire Rs 625 crore primary IPO proceeds utilized for working capital.
- Refining capacity stands at 284 metric tonnes (utilization is traditionally lower on an annual basis due to the requirement for high daily capacity to prevent interest costs on expensive inventory).
- Diamond marketplace expanded to ~1 million stones listed from growers/dealers; 8,000 carats sold with 250 dealers.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Gold businesses are restricted from leveraging balance sheets via bank debt under RBI rules; growth is funded entirely through equity and internal accruals.
- Trade receivables increased due to last-day sales near quarter-end, which were realized in the first week of October (not expected to be a recurring bottleneck).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Working capital constraints and the inability to use bank leverage require careful capital rotation (every ~4 days).
- Metal price volatility and international-to-domestic price disparities impact import viability and revenue velocity.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Vedansh Dokania (ZTO Capital) on EGR timeline and impact:** Management stated it is early to quantify near-term revenue, but integration is underway and the product will go live in the current quarter. *(Did not provide specific topline/bottomline guidance).*
2. **Divyesh Mehta (Dinero Capserv) on scrap procurement sustainability vs imports:** Management replied that scrap sourcing will remain at healthy levels given consumer exchange trends, though mix depends on market disparity and pricing.
3. **Pakshal Jain (Axiom Research) on revenue drop vs working capital constraints:** Management explained that higher scrap sourcing requires blocking capital for 4 days instead of 2, reducing revenue velocity while expanding margins and total profitability.
4. **Nishant Gupta (Minerva Capital) on definition and margins of scrap:** Management clarified that scrap arrives as old jewellery or melted ingots from retail stores, which must be refined to remove impurities before resale, capturing spreads and refining fees.
5. **Rishabh Dharewa (Nirmal Bang) on refinery utilization:** Management explained that gold refiners globally maintain high daily capacity with lower annual utilization to instantly process expensive metal and avoid high interest carrying costs.

### One-line takeaway
Augmont leveraged a strategic shift toward domestic gold recycling in Q2 FY27, sacrificing top-line revenue for higher-margin operations and driving a sharp increase in core profitability.
