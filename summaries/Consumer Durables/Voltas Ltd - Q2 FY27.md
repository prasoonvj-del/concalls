# Voltas Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-12 · Consumer Durables*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Strong volume growth and market share gains in RAC, offset by margin compression due to severe commodity and currency inflation.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Voltbek EBITDA breakeven shifted to next financial year (FY28) due to severe commodity price shocks.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from strong summer-led optimism to managing unprecedented black swan cost inflation and price lock-in constraints during a traditionally lean quarter.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter: Detailed discussion on the severe impact of locked-in seasonal pricing with organized trade/e-commerce failing to absorb a sudden 18% cost inflation.
- Last quarter's promises: Atomberg JV and operational transition continue as planned; Sidra Bank guarantee cancellation fully upheld by the Court of Cassation.

### Key numbers
- Consolidated total income: Rs 2,746 crores vs Rs 2,412 crores YoY
- Profit before tax (PBT): Rs 83 crores vs Rs 54 crores YoY
- Net profit (PAT): Rs 48 crores vs Rs 32 crores YoY
- RAC volume growth: 32% YoY
- Unitary Cooling Products (UCP) revenue growth: 29% YoY
- YTD September 2026 RAC market share: 17.4% (up ~1.5 percentage points YoY; 4.2% lead over nearest competitor)
- Voltbek market share (YTD Sep 2026): 8.8% in washing machines, 7% in refrigerators; 14.9% in semi-automatic washing machines (Rank 2)
- Segment B carry-forward order book: Exceeding Rs 6,800 crores
- Sidra Bank guarantees canceled: QAR 167 million (equivalent to ~Rs 438 crores)
- Channel inventory: Around 3 weeks

### Highlights
- Achieved highest ever Q2 sales in both value and volume for Voltbek.
- Launched new marketing campaign "Voltas Beko Sense Toh Banta Hai."
- The Court of Cassation dismissed the main contractor's appeal regarding Sidra Bank guarantees, upholding the earlier favorable cancellation of QAR 167 million (~Rs 438 crores).
- Segment B secured orders across industrial infrastructure, electronics manufacturing, metro/tunnel projects, and data centers.
- Engineering Products and Services recorded high double-digit revenue growth driven by mining, construction equipment, and textile machinery.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects Voltbek to achieve EBITDA break-even sometime by next financial year (FY28).
- Management anticipates sequential performance improvement in Q3 and Q4, supported by an extended summer and normalization of price pass-throughs.
- Segment B Electro-Mechanical Projects margins expected to sustain closer to 4%–6% over the longer term.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Domestic consumer durables industry benefited from relatively stable demand, with an extended summer supporting cooling during a lean quarter.
- Commercial Air Conditioning saw stable demand across real estate, healthcare, hospitality, retail, manufacturing, and data centers.
- Home Comfort Products (air coolers, water heaters, fans) recorded healthy growth, while Commercial Refrigeration remained relatively flat.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Industry faced cumulative cost inflation of ~18% due to West Asia geopolitical crisis, ocean freight jumping 2x, and volatility in copper, aluminum, plastics, and currency.
- Voltas implemented a 14% price hike (including BEE table changes) and announced an additional 5%–7% price increase effective October (Q3) to fully cover cost inflation.
- Segment B net operational impact after provisions was around 2.5%–3%.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- South (Chennai) facility operates with a capacity of around 1.5 million units.
- In-house vs outsourced manufacturing mix targeted at around 75%–80% in-house and 20% outsourced on an annualized level.
- Focus remains on localization of critical components, including compressors.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Maintained a healthy liquidity position and disciplined working capital management.
- Focused on cash flow generation, inventory control, and receivables.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Global geopolitical uncertainties, commodity inflation, and currency volatility creating cost pressures.
- Long seasonal price lock-ins (4-6 months) with organized trade and e-commerce (35% of volume) preventing timely pass-through of sudden cost spikes.
- Softer demand in Commercial Refrigeration.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Natasha Jain (PhillipCapital) asked about operating leverage delays and UCP margin erosion.** Management stated that Q2 is seasonally softer and unexpected cost volatility during locked-in pricing caused temporary margin pressure, which will normalize with the new 5%–7% price hike.
- **Aditya Bhartia (Investec) asked why the RAC industry struggled to pass on costs compared to autos.** Management explained the extreme seasonality (70% sales in H1 calendar year) and long-term price lock-ins with 35% modern trade/e-commerce during a black swan cost spike.
- **Achal Lohade (Nuvama) asked about market share strategy versus profitability and Voltbek break-even.** Management reiterated that market share and absolute gross margin quantum are prioritized over percentage margins; Voltbek break-even is targeted for next financial year.
- **Sameer Gupta (IIFL Capital) asked about raw material procurement lock-ins and currency hedging.** Management clarified that while material prices are locked, payment currency movements and 2x ocean freight jumps caused unhedged deltas, and forward premiums are actively monitored for hedging.
- **Tanay Shah asked how Voltas is confident of margin recovery at low penetration.** Management cited distribution muscle, procurement advantages as the clear market leader, and an expanding replacement cycle from a 26 million installed base.
- **Dhruv Jain asked about Sri City facility under-absorption.** Management stated there was no material under-absorption in the first half and the facility will run on a 12-month basis.

### One-line takeaway
Voltas delivered 32% RAC volume growth and extended its market leadership to 17.4%, but margins were compressed by an unprecedented 18% cost inflation locked into seasonal trade contracts.
