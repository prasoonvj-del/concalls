# Stanley Lifestyles Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Consumer Durables*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27 (Quarter and half year ended 30th September 2026)
**Tone:** Mixed - Sequential revenue recovery and strong order book offset by extended conversion cycles, rupee depreciation pressure on margins, and negative same-store sales growth.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: maintained (1000 cr in 1000 working days target reiterated; double-digit revenue growth expected after stabilization)
- Tone and demand view: Improved slightly - sequential revenue growth of 7.8% QoQ, order book up YoY (Rs 60 cr vs Rs 53 cr), but sequentially down from Rs 68 cr in Q1; management expects conversion pipeline to strengthen from Q3/Q4 as handovers progress.
- New topics or risks: QCO implementation disruption and extension to Jan 2027; potential inclusion of sofas/recliners under QCO by Dec 17; fraud update - ex-CS arrested, confessed, potential Rs 2.83 cr reversal in Q3; builder tie-ups for mock-ups initiated; new CMO hired; brand architecture finalized (Superlative in 4 metros, Sofas & More in 80-100 cities); imports down 6% YoY in H1.
- Old ones that disappeared: Middle East geopolitical logistics disruption (B2B) not mentioned; fraud investigation was ongoing, now arrest made.
- Last quarter's promises delivered: Sri Lanka store opened (JV with Singer); Hyderabad Superlative store (72,000 sq ft) opened; 3 stores opened, 3 closed (vs 3 opened, 4 closed in Q1); amalgamation progressing; senior management hiring completed.

### Key numbers
- Revenue: Rs 107.06 cr (Rs 10,706 lakh) in Q2 FY27 vs Rs 105.42 cr YoY (+1.5%), +7.8% QoQ from Rs 99.35 cr; H1 FY27 Rs 206.41 cr vs Rs 214.09 cr YoY (-3.6%).
- EBITDA: Rs 19.89 cr (Rs 1,989 lakh), margin 18.6% vs 17.3% in Q1 FY27 (+130 bps QoQ); +15.5% QoQ.
- Order Book: Rs 60 cr (vs Rs 53 cr YoY, vs Rs 68 cr in Q1 FY27).
- Cash: ~Rs 170 cr (per analyst, not disputed).
- Exceptional Item: Rs 2.83 cr charge for fraud by ex-Company Secretary; potential reversal in Q3.
- Imports: Declined 6% YoY in H1 FY27.
- Manufacturing Capacity: Sufficient for 2x current revenue (~Rs 800+ cr).
- Store Count: 70 stores; 15-18% loss-making (mostly <18 months old); 3 opened, 3 closed in Q2.
- Superlative Store (Hyderabad): 72,000 sq ft, 60 days old, Rs 7-8 cr pipeline, target Rs 70-80 cr revenue in Year 2-3, target EBITDA >19-20% pre-Ind AS.
- Marketing Spend: 7-9% of sales.
- Opex Target: Mature stores 10-11% of revenue; new Superlative 15-16% initially.
- Same-Store Sales Growth (stores >3 years): Negative.

### Highlights
- Opened 3 stores: Colombo (Stanley Boutique, JV with Singer Sri Lanka PLC), Jaipur (Sofas & More), and one other; closed 3 mature stores with limited growth potential.
- Launched first "Stanley Superlative Living" flagship in Hyderabad (72,000 sq ft) for complete luxury home solutions; plan to replicate in Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore.
- QCO disruption (Aug 2026) caused import container hold-ups, front-loading, discounting; one-time extension to Jan 1, 2027; potential inclusion of sofas/recliners by Dec 17 deadline seen as structural opportunity for domestic B2B supply.
- B2B opportunity: ~800 retailers, 80% import-dependent for leather sofas/recliners; estimated market ~US$100 mn; Stanley's repetitive production facility ready to scale.
- Proposed amalgamation of subsidiaries/step-down subsidiaries progressing to simplify structure.
- Strategic sourcing: Best-cost country model, localization driving 6% YoY import reduction in H1.
- Fraud: Ex-Company Secretary arrested, confessed; Rs 2.83 cr exceptional charge potentially reversible in Q3.
- Brand architecture shift: "1 city, 1 mega store" for Stanley Superlative in 4 major metros; existing boutiques to convert to Sofas & More; hub-and-spoke model.
- Builder partnerships: Luxury builders now inquiring for Stanley-branded mock-ups; hiring Chief Business Development Officer.
- New CMO hired to modernize marketing (currently 7-9% of sales, print-heavy for 45+ demographic).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects delivery pipeline to improve from Q3/early Q4 as residential handovers progress.
- Revenue growth to reach double digits after a quarter or two of stabilization; 1,000 working days to Rs 1,000 cr target remains (approx 550 working days elapsed).
- Four Superlative stores planned in Hyderabad (done), Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore.
- Sofas & More expansion target: 80-100 cities.
- Superlative stores target Rs 70-80 cr revenue by Year 2-3, EBITDA >19-20% pre-Ind AS.
- No share buyback currently explored but will consider.
- Amalgamation to complete in due course.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Retail demand challenged by 12-18 month delays in premium/luxury residential project handovers; 80-85% customers are new home buyers waiting for possession.
- Conversion cycles extended; recovery taking longer than anticipated.
- Luxury housing pipeline robust in 6 major metros (80% of India's luxury housing).
- QCO implementation created temporary oversupply and discounting; long-term positive for organized domestic manufacturers.
- Potential QCO inclusion of sofas/recliners will halt individual imports and shift B2B dealer demand to domestic suppliers.
- Luxury customers from smaller towns (25% in Hyderabad/Bangalore) gravitate to metros for choice; supports metro-focused Superlative strategy.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Rupee depreciation pressured gross margin in Q2.
- Employee cost increased (H1: Rs 17.11 cr vs Rs 14.29 cr YoY) due to increments, NPD cost amortization, senior management hiring.
- Imports down 6% YoY in H1 via strategic sourcing/localization.
- Marketing spend maintained at 7-9% of sales.
- Target opex: 10-11% for mature stores (8-10% PAT); new Superlative stores 15-16% initially, declining to 12%.
- B2C gross margin higher than B2B; consolidated gross margin not disclosed.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Current manufacturing capacity can support 2x revenue (~Rs 800+ cr) with minor warehousing adjustments.
- Superlative store capex: up to Rs 20 cr (per prior guidance); Sofas & More ~Rs 2 cr.
- 3 stores opened in Q2 (Colombo, Jaipur, one other); 3 closed.
- Hyderabad Superlative operational (60 days), pipeline Rs 7-8 cr.
- No shop-in-shop model; focus on owned large formats.
- Expansion selective, regular performance assessment.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Cash position strong (~Rs 170 cr per analyst).
- No debt figures disclosed.
- Exceptional item Rs 2.83 cr for fraud; potential recovery in Q3.
- Franchisee model cash-and-carry (per prior call).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Residential project handover delays continue to defer conversions.
- Rupee depreciation impacting gross margins.
- QCO regulatory uncertainty (extension to Jan 2027, final product list pending).
- Fraud recovery not guaranteed (though management confident).
- Same-store sales growth negative for mature stores (>3 years).
- Employee cost inflation from senior hires and increments.
- High opex in new Superlative format during ramp-up (15-16% vs 10-11% target).

### One-line takeaway
Sequential recovery and QCO-driven structural opportunity provide visibility, but sustained margin improvement hinges on residential handover pickup, Superlative store ramp-up, and cost control amid negative same-store sales.
