# Stanley Lifestyles Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-20 · Consumer Durables*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Mixed - Revenue improved sequentially, but retail demand remains challenged by delayed residential project handovers and currency depreciation pressures on margins.

### What changed vs last quarter
- Guidance: Maintained double-digit revenue growth outlook and store network rationalization strategy.
- Tone and demand view: Shifted from geopolitical supply chain disruptions (previous quarter) to the impact of BIS Quality Control Orders (QCO) and extended retail conversion cycles driven by home handover delays.
- New topics or risks that weren't there last quarter, and old ones that disappeared: QCO implementation, front-loaded market inventory, and recovery of funds related to the former Company Secretary's fraud via an exceptional charge reversal appeared; Middle East logistics issues were not discussed.
- Last quarter's promises (targets, launches, capex, timelines) and whether they were delivered: Delivered on international expansion (Colombo store opened) and large-format experiential retail (Hyderabad Superlative Living store opened).

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** Rs 107.06 crore (Rs 10,706 lakh) vs Rs 105.42 crore (Rs 10,542 lakh) in Q2 FY26; up 7.8% QoQ from Rs 99.35 crore (Rs 9,935 lakh) in Q1 FY27.
- **H1 FY27 Revenue:** Rs 206.41 crore (Rs 20,641 lakh) vs Rs 214.09 crore (Rs 21,409 lakh) in H1 FY26.
- **EBITDA:** Rs 19.89 crore (Rs 1,989 lakh), up 15.5% QoQ from Rs 17.22 crore (Rs 1,722 lakh) in Q1 FY27.
- **EBITDA Margin:** 18.6%.
- **Order Book:** Rs 60 crore vs Rs 53 crore in the same period last year.
- **Imports:** Declined by 6% YoY during the half year.
- **Loss-making stores:** 15% to 18% of the current 70 stores.
- **Exceptional item charge:** Rs 2.83 crore remaining balance taken as a charge.

### Highlights
- **Store Expansion & Rationalization:** Opened 3 new stores and closed 3 matured stores with limited customer acquisition scope. Opened the first international store in Colombo, Sri Lanka, via a partnership with Singer Sri Lanka PLC. Entered Rajasthan with a Sofas & More store in Jaipur.
- **New Format Launch:** Opened the first "Stanley Superlative Living" large-format experience center (72,000 sq. ft.) in Hyderabad to consolidate smaller format stores into a complete home solutions destination.
- **Corporate Restructuring:** Progressing with the amalgamation of subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries into Stanley Lifestyles Limited to simplify corporate structure.
- **Fraud Recovery:** Management stated the former Company Secretary involved in financial fraud was arrested and confessed; the Rs 2.83 crore exceptional charge has potential for reversal in Q3.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Management** expects revenue to start growing at double digits going forward as structural changes stabilize and new formats mature.
- **Management** plans to replicate the mega-store consolidation strategy in four major A-class metros (Hyderabad, Mumbai, Delhi, and Bangalore).

### Demand & industry commentary
- **B2C Demand:** Conversion cycles remain extended as customers wait for residential property handovers and project completions in the premium and luxury residential segment.
- **B2B / Industry (BIS QCO):** Implementation of the Quality Control Order (QCO) in August 2026 disrupted the imported furniture market, causing containers to be held up at seaports and leading to excess market inventory and discounting. The government extended the deadline to January 1, 2027. Management sees a near-term opportunity to supply dealers shifting away from imports through its B2B business.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- **Gross Margins:** Put under pressure by the depreciation of the rupee.
- **Employee Expenses:** Increased due to annual increments, capitalization of employee costs for new product development (NPD) being amortized, and senior management hires/overlap.
- **Marketing Expenses:** Maintained between 7% and 9% (historically below double digits).
- **Store Recurring Opex:** Estimated at 12% to 13% per store (targeted to drop to 10%-11% at maturity).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- **Manufacturing Capacity:** Existing facilities are capable of supporting almost 2x current revenues without incremental capacity additions.
- **B2B Facility:** Set up for repetitive production with the ability to scale output quickly for dealer demand.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- **Cash Position:** Remains strong following investments in large-format store openings.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- **Operational:** Delays in residential project completions and handovers by builders affecting retail purchase timing.
- **Macro/Forex:** Depreciation of the rupee creating gross margin pressure.
- **Corporate Governance:** Past financial fraud by a former Company Secretary (resolved with arrest and expected recovery).

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Saket Kapoor (Kapoor Family Office) asked about order book status and revenue capability:** Management stated the order book improved to Rs 60 crore (vs Rs 53 crore YoY) and existing manufacturing capacity can support up to 2x revenues. Management did not dodge.
- **Madhur Rathi (Investments) asked about loss-making stores and buyback plans:** Management stated 15% to 18% of stores are loss-making (mostly under 18 months old), and while buybacks are a good thought, they are not currently being explored. Management did not dodge.
- **Naysar Shah (Private Investor) asked about same-store sales growth (SSSG) for older stores:** Management acknowledged that SSSG for stores older than three years is negative as COCO stores undergo structural transition. Management did not dodge.
- **Kaushik Poddar (Private Investor) asked about the performance of the Hyderabad Superlative Living store:** Management stated the store completed 60 days with massive footfalls, generating a prospective pipeline of Rs 7 crore to Rs 8 crore. Management did not dodge.
- **Saket Kapoor asked about store opex costs:** Management stated that recurring opex is around 12% to 13% per store and targets 10% to 11% at maturity. Management did not dodge.

### One-line takeaway
Sequential revenue recovery and large-format store expansions are underway, though retail growth continues to be impacted by delayed residential handovers and currency-led margin pressures.
