# Jungle Camps India Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-24 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Management highlighted revenue growth, expanded capacity, and a robust project pipeline while addressing seasonality and stability phases.

### Key numbers
- **Revenue from Operations:** Rs 5.97 crore (up 12% YoY from Rs 5.35 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Other Income:** Rs 0.28 crore (vs Rs 0.35 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Total Income:** Rs 6.25 crore (vs Rs 5.70 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Total Operating Expenses:** Rs 4.56 crore (vs Rs 3.89 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA:** Rs 1.69 crore (vs Rs 1.81 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **EBITDA Margin:** 27% (vs 32% in Q1 FY26).
- **Profit Before Tax (PBT):** Rs 0.62 crore (vs Rs 1.43 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 0.47 crore (vs Rs 1.13 crore in Q1 FY26).
- **PAT Margin:** 8% (vs 20% in Q1 FY26).
- **Basic EPS:** Rs 0.27 per share (vs Rs 0.66 in Q1 FY26).
- **Exceptional Expenses:** Rs 0.52 crore related to Parsili project write-off.
- **ADR (Average Daily Rate):** Rs 10,539 (up 5% YoY from Rs 10,072 in Q1 FY26).
- **RevPAR:** Rs 4,763 (up 9% YoY from Rs 4,353 in Q1 FY26).
- **Occupancy:** 45% (up 2% YoY from 43% in Q1 FY26).
- **Total Room Nights Occupied (Q1 FY27):** 3,907 (vs 3,736 in Q1 FY26).
- **Mathura Hotel Project Cost:** Rs 49 crore (Rs 17 crore internal/IPO, Rs 32 crore HDFC Bank loan at 8.14% interest).
- **Sheopur Fort Budget:** Rs 25 crore (Rs 10.5 crore internal accruals, Rs 17.5 crore HDFC Bank loan).
- **Devprayag Capex:** Maximum Rs 1 crore.
- **Kukru Jungle Camp Capex:** Rs 7-7.5 crore.
- **Projected Peak Debt (FY28):** Around Rs 50 crore.
- **Combined Monthly EMI (Mathura & Sheopur):** Around Rs 53 lakh per month (~Rs 6.5 crore annually) after a 2-year moratorium.

### Highlights
- Portfolio reached 12 properties spanning 4 states with 137 keys (3 owned, 5 leased/managed, 4 in pipeline).
- Added Palash Kothi (Bandhavgarh, 20 rooms) under a 5-year management agreement (13% revenue share) with soft opening in 2026.
- Secured Devprayag Tourist Rest House project in Uttarakhand (22 rooms) on lease.
- Discontinued the Parsili project due to regulatory constraints; received Rs 1.22 crore upfront premium refund (GST excluded), Rs 0.50 crore performance security return, and redeployed IPO proceeds to Shield 4.
- Expanded Mathura Hotel planned capacity from 60 to 105 rooms (partnered with IHG under Holiday Inn Express brand); targeted opening FY28.
- Commenced development of Sheopur Fort as a 60-key luxury boutique heritage hotel (35-40 rooms in phase 1); targeted opening FY28.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 (October to March) to be stronger than H1 due to wildlife season seasonality.
- Management expects ADR and revenue to improve in H2, targeting ~5% YoY growth in ADR for the full year.
- Top line and bottom line expected to improve this year driven by new additions (Devprayag and Bandhavgarh) and stabilization of recent properties.
- Mathura Hotel targeted to open in FY28 with estimated annual revenue of Rs 18-20 crore.
- Sheopur Fort targeted to open in FY28 with estimated annual revenue of at least Rs 12 crore in year 1 (operating 35 rooms).
- Kukru Jungle Camp targeted to be operational by late FY28 (March).

### Demand & industry commentary
- Wildlife safari season runs from October to December and January to March; April to June is slower due to high temperatures. Parks are generally closed in July, August, and September, though properties remain open.
- Wildlife occupancy typically peaks between 50-60% due to park closures and safari slot limits (e.g., Tadoba occupancy at 57%).
- The company's USP is naturalist-led, safari-focused experiences rather than pure luxury rooms, insulating it from direct competition impact.
- Holiday Inn Express brand chosen for Mathura due to high-volume, budget-traveler dynamics at the destination.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Operating expenses increased due to setup and stabilization costs of new properties like Peepal Restaurant.
- Staffing for wildlife properties averages ~2 employees per room, while Mathura (Holiday Inn Express model) is expected to operate with ~50 staff for 105 rooms, keeping costs lower.
- Long-term normalized EBITDA margin target is ~30%, though new properties temporarily moderate margins during initial stabilization (4-6 months).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Mathura Hotel: Rs 49 crore total cost (Rs 17 crore internal/IPO + Rs 32 crore debt).
- Sheopur Fort: Rs 25 crore total budget (Rs 10.5 crore internal + Rs 17.5 crore debt); initial phase 35-40 rooms.
- Devprayag: Max Rs 1 crore capex utilizing shifted furniture from other properties; 22 rooms.
- Kukru Jungle Camp: Rs 7-7.5 crore for 15-20 luxury tents; funded via internal approvals.
- Advanced discussions ongoing for properties in Panna, Satpura, Sariska, and Javai.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Total debt expected to reach around Rs 50 crore by FY28 upon completion of Mathura and Sheopur projects.
- HDFC Bank loans for Mathura and Sheopur carry a 2-year moratorium followed by a 7-year repayment period.
- Combined monthly EMI for Mathura and Sheopur will be ~Rs 53 lakh (~Rs 6.5 crore annually).
- Management does not anticipate any requirement for working capital debt.
- Parsili land dispute: Received Rs 1.35 crore; filed suit for the remaining balance. Written off ~Rs 50 lakh for a separate land dispute where forest permissions could not be secured.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Land disputes and forest clearance hurdles can cause project delays and write-offs (e.g., Parsili project cancellation, prior land dispute write-off of ~Rs 50 lakh).
- Seasonality heavily dictates wildlife tourism revenue, leaving H1 subdued.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Keshav Garg asked about low overall occupancy (40%) and how it impacts profitability.** Management explained that parks are closed for 3 months, capping wildlife occupancy potential at 50-60%, but performance will improve with new non-wildlife and all-season assets like Mathura.
2. **Keshav Garg questioned the low revenue contribution from leased/managed properties (Rukhad and Bison).** Management stated these properties are small (8 rooms, highway motel) with low tariffs, limiting their top-line contribution compared to core owned lodges.
3. **Vinay Ambekar inquired about debt repayment structure, moratorium, and downside revenue risks for Mathura.** Management stated loans carry a 2-year moratorium and 7-year repayment (~Rs 6.5 crore annual EMI), and even if Mathura generates Rs 12 crore instead of the estimated Rs 18-20 crore, debt service remains comfortable.
4. **Vinay Ambekar raised concerns regarding land acquisition risks and past write-offs.** Management explained that enhanced due diligence will now involve upfront engagement with Forest Departments and exhaustive revenue/court record checks.
5. **Vipul Makwana asked about the nature of "other income" and the status of Kukru.** Management clarified that other income represents interest earned on parked IPO funds, and Kukru is currently undergoing basic infrastructure and water pipeline work.

### One-line takeaway
Jungle Camps India is scaling its asset-light and owned portfolio via major pipeline projects like Mathura and Sheopur—funded by IPO proceeds and Rs 50 crore peak debt—while managing wildlife seasonality and tightening land due diligence.
