# Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-10-22 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered strong 30% RevPAR growth, 33% operating revenue growth, and 36% operating EBITDA growth, driven by robust luxury demand, pricing power, and broad-based premiumization.

### Key numbers
- **Operating Revenue:** Rs 419 crore (INR 4,119 million), up 33% YoY.
- **Operating EBITDA:** Rs 187.7 crore (INR 1,877 million), up 36% YoY; Margin at 46% (up 118 bps YoY).
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Rs 94.6 crore (INR 946 million), up 27% YoY.
- **RevPAR Growth:** 30% YoY for owned domestic hotels; India luxury market RevPAR grew 13%, while Leela outperformed at 17% (April-August 2026).
- **Average Daily Rate (ADR):** Up 30% YoY to Rs 25,046 for owned hotels.
- **Occupancy:** India headline occupancy at 69% (same-store city hotels at 74%, resort same-store occupancy near 60%).
- **Net Debt:** Rs 1,215 crore (INR 12,150 million) as of September 2026; Net Debt-to-LTM EBITDA improved to 1.4x.
- **Finance Costs:** Rs 38.4 crore (INR 384 million) for the quarter; down 37% to Rs 77.7 crore (INR 777 million) for H1.

### Highlights
- Suite occupancy rose by over 700 bps (blended suite occupancy in mid-40s paid).
- F&B revenue grew 21% YoY to Rs 145.6 crore (INR 1,456 million); Management fees grew 76% YoY to Rs 28.9 crore (INR 289 million).
- Direct brand website contribution to room revenue increased to 15.1% (vs 10.5% last year, 5.5% two years ago).
- Net Promoter Score (NPS) for H1 stood at an industry-leading 87.
- Leela Palace New Delhi recorded exceptional performance during the BRICS Summit.
- The Leela Coorg Forest Sanctuary (71 keys) rebranded on July 8, 2026, doubling ADR pre-acquisition.
- Mumbai BKC lease deed executed with MMRDA in September 2026 for a mixed-use project (250-key Leela Palace and 700k sq ft office in a 50:50 JV with Brookfield).

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains confident on delivering mid-to-high teens EBITDA growth for the balance of the year (H2), building on H1 outperformance.
- Pipeline projects on track for FY27 openings: The Leela Jaisalmer and The Leela Luxury Residences (Mumbai).
- Pipeline of over 1,000 keys under development across marquee destinations; targeting over 5,200 keys by FY30 (approx. 50:50 owned and managed split).
- Total capex for five contracted pipelines (Agra, Srinagar, Ayodhya, Ranthambore, Bandhavgarh) is Rs 1,432 crore; Rs 150 crore spent, expected Rs 450 crore by end of FY27, and Rs 600 crore next year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Luxury demand in India is broadening and deepening; supply remains constrained.
- Broad-based demand across corporate travel, leisure, celebrations, and events. International demand returning slowly with low double-digit growth.
- India's growing events and MICE calendar acts as a structural tailwind for rates.
- City hotels witnessing increased weekend occupancy from staycations; resorts becoming all-season destinations (Jaipur H1 occupancy well above 60%).

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Staff costs impacted by ESOP expenses expensed from the quarter and additional headcounts for new value drivers (Arq clubs, Azulian House). Excluding these, flow-through would be around 60%.
- Borrowing costs reduced by a further 30 bps from mid-October via renegotiations with lenders.
- Effective tax rate anticipated to be in the 20s in FY27 (H1 tax rate was 16% of PBT prior to JV share of profit).

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Mumbai BKC project: Planning to buy the entire FSI required to leverage an existing government scheme; requires shareholder approval for related-party investment. Long-term plan to divest office stake while keeping 50% hotel stake. Total capex commitment remains Rs 800 crore (Rs 400 crore equity, Rs 400 crore debt) subject to truing up via demerger proposals.
- Upcoming openings: Srinagar and Bandhavgarh in late CY27; Agra, Ayodhya, Ranthambore, and Sikkim in CY28; Tadoba in CY30.
- Dubai Palm Jumeirah asset currently operationally breaking even on EBITDA; planned for renovation in early FY28 for about a year before rebranding.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- International tourism demand for Dubai lagged, impacting ADRs and dragging PAT via share of loss from the 25% JV stake.
- Geopolitical uncertainties and global situations remain hard to predict.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Karan Khanna (Ambit Capital) on RevPAR sustainability and ADR ceilings:** Management noted that Indian luxury ADRs are significantly lower than regional peers (Bali, Singapore, Thailand), leaving a multi-decade runway for growth with strong demand and low inbound penetration.
2. **Prateek Kumar on margin flow-through and Coorg/ESOP impact:** Management explained that payroll rose due to new headcount for value drivers and new ESOP expenses, while Coorg is still in ramp-up; adjusting for these, flow-through is ~60%.
3. **Prateek Kumar on Mumbai BKC FSI buy-out capex:** Management clarified that the overall committed capex of Rs 800 crore remains the same for now, but will be trued up when the demerger and office divestment proposal goes to shareholders.
4. **Vikas Ahuja on non-BRICS ADR growth:** Management stated that even excluding Delhi's BRICS impact, overall portfolio ADR growth would have been in the high-teens.
5. **Murtuza (Kotak Securities) on Dubai asset timeline:** Management stated the hotel currently breaks even on EBITDA, will be handed over in early 2027, undergo a 12-month renovation starting early FY28, and be rebranded thereafter.
6. **Achal Kumar on second-half growth guidance:** Management clarified that the "high-teens" guidance applies specifically to H2 growth over a high base, implying a full-year FY27 outperforming initial mid-teens guidance.

### One-line takeaway
The Leela reported a stellar quarter with 33% revenue and 36% EBITDA growth, backed by sustained luxury pricing power, robust non-room revenue streams, and a strong expansion pipeline.
