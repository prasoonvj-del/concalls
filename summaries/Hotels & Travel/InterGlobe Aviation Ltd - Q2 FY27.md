# InterGlobe Aviation Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-04 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Cautious - Elevated fuel costs and a seasonally soft quarter led to a net loss of 19 billion rupees, though strong pricing actions and liquidity partially cushioned the impact.

### Key numbers
- **Total Income:** 243 billion rupees, up 24% YoY
- **Yield Growth:** Over 34% YoY
- **PRASK Growth:** 31% YoY
- **Load Factor:** Down 2 percentage points YoY
- **EBITDAR:** 25 billion rupees (margin around 11%), compared to 11 billion rupees (margin 6%) YoY
- **Net Loss:** 19 billion rupees, compared to a loss of 26 billion rupees YoY (excluding MTM and hedging, net loss of ~7 billion rupees vs. net profit of ~1 billion rupees YoY)
- **CASK ex-fuel ex-forex MTM:** INR 3.45, up ~15% YoY
- **Fuel CASK:** Up 69% YoY
- **Free Cash:** ~399 billion rupees (Total cash: 527 billion rupees, including restricted cash of 128 billion rupees)
- **Fleet:** 442 aircraft at quarter-end (22 inducted, 10 redelivered, 2 damp-leased aircraft redelivered)
- **Ancillary Revenue per Passenger:** Increased from INR 525 to INR 590 YoY
- **Cargo Revenue Growth:** 37% YoY

### Highlights
- Management noted exceptionally high fuel costs (Singapore jet fuel index 60% higher YoY) and a seasonally soft monsoon period falling between summer and festive travel peaks.
- Decisive capacity rationalization and pricing actions supported stronger revenue performance, with industry seats reduced by 5%.
- Concluded widebody damp-lease operations with Norse effective November 1 to save costs amid high fuel prices and currency depreciation; gained long-haul operating experience ahead of A350 deliveries.
- IndiGo BluChip loyalty program completed two years, approaching 20 million members with over 30 partners.
- Inducted all 22 aircraft during the quarter through GIFT City.

### Guidance & outlook
- **Q3 FY27 Capacity:** Expected to grow in the early single digits YoY.
- **Q3 FY27 PRASK:** Expected to grow by more than 25% YoY based on October trends.
- **FY27 Capacity:** Expected to grow in the early single digits.
- **FY27 CASK ex-fuel ex-forex MTM:** Expected to increase in the high single digits to early double digits range.
- **FY28 Capacity Growth:** Maintained at mid-teens for now, but kept under review.
- **Long-term ASK Guidance:** Management remains committed to the 300 billion ASK target by FY30.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Global aviation industry faces uncertainty from geopolitical events and elevated fuel costs.
- Industry seat deployment was approximately 5% lower YoY during the quarter.
- Domestic demand remains strong, particularly going into the festive season in Q3; international demand also holding up.
- Long-term aviation growth foundations in India remain robust, supported by infrastructure investment and increasing connectivity.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Singapore jet fuel index was 60% higher YoY; crack spreads averaged around 60%-65% this year compared to historical levels of ~20%.
- Rupee depreciated by 9% over the past 12 months, increasing USD-denominated expenses.
- Capacity rationalization led to under-absorption of fixed costs across the operating fleet.
- Non-fuel CASK inflation driven by currency depreciation and regular contractual escalations; steady-state ex-fuel CASK inflation expected in the mid-single-digit range excluding FX impact.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Q2 domestic ASKs were broadly flat, international ASKs down ~2.9%.
- Q3 capacity cuts will focus more on international operations.
- Announced Dar es Salaam as a new international destination with daily direct services from Mumbai beginning in November.
- Committed to Airbus fleet orders and A350 deliveries in 2028, despite minor OEM delays. Using XLRs to maintain Amsterdam slots following widebody lease exit.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Strong liquidity maintained with total cash of 527 billion rupees (free cash of 399 billion rupees, restricted cash of 128 billion rupees).

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Elevated fuel costs and exceptionally high crack spreads.
- Volatility from geopolitical events affecting travel patterns and operating costs.
- 9% depreciation of the Indian rupee against the US dollar increasing USD-denominated costs.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Binay Singh (Morgan Stanley) on Q3 load factors and widebody exit impact:** Management stated Q3 load factors are expected to be flat YoY, and widebody removal will be accretive to spreads. *(Management answered directly)*
2. **Arvind Sharma (Citi) on fuel CASK decline QoQ and winter schedule/Navi Mumbai:** Management explained Q1 had a government fuel cap (removed June 8), and Q2 benefited from VAT reductions and lower SingJet benchmarks. Navi Mumbai is a strategic long-term investment. *(Management answered directly)*
3. **Krupashankar (Avendus) on balancing low-cost core with wide-body ambitions and fare surge regulations:** Management stated short-haul low-cost operations will not be compromised for long-haul feed. On government fare intervention, they would look to participate if terms make sense. *(Management answered directly)*
4. **Prateek Kumar (Jefferies) on CASK ex-fuel guidance reconciliation and fleet review:** Management attributed lower H2 cost growth expectations to capacity utilization catching up. They confirmed no review or cancellation of the Airbus fleet order. *(Management answered directly)*
5. **Achal Kumar (HSBC) on domestic Tier 2/3 demand and ancillary/premium product performance:** Management sees no need for major domestic restructuring due to agile capacity management. Ancillary revenue per passenger rose from INR 525 to INR 590, and the Stretch premium product is showing encouraging early results. *(Management answered directly)*

### One-line takeaway
IndiGo reported a net loss of 19 billion rupees in Q2 FY27 due to severe fuel cost inflation and seasonal softness, but strong pricing discipline, robust PRASK growth, and solid liquidity position the airline well for the festive season.
