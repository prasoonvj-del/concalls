# Hotels & Travel - Travel services & online travel: what managements are saying (Q2 FY27)

## Big picture
The Q2 FY27 earnings season for the travel services and online travel sector reflects a bifurcated environment. While domestic travel, rail, bus segments, and leisure cruising continue to exhibit strong structural growth, international travel and flight segments faced considerable headwinds from West Asian geopolitical conflicts, high airfares, and capacity constraints. OTAs and travel platforms are aggressively investing in alternative segments—such as hotels, non-airport lifestyle services, and cruises—to diversify revenue streams while navigating margin pressures from marketing, discounts, and affiliate commissions.

## Demand outlook - consensus and divergence
* **Consensus:** Managements across the board agreed that international outbound travel and flight bookings were pressured during the quarter due to geopolitical tensions (particularly in the Middle East), high airfares, and elevated visa rejection rates. Furthermore, Q3 festive demand was noted by several players to be a shade softer or more volatile than anticipated.
* **Divergence:** 
  * **Cruises & Domestic Leisure:** Waterways Leisure reported surging cruise demand with high load factors (91.23% in H1), targeting a significant increase in wedding dates. Thomas Cook and Le Travenues highlighted robust domestic travel and booming hotel demand.
  * **Flights vs. Trains/Buses:** While Le Travenues and Yatra cited a contraction or slowdown in domestic flight volumes (industry down ~6% in Q2), ground transport (trains and buses) and corporate bookings remained bright spots. Yatra and Le Travenues both outperformed broader domestic aviation volume trends.
  * **Regional Performance:** TBO Tek noted that Europe has become its largest source market with growth exceeding 30% YoY, whereas its Middle East business grew at a more modest 10% due to regional conflicts.

## Costs, raw materials & margins
* **Fuel & Operating Pressures:** Waterways Leisure noted a sharp rise in fuel costs per APCD (Rs 2,471 vs Rs 1,434 YoY), prompting fuel surcharges ($15-$20 per person daily) and speed optimization. LGT Global and Thomas Cook also cited Middle East fuel price volatility and operational cost impacts.
* **Take Rates & Commissions:** TBO Tek experienced a decline in air take rates to ~2% due to lower incentives and refund income. Le Travenues funded elevated flight discounts to cushion high airfare deltas for budget users, while Yatra reported an increase in "other expenses" driven by temporary affiliate commission accounting treatments designed to protect market share.
* **Segment Investments:** Le Travenues and Thomas Cook (via Sterling Holidays) absorbed near-term contribution losses and one-off costs (such as Sterling's GST ITC cost impact) to scale their hotel accommodation segments.

## Capex & capacity
* **Asset Expansion & Fleet Additions:** Waterways Leisure expanded its fleet with the early delivery of *Cordelia Sky*, increasing capacity by ~126%. 
* **Asset-Light & Franchise Models:** Thomas Cook's Sterling Holidays added 7 resorts in a single quarter to cross 4,000 keys via management contracts and revenue shares. LGT Global and Le Travenues pursued capital-efficient omnichannel expansions and direct hotel tie-ups (Le Travenues crossing 12,000 directly contracted hotels) rather than heavy greenfield capex.
* **Strategic Partnerships:** Companies like Yatra and LGT Global continued deploying capital into tech platforms, SME tools (Travel Pro, RECAP), and strategic B2B/MICE acquisitions (Traflix and Yajat by Lgt Global).

## What changed since last quarter
* **Yatra Online:** Management formally stopped providing forward guidance due to macro volatility, shifted tone from "resilient" to "challenging," and addressed a sharp spike in affiliate commission accounting treatment within "other expenses."
* **Guidance Shifts:** TBO Tek and Yatra highlighted a tougher-than-expected Q3 outlook characterized by heightened volatility, contrasting with the steady optimism in domestic leisure expressed by Waterways Leisure and Thomas Cook.

## What to watch next quarter
* Post-festive season volume sustainability in rail, bus, and domestic air travel.
* Normalization of fuel prices and the commercial stabilization of newly added capacities (such as Waterways Leisure's *Cordelia Sky* and Sterling's new resort keys).
* Resolution or easing of West Asian geopolitical conflicts and their impact on international MICE, outbound travel, and regional take rates.

## Company one-liners
* **DreamFolks Services:** Reported revenue contraction and net losses while scaling non-airport lifestyle, golf, and global lounge networks.
* **LGT Global Hospitality:** Delivered 35% revenue growth and doubled PAT in H1 FY27, expanding its footprint through specialized B2B platform acquisitions.
* **Le Travenues Technology:** Achieved resilient Q2 GTV growth led by buses and trains while aggressively scaling its hotel segment.
* **TBO Tek:** Navigated regional volatility and geopolitical headwinds through operational leverage and luxury segment integration (Classic Vacations).
* **Thomas Cook (India):** Delivered a steady Q2 performance with strong growth in India travel and financial services offsetting international West Asian headwinds.
* **Waterways Leisure Tourism:** Posted 31% revenue growth and expanded cruise capacity ahead of schedule with the delivery of *Cordelia Sky*.
* **Yatra Online:** Demonstrated core market share resilience in corporate and air travel despite margin compression from MICE headwinds and affiliate expenses.
