# Kamat Hotels (India) Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-06 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q2/H1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Decisive turnaround in profitability, strong EBITDA growth, and significant margin expansion despite a seasonally soft quarter.

### Key numbers
- Revenue (Q2 FY27): Rs. 86 crore (up 12% YoY)
- EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Rs. 22 crore (up 131% YoY, more than doubling)
- EBITDA margin (Q2 FY27): 26% (up 1300 bps YoY)
- PAT (Q2 FY27): Rs. 5 crore (vs. loss of Rs. 2 crore in Q2 FY26)
- PAT margin (Q2 FY27): 6%
- Revenue (H1 FY27): Rs. 179 crore (up 11% YoY)
- EBITDA (H1 FY27): Over Rs. 50 crore (up 67% YoY)
- EBITDA margin (H1 FY27): 28%
- PAT (H1 FY27): Rs. 15 crore (PAT margin at 8%)
- Same-store revenue (Q2 FY27): Up 31% YoY to Rs. 85 crore
- Same-store EBITDA (Q2 FY27): Up 98% YoY to Rs. 23 crore (27% margin, up 900 bps)
- Portfolio occupancy: Increased from 50% to 58% (up 800 bps)
- RevPAR growth: Improved by 20%
- Gross debt (as of September 30, 2026): Rs. 96 crore
- Cash and cash equivalents (as of September 30, 2026): Rs. 65 crore
- Net debt (as of September 30, 2026): Rs. 31 crore
- Net debt to EBITDA: 0.23x (down from 0.52x in the previous quarter)

### Highlights
- Operating leverage strongly aided profitability, with each incremental revenue rupee flowing faster to the bottom line.
- Same-store metrics excluded IRA Mumbai and Orchid Shimla from Q2 FY26, and IRA Bhavnagar from Q2 FY27 (due to lease expiry, conversion to management contract, and new addition respectively).
- Subsequent to the quarter end, the company repaid a Rs. 30 crore loan to Axis Finance Limited.
- Continued execution of sustainability initiatives, including operating a 100% EV hotel fleet and upgrading to electric kitchens to mitigate utility shocks.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects H2 FY27 (the strongest half of the year) to be robust and encouraging, with heightened revenue and EBITDA growth compared to the previous H2.
- KHIL 3.0 Vision for FY2030: Expand footprint from 10 to 11 states; portfolio from 24 to 31 properties; inventory from 1,900+ keys to 3,000+ keys; and portfolio-level ARR target of Rs. 8,000 (a 33% lift from the existing Rs. 6,000).
- Management projects cumulative 5-year EBITDA around Rs. 750 crore.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Indian hospitality delivered resilient results despite challenges including war, fuel shortages, monsoon seasonality, erratic aviation, and West Asian geopolitical tensions.
- Q2 typically accounts for a seasonally soft monsoon period with lower spending sentiment (due to Pitrupaksh, etc.), but performance was excellent.
- Demand drivers include sustained leisure travel, strong spiritual tourism, and ongoing benefits from the premiumization trend. Large-format MICE and weddings provided steady weekday/weekend support.
- Foreign tourist recovery is gradual; domestic tourists are spending better than many foreign tourists on shoestring budgets, though Goa (Orchid Passaros) saw robust foreign demand.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Average Room Rate (ARR) growth was flat at around 3% for the quarter, but higher occupancy (50% to 58%) drove revenue and EBITDA expansion.
- Stringent cost controls implemented across head costs to maximize margins.
- Transitioning kitchens to 100% electric helps protect against PNG/LPG price volatility.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- New properties like Gwalior (under lease, opening Dec/Jan), Dwarka, and Nasik (under management) will add 266 keys, alongside upcoming properties in Rishikesh and Dehradun.
- Asset-light expansion model prioritized: management contracts charge a flat 6% of top line (GOP-based incentive fees eliminated). Leased properties target 80-100+ keys for viability due to GST structure challenges on smaller 40-50 room hotels.
- New properties (such as Bhavnagar and Rishikesh) typically take about one year to stabilize.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Net debt reduced to Rs. 31 crore as of September 30, 2026, with a subsequent Rs. 30 crore debt repayment made post-quarter end.
- Company maintains a net-cash-positive philosophy and aims to retire debt faster than required.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- GST structure on smaller hotels (where input tax cannot be fully recovered above a 5% customer charge) creates sandwiching issues that challenge the viability of 40-50 room hotels.
- External macro risks include geopolitical tensions and aviation erraticness.

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Anil (Individual Investor) on EBITDA margin sustainability:** Management explained that occupancy rose from 50% to 58%, and fixed/variable cost controls at hotels like Pune and Bhubaneswar drove the margin expansion, which remains sustainable.
2. **Rohit Dhawan (Individual Investor) on IRA by Orchid performance:** Management clarified that the drop in metrics was primarily due to the exclusion of IRA Mumbai (which exited the portfolio) and a minor occupancy hit in Ayodhya.
3. **Devansh Jain on expansion models and ROI:** Management detailed why 40-50 room hotels lack economic viability for leases under current GST rules, explaining that they strictly target 80-100+ keys for leases and 40+ keys for asset-light management contracts (flat 6% top-line fee).
4. **Guneet Singh (Countercyclical PMS) on Mumbai hotel land royalty and deposits:** The analyst questioned the 1994 interest-free deposit (~Rs. 60-80 crore) and the recent royalty increase to 5% paid to promoter entity Plaza Hotels. Management acknowledged the concern, stating they are evaluating potential amalgamation/fair valuation structures to balance minority shareholder interests.

### One-line takeaway
Kamat Hotels delivered a breakout Q2 FY27 with a 131% surge in EBITDA and 1300 bps margin expansion, supported by higher occupancy and a near-zero net debt balance sheet heading into the seasonally stronger H2.
