# Travel Food Services Ltd - Q1 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-08-21 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q1 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Delivered double-digit growth in sales and profitability despite temporary flat passenger traffic caused by Middle East conflict disruptions.

### Key numbers
- System-wide sales: Rs 8.4 billion (+18% YoY)
- Consolidated revenue from operations: Rs 4.5 billion (+20.6% YoY)
- Consolidated Profit After Tax (PAT): Rs 1.3 billion (+35.6% YoY)
- PAT margin: 28.5% (vs 25.3% YoY)
- EBITDA: Rs 1.6 billion (+11% YoY)
- EBITDA margin: 35.8% (moderated due to higher employee and operating costs from newly commissioned airports/initiatives)
- Consolidated Like-for-Like (LFL) sales growth: 4.2%
- System-wide LFL sales growth: 0.8% (or ~7% YoY excluding specific impacted markets like Mumbai, Guwahati, and South India)
- Net contract gains: 15.9% YoY system-wide (20.2% consolidated)
- Reported gross profit: Rs 3.9 billion (margin: 85.7%); Adjusted gross profit: Rs 3.65 billion (margin: ~81% after adjusting Rs 223 million of lounge aggregation costs)
- Consolidated cash balance: Approximately Rs 9.7 billion (debt-free balance sheet)

### Highlights
- Commenced operations at Noida International Airport and launched passenger services (Elite Assist meet-and-greet/porter services).
- Added 87 travel QSR outlets and 2 lounges over the last 12 months, taking the system-wide footprint to 580 outlets and 153 brands across 21 airports.
- Over 50 outlets currently under development.
- PAT included a benefit of Rs 131 million from the write-back of a GST provision following a favorable rectification order.
- Set up entities in Dubai and Indonesia to pursue regional Asian and Middle Eastern opportunities.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management expects a strong H2 FY27 for passenger traffic and restoration of long-haul international routes by September/October.
- New units under development and ramp-up are expected to provide a meaningful uplift to revenue and earnings over the next 12 to 18 months.
- No specific quantitative guidance numbers provided for FY27.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Passenger traffic was flat YoY globally/domestically for the quarter due to Middle East conflict disruptions impacting international routes, whereas domestic traffic saw robust moments (including the highest-ever single month of domestic air traffic in May).
- Long-term drivers of Indian aviation remain firmly intact.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Employee costs and other expenses increased due to annual compensation revisions, onboarding manpower for newly opened outlets (Noida, Cochin), and pre-operating expenses.
- Lounge aggregation costs of Rs 223 million were reclassified into cost of sales (previously in other expenses).
- Impact of the new labor code is estimated to be minimal (below Rs 8 crore to Rs 10 crore).
- Fixed occupancy costs (CAM charges, etc.) comprise roughly 8% to 10% of total costs.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Capital allocation remains prudent; expansion is funded internally via strong cash generation.
- Expanding into highways (Wayside Amenities / WSA plan) is targeted as a medium-to-long-term strategy.
- International expansion is currently focused more on Asia (Malaysia, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Dubai) than the Middle East until regional conditions normalize.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- The company remains completely debt-free with a consolidated cash balance of around Rs 9.7 billion as of June 30, 2026.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Traffic disruptions from geopolitical tensions/conflicts (Middle East) impacting international routes and high-spending consumers.
- Traffic migration/softer LFL performance in specific airports like Mumbai, Guwahati, and South Indian markets.

### Notable analyst Q&A
- **Akshay (ICICI Securities) on LFL drivers:** Management clarified that LFL is a blend of calibrated pricing, menu innovations, premiumization, and bundles, targeting a delta 5% to 7% above passenger traffic.
- **Achal Kumar on Second Quarter outlook & new units:** Management stated that Q2 traffic trends up to August mirror Q1, but H2 should see a strong bounce back. The 50 units under development are a mix across existing and new airports (like Navi Mumbai and Bhogapuram).
- **Aachal Pal on contract renewals:** Management noted that Delhi T3 concession expires September 30, 2026 (bidding via a new GMR joint venture), followed by Chennai and Kolkata in March 2027 / early Q1 FY28.
- **Navin on JV vs. Consolidated business:** Management explained that JVs include both domestic operator partnerships and international business (Malaysia, Hong Kong); JV and non-JV models have similar margin profiles upon maturity.
- **Vansh Gupta on concession escalations:** Management confirmed that minimum guarantees (MG) have yearly escalations, while revenue share percentages remain constant throughout the life of the contract.

### One-line takeaway
Travel Food Services delivered double-digit sales and profit growth in Q1 FY27 despite flat passenger traffic caused by geopolitical headwinds, supported by aggressive network expansion and a robust debt-free balance sheet.
