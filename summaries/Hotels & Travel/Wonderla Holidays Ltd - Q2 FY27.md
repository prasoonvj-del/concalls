# Wonderla Holidays Ltd - Q2 FY27 concall
*Filed on BSE 2026-11-07 · Hotels & Travel*

**Quarter:** Q2 FY27
**Tone:** Positive - Revenue and EBITDA grew strongly YoY, driven by the Chennai park ramp-up, existing parks, and resorts.

### Key numbers
- **Q2 FY27 Revenue from operations:** INR116.57 crores (up 45% YoY) / Income grew 42% YoY to INR126 crores.
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA (including other income):** INR30.11 crores (up 90% YoY).
- **Q2 FY27 EBITDA Margin:** 24% (expanded from 18% YoY).
- **Q2 FY27 PAT:** INR5 lakhs (compared to a loss in Q2 last year).
- **H1 FY27 Revenue from operations:** INR359.2 crores (up 45% YoY) / Income grew 41% YoY to INR378 crores.
- **H1 FY27 EBITDA (including other income):** INR152.1 crores (up 47% YoY).
- **H1 FY27 PAT:** INR72.8 crores (up 43% YoY).
- **Chennai Park Q2:** 1.90 lakh visitors, INR28 crores in revenue (contributed ~INR10.37 crores to EBITDA increase).
- **Chennai Park H1:** 4.32 lakh visitors, ~INR73 crores in revenue.
- **Existing Parks Q2 Footfalls & Revenue:** Footfalls up 6%, revenue up 8% YoY. Bengaluru footfalls 2.13 lakh (up 9%), Kochi 2.02 lakh (up 6%), Hyderabad 0.99 lakh (up 7%), Bhubaneswar 20,000 (down 20% YoY).
- **Q2 ARPU:** Average ticket price (ATP) declined 3% YoY to INR983; non-ticket revenue per guest up 11% YoY to INR510; average revenue per user (ARPU) up 1% YoY to INR1,491.
- **H1 ARPU:** Average ticket price INR1,192; non-ticket spend per guest up 16% YoY to INR559; ARPU up 5% YoY to INR1,750.

### Highlights
- Chennai operations continued to ramp up successfully, contributing ~73% of the Q2 EBITDA increase and ~58% of H1 EBITDA growth.
- Bengaluru and Kochi delivered their best-ever Q2 performance; Hyderabad delivered its best-ever H1.
- Resorts business delivered its best-ever Q2, growing 57% YoY.
- Other income for Q2 stood at INR9.1 crores and for H1 at INR18.6 crores, primarily comprising gains from investments.
- Spend per guest grew 11% in Q2 and 16% in H1, driven by value-added and premium offerings.

### Guidance & outlook
- Management remains bullish on prospects for H2 FY27 and the rest of the financial year.
- Management expects Chennai park to achieve optimal utilization and sustainable profitability in 2 to 3 years.
- Management aims to conclude some deals for 2 to 3 new locations before the end of the financial year.

### Demand & industry commentary
- Q2 footfalls at Bhubaneswar park were impacted by weather disruptions (extreme cyclones and 900% growth in rains affecting 20 out of 30 districts).
- Non-ticket revenue continues to trend toward global standards of 30% plus of total ARPU.
- Lean season demand is being addressed through price-led, cohort-led interventions and larger group bookings.

### Costs, margins & raw materials
- Depreciation increased by INR10 crores in Q2 and INR21.5 crores in H1 due to the commencement of operations at Chennai Park.
- Tax expenses increased by INR2.23 crores in Q2 (including INR1.12 crores of prior period taxes) and by INR5 crores in H1.

### Capex, capacity & expansion
- Management expects a typical large park land parcel to cost around INR200 crores in a city like Bombay (compared to INR100 crores for Chennai).
- Management aims to look at at least 1 big project in the next 1-2 years, and maybe 1 Tier-2 city or 2 large cities.
- Parks have roughly 40% to 50% unutilized land available for future expansion (lesser in Hyderabad).
- All parks have captive solar power operations.

### Balance sheet, debt & working capital
- Management stated they are not looking to do any further equity dilution and intend to fund future capex through internal cash flows, potentially taking some debt at a later point.

### Risks & concerns flagged
- Land acquisition and government matters involve usual delays.
- Seasonal monsoons impact park footfalls (e.g., Northeast monsoon in Chennai, extreme cyclones in Bhubaneswar).

### Notable analyst Q&A
1. **Shamit Ashar (Ambit) asked about Chennai Park's lower ticket price in Q2 versus previous quarters and Bangalore.** Dheeran Choudhary explained that Q2 in Chennai had a higher skew of group footfalls (which are more discounted) compared to retail; ARPU is expected to stabilize and grow similarly to Cochin and Hyderabad over time.
2. **Sonal Rajpal (ICICI Direct) asked about Chennai and Bhubaneswar Q3 footfall impact due to monsoons/cyclones.** Dheeran Choudhary stated Chennai is experiencing its first monsoon (mid-October to end-November) so full impact will be assessed year-end; Bhubaneswar was impacted by extreme weather and cyclones.
3. **Vivek Kumar (Geometric Securities) asked about capital requirements and timelines for new large parks.** Arun Chittilappilly stated land costs could be ~INR200 crores in Bombay, and while they want to do 3 parks in 3-4 years, it is hard to give a definitive timeline due to land acquisition and government delays.
4. **Keshav Garg (Counter Cyclical PMS) asked about utilizing parks at night for banquets/weddings and EsselWorld acquisition.** Dheeran Choudhary noted nighttime events are unviable due to outskirts locations and required maintenance. Arun Chittilappilly stated EsselWorld is not looking to sell, and Wonderla prefers greenfield projects over takeovers.
5. **Rohit (ithoughtPMS) asked about long-term footfall growth and capacity additions in Bangalore.** Dheeran Choudhary highlighted that post-COVID pent-up demand was an anomaly, and the company focuses on input metrics (ARPU and sustainable 8-9% CAGR revenue growth); capacity is being added via flagship global rides and timing optimization.
6. **Anurag Jain (Individual Investor) asked about capital funding for future new parks.** Arun Chittilappilly confirmed no further equity dilution is planned, and future capex will be funded via internal cash flows, possibly supplemented by debt.

### One-line takeaway
Wonderla Holidays delivered a strong Q2 and H1 FY27—marked by a 42% YoY income growth and 90% EBITDA growth—anchored by the successful ramp-up of the Chennai park, robust resort performance, and steady momentum across mature existing parks.
